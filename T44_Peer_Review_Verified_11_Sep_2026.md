# T44 — Peer review against the primary tender bundle
**Reid v Commissioner, NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission — ART 2023/3363 (and 3361, 4494)**
Prepared 11 September 2026. Every point below was checked against the actual pages of `20240613 Tender Bundle [Finalised] 23004627.pdf` (1,506 PDF pages) and the primary briefs held in the NDIS Case folder. Scanned statements were OCR'd; quotes from OCR were read against context and should be eyeballed on the page before filing.

**Citation convention:** "p." = printed bundle page (bottom-centre stamp). PDF page = printed page + 34 in the T29–T63 range (+10 for T1–T10, +18 for T11–T28, because of the 527A–C, 527D–E, 532A–C and 761A–P inserts).

---

## Bottom line (read this first)

1. **T44 is what the brief thought it was, and less.** It is the Commission's own working summary — 37 complaint rows and 33 NDIA-statement rows — attached as Attachment M to the 17 Nov 2022 NOI brief EC22-002103. It is not evidence of anything except what the Commission had collected and how it labelled it.
2. **It was before both decision-makers, by incorporation.** Simon's decision brief EC22-002521 (21 Dec 2022) says "please refer to EC22-002103 to review evidence" and repeats para 14's reference to "Attachment M (pages 9-13)". The internal reviewer lists EC22-002103 among the materials before the original decision-maker (IR reasons ¶11(f)(i)).
3. **None of the Simon-personal allegations in T44 (whisky/sleeping tablets, drug use, $140k salaries, "Suitability – Simon Reid") was put to Simon or relied on** in the NOI (T46), the banning order reasons (D23 41713) or the internal review reasons (D23 56430). The stated basis for his ban was derivative: sole director of Hybrid Hive + joint bank signatory + "likely benefited" (EC22-002103 ¶25; EC22-002521 ¶9).
4. **The Commission's own internal reviewer has already conceded the two defects the brief asks about.** IR reasons ¶32: the original decision-maker "at times (a) attributed liability to Mr Reid for conduct which occurred during a period of time where he was not the director of Hybrid or FFG … and (b) considered the same set of facts (or allegations) twice given they were variously expressed in different pieces of evidence." ¶33: "For those reasons, I have not considered or given weight to such material." That is misattribution and double-counting, admitted, in the Commission's own reasons.
5. **T44 omits favourable evidence that sits in the same bundle:** Meghan Goodwin's sworn statement — "Simon never was involved with providing the services. I think he was in finance" (T18 ¶5, p.599); Danica Hall's Commission statement — "Simon being an absent boss most of the time" (T9.11, p.225); Jonathan Shapiera — happy with FFG/HH services, "they have improved my lifestyle" (T19 ¶¶11, 22, pp.631, 633). None appears in T44.
6. **Strongest, safest use: reliability/weight + targeted production, and individualised suitability.** Improper purpose gets nothing from T44 that EC22-002103 ¶¶31–33 does not already give. Procedural fairness is real but moderate (material was in the pack, never put, but not visibly relied on). T44 is a map and a cross-examination tool, not a silver bullet — the 33 underlying statements are in the bundle and the Tribunal will read those, not T44.

## 13. Legal significance — assessed

**Supported (strong):**
- Structured adverse matrix assembled — yes (T44 on its face).
- Contains NDIA-derived complaints and statements — yes (7 PCI rows + 33 statements; ¶31).
- Source material necessary to test reliability — yes; the summary was written before exhibits were complete ("Need exhibits attached from NDIA").
- Commission-inserted classifications — yes ("Benefiting from fraud – V and S Reid"; "Suitability – Simon Reid").
- Personal suitability inferred from allegations principally about Vida/FFG/employees — yes, and the Commission's own reviewer conceded misattribution (IR ¶32(a)).
- Production is not a fishing expedition — yes: the decision brief incorporated EC22-002103 wholesale, and the tender omits Attachments I, J, K, L(a), P, Q and all 37 complaint files.

**Supported (moderate):**
- Procedural fairness — adverse personal material (whisky, drugs, salaries, "Suitability – Simon Reid") sat in the pack before the delegate and was never put. The counter is that the reasons do not rely on it. Frame it as: the only individualised material about Simon in the Commission's file was never tested with him, and what was relied on was derivative.

**Not supported by T44:**
- Falsity of the allegations; malice; conspiracy; NDIA "using" the Commission; reallocation to competitors; improper purpose. T44 is neutral on purpose. The improper-purpose case still rests on EC22-002103 ¶¶31–33, the Halls/Lundgren emails, and the timeline — not on T44.

**Blunt assessment:** on a de novo merits review the Tribunal will read the 33 statements directly. Many of them say services were claimed and not delivered by Hybrid Hive, Simon's company. T44's defects do not make that evidence go away. T44's real value is (i) a road-map for production and cross-examination, (ii) documentary proof that the Commission's own working paper double-counted, misattributed and dropped favourable evidence, and (iii) proof that the only Simon-personal allegations were thin, unsourced or from a conflicted witness and were never put to him.
