## EMAIL 2 — CDPP: internal review (DUE 16 SEP 2026 — send now)

**To:** foi@cdpp.gov.au
**Subject:** 2026FI00018 — Application for internal review under s 54 of the FOI Act

Dear FOI Coordinator,

I apply under s 54 of the FOI Act for internal review of the decision of Ms Seyi Akosile dated 17 August 2026, which under s 25 neither confirmed nor denied the existence of documents responsive to my request of 3 July 2026. [Mrs Vida Reid's signed authority for me to make this request on her behalf is attached.]

**Ground 1 — s 25 is not available for the concluded prosecution.**

Section 25 applies only where information as to the existence or non-existence of a document would itself cause a document to be exempt under s 33 or s 37(1). The existence of a CDPP prosecution of Mrs Reid is not information capable of being protected. It is a matter of public record:

- On 23 February 2024, in the Magistrates Court of Western Australia, on the application of the Commonwealth prosecutor, all charges against Mrs Reid were dismissed for want of prosecution under s 25 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2004 (WA) and Mrs Reid was formally discharged. A certified transcript of that hearing is held by us and can be provided.
- The dismissal was reported in the press on 28 February 2024.
- The Commonwealth, through the Australian Government Solicitor, has referred to the prosecution and its discontinuance in filed submissions to the Administrative Review Tribunal in matters 2023/3361, 2023/3363 and 2023/4494 (including submissions dated 28 February 2024, 11 March 2025, 17 September 2025 and 19 March 2026).

Confirming that the CDPP holds records of a prosecution that the CDPP itself conducted in open court, and itself brought to an end, cannot reasonably be expected to prejudice the conduct of an investigation or the enforcement of the law. To the extent the decision relies on s 25 for records of that matter, it is in error. The correct course is to confirm the documents exist and then consider access, applying any exemption (including s 37 or s 47E) document by document, with a schedule.

**Ground 2 — the decision does not distinguish between the three parts of the request.**

The request had three parts: (i) records of the existence, commencement and disposition of any referred matter; (ii) current status; (iii) inter-agency correspondence. A single s 25 response over all three, without engaging with the public-record status of part (i), is not the "narrow circumstances" the decision itself describes. The reviewer is asked to consider each part separately and, if s 25 is maintained for any part, to state which.

**Ground 3 — the Commonwealth's position on the same question in another forum.**

The Commonwealth has told the Tribunal that the charges against Mrs Reid "have been dropped" and, on 11 March 2025, that no current investigation pertained to Mrs Reid or Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd. The applicants relied on that representation in waiving a stay. If the CDPP's position is that there is nothing to confirm, s 25 is unnecessary; if its position is that confirmation would prejudice a current matter, that is information the Commonwealth ought not be conveying to a tribunal in one form and refusing to confirm in another. I raise this not as an FOI exemption argument but so that the reviewer is aware the decision will be relied on in the Tribunal as the CDPP's formal position.

**Outcome sought**

1. Confirmation that documents exist in respect of the prosecution of Mrs Reid concluded on 23 February 2024 (Ground 1), and a decision on access to those documents with a schedule.
2. A separate, reasoned decision on parts (ii) and (iii) of the request.

I ask that the review be conducted within the 30 days provided by s 54C.
