## B. Summary of the Applicant's contentions

3. The Applicant contends:

   (g) the Applicant is presently suitable, and leads affirmative evidence of that fact (Part G).

## C. The Tribunal's task

4. The Tribunal must make a decision affirming, varying, or setting aside and substituting/remitting the decision under review: ART Act 2024 (Cth) s 105. The review is de novo: the Tribunal stands in the shoes of the decision-maker, exercises the same power subject to the same constraints (*Frugtniet v ASIC* [2019] HCA 16; (2019) 266 CLR 250), decides the "correct or preferable" decision (*Drake v Minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs* (1979) 46 FLR 409; (1979) 2 ALD 60), and does so on the material and circumstances as they exist **at the time of its own decision** (*Shi v Migration Agents Registration Authority* [2008] HCA 31; (2008) 235 CLR 286).

5. The Applicant adopts the following propositions from *Abdullah* [2026] ARTA 743:

   (a) the s 73ZN(2) power "is fundamentally protective in character" (consistent with NDIS Act s 3(1)(ga)); it is forward-looking, not punitive;

   (b) the Commissioner is obliged to conduct compliance and enforcement activities "in a risk responsive and proportionate manner" (NDIS Act s 181D(4)(b) — the obligation the decision under review itself recites at ¶44), and "it is arguable that the same obligation extends to the Tribunal on review";

   (c) findings adverse to an applicant must be "supported by the evidence" to the Tribunal's comfortable satisfaction — in *Abdullah*, findings "based on evidence from **independent sources** that were **tested** during the ART hearing"; and

   (d) duration falls to be assessed by reference to the disqualification considerations in *ASIC v Adler* [2002] NSWSC 483 (seriousness, propensity, likelihood of recurrence, contrition/insight).

6. The Applicant accepts, consistently with *Abdullah*, that defects in the Respondent's process are in general "cured" by de novo review. **The Applicant therefore advances no freestanding procedural-fairness or improper-purpose ground in this Tribunal.** The matters pleaded at Parts E and H below are advanced because they bear directly on the issues the Tribunal must decide: whether the evidence relied on for present unsuitability is independent, reliable and cogent; whether any present protective need exists; and what scope and duration (if any) would be proportionate. Gravity matters to satisfaction: the unsuitability finding sought against the Applicant is fraud-flavoured and made against a person never charged, still less convicted; the seriousness of such a finding requires evidence of commensurate cogency before the Tribunal could be comfortably satisfied of it (cf *Briginshaw v Briginshaw* [1938] HCA 34; (1938) 60 CLR 336, the standard discussed and applied through "comfortable satisfaction" in *Abdullah*).

## E. Lead ground — no independent, tested evidence of present unsuitability

### E.1 The investigation foundation has fallen away

The Respondent's solicitors informed the Tribunal on 28 February 2024 that "**a brief of evidence in relation to Mr Simon Reid has not been sent by the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) to the Commonwealth DPP**" and that it "cannot say that criminal proceedings are being actively considered by the Commonwealth DPP, as no brief of evidence has been provided" (AGS reply, 28 February 2024, ¶¶6, 8).

12. The decision's own logic (¶47) therefore self-terminates: the "processes in motion in relation to Mr Reid" concluded without any charge against him, and the promised revisitation never occurred. On a *Shi* review deciding present suitability, an order whose stated foundation was an investigation that produced no charge, no brief and no conviction has no foundation at all — unless the Respondent can prove present unsuitability by other evidence that is independent and tested. It cannot, for the reasons that follow.

### E.3 The three participant statements are untested and cannot carry the finding alone

15. The Regali, Benediktsson and Smith statements (¶¶30–31) were taken by NDIA Fraud Investigations officers in a synchronised sitting in October 2022 in aid of the criminal investigation. The Tribunal is not bound by the rules of evidence and no formal onus applies on merits review; but weight is another matter: untested, provenance-affected assertions of serious dishonesty cannot comfortably satisfy the Tribunal of present unsuitability (*Abdullah*: the operative findings there rested on independent sources tested at hearing — and on objective court records of convictions, of which here there are none).

### E.4 The provenance of the complaint stream deprives it of independence

16. The complaint and statement stream on which the banning decisions rest was not independent of interest. The Applicant relies on the following, each evidenced by documents in the Respondent's own tender bundle or by primary correspondence (Schedule of Exhibits):

17. **These matters are not pleaded as a complaint about the Respondent's process** (which de novo review would cure: *Abdullah*), and the Applicant invites no finding of bad faith against any officer in this proceeding. They are pleaded because they answer the question the Tribunal must ask of the Respondent's case under *Abdullah*: is the evidence of unsuitability **independent**? Material generated, solicited, curated or supplied by an agency running a "coordinated provider exit", or by commercial rivals who benefited from that exit, is not independent; convergence among such sources is repetition from a common origin, not corroboration; and anonymous tip-offs cannot be tested. Such material cannot, alone or together, support comfortable satisfaction of present unsuitability.

### E.5 The director-attribution reasoning is wrong in law (retained)

18. The reasons attribute the entities' alleged conduct to the Applicant because "[t]he law regards Mr Reid as being in a position of responsibility, authority and control" and because of the s 180 *Corporations Act* duty of care and diligence: "Mr Reid **should have been aware** of the conduct allegedly being engaged in by, and within Hybrid" (¶38). That is not the law: there is no strict or status-based liability of a director for corporate conduct, under s 180 or under the NDIS Act; an individualised analysis of the director's own knowledge, conduct, reliance (s 189) and period of office is required. ## F. The "circumvention" findings cannot stand

## I. Scope and duration

25. If (contrary to the lead ground) any order is warranted, the present order fails the Respondent's own standards:
