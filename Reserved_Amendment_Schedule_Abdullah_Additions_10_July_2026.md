## ITEM 1 — FETTERING / PROVISION-SELECTION / DURATION (Vida primarily; adapt for Simon and FFG)

**Source documents (verified in case DB 10 Jul 2026):** Internal Review Vida (also at 20240613 Tender Bundle [Finalised] 23004627.pdf):
- ¶59: "In my view, there is no compelling reason to diverge from the usual practice of the NDIS Commission making such an order under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act and aberrantly relying upon a different statutory basis."
- ¶60: "The NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk responsive and proportionate manner (section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act)."
- Duration pegging: pleaded SEPARATELY per matter — Vida "not suitable at this time" tied to the then-pending prosecution; FFG term expressly pegged to the expected duration of the criminal proceedings (10yr varied down on IR); Simon IR ¶47 (duration = expected life of "processes in motion in relation to Mr Reid"; promised revisitation never performed).

**Draft pleading (three separated propositions — do NOT recombine):**

> (a) In selecting s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii), the internal reviewer relied on an asserted "usual practice" of the Commission (IR ¶59) rather than independently determining the proper statutory basis for the order under review — a fetter on the individual statutory judgment the Act requires.
>
> (b) Although the reviewer expressly acknowledged the Commission's obligation to act in a risk-responsive and proportionate manner (IR ¶60, citing s 181D(4)(b)), the reasons do not connect the scope or duration of the order to an individually assessed present risk posed by the applicant.
>
> (c) Separately, the duration of each order was pegged to the expected life of criminal proceedings [pin-cites per matter]. Those proceedings were dismissed on 23 February 2024 (certified transcript). A banning-order duration must reflect the seriousness of established conduct and the period reasonably required to address present risk; it cannot lawfully operate as a placeholder for a prosecution that no longer exists, and the promised revisitation never occurred.

## ITEM 2 — LIMITED-ORDER ALTERNATIVE RELIEF (Simon; adapt for Vida)

**Statutory hook (verified):** s 73ZN(3) NDIS Act — a banning order "may (a) apply generally or may be of limited application; and (b) be permanent or for a specified period; and (c) be made subject to specified conditions."

**Draft relief paragraph (alternative to primary set-aside relief — primary relief unchanged):**

> Further or in the alternative, and without any concession of unsuitability, if the Tribunal is not satisfied that the order should be set aside, the correct or preferable decision is an order of limited application under s 73ZN(3), confined to conditions such as:
> (a) no provision of direct supports or services to NDIS participants;
> (b) no access to participant plans, funds, MyGov or bank accounts;
> (c) no authority over NDIS claiming or invoicing;
> (d) no key-personnel role in a registered NDIS provider;
> (e) no participant recruitment and no control over participant accommodation or finances.
>
> The Respondent should be required to identify why a prohibition on "any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity" — extending to ordinary commercial dealings with providers (construction, labour supply not involving participant care, property, software, accounting) — is necessary where each identified risk is addressed by a specific condition. Note: the "any capacity" breadth was ADDED by the 21 April 2023 variation; the construction/scope challenge to that wording is maintained.

**Forensic purpose:** forces the proportionality question Abdullah answered against an applicant with convictions, admissions and tested adverse witnesses — none of which exist here.

## ITEM 3 — VIDA: PENDING/DISMISSED CHARGES ARE NOT PROVED CONDUCT

**Draft pleading:**

> In Abdullah [2026] ARTA 743, the Tribunal expressly excluded pending criminal charges from its criminal-history findings because the applicant had pleaded not guilty [⚠ pinpoint]; what weighed against him was the failure to disclose the charges' existence, not any assumption that the charged conduct occurred. A fortiori here: the charges against the Second Applicant were dismissed on 23 February 2024 for want of prosecution, with a formal discharge (certified transcript). The NDIA Statement of Material Facts is an untested prosecutorial assertion, not an adjudicated account, and cannot be treated as a conviction by another name. Any allegation drawn from the abandoned prosecution must be proved in this Tribunal by reliable, individually attributable evidence, tested by cross-examination (s 74 summonses maintained).

**Candour note (both individual applicants):** Abdullah was damaged by non-disclosure of his current charges. The applicants' filings must disclose all current regulatory/legal facts fully — the shield only works with clean hands on candour.
