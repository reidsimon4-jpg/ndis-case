We write to you in your capacity as director of Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (FFG) 
and refer to your email to the Tribunal dated 14 January 2026 in the proceedings 
referred to above.  
As you are aware, the issues raised by your email will be considered by the Tribunal 
at an interlocutory hearing on 20 March 2026. One of these issues relates to the expiry 
of the banning order made the Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards 
Commission (NDIS Commission) against FFG, which occurred on 20 December 
2025. Your email expressed concerns that, as of 14 January 2026, the NDIS 
Commission’s public register of banning orders still listed the order against FFG as 
active and indicated your view that this “introduces a separate and unrelated 
administrative error that has become part of FFG’s ongoing claim”. 
Ahead of the hearing on 20 March 2026, we have taken instructions on this point to 
try and narrow the issues in dispute between the parties.  
We are first instructed to draw to your attention that the NDIS Commission’s banning 
order register has been updated since the date of your email. The register now shows 
that the banning order against FFG expired at 6pm on 20 December 2025 and is no 
longer in force. Please refer to the below extract taken from the NDIS Commission 
website on the next page of this letter.

In circumstances where the Commission’s register now correctly records the expiry 
of FFG’s banning order, there can be no relevant prejudice or loss which would give 
rise to a separate or actionable claim by FFG as suggested by your email. 
Moving forward, our client’s instructions are that it is standard practice for expired 
banning orders to remain on the NDIS Commission register indefinitely with a status 
of ‘no longer in force’. This is consistent with the goals of the NDIS Commission’s 
Publishing Regulatory Activity policy (Policy) to provide greater transparency and 
visibility over the NDIS Commission’s regulatory activities and to influence behaviour 
and deter future wrongdoing in the NDIS market. You can view a copy of the Policy 
on the NDIS Commission’s website at https://www.ndiscommission.gov.au/about-
us/legislation-rules-and-policies/publishing-regulatory-activity-policy. 
The Policy provides that in exceptional cases, an individual or provider may submit a 
written request to the Commission for their name or business name to be removed 
from the register following the expiry of a banning order. If you would like to make 
such a request, you may send an email attaching written reasons to 
internalintegrity@ndiscommission.gov.au.  
For the avoidance of doubt, the decision to remove (or not remove) the expired 
banning order will be made solely by the NDIS Commission. We have no control over 
the outcome of such an application.   
Given the banning order against FFG has expired, we further invite you to consider 
whether there may be utility in withdrawing FFG’s appeal against the NDIS 
Commission’s decision to ban.  
We otherwise refer to our existing submissions on the issues raised by your email. 
Please contact us if you would like to discuss further.
