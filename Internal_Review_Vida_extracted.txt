a) providing disability supports and services, directly or indirectly, to NDIS-funded 
participants; 
b) providing labour hire workers to support NDIS providers; or  
c) providing support, funding, guidance or assistance of any form to an NDIS provider.

Relevant legislation  
13. Section 73ZN(2) of the NDIS Act gives the Commissioner the discretion to make a banning order 
prohibiting or restricting a person employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider as 
follows: 
‘The Commissioner may, by written notice, make an order (a banning order) prohibiting or restricting 
a person who is or was employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider, or a person who is or 
was a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider, from engaging in specified activities if: 
(a)  the Commissioner reasonably believes that: 
i. the person has contravened, is contravening, or is likely to contravene this Act; or 
ii.  the person has been involved in, or is likely to become involved in, a contravention of this 
Act by another person; or 
iii. the person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or services to people 
with disability, having regard to any matters prescribed by the National Disability 
Insurance Scheme rules for the purposes of this subparagraph; or 
iv.  there is an immediate danger to the health, safety or wellbeing of a person with 
disability if the person continues to engage in the specified activities; or 
(b)  the person is convicted of an offence involving fraud or dishonesty; or 
(c)  the person becomes an insolvent under administration.’ 
14. Under section 73ZN(3) of the NDIS Act, a banning order may apply generally or be of limited 
application, and may be permanent or for a specified period. 
15. Section 73ZN(7) of the NDIS Act states that the Commissioner may only make a banning order 
against a person after giving the person an opportunity to make submissions to the 
Commissioner on the matter (except in certain circumstances outlined in section 73ZN(8) which 
are not relevant to this matter). 
16. Section 73ZN(9) sets out the various requirements surrounding any banning order made as 
follows:

Findings and Reasons 
28. I have considered the original decision made on 20 December 2022.  
29. I am of the view that the original decision was the correct and preferable decision to be made, 
however, I disagree with the basis for the original decision, namely the original decision-maker 
forming a reasonable belief that Mrs Reid had contravened, was contravening, or likely to 
contravene the NDIS Act. Section 73ZN(2)(a)(i) allows for a banning order to be made against a 
person in such circumstances.  
30. In my view, the original decision should have been made pursuant to section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of 
the NDIS Act, which provides a banning order can be made where a decision-maker forms a 
reasonable belief that a person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or 
services to people with disability. 
31. A banning order on this latter basis accords with the usual approach followed by the NDIS 
Commission in matters where a person is alleged to have engaged in fraudulent activity against 
the NDIS (particularly where criminal charges are laid) and is also more consistent with the 
NDIS Commission’s Banning Order Policy.

Section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act – the suitability of Mrs Reid to provide supports and services to 
people with disability 
36. In light of the above information, I am of the reasonable belief that Mrs Reid is not suitable to 
be involved in the provision of supports or services to people with disability.  
37. In forming this view, I have taken into consideration and balanced the following factors: 
a. 
The allegations against Mrs Reid, though unproven, are extremely serious. Some of the 
offences which Mrs Reid has been charged with carry a maximum penalty of 10 years 
imprisonment. The alleged conduct falls well below the standard and quality of care 
expected from a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider, particularly a 
director.  
b. 
I acknowledge Mrs Reid has considerable experience in, and links to the disability care 
and support sector. Whilst these matters are relevant to an assessment of Mrs Reid’s 
suitability to be involved in the sector, I find that they do not outweigh the other 
factors that favour the making of a banning order, particularly the fact that Mrs Reid 
engaged in the alleged criminal conduct whilst she was the director of two NDIS 
providers. In fact, the position she held within those NDIS providers significantly 
facilitated the commission of the alleged fraud.  
c. 
Section 3 of the NDIS Act contains the objects of the NDIS Act, which includes: 
i. 
supporting the independence and social and economic participation of people 
with a disability;

enabling people with a disability to exercise choice and control in the pursuit of 
their goals and the planning and delivery of their supports; 
iii. 
promoting the provision of high quality and innovative supports that enable 
people with a disability to maximise independent lifestyles and full inclusion in 
the community; and 
iv. 
protecting and preventing people with disability from experiencing harm arising 
from poor quality or unsafe supports or services provided under the NDIS. 
In giving effect to the objects of the NDIS Act, section 3(3)(b) of the NDIS Act requires 
regard to be had to the need to ensure the financial sustainability of the NDIS.  
Similarly, as set out in section 4(17)(b) of the NDIS Act, it is the intention of Parliament 
that the Commissioner, amongst others, has regard to the need to ensure the financial 
sustainability of the NDIS, in performing her functions and exercising her powers under 
the NDIS Act. 
The allegations made against Mrs Reid by the NDIA are likely to cause harm to, or 
jeopardise, public trust in the NDIS and, if made out, are likely to impact the financial 
sustainability of the NDIS.  
d. 
The need to protect the interests of people with disability, who may be particularly 
vulnerable, and ensure that they are kept safe, requires that in matters of this kind, a 
person facing such serious criminal charges must be prevented from having access to 
the NDIS plans of people of disability and delivering supports or services to them. 
Allowing Mrs Reid to continue working in the NDIS would undermine the objects of the 
NDIS, which includes protecting people with disability from experiencing harm. 
e. 
I acknowledge the making of a banning order in respect of Mrs Reid will impact her 
ability to earn an income in the disability services sector, and I have taken this into 
account. However, I consider that the competing interest of ensuring NDIS participants, 
who may not be able to fully protect their own interests and who have a statutory right 
to safe services and protection from violence, abuse and exploitation, is paramount and 
takes precedence over the detriment Mrs Reid may experience if banned.

The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy  
56. The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy contemplates that a banning order can be ‘issued 
pending the determination of [criminal] charges’ if, in the circumstances, the information and 
evidence obtained by the NDIS Commission supports a view that a banning order is warranted 
and appropriate.   
57. In practice, this is ordinarily operationalised by the NDIS Commission making the banning order 
under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act as opposed to the other grounds set out in the 
NDIS Act. The reason being that criminal wrongdoing (whether alleged or proven) directly 
relates to the suitability of the relevant person to engage in the NDIS sector and section 
73ZN(2)(a)(iii) is therefore the most relevant statutory provision in those circumstances.  
58. Therefore, for the reasons noted above (particularly in paragraphs 37(a) to (e) and 51 to 55), I 
consider a banning order against Mrs Reid to be warranted and appropriate.  
59. In my view, there is no compelling reason to diverge from the usual practice of the NDIS 
Commission making such an order under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act and aberrantly 
relying upon a different statutory basis.  
Requirement to take proportionate and risk responsive action under NDIS Act 
60. The NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk 
responsive and proportionate manner (section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act).  
61. The NDIS Commission’s Banning Order Policy notes that a banning order can have a significant 
impact on the recipient and on people with disability, and as such, it will be reserved for 
circumstances where it is needed to protect people with disability from the risk of being 
exposed to conduct that falls well short of acceptable standards. However, a banning order 
should not be for a longer period than is considered necessary.  
62. Having regard to the criminal proceedings presently pending against Mrs Reid, and in particular 
the serious and systemic nature of the allegations raised in those proceedings, I consider Mrs 
Reid is not suitable, at this time, to be an NDIS provider.
