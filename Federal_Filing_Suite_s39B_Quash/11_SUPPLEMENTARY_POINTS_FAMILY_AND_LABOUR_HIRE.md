# Supplementary settling note — Simon family carveout and labour-hire email

**Added:** 15 July 2026

## 1. Family-support carveout

### Verified instrument comparison

Vida's 21 April 2023 varied order expressly states:

> This banning order is subject to the exception that Mrs Reid is able to seek disability supports or services for herself and her son, Mr Alex Reid.

Simon’s original and varied orders contain no corresponding exception.

The family evidence to be proved is that:

1. Vida is Simon’s wife and an NDIS participant;
2. Alex is Simon and Vida’s son and an NDIS participant;
3. Simon has actual family or caring responsibilities concerning their supports; and
4. the absence of an exception has affected, deterred or made uncertain identified ordinary family activities.

### Proper forensic use

The missing exception is not, by itself, a separate statutory prohibition in s 73ZN. It should be used as:

- a concrete illustration of the uncertainty created by “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”;
- evidence that the order is broader than the protective risk identified in the reasons;
- a hardship and proportionality fact relevant to legal unreasonableness and discretionary relief;
- evidence supporting expedition and a narrow interim family-support exception; and
- a comparison showing that the same reviewer knew how to draft such an exception in Vida’s order but did not include one for Simon.

The affidavit must identify the actual activities affected. Examples may include attending planning or provider meetings, helping Vida or Alex communicate with providers, arranging appointments, advocating about services, assisting with service agreements, transporting a family member, or ordinary unpaid family support. Include only activities Simon actually undertook or needs to undertake.

Do not allege that every one of those activities necessarily breaches the order. The point is that the universal chapeau and “assistance of any form” wording do not tell Simon whether they do. That uncertainty is especially serious because breach carries a civil penalty.

### Proposed narrow interlocutory protection

If a complete interim suspension is considered too broad, seek an order or written undertaking that the Commission will not enforce the order against Simon merely because he:

1. seeks, arranges or communicates about supports or services for Vida or Alex in his capacity as spouse, parent, carer, guardian or nominee;
2. attends meetings or assists either participant to express preferences and make decisions; or
3. provides ordinary unpaid familial assistance,

provided he does not receive NDIS funding, submit or authorise claims, control provider funds, or supply paid support workers through a provider.

That alternative directly manages participant-protection concerns while preventing the broad wording from interfering with family life before final judgment.

## 2. Dream Big: the Commission's own section 56 record

The high-value record is T58. It permits the point to be made from the Commission's own investigation without asking Simon to give a new account of the underlying business facts.

1. The Dream Big ASIC application was submitted by Simon on 29 October 2022, before the 17 November 2022 notice of intention and the 21 December 2022 original order.
2. On 20 January 2023, the Commission issued a section 56 notice expressly investigating whether Simon and Vida were contravening their banning orders through Dream Big.
3. Dream Big's written response said Simon had assisted only by submitting the ASIC form, had no ongoing role in its operations or service delivery, and was not a bank signatory. It identified Virginie Fanny-Dulloo and Tori Fitton as the bank signatories.
4. At the Commission examination on 9 March 2023, Ms Fanny-Dulloo said Simon was not involved in FFG, had only helped with the ASIC application before he was banned, did not have access to Dream Big's email and had no ongoing role.
5. The list of material in Simon's review reasons identifies the ASIC application, the Vitality email and the Burke statement, but does not identify the T58 written response or examination transcript.
6. The review reasons nevertheless said the relevant involvement appeared to commence around the time of the banning order and treated Dream Big and Vitality as possible strategic or tactical circumvention.

### Proper forensic use

The point is not that the Court should decide the merits of who was involved in Dream Big. It is that the Commission's own targeted investigation produced contemporaneous material inconsistent with the temporal and factual premise expressed in the review reasons. That supports a record-based contention of material factual misunderstanding or illogicality, and strengthens the argument that pre-order conduct was used to justify a much broader prospective instrument without an intelligible activity-specific bridge.

Do not presently allege that the reviewer deliberately ignored or concealed T58, or that T58 was a mandatory relevant consideration. First obtain the review workflow, complete materials-before-reviewer list and any internal analysis showing whether T58 was before, considered by, or summarised for the reviewer.

## 3. Labour-hire recruitment email

### Verified document chain

The source document is T65/T65.1 in `Tender_Bundle_Fixed_Part_5`, tender bundle pages 1346–1352. The email appears at tender pages 1349–1352.

1. T65.1 records an email dated 9 February 2023 at 1:43 pm AWST from `simon@vitalitywa.au` to numerous recipients with the subject “employment Oppurtunities in the disability sector”. The reviewer treated it as Simon’s email.
2. The document said that Vitality Employment was recruiting disability support workers and described support services to disability providers.
3. The distribution included a large recipient list. The document itself does not label every recipient as former FFG or Hybrid staff. Do not require Simon to identify them in a filed affidavit while criminal jeopardy may remain live; if the point is indispensable, use existing employment records or another non-applicant witness after criminal-defence review.
4. On 22 March 2023, the reviewer sent T65 to Simon’s solicitor. It identified the email and proposed to broaden Simon’s order expressly to include “providing labour hire workers to support NDIS providers” and open-ended support or assistance to providers.
5. The final reasons at paragraph 42(b) described the email as seeking to recruit numerous individuals as disability support workers for Vitality, “a company that provides labour hire workers to NDIS providers and of which Mr Reid is a director”.
6. At paragraph 43, the reviewer treated Vitality and Dream Big as possible strategic or tactical circumvention and said it was necessary to broaden the order accordingly.
7. The final varied order then included the labour-hire limb word for word.

### What the document establishes

The document supports a direct inference that the labour-hire limb was formulated to stop the activity evidenced by the 9 February email. It also demonstrates that the reviewer was able to identify and describe a concrete activity when she chose to do so.

The evidence therefore supports the applicants’ case in three limited ways:

1. the identified concern was recruitment and labour supply, not every possible relationship with the NDIS sector;
2. a targeted labour-hire restriction was an obvious narrower regulatory response, undermining the need for the universal chapeau and the “support, funding, guidance or assistance of any form” limb; and
3. the original personal order prohibited Simon from providing disability supports and services to NDIS-funded participants. The reasons do not explain how sending a recruitment email or operating a labour-hire business breached that text. Instead, the email was treated as circumvention and used to enlarge the order.

This point is reinforced by *Sunflower* at [226]: variation can govern the future, but it cannot make conduct that was lawful under the original instrument unlawful after the fact. The reviewer could treat the email as new evidence relevant to a prospective, specifically described activity. The reviewer still had to explain why that evidence justified every capacity captured by the new chapeau and why the specific labour-hire restriction was insufficient.

The point should be expressed as follows:

> The reviewer’s own new evidence identified, at its highest, a specific labour-recruitment and labour-supply concern. That evidence may have supported consideration of a clearly expressed restriction on Simon personally supplying labour-hire workers to NDIS providers. It did not establish an evident and intelligible justification for prohibiting any involvement with an entire sector, direct supports, or support, funding, guidance and assistance of every form. The activity-specific evidence demonstrates the availability of a narrower order and exposes the disproportionality of the order actually made.

### Adverse implications that must be confronted

- The 22 March 2023 letter expressly notified Simon of the proposed labour-hire and wider wording. This evidence weakens a Simon procedural-fairness case based on absence of notice. No such ground should be pleaded without another undisclosed decisive matter.
- The Commission will say the email showed deliberate circumvention shortly after the original order and supported a preventative restriction. Do not answer that case through a new sworn explanation from Simon while criminal jeopardy may remain live. Meet it, so far as possible, from the text of the original order, T65/T65.1, the reviewer’s reasons and the legal availability of a targeted restriction.
- The labour-hire limb is much closer to a “specified activity” than the universal chapeau. The Commission may seek severance of that limb. The applicants’ answer is that the limb sits under an invalid, non-exhaustive chapeau, while accepting that a future lawful decision-maker could consider a targeted labour-hire restriction.
- The case should not allege improper motive merely because the wording was designed to stop the activity. Protective tailoring is not unlawful. The error is the leap from one identified activity to an uncertain sector-wide prohibition.

## 4. Minimum-evidence protocol

Simon’s affidavit should be confined to:

1. Vida’s and Alex’s participant status, proved with minimally necessary redacted documents;
2. one or two actual ordinary family activities affected by the broad wording;
3. one or two concrete present effects relevant to expedition or confined interim relief; and
4. undertakings excluding provider management, funds, claims, paid supports and labour supply.

Do not add an explanation of Vitality, the recipient group, former staff, placements, revenue, contracts, Simon’s understanding of the original order, communications with investigators, or legal advice unless criminal defence counsel gives written clearance and Federal Court counsel considers the evidence indispensable.

Add to the court book or common solicitor affidavit:

- T58 section 56 notice, Dream Big written response and the 9 March 2023 examination extract;
- T65, the 22 March 2023 notice to Simon;
- T65.1, especially tender pages 1349–1352;
- the original Simon order for exact comparison;
- Simon review reasons at [41]–[43];
- both April 2023 orders showing the unequal family exceptions;
- the certified 23 February 2024 transcript recording dismissal for want of prosecution, formal discharge and the stated intention to recharge;
- any current CDPP correspondence about recharge status; and
- minimally redacted proof of Vida’s and Alex’s participant status.

The Commission’s documents are relied upon as parts of the administrative record, not as admissions by Simon or Vida.
