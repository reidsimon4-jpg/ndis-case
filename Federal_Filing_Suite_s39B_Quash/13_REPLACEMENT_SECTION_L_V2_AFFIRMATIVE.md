## L. Discretion, the ART proceedings and delay

50. The starting point is the character of the error alleged. In *Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2)* [2025] FCA 1442 at [117], applying *Bechara v Bates* [2021] FCAFC 34; 286 FCR 166 at [157]-[164], the Court held that where a clear want or excess of jurisdiction is shown by an aggrieved party, the constitutional writ ordinarily issues almost as of right unless it is just to withhold relief. The present applicants allege an excess apparent from the legal operation of the instruments themselves: the orders moved beyond prohibiting or restricting "specified activities" under former s 73ZN(2) and purported to regulate indirect relationships, statuses and forms of involvement.

51. If Grounds 1 to 3 are established, the error is not a technical defect in a step toward a discretionary merits outcome. It is an anterior limit on the content of the executive instrument. The same statutory provision and materially similar drafting were considered in *Sunflower* at [127]-[141]. The applicants are persons directly and continuously affected by the instruments. Those matters supply the affirmative reason for relief. The matters below concern whether, despite that starting point, relief should nevertheless be withheld or deferred.

52. The applicants accept that the existence and powers of the Administrative Review Tribunal are substantial discretionary considerations. They commenced merits review in the Administrative Appeals Tribunal on 10 May 2023 and those proceedings continued in the ART. Under the *Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024* (Cth) (ART Act), the Tribunal may:

   (a) stay or otherwise affect operation or implementation of the decisions under s 32;

   (b) control its procedure and, by limiting the questions, evidence and issues it considers, the scope of review under ss 49 and 53;

   (c) exercise the decision-maker's powers and discretions under s 54; and

   (d) affirm, vary, set aside and substitute, or set aside and remit, the decisions under s 105.

   The applicants do not submit that merits review is legally incapable of providing substantial prospective relief.

53. The applicants also confront the adverse comparison with *McGowan v Migration Agents Registration Authority* [2003] FCA 482; 129 FCR 118. As recorded in *Sunflower* at [118], the s 39B proceeding in *McGowan* was summarily dismissed because the extant AAT review was a more convenient and satisfactory remedy. The present case is less favourable than *Sunflower* in several respects. In *Sunflower*, delay was about two months, alternative review had not been pursued through oversight, and the Federal Court proceeding was already on foot: *Sunflower* at [119]. Here, merits review has long been on foot; interlocutory processes have been used in that forum; and this proceeding was not already advanced when *Sunflower* was delivered. The applicants do not contend that *Sunflower* automatically answers the alternative-remedy objection.

54. Those distinctions do not displace the affirmative starting point because the actual history and relief here are materially different from a simple attempt to bypass merits review:

   (a) the applicants invoked merits review promptly and have remained in that process for more than three years without a final determination;

   (b) the threshold grounds are confined questions about the statutory authority for fixed, public executive instruments, not an invitation to the Court to choose the correct or preferable regulatory outcome;

   (c) a decision substituted now by the Tribunal may alter the prospective position but does not itself answer whether the impugned instruments were authorised when made or validate their historical operation;

   (d) the official instruments, reasons and statutory texts are fixed, and the facial question should not require evidence from Mr Reid about conduct potentially relevant to contemplated criminal proceedings;

   (e) the instruments continue to operate until December 2027 unless displaced, so delay both accumulates harm and progressively consumes the practical value of relief; and

   (f) the proceeding can be confined and coordinated: the Court may determine the anterior statutory-authority ground while fact-dependent grounds are stayed or deferred.

55. The chronology must nevertheless be candid. The Commission sought an early stay or adjournment connected with contemplated criminal proceedings. Later confidentiality, stay and severance disputes included forensic choices by the applicants. Ms Reid withdrew her stay application in March 2026. Not every interval can be attributed to the Commission. The applicants rely only on the dated chronology supported by Tribunal orders, transcripts, correspondence and court records.

57. Section 185 of the ART Act is a possible coordination mechanism, not an automatic answer. The Tribunal may, with the President's agreement, refer a question of law arising in the ART proceeding to the Federal Court, on a party's request or on its own initiative. However:

   (a) the applicants cannot compel a reference;

   (b) under s 185(5), while a reference is pending the Tribunal must not give a decision to which the question is relevant, so a reference can itself postpone final merits relief;

   (c) a refusal accompanied by a prompt and reliable ART hearing or decision timetable may strengthen, rather than weaken, the contention that merits review is the more convenient remedy; and

   (d) a refusal, or non-determination, accompanied by continuing absence of a prompt timetable may support the contrary conclusion.

   The discretionary assessment must therefore use the actual result and timing of any s 185 request, not the mere availability of the section.

58. Utility must be assessed separately for each applicant.

   (a) Vida Reid's original order itself used broad language including conduct undertaken "directly or indirectly". Subject to the precise terms of the instruments and relief, a ruling concerning both the original and review decisions can therefore have immediate legal and historical utility.

   (b) In Simon Reid's case, quashing only the varied review order may leave or revive the original 2022 order. The relief must attack every operative layer necessary to produce a useful result. The applicants do not submit that success against the variation alone necessarily ends Simon's ban.

   (c) The current form of s 73ZN may permit the Commissioner to consider a fresh order in terms not available under former s 73ZN(2). The applicants seek correction of the legality and consequences of the historical instruments, not immunity from a lawful future decision made under the current Act after procedural fairness.

   (d) A possible fresh decision reduces any claim to finality but does not retrospectively validate years of operation under an instrument that exceeded the power then available.

59. On the completed evidence, the applicants submit that the affirmative factors are decisive: the alleged excess is clear and anterior; the applicants are directly aggrieved; the documentary question is fixed and substantially governed by *Sunflower*; merits review has been pursued for more than three years without final relief; the instruments continue to operate; and determination of the confined ground has historical and prospective utility which a substituted merits decision does not wholly provide. It is therefore not just to withhold relief merely because the ART proceeding remains on foot.

60. Alternatively, if the Court is not presently persuaded to determine the fact-dependent grounds, the applicants seek directions which permit the facial statutory-authority ground to be heard first and stay or defer the remaining grounds. That course gives effect to the constitutional starting point while avoiding unnecessary duplication of the merits review.
