# Covering note for the solicitor

This is a working draft of the letter to the Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions contemplated at section 4A.3.4 of the reasonableness memorandum dated 27 May 2026. Its purposes are: (a) to put the CDPP on notice of the totality position so that any charging decision is taken on a properly informed public-interest assessment; (b) to invoke the pre-charge representations facility under the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth so that any such decision must be communicated to the applicants in advance; (c) to begin the documentary record that will support either a pre-emptive Federal Court declaratory application or a permanent-stay application in the criminal court, depending on what the CDPP discloses; and (d) to deter the laying of charges by making explicit that the cumulative-conduct record has been reduced to a strategic file and that any permanent-stay application would be filed on day one of any future charging.

The letter has been updated from the earlier draft to: (i) confirm Commonwealth (not WA-state) jurisdiction; (ii) elevate the AGS "no investigation" statement and the contemporaneous lawyer evidence to the front of the letter; (iii) incorporate verbatim the FOI-released ABF documents indicating the September 2024 border stop was "on behalf of another agency" with "law enforcement interest"; (iv) elevate the September 2024 border stop and the existence of a current OAIC review (MR26/00947).

Before sending, please verify: (1) the current Director's name and the addressee for Perth Office correspondence; (2) the current clause numbering in the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth as referenced below; (3) whether known CDPP officers from the original 2022–2024 prosecution (the 29 September 2024 OAIC/complaint correspondence was cc'd to Isobelle.Saville@cdpp.gov.au and Kirsty.Stynes@cdpp.gov.au — these may have been the prosecution lawyers but verification is needed); (4) the standing instructions on signing.

**Re:	Mrs Vida Reid; Mr Simon Reid; Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd; Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd and associated entities — request for confirmation regarding any current consideration of criminal proceedings**

We act for Mrs Vida Reid and Mr Simon Reid (the applicants) and have instructions in relation to Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd, Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd and the associated corporate entities the subject of regulatory action by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission since December 2022. We write further to the discontinuance by the Office of all charges against Mrs Reid on 23 February 2024 for want of evidence, and to the procedural events more recently affecting our clients and detailed below.

**1.	The position our clients are required to act on, and the position the Commonwealth has formally taken**

1.1	On 11 March 2025, by letter from Ms Karlie Dickinson, Senior Lawyer, Australian Government Solicitor (Our ref. 23004627) addressed to the Administrative Review Tribunal — Brisbane Registry and to Mrs Vida Reid, the NDIS Commission's legal representatives stated to the Tribunal:

> *"Following receipt of Mrs Reid's submissions, the Commission has made enquiries with the NDIA and has been informed that there is no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to FFG. In light of this information, the Commission cannot agree to the stay requested by Mrs Reid, having regard to the Tribunal's objectives in section 9 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024."*

1.2	On the strength of that representation our clients withdrew their application for a stay of the merits-review proceedings in the Administrative Review Tribunal, exposed themselves to the substantive hearing of those proceedings, and committed time and resources accordingly. The 19 March 2025 follow-up letter from the AGS expressly confirmed only that the Commission's enquiries "confirmed the NDIA's position as was relayed to the Commissioner," and the AGS expressly disclaimed acting for the National Disability Insurance Agency.

1.3	**The 11 March 2025 representation cannot be reconciled with documentary material now in our clients' possession.** Two months before the AGS representation, on 9 January 2025, Mrs Reid's then-solicitor Mr Charles Dallimore, Senior Associate at Weeks & Co Lawyers, wrote to Mrs Reid in connection with the same matter that:

> *"It certainly does not look like the investigation has ended."*

That contemporaneous statement by Mrs Reid's then-solicitor was made by reference to materials and dealings in his possession and is on its face inconsistent with the position the Commission was, two months later, told by the NDIA and conveyed to the Tribunal.

1.4	**The position is materially reinforced by the ABF's own documents released by the Department of Home Affairs in respect of the 25 September 2024 stop at Perth Airport.** In response to Mrs Reid's FOI request FA 24/10/01764 (file ADF2024/261542, 132 folios; decision dated 2 April 2026), the Department released ABF documents which include, verbatim, the following:

(a)	in the Australian Border Force's own narrative of the incident: *"The complainant stated they are aware as to why they were subject to Law Enforcement interest. The complainant stated that they had court proceedings against them from the NDIS dropped. The complainant was then told ABF are completing a baggage examination on behalf of another agency"* (capitalisation as in the original);

(b)	in the ABF's responsive Q&A account: *"ABF told the Pax as we are not aware charges have been dropped and were conducting the examination on behalf of another agency. ABF advised that they would need to speak with AFP or their Lawyer if charges against them have been dropped and that the examination would still need to take place"*; and

(c)	in the ABF officers' contemporaneous narrative recording the exchange with Mrs Reid: *"ABF officers then asked if she had been in any trouble with any law enforcement agencies previously? To which the passenger replied 'yes NDIS, I had to sell my house to pay approx. AUD 850,000 in legal fees'."*

The identity of the "another agency" referenced throughout the ABF records was withheld under section 37(2)(b) of the *Freedom of Information Act 1982* (Cth) — the exemption directed at material that would disclose the existence or identity of a confidential source of information in relation to the enforcement or administration of the law. Related material was withheld under sections 22(1)(a)(ii), 47D and 47E(d). An Information Commissioner Review (reference MR26/00947) is on foot to test the redactions.

1.5	The ABF documents are admissible documentary evidence of two propositions independent of any informant or other untested source. First, on 25 September 2024 — seven months *after* the CDPP discontinuance of 23 February 2024, and almost six months *before* the AGS letter of 11 March 2025 — the Commonwealth's border-control machinery was deployed against Mrs Reid at the request of a Commonwealth agency whose identity the Department has withheld under the FOI law-enforcement-source exemption, and whose conduct is characterised by the ABF in its own narrative as "Law Enforcement interest." Secondly, ABF directed Mrs Reid that the appropriate parties for her to consult in respect of the matter were "AFP or their Lawyer" — the only Commonwealth law-enforcement agency named in the released documents.

1.6	The implication is unavoidable. On any defensible reading of the language used in the ABF release, a Commonwealth law-enforcement agency (which the ABF directed Mrs Reid towards as the AFP, although the s 37(2)(b) redaction leaves the identity of the requesting agency formally undisclosed) was conducting some form of "law enforcement interest" activity in respect of our clients at a date which the AGS letter of 11 March 2025 cannot have accurately captured. The position relayed to the Tribunal — that "there is no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to FFG" — was, at least on the documents now in our clients' possession, narrower than the actual position. Our clients have, in addition, recently received credible information from a source they are not at liberty to identify that the criminal matter against the family remains on foot.

1.7	The ABF documents also record, on the face of the released material, that our clients' disabled son was present throughout the September 2024 examination: *"I asked the passenger if she would prefer that her son sit with their elder daughter in the seating area at the outwards referrals area, which she agreed was a good idea. The child was restless there, so after some time I offered him a chair at the bench in the outwards examination area, so he could be close to his parents."* The exposure of the child to the conduct described, on his return from international travel, is itself part of the public-interest assessment we ask the Office to make in connection with any future charging decision.

**2.	The wider documentary record of investigator conduct after 23 February 2024**

For completeness, and so that the Office is aware of the matters our clients consider relevant to any current public-interest assessment, the conduct on the record since the discontinuance includes:

(a)	on or about 27 March 2024 — four weeks after the discontinuance — a telephone call to Modplex Construction by Mr Jason Moore of the National Disability Insurance Agency's Service Compliance and Non-Compliance Team in relation to our clients;

(b)	on 25 September 2024, the ABF stop at Perth Airport described in paragraph 1.4 above;

(c)	in late 2024, home visits to Ms Bec Hayward and Mr Alex Bowers (principals of Dream Big Employment Pty Ltd) in connection with our clients;

(d)	on 7 January 2025, a home interrogation of Ms Virginie Fanny-Dulloo by Mr John Maloney, NDIS investigator, during which the investigator is recorded as having stated (i) that telecommunications interception had been conducted in respect of our clients ("*we have been listening to all your phone calls with Vida*"), (ii) that public-shaming publicity was a tool available to the agency ("*we can put your face all over the news like we did to Vida very publicly*"), and (iii) that the avoidance of imprisonment was being conditioned upon a coerced witness statement ("*it's your neck or her neck*");

(e)	on 7 January 2026, a recorded statement by a senior officer of the Australian Taxation Office concerning approximately $87,000 of Superannuation Guarantee / Part 7 / General Interest Charge entries attached anomalously to Hybrid Hive's account, observed by the officer to have "no documentation backing them up — really rare to see what I'm seeing";

(f)	the documented offer by Mr Bruce Graydon of the NDIA Fraud Fusion Taskforce of a $200,000 NDIS funding inducement to Mr Jonathan Shapiera (aged 66 and disabled), under threat of imprisonment, in pursuit of statements adverse to our clients;

(g)	the recanted sworn statement of Ms Cassandra Burke; and

(h)	the audio recording of NDIA WA Assistant Director Mr Nathan Halls's direction to a service provider not to pay invoices owed to Forward Focused Group, made in 2022 with no documented authority.

**3.	The harm already visited on our clients and on a vulnerable third party**

The cumulative impact of the conduct since December 2022 has included: the loss of livelihood for our clients and their corporate entities; the loss of a six-year Commonwealth Workforce Australia services contract (ES-22-0090, Department of Employment and Workplace Relations); the documented coercion of witnesses (Ms Fanny-Dulloo, Mr Shapiera) and the recantation of another (Ms Burke); the use of border-control machinery against Mrs Reid on the request of a law-enforcement agency whose identity remains undisclosed; the documented telecommunications interception admission of 7 January 2025; and — directly relevant to the public-interest assessment under the Policy — the cessation of NDIS-funded supports for our clients' own disabled child, with the consequence that the applicants have provided his care themselves in circumstances where funded providers have not been prepared to engage.

**4.	The purpose of this letter**

4.1	Our clients understand that the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth (the Policy) [clause numbering to be verified before sending] governs the manner in which the Office considers whether to commence proceedings; that the Policy contemplates a two-stage test of reasonable prospects of conviction and public interest; that the Policy contains specific provisions regarding the commencement of proceedings after discontinuance; and that the Policy contemplates pre-charge representations by an affected person in defined circumstances. Our clients invoke each of those facilities by this letter.

**5.	Specific requests**

We respectfully request that the Office confirm to us, in writing and within twenty-one (21) days of the date of this letter:

(a)	whether any brief of evidence has been referred to the Office, by any Commonwealth or State agency, in respect of Mrs Vida Reid, Mr Simon Reid, Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd, Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd or any of the associated entities listed in EC22-002103, since 23 February 2024;

(b)	whether any decision to commence proceedings in respect of any of the applicants or entities is currently under consideration;

(c)	if the answer to (a) or (b) is in the affirmative, whether the Office will provide our clients with a reasonable opportunity (not less than twenty-eight (28) days) to make written representations as to whether such proceedings should be commenced, before any such decision is taken;

(d)	whether the Office is conducting, or has conducted since 23 February 2024, any liaison with the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, the National Disability Insurance Agency (including its Fraud Fusion Taskforce), the Australian Federal Police, the Australian Border Force, the Australian Taxation Office or any other Commonwealth or State agency in respect of any of the applicants or entities;

(e)	whether the Office is the "another agency" on whose behalf the Australian Border Force conducted the baggage examination of Mrs Reid on 25 September 2024 as described in the ABF documents released under FOI request FA 24/10/01764, or, if not, whether the Office is aware of the identity of that agency and of any current investigation it is conducting in respect of the applicants;

(f)	what position the Office takes on the principles applicable, under the Policy and at common law, to the commencement of proceedings in respect of conduct on which charges have previously been discontinued for want of evidence; and

(g)	confirmation of the names of the CDPP officers who had carriage of the original 2022–2024 prosecution of Mrs Reid, so that any further correspondence may be appropriately directed.

**6.	Reservation of position**

6.1	Our clients reserve all of their rights in respect of the conduct outlined in paragraphs 1.4, 2 and 3 above, including their rights to bring proceedings for misfeasance in public office (*Northern Territory v Mengel*; *Sanders v Snell*), declaratory and injunctive relief under section 39B of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth) and the Federal Court's equitable jurisdiction, judicial review under the *Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977* (Cth), referrals to the National Anti-Corruption Commission, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, and proceedings under the *Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979* (Cth) and the *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth) where applicable.

6.2	In the event that, contrary to our clients' position, proceedings are commenced against either of them in respect of the conduct underlying the December 2022 regulatory decisions, our clients will apply for a permanent stay of those proceedings on the grounds (without limitation): (a) abuse of process by reason of post-discontinuance persistence and the totality of the conduct of Commonwealth officers since December 2022 (*Jago v District Court (NSW)*; *Walton v Gardiner*; *Williams v Spautz*); (b) executive misconduct in the *Moti v The Queen* sense, including reliance on a brief tainted by the matters identified in section 2 above; and (c) breach by the Commonwealth of its model-litigant obligations under the *Legal Services Directions 2017* (Cth). The application will be filed in the criminal court in which any such proceedings are laid, on the day of filing of charges.

**7.	The substantive position our clients put on the public-interest assessment**

7.1	Our clients ask the Office, in considering any future charging decision in respect of either of them, to weigh the following matters in the public-interest assessment under the Policy:

(a)	the fact of the 23 February 2024 discontinuance for want of evidence in respect of Mrs Reid, and the absence (so far as our clients are aware) of any genuinely new evidence sufficient to satisfy the reasonable-prospects-of-conviction test;

(b)	the fact that Mr Reid has never been charged in five years of investigation and that the materials identified in section 2 above directly implicate the integrity of the investigative process underlying any case the Office might receive;

(c)	the inconsistency between the AGS representation to the Tribunal of 11 March 2025 and (i) the 9 January 2025 contemporaneous statement of Mrs Reid's then-solicitor that the investigation did not look to have ended, and (ii) the ABF's own documented characterisation of the 25 September 2024 stop as conducted "on behalf of another agency" with "Law Enforcement interest";

(d)	the documented coercion of witnesses (Ms Fanny-Dulloo, Ms Burke, Mr Shapiera);

(e)	the documented telecommunications interception admission (Maloney transcript, 7 January 2025);

(f)	the documented multi-agency persistence (NDIS Commission, NDIA, ABF, ATO, AGS, and at least one further unidentified Commonwealth law-enforcement agency) over a continuous period exceeding five years;

(g)	the impact of the conduct on the applicants' disabled child, whose NDIS-funded supports were terminated as a direct consequence of the regulatory action against his parents and who has, by reason of the climate of investigation and the refusal of providers to engage, been cared for by the applicants alone; and

(h)	the absence to date of any adjudicated finding in any forum on the underlying allegations.

7.2	On any reasonable view of these matters, the public-interest threshold for commencement of proceedings is, in our submission, not met and cannot be met on the existing record.

**8.	Conclusion**

We invite the Office to engage with us in correspondence on the matters set out above. If the Office is unable, by reason of the operational confidentiality of any current consideration, to address each of the requests in section 5, we ask that the Office at minimum confirm in writing the procedural commitment at paragraph 5(c) — that any decision to commence proceedings will be preceded by a reasonable opportunity for our clients to make written representations under the Policy.

We are available to meet with the Office in person, or to attend by videoconference, at the Office's convenience.
