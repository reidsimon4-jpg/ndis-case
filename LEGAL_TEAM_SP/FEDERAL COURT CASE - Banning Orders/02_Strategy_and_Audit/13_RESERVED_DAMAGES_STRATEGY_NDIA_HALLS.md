# Reserved compensation strategy — NDIA and Nathan Halls

**Prepared:** 15 July 2026  
**Status:** confidential counsel work plan; do not file in the judicial-review proceeding  
**Purpose:** preserve and investigate possible compensation claims without overstating the present evidence

## Bottom line

The present documents justify preservation, targeted production and a limitation opinion. They do not yet justify pleading malicious prosecution against Nathan Halls personally.

Malicious prosecution is potentially available to Vida because she was prosecuted. It is not Simon's tort merely because he was investigated, banned or economically affected. FFG also was not criminally prosecuted. Simon and FFG would require a different cause of action and proof of their own loss.

The most important missing proof is the prosecution-causation chain: who decided to refer what, what Halls knew, what he communicated to investigators, whether he withheld or distorted material, and whether that conduct was sufficiently instrumental in the CDPP prosecution. A referral, suspicion, cooperation with investigators or operational action is not by itself procurement of a prosecution.

## 1. Separate the three potential plaintiffs

### Vida

Potential causes requiring counsel review:

- malicious prosecution;
- misfeasance in public office;
- negligence or breach of a recognized duty, if available on the pleaded facts;
- unlawful interference/economic torts only if the strict elements can be particularized; and
- public-law compensation routes, if any, arising from proven statutory contraventions.

### Simon

Simon was not charged on the available record. He cannot simply adopt Vida's malicious-prosecution claim. Possible claims would have to arise from acts directed to him and proven damage, potentially including misfeasance in public office or another recognized cause. The threshold is high and the record presently shows administrative action, not the necessary state of mind.

### FFG

FFG's possible economic loss from payment interruption, revocation, banning, participant/provider displacement and reputational damage must be matched to a viable duty or tort, causation and recoverability. Corporate loss is not automatically recoverable through Vida or Simon.

## 2. Malicious prosecution — elements to prove

Under *A v New South Wales* [2007] HCA 10 and *Beckett v New South Wales* [2013] HCA 17, counsel should test at least:

1. the defendant initiated, procured or maintained the prosecution;
2. the prosecution terminated favourably to Vida;
3. the defendant acted without reasonable and probable cause at the relevant time; and
4. the defendant acted maliciously, meaning for a dominant purpose other than the proper invocation of the criminal law.

Damage must also be pleaded and proved.

### Favourable termination

The 23 February 2024 certified transcript records dismissal for want of prosecution and formal discharge. It also records a Commonwealth intention to recharge. *Beckett* confirms that favourable termination does not necessarily require proof of innocence, but the legal effect of this particular WA dismissal and the continuing recharge position needs a specific opinion. Do not state that the transcript is an acquittal.

### Defendant's instance

The CDPP ordinarily exercises an independent prosecutorial function. To sue Halls or the NDIA, Vida would need evidence that the proposed defendant did more than provide information or cooperate:

- knowingly supplied decisive false information;
- deliberately withheld decisive exculpatory information;
- pressured or procured the charging decision;
- maintained a prosecution after the evidentiary basis materially collapsed; or
- otherwise became sufficiently instrumental that the prosecution was in substance at that defendant's instance.

This is the central evidentiary gap.

### Absence of reasonable and probable cause

The inquiry is temporal. Later dismissal and the present absence of an investigation do not themselves prove that reasonable and probable cause was absent when charges were initiated or maintained. The case requires reconstruction of the information honestly held and reasonably available at each relevant time.

### Malice

Improper operational conduct, competitive benefit, hostility, reputational protection, retaliation or a desire to force a provider exit may be evidentiary, but malice requires proof of the operative dominant purpose concerning prosecution. Avoid inferring malice simply from an adverse email, legal error, a harsh ban or a failed prosecution.

## 5. Misfeasance in public office — distinct and demanding

Misfeasance is not a fallback label for unlawful administration. Counsel must identify:

- the particular public power or purported power;
- the particular officer;
- the act beyond or in abuse of power;
- actual knowledge of invalidity and likely harm, or the required form of reckless indifference;
- causation; and
- legally recoverable loss.

Examples of evidence that could support, but would not automatically establish, reckless indifference include:

- a contemporaneous warning that the officer lacked authority, followed by deliberate action without checking the limit;
- a recorded awareness that decisive information was false or materially incomplete, followed by use of it anyway;
- deliberate refusal to inspect readily available exculpatory material while asserting the opposite as fact;
- an audit trail showing the officer knew statutory preconditions were absent but represented they were satisfied; or
- conscious use of a coercive power for an identified foreign purpose despite advice about the lawful purpose.

The Commission's procedural-fairness comment is not enough on its own. It identifies litigation risk and recommended disclosure; it does not establish Halls' knowledge, NDIA abuse of power or prosecution causation.

### New internal-record leads, not present liability proof

The final Commission audit adds four state-of-mind and causation leads:

1. **T6 policy/reasons collision:** the policy required direct involvement or a responsible failure significantly contributing to the conduct before key personnel would ordinarily be banned, while Simon's review used directorship, attributed liability and constructive awareness. This supports inquiry into what the reviewers and delegates understood. It does not itself prove subjective knowledge that power was exceeded.
2. **T49–T53 version path:** legal/process comments recommended providing requested information to reduce procedural-fairness risk, but the final path selected refusal and a shorter extension. This may become relevant to knowledge of process risk. It is not proof of knowing illegality, and the commenter identity/full advice remain unproved.
3. **Review corrections and the TIR:** Simon's reviewer corrected responsibility-period attribution, double counting and a major amount after the TIR recorded that the proportion of claims corresponding to services actually provided was unknown. This identifies specific correction dates and custodians for any later-maintenance theory. It does not prove what the original delegate or prosecution honestly knew at an earlier date.
4. **FFG review concessions:** the Commission later acknowledged deficiencies in information and articulation of analysis/weight in the original Project Archer decision. This supports targeted production. It does not establish malice, prosecution procurement or compensable tort liability.

For damages purposes, obtain the native advice, metadata, workflow/open logs and communications before pleading any subjective state of mind.

Obtain a written WA/Commonwealth limitation opinion immediately. Questions include:

- when each cause accrued;
- whether the 23 February 2024 dismissal is the accrual date for malicious prosecution;
- whether any notice-before-action regime applies;
