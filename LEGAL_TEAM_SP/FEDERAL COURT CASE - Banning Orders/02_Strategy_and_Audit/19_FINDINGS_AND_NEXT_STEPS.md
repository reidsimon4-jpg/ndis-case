# Final findings and next steps

## The proposed Federal Court opening

The shortest defensible opening is:

1. Section 73ZN(2) authorised prohibition or restriction of a person from engaging in specified activities.
2. The April 2023 orders instead prohibit any involvement with or in an entire sector, in any capacity, expressly including indirect conduct and an open-ended residue.
3. *Sunflower* holds materially narrower wording beyond that power and requires a personal, activity-specific, risk-responsive connection.
4. The reasons do not supply that connection or justify five years by reference to the risk of each activity.
5. Simon's record admits limited information about his actual operational involvement; the Commission's own policy required direct involvement or a responsible failure significantly contributing to the conduct; and the reviewer later substituted corporate attribution and “should have been aware”.
6. Vida's review changes the decisive statutory basis without notifying that change.
7. The ongoing ART cases have not produced an outcome after years; each further month consumes the remaining order term; the legality issues are short and documentary.

## Evidence discipline

Use three labels consistently:

- **Original delegate material:** available before 20–21 December 2022.
- **Review material:** first introduced before or on 21 April 2023.
- **Later/current material:** relevant to delay, risk, relief or compensation, but not to what the original delegate knew.

Do not use later material to prove the historical reasonable belief unless the legal ground concerns maintenance, current relief or review.

## Realistic assessment

The facial *Sunflower* ground is the best prospect of a short legal win, but no Federal Court outcome is “easy”. Delay, alternative remedy, standing/utility, severance, relief and the Commission's power to make a fresh lawful order remain real issues. The internal documents improve the case because they supply record-based support and admissions. They should be used precisely, without turning a strong legality case into an unprovable bad-faith case.

The most interesting new internal point is the policy/reasons collision in Simon's case. The policy required direct involvement or a responsible failure significantly contributing to the conduct before key personnel would ordinarily be banned. The review reasons instead treated directorship as the link to liability and said Simon should have been aware. That does not make the decision automatically invalid, but it gives the personal-assessment ground a much clearer documentary foundation without requiring Simon to answer the factual allegations in an affidavit.
