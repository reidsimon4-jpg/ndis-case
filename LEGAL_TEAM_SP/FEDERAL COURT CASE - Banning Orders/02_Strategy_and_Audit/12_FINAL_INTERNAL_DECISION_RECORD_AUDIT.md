# Final audit of the internal banning-order decision record

**Prepared:** 15 July 2026  
**Purpose:** counsel working paper for proposed Federal Court judicial review  
**Status:** confidential draft; not evidence, not a pleading, and not legal advice

## Executive conclusion

The internal record materially strengthens a narrow judicial-review case. The best case is not that the Commission had no information or that every allegation was false. It is that:

1. the operative April 2023 instruments use the same kind of indirect, relational and non-exhaustive wording held beyond s 73ZN(2) in *Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2)* [2025] FCA 1442;
2. the record does not show an individual, activity-by-activity and risk-responsive path to the breadth or five-year duration of either personal order;
3. Simon's original personal case was built principally from corporate office, bank-signatory status and assumed benefit even though the investigation note admitted the Commission had limited information about his actual operational involvement;
4. the Commission's own Banning Order Policy said key personnel would be unlikely to be banned without direct involvement or a responsible failure that significantly facilitated, contributed to or exacerbated the conduct;
5. Simon's reviewer later attributed corporate and worker wrongdoing to him, treated activity not prohibited by the original order as possible “circumvention”, and expanded the instrument from provision of supports to every form of NDIS-sector involvement;
6. Vida's reviewer changed the decisive statutory basis from s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) to s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii), did not notify that change in the 22 March 2023 notice, and then treated detailed examination of much of the contravention response as unnecessary;
7. the Commission's own legal comment recommended giving requested information to reduce procedural-fairness risk, while the final brief proceeded on the view that sufficient information had already been supplied; and
8. the FFG review reasons later acknowledged distinct deficiencies in the original project, including inadequate information about some allegations, inadequate articulation of analysis and weight, and failure to give a separate banning-order decision independent reasons.

The Court case should lead with the instruments and *Sunflower*. The internal record should support construction, legal unreasonableness, procedural fairness, materiality and discretionary relief. It should not be used to allege corruption, recklessness or malice in the opening case.

### Complete executive-brief set located

The audited corpus contains five executive briefs: EC22-002000, EC22-002103, EC22-002429, EC22-002581 and EC22-002521. No additional executive brief was located by identifier, title or content. That is a result about the available corpus, not proof that no other workflow document exists. See 20_DECISION_RECORD_COMPLETENESS_MATRIX.md for the attachment-level audit.

### Banning Order Policy contrast

T6 says a banning order is the Commission's most serious regulatory response; each decision is case-by-case; a general prohibition and its duration should extend no further than necessary; a pending charge alone is unlikely to justify a ban; and key personnel are unlikely to be banned unless directly involved or unless a responsible failure significantly facilitated, contributed to or exacerbated the conduct.

The Simon review did not identify that policy link. It instead said corporate and worker wrongdoing could be attributed to Simon because he was a director, and that he “should have been aware”. The policy is not enforceable as if it were legislation, but the contrast supports the statutory submission that the decision-maker substituted corporate attribution and constructive awareness for the required personal, activity-specific assessment.

## 2. Original FFG/Vida executive brief EC22-002000

### Material internal admissions

- The brief recommends immediate suspension, intention to revoke FFG registration and a proposed ten-year banning order against Vida.
- Paragraph 3.1 records that documents had been submitted to the Legal Services Branch and identifies legal input at Attachment 55.
- Paragraphs 3.4–3.5 refer to a considerations document separating alleged contraventions from “state of mind” and transposition of evidence to the notices to ensure procedural fairness.
- Under risks and sensitivities, the brief says the action may be viewed as “quite strong” because the complaints and breaches focus on administrative and personal errors.
- It records that loss of income would be substantial and anticipates a “retaliatory response”.
- The consultation list includes LSB, Registration and the NDIA.

### Significance

The brief is valuable because it shows contemporaneous awareness of severity and process risk. It is not an admission that the proposed action was unlawful. The safest use is to show that:

1. the Commission knew the effect was severe;
2. legal and procedural-fairness issues were live before the first decision;
3. the decision required careful, individual reasoning; and
4. the later refusal to supply more information was a conscious decision made in that setting.

Do not use “retaliatory response” as proof that the Commission acted in retaliation. In context it predicts the applicants' response to enforcement.

## 3. Investigation note: internal tensions

The investigation note states that NDIA/AFP investigations were pending and had not been considered further in the briefing note, but the assessment also relies on statements and information from the NDIA and complainants. That is a drafting and provenance tension, not necessarily a contradiction. Counsel should obtain the full document chain before alleging that excluded material was actually relied upon.

### Simon

The note says:

> Despite emails for FFG and The Hive being directed to or including Simon Reid the Commission has limited information about his actual involvement in the operations.

That is the most important original internal statement for Simon. Later material moved from this admitted limitation to a personal banning order through:

- directorship and corporate authority;
- joint bank-signatory status;
- monies being paid into company accounts;
- an inference that Simon likely benefited; and
- on review, attribution of entity and worker wrongdoing.

Those facts may justify inquiry and some inferences. The judicial-review point is that the record must still rationally support the statutory belief about Simon personally and the risk posed by each activity prohibited.

### Vida and FFG

The note treats the alleged fraud risk as serious and relies heavily on NDIA material. It also says action may increase community confidence and that failure to take all available measures may reflect poorly on the Commission and the NDIS.

Participant protection and public confidence can be legitimate regulatory context. The potential legal point is narrower: institutional reputation cannot replace the statutory test, individual evidence, or a proportionate choice of activities and duration. This should remain an alternative ground unless the full record shows it was a substantial operative reason.

### Family exception

The investigation material expressly contemplated that Vida and her son would retain access to NDIS supports, although Vida could not act as a provider to her son. The same protective calibration is missing from Simon's later, broader order even though Vida and their son are participants. That contrast supports uncertainty, unreasonableness and confined interim relief; it is not itself an automatic statutory invalidity rule.

## 4. Internal legal/procedural-fairness warning

The tracked internal comment in the disclosed tender material states, in substance, that earlier AGS advice recommended providing the information requested, because that course would limit the risk of procedural fairness being relied on to challenge the decision. It further recommended, if the risk was accepted, putting to the delegate why FFG/Vida were considered to have sufficient information.

The adjacent comment records that LSB had not reviewed the suspension aspect of the original package and offered advice on addressing any procedural-fairness issue on internal review.

### Proper forensic use

- This is strong evidence that a process risk was identified before the final decision.
- The final brief nevertheless records the conclusion that the notice had adequately disclosed the reasons and information and declined further disclosure.
- The missing covering email, full advice and privilege context matter. Do not characterize the comment as the complete legal advice.
- It may support a procedural-fairness ground and a request for the non-privileged decision record.
- It does not, without more, prove reckless indifference, dishonesty, malice or knowing abuse of power.

## 5. Simon's three executive briefs

### EC22-002103 — intention brief

The recommended personal case relied on Simon's responsibility and authority over Hybrid, alleged false claims by the entity, joint signatory status, and alleged benefit from monies paid to relevant accounts. The underlying evidentiary material was substantially NDIA criminal-investigation material and witness statements concerning the entities, Vida, Alam and workers.

Paragraph 20.3 records that no immediate danger to health, safety or wellbeing had been identified. The brief nevertheless proposed a five-year term and a short submissions period.

### EC22-002429 — extension brief

This two-page document should be included in the opening court book. It records:

- the first notice gave only 13 days;
- the 22 November extension request was not made available to the compliance director until 30 November;
- there was no information identifying an immediate danger posed by Simon; and
- LSB advice had not been sought on that response.

This is not a complete standalone denial-of-fairness case because an extension was ultimately given. It is important evidence against any suggestion that urgency justified truncation, non-disclosure or a sweeping interim approach.

### EC22-002521 — final decision brief

The final recommendation repeats the corporate-authority, signatory and likely-benefit bridge. It does not identify a Commission investigation producing a distinct body of Simon-specific conduct evidence. It records reliance on NDIA statements obtained for criminal investigation/proceedings and NDIA agreement to regulatory reliance subject to caveats.

The resulting original order was narrower than the later order. It prohibited Simon from providing disability supports and services to NDIS-funded participants. It did not prohibit labour hire, business ownership, advice, funding or every sector relationship.

## 6. Simon review: what changed and what did not

### Fair notice was given of the proposed labour-hire wording

The 22 March 2023 review notice enclosed the 9 February 2023 Vitality email and proposed the labour-hire and “any capacity” wording. The Federal Court case should not plead that Simon had no notice of that limb.

### The stronger point is lawful activity used to support an overbroad variation

- Dream Big's incorporation application pre-dated the original order.
- The Vitality email post-dated the original order, but the original instrument did not prohibit labour hire or all NDIS-adjacent business.
- The reviewer described the entities as possible strategic or tactical ways to “circumvent” the original order.
- The reviewer then expanded the order to any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity, including indirect supports, labour hire and assistance of any form.

Under *Sunflower* at [226], a variation can regulate conduct prospectively if authorised, but cannot make conduct lawful under the original instrument unlawful after the fact. The email could be treated as new evidence relevant to a prospective, specifically described labour-hire restriction. It did not relieve the reviewer of justifying the universal chapeau and every other activity.

### Attribution and corrected findings

The Simon review reasons corrected or reduced weight on parts of the original case:

- Vida's bail status was treated as irrelevant to Simon;
- another employee's conduct was not attributed to him on one issue;
- limited weight was given to the expired-plan figure because the NDIA tactical report did not establish what portion reflected services actually delivered; and
- conduct outside Simon's relevant responsibility periods was not attributed to him; and
- instances of misattribution and double counting were corrected.

Despite those corrections, paragraph 38 states it was appropriate to attribute liability to Simon for wrongdoing of the corporate entity and workers and relies on director responsibility and what he “should have been aware” of. That is the sharpest individualized-assessment issue.

The conclusion that Simon was “not suitable at this time to be an NDIS provider” does not explain the wider prohibition on all sector involvement, family assistance, labour hire, funding, guidance and assistance.

## 7. Vida review: the stronger procedural-fairness ground

The Vida reviewer expressly disagreed with the original reliance on s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) and decided that the order should rest on s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii). The reasons say paragraph (iii) was the Commission's usual approach where fraud was alleged and charges were pending and that there was no compelling reason to depart from that practice.

The 22 March 2023 notice disclosed Dream Big material and proposed broader wording. It did not say that:

- the statutory gateway would change from contravention to personal suitability;
- the new gateway would become dispositive;
- submissions addressing the original contravention case would largely be put aside; or
- the reviewer would apply a “usual practice” unless a compelling departure reason was shown.

The reasons then say detailed examination of the contravention submissions was not necessary or conducive because the review proceeded on suitability, while still making adverse findings concerning the alleged conduct.

The reviewer also acknowledged that the original notice/reasons did not contain the level of detail later supplied about charges and the NDIA Statement of Material Facts, but considered knowledge acquired through the criminal proceeding sufficient. Knowing factual allegations is not necessarily notice that an administrative reviewer will decide on a materially different statutory issue.

Materiality should be put without an incriminating merits affidavit. The realistic lost opportunity included submissions about:

- why paragraph (iii) should not displace paragraph (i);
- the distinction between alleged entity conduct and Vida's personal suitability for each activity;
- current risk after the criminal status changed;
- narrower activities, conditions and family exceptions; and
- duration not pegged to criminal proceedings.

## 8. FFG internal review: express admissions about the original project

The FFG reviewer agreed with the result but disagreed with significant aspects of the original reasoning. The reasons state:

1. the original banning decision adopted the same reasons as revocation, although a separate administrative decision required distinct or independent reasons;
2. the original decision was based on a broader set of matters than necessary and should be confined to the alleged fraud case;
3. there were deficiencies/failings in sufficiently providing information about some allegations; and
4. there were deficiencies/failings in clearly articulating the analysis and weight given to submissions.

The reviewer sought to cure those matters through review and later disclosure. Those admissions do not automatically invalidate Simon's or Vida's decisions. They are important because they arose from the same Project Archer record and confirm that process and reasons defects were real, not invented after *Sunflower*.

Counsel should decide whether to use the FFG review reasons in the opening case. The safer first use is in the confidential chronology, disclosure request and reply to any assertion that the original process was uniformly complete.

## 9. The orders themselves

### Simon original — 21 December 2022

The original order prohibited Simon from providing disability supports and services to NDIS-funded participants for five years. Its text is more activity-like than the later order. The challenge is principally the personal reasoning path and duration, not the word “providing”.

### Vida original — 20 December 2022

The original order prohibited Vida from providing supports and services “directly or indirectly”. That is a direct *Sunflower* issue. It contained express exceptions for Vida and her son.

### Both varied orders — 21 April 2023

Each order prohibits:

> any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity

and says this “includes” direct or indirect support provision, labour hire, and support, funding, guidance or assistance of any form to an NDIS provider.

The chapeau is relational rather than activity-specific; the list is non-exhaustive; the first limb uses “indirectly”; and the third limb has no evident outer boundary. Those defects closely match or exceed the wording invalidated in *Sunflower*.

## 10. 2026 amendment does not retrospectively cure the 2023 orders

From 9 April 2026, new s 73ZN(2B)–(2C) expressly allows certain orders concerning persons involved in services that “enable or facilitate” supports or services for people with disability. The continuing s 73ZN(2) still speaks of a person engaging in “specified activities”.

The later provisions do not retrospectively validate an order made in 2023. They should be used carefully:

- not as a substitute for the text and *Sunflower*;
- not as proof of what Parliament subjectively intended in 2023;
- as confirmation that the present Act now contains an express facilitative-services route;
- to rebut any suggestion that the Commission may simply treat the 2026 text as having always applied; and
- to recognize that a new prospective order may be possible only after current statutory preconditions and a fair process are satisfied.

## 11. Affidavit and court-book treatment

### Put in the common/documentary affidavit

- signed original and review orders;
- review notices and reasons;
- certified criminal-court transcript;
- objective ART chronology and directions;
- AGS/Commission letters concerning present investigation status;
- date and circumstances of receiving omitted Simon briefs in 2026;
- current register entries;
- EC22-002103, EC22-002429 and EC22-002521; and
- only those FFG/Vida internal documents counsel considers necessary and admissible.

### Keep Simon's personal affidavit minimal

Prove identity, receipt of instruments, family-participant facts, current practical effect, delay chronology within knowledge and any interim undertaking. Do not add a new sworn account of corporate operations, finances, PRODA, recruitment, the Vitality recipient list or underlying allegations without criminal-defence clearance.

### Keep Vida's personal affidavit minimal

Prove identity, receipt and content of notices, absence of notice of the statutory-ground change, the categories of non-incriminating submissions she lost the opportunity to make, objective criminal status, present effect and delay. Do not answer the alleged fraud case in the affidavit.

### Do not annex wholesale

Do not annex the entire tender bundle, privileged solicitor correspondence, speculative dossiers, Gmail hearsay or internal strategy memoranda. Use authenticated native documents and a concise index.
