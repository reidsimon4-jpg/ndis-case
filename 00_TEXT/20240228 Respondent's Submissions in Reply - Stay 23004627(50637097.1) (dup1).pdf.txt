       Legal Submissions

       reference), where the law is set out in paragraphs 20 – 32 (inclusive).

Application of YFFM to these proceedings

5.    Mrs Vida Reid appeared before the Perth Magistrates Court for a mention hearing on
      23 February 2024 in relation to the criminal proceedings lodged against her. These
      criminal proceedings have not progressed as there remains an outstanding legal
      professional privilege objection in relation to documents obtained pursuant to a search
      warrant. We understand that the expectation is for this issue to be discussed and,
      ideally resolved, between the Commonwealth DPP and Mrs Reid’s legal team.

6.    As a result of the issue set out above, a brief of evidence in relation to Mr Simon Reid
      has not been sent by the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) to the
      Commonwealth DPP.

7.    For completeness, Mr Simon Reid was offered the opportunity to speak with the NDIA
      investigators at the time when the search warrant was executed on the marital home,
      he declined.

8.    For the reasons set out in YFFM, and in particular in paragraph 48, the Respondent
      submits “that the prospect of the applicant being required to give evidence, and being
      subject to cross-examination, in relation to the same transactions which would be the
      subject of the possible criminal proceedings is sufficient to create a plausible
      apprehension of injustice and therefore contempt of any criminal proceedings which
      may be commenced”. The only substantive difference in this application is that the
      Respondent cannot say that criminal proceedings are being actively considered by the
      Commonwealth DPP, as no brief of evidence has been provided.

9.    Furthermore, given the same factual circumstance will need to be considered by the
      Tribunal in determining whether the banning order made against Simon Reid is
      reasonable, there is a significant risk that “potential damage…..could be caused to
      criminal proceedings if the jury were to learn that this Tribunal had made findings
      against the applicant….” Similarly, to YFFM, “the prospect of that occurring in this case
      is not fanciful given that the banning order and also the Tribunal’s decision will be
      easily searchable on the internet as it will be a published decision of this Tribunal and
      will be “searchable” on the respondent’s website. Then, of course, there is the
      prospect of the matter being given publicity in the media”. It is well known that Mrs
      Reid’s criminal proceedings have been reported in the media.

10.   For these reasons, namely both to avoid a situation where the Tribunal may be in
      contempt of court and to preserve the integrity of the Tribunal process, the respondent
      submits that the Tribunal should grant this stay.

11.   In response to paragraph 33 of the AS, and the submission that “It is clear by the
      Respondent’s T-doc disclosure to the Applicant of only 50 pages, in contrast to Ms
      Vida Reid’s T-doc of 1,162 pages, that the Commissioner’s decision for Mr Reid was
      manifested by using untested evidence and unfounded allegations that before the
      Court, which he is not part of”, the Respondent reminds the Applicants that Ms Vida
      Reid’s T-doc disclosure is the lead bundle for the purposes of these applications and
      documents that relate to all three applicants are included in Mrs Reid’s T-doc bundle.
      Mr Reid’s T-doc bundle contains documents that are relevant only to Mr Reid. It is
      therefore not accurate to say there are only 50 documents that are relevant to Mr Reid,
      there are, in reality 1212 pages of relevant material.



Summary and Order sought

12.   Having regard to the matters referred to above, the Commissioner continues to seek
      an order that these AAT applications be stayed until the concurrent criminal
      proceedings or any future criminal proceedings arising from the same set of
      circumstances has been determined.
