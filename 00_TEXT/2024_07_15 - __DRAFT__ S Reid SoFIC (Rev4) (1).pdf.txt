Nature of Application

1.    The decision subject of review in this application is a reviewable decision of the
      Respondent, dated 21 April 2023 (Decision Letter). It can be found at T document
      64.1.

2.    The Decision Letter was a decision pursuant to section 100 of the National Disability
      Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (Cth) (NDIS Act).

3.    The Decision Letter decided that the Applicant was not suitable to be involved in the
      provision of supports or services to people with disability of the National Disability
      Insurance Scheme (NDIS), pursuant to section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act.

4.    The ultimate consequence of the Decision Letter was to amend the banning order
      originally made against Mr Reid under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii).

5.    There are three applicants to this matter, being Ms Vida Reid (2023/3361), Simon
      Reid (2023/3363) and Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (2023/4494).

6.    The matters were joined as per Deputy President Mischin’s Directions dated 19 July
      2023.

7.    This Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions relates to Mr Simon Reid’s application
      to the Tribunal only.

            restricting a person who is or was employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS

Other persons may be banned under the NDIS Act


97.     The central allegation against the Mr and Mrs Reid relates to FFG and Hybrid Hive
        wrongfully making claims against NDIS. However multiple persons were involved in
        those claims. Yet only Mr and Mrs Reid have been banned.

98.     Section 73ZN(2) of the NDIS Act allows for banning orders in relation to “a person who
        is or was employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider, or a person who is or was
        a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider”,

99.     Section 11A defines ‘key personnel’ as:


        “(a) a member of the group of persons who is responsible for the executive decisions of
                 the person or entity;


         (c)        any other person who has authority or responsibility for (or significant
         influence over) planning, directing or controlling the activities of the person or entity.”


100. The above means that other people may be subject to sanction for wrongdoing, not
        just directors.

101. Mr Reid relied upon and was entitled to rely on others as to the accuracy and
        correctness of claims, especially the Chief Financial Officer and accounting staff of the
        corporations, as is specifically provided for in section 189 of the Corporations Act 2001
        (Cth).

102. Yet it appears the Respondent has failed, possibility deliberately so, to investigate any
        other key personnel of FFG or Hybrid Hive.

103. Indeed, there has been no evidence produced that the Chief Financial Officer of the
        two companies, Mr Rejaul Alam, was ever questioned by the Respondent’s
        investigators.

104. Currently before the Tribunal, there is no evidence as to who actually produced the
        invoices in question. No evidence as to why the said invoices were issued or the basis
        in doing so. Lastly there is no evidence as to Mr Reid directing anyone to raise a false
        invoice.

Ambit of current banning order excessively wide


105. At its highest the Decision Letter found Mr Reid to have not provided sufficient
         oversight of corporate operations. No finding as to fraud was made against Mr Reid.

106. As to working in the NDIS sector post banning, the Respondent’s reasoning as outlined
         in the Decision Letter does not make sense. Either Mr Reid breached the order, or he
         did not. If he did, then why was it necessary to broaden its scope?48 If he did not, then
         he has done nothing in relation to setting up VWA.

107. Given the above, it is submitted that the ultimate orders made on 21 April 2023 can
         be described as overly excessive to ridiculous. The order states Mr Reid may not have
         “…any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”, this means, for example,
         he:


         (a)   cannot operate a taxi if the passenger, payer or destination is in any linked to
               NDIS;

         (b)   provide handyman services to a property if the customer or property is any way
               linked to NDIS; or

         (c)   sell food from a stall or shop (including as an employee) if the customer or
               payee is any way linked to NDIS.


108. The orders are grossly disproportionate to what the Respondent found as to Mr Reid’s
         conduct in the Decision Letter and the evidence before the Respondent.
