I, VIDA REID, of 96 Amethyst Crescent Mount Richon 6112, unemployed, in the State of
Western Australia, sincerely declare and affirm as follows:

1.    I make this affidavit in support of the application to stay these proceedings on behalf
      of Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (FFG) and on my own behalf.

Background
2.    I was born on 11 January 1987 in Iran.

3.    I grew up primarily in Malaysia and moved to Australia on 1 October 2011.

4.    I was a registered director of FFG from 28 August 2020 to 1 November 2022.

5.    Attached and marked “VR-1” is a true copy of a current and historical company
      extract in respect of FFG.

6.    I was the subject of a criminal prosecution initiated by the National Disability
      Insurance Agency (NDIA) on or about 18 October 2022.

7.    On or about 23 February 2024, that prosecution was dismissed for want of
      prosecution.
8.    Attached and marked “VR-2” is a true copy of the “Outcome Details” in relation to
      the prosecution dated 26 February 2024.

Australian Border Force

9.    On or about 25 September 2024, my family and I were at Perth International Airport
      set to board flight No. QZ537 destined for Bali, Indonesia.

10.   My family includes my husband, Simon Reid, and my children, Alex and Atrisa.

11.   Alex is 9 years of age. Alex suffers from conditions including Autism level 3, Global
      Developmental Delay, Speech Delay, Attention Deficit Disorder, Attention-
      Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Profound Intellectual Disability and Speech
      Impairment.

12.   My daughter is now 16 years of age. She was 15 at the time of the events described
      below.

13.   On that day, my family and I were stopped by persons who identified themselves as
      belonging to the Australian Border Force (ABF). We were stopped at the
      immigration gates, which would not open for us to pass through after about 5
                                                                                         3


      minutes of waiting. I could see the ABF officers pointing to us and making phone
      calls from their desk which was behind the automatic gates.

14.   A female ABF officer approached us. Although I do not recall the precise words she
      used, I recall the gist of what she said, being, that she would open the gates but we
      must immediately go to the left hand side and wait for a few officers who will come
      down and want to speak to us.

15.   There were four to six ABF officers in the group who approached us. One of the
      officer’s names was Julian. I cannot remember the other male officers’ names, but I
      recall that one of them was of Asian ethnicity. I observed the officers make several
      phone calls and then other officers came towards us, who were asked by the officers
      already standing with us to take our luggage from the plane.

16.   I asked the officers why we were being stopped. I do not recall the exact words
      used, but I recall the gist of the conversation, being:

      16.1. One of the officers asked us if we had been in any trouble lately;

      16.2. Simon responded by saying, yes, my wife was charged with fraud by the NDIS;

      16.3. One of the officers then responded by saying, yes that’s right, this is why we
            have stopped you, it has to do with that;

      16.4. I then turned to them and said, I went to court and all charges have been
            dropped, why are you doing this?

      16.5. One of the officers said to me, the NDIS and the NDIS minister have asked us
            to search you every time you enter or leave the Country, we’re just doing our
            jobs and we get our orders from the top;

      16.6. Simon said, why, the charges have been dropped?

      16.7. One of the officers responded by saying, you have been put on a list and have
            been flagged and we have been instructed to stop you every time you come
            across the border.

17.   My family and I were then detained by the ABF officers and searched for about three
      hours. When I say “detained”, I mean we had our passports taken from us, meaning
      we could not go back as the gates were locked behind us, or go forward to board
      our flight, and we were told we could not leave.

18.   We were taken to an area that was separated from everyone else at the airport. We
      were told we could not move until told otherwise, and any disagreements may lead
      to our arrest. The officers took Simon’s phone and my phone.

19.   The officers searched us at a metal table and took our other electronic devices. The
      officers took our devices to a different room which an officer told us was where the
      data would be downloaded and copied over.

20.   One of the officers asked Simon for the passcode to his phone. I cannot recall the
      exact words used, but I recall the gist of the conversation, being:

      20.1. Simon said that he could not remember the passcode as he used a thumbprint
            reader to access the phone;

      20.2. Julian and/or the other Asian background officer said something to insinuate
            that Simon was lying, and then took Simon’s phone and said they would send
            it to Canberra to be “jailbroken” and that this will wipe his apps and data.

21.   Around this time, our son was visibly becoming quite distressed while the officers
      were raising their voices at us.

22.   Our son also has trouble staying still for extended periods and was running around
      everywhere.

23.   During this time:

      23.1. The officers searched our belongings, including my daughter’s luggage which
            they had brought back from the plane and asked her to unlock. The officers
            went through all her belongings including her underwear, clothes, bra and
            sanitary pads;

      23.2. The officers asked me about a cheap fake gold ring they found in my
            daughter’s suitcase and asked me what it was;

      23.3. The officers took my son’s bag of story books away from him, even though I
            asked them not to, telling them that he has disabilities and is attached to the
            books. The officers went through the books one-by-one while my son was
            screaming for them back, visibly quite distressed;

      23.4. The electronic devices the officers took included my phone, Simon’s phone,
            and both our Surface Pro laptops. When taking these items the officers told
                                                                                         5


           us, in substance, that they did not need our consent under the customs laws
           and have the powers to do what they want;

      23.5. I asked on more than one occasion for permission to contact our lawyers,
           which they refused;

      23.6. We were not advised of any rights we had during this process. We informed
           the officers on various occasions that there were items of legal professional
           privilege amongst our electronics to which they responded by stating, in
           substance, that legal professional privilege does not apply to them at Border
           Force;

      23.7. We were explicitly instructed to unlock the devices to allow their direct
           connection for data extraction, being stated to us that, in substance, without
           unlocking, the devices could neither be searched nor the content downloaded.
           We were further warned that if we refused to comply, the devices would be
           confiscated, accessed, and searched at a later time regardless of our consent;

      23.8. The officers stated, in substance, that we could be arrested and detained for
           longer if we failed to cooperate. We complied with their requests in these
           circumstances, as I felt we had no other choice under duress. They plugged
           the phones into machines, and my phone was not the same when returned to
           me. For example, different apps were open, passwords were deleted and
           things searched including conversations with our legal teams, which I could
           see as several messages were left open;

      23.9. The officers made copies of paperwork we had with us despite our claims for
           legal professional privilege.

24.   The officers did not return Simon’s iPhone 11 to him on that day. One of the officers
      said that they did not know when or if they would return the iPhone 11 to us. We
      eventually received an email on or about 2 December 2024 from OSCG Perth Airport
      stating the iPhone 11 was now in Perth Airport ready for us.

25.   Simon and I instructed the law firm Hammond Legal to write to the ABF about these
      events.

      October 2024 and 23 October 2024 between Hammond Legal and the ABF.

We have, below, responded to your request for further particulars in regards to the

The basis for detention, searches and property seizure
With respect to your clients’ interactions with the Australian Border Force (ABF) on 25
September 2024 at the Perth International Airport, I can advise that at no point were they
subject to detention, or was any of their property seized.
Goods belonging to your clients were examined by the ABF in accordance with the
sections of the Customs Act referenced below. Your clients were present during their
examination but they were not compelled to remain with their goods at the screening
point by the ABF.
Under section 186 of the Customs Act 1901 (Customs Act), ABF officers are entitled to
examine goods (including electronic devices) subject to customs control.
Section 30 of the Customs Act provides for when goods are subject to customs control.
