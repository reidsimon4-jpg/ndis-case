STYNES, MS:   Your Honour, section 25, for the charges to
be dismissed.

HIS HONOUR:     Yes.

STYNES, MS:   The reason for that, your Honour, is that
there has been some 15 months since the charges were
originally commenced. There is legal professional
privilege claims outstanding that the accused has made,
which I understand are going to take some time. So no
evidence has been led. I think that was one of the queries
your Honour had made. The committal brief – part of it has
been served on the accused. It’s incomplete, but the
Commonwealth has made the decision to discontinue the
charges at this stage, having regard to outstanding
disclosure and the LPP that’s outstanding that needs to be
reviewed. I understand there’s six devices of the accused.

HIS HONOUR:     Ms Stynes, I don’t want to be obstructionist.

STYNES, MS:     Yes, your Honour.

section 25 of the Criminal Procedure Act there in front of
you.

STYNES, MS:     I believe we do, your Honour.

HIS HONOUR:     Doesn’t it not read:

      If no evidence has been adduced in relation to the
      charge, the prosecutor may inform the court that the
      prosecutor is discontinuing the prosecution of the
      charge.

Two:

      On being so informed, the court may consent or, if
      satisfied that discontinuance would be an abuse of
      process, refuse to consent to the discontinuance of the
      prosecution concerned.

STYNES, MS:     Yes, your Honour.

HIS HONOUR:   So the power is premised on the basis that if
no evidence has been adduced in relation to the charge – I
will pause there.

STYNES, MS:     Yes, your Honour.
