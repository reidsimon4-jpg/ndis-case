Furthermore, the principle of individual justice, as emphasized in Johnson v. Johnson
(2000) 201 CLR 488, supports the necessity for separate proceedings where distinct
interests are at stake. The conflation of my case with that of my husband's not only
undermines procedural integrity but also deprives me of a fair and impartial hearing,
violating the principles of natural justice.

I formally request that my matter, and that of Forward Focus Group, be separated from
my husband's. The argument that our matters are inextricably linked is unfounded and
unjust, as we are distinct individuals entitled to independent and fair consideration of
our cases. It is paramount that the Tribunal intervene to rectify this situation, which has
persisted for over two years, causing undue hardship and delay.

2. My Legal Standing is Independent from Mr. Simon Reid

The Tribunal must assess my application on its own merits, independent of Mr. Simon Reid.
The notion that my application should be contingent upon his position is legally unsound and
contravenes my individual rights under Kioa v West (1985) 159 CLR 550. The Tribunal is
obligated to ensure procedural fairness for each applicant separately.

Furthermore, throughout my arrest, detention, and court hearings brought on by the
respondant, the authorities repeatedly asserted that I used Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd
(FFG) as a vehicle to commit alleged offenses. Given that the Respondent has consistently
linked my personal matter with FFG's, it is appropriate and necessary for both matters to be
stayed together to ensure procedural fairness and consistency. The Respondent’s assumption
that my case is inherently tied to Mr. Reid is erroneous, quite frankly dishonest, unethical,
without merit and undermines my legal rights as an independent applicant.

3. Procedural Unfairness and Model Litigant Obligations

The Australian Government Solicitor (AGS) and the Respondent are subject to the Model
Litigant Principles, which require them to act fairly and efficiently. The AGS’s request to
delay my application pending Simon Reid’s response is an unnecessary procedural hurdle
that violates these obligations.

Additionally, the Tribunal is bound by its informality principle, ensuring that proceedings
are conducted without undue technicality. Unjustified procedural delays contravene the spirit
of the AAT Act and undermine public confidence in the fairness of these proceedings.
