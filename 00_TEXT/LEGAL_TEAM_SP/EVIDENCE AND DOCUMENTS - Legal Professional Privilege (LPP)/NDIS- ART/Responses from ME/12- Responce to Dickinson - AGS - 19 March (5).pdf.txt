Dear Ms. Dickinson,

I write again regarding the inconsistencies in the NDIS Commission’s position as it
relates to any purported “ongoing investigation,” particularly in light of the dropped
charges that originally formed the core rationale for my banning order. While you
confirm that the Commissioner has asked the NDIA about investigations, no steps have
been taken by the Commission to revisit or lift my ban despite the changed
circumstances.

Below I outline why a stay of these proceedings remains essential, especially as new
evidence from the tender bundle confirms the Commission’s own delegate stated that
the ban would be reconsidered if criminal charges were dropped.

1. Direct Quotations from the Tender Bundle

During my internal review (21 April 2023), Ms. Sian Leathem (delegate of the
Commissioner) explicitly linked the entire merit of the ban to the existence of criminal
charges. Some examples, as recorded in the tender bundle:

 A. “The Commission’s belief in Ms. Reid’s unsuitability is strongly reinforced by the
criminal charges currently laid against her, indicating serious alleged dishonesty.”

  B. “Should those charges be dismissed or withdrawn, the Commission would be
prepared to reconsider the banning order, given that the charges significantly underpin
the unsuitability ground.”

These remarks show that unsuitability (under s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii)) was justified based on
the commissions views predominantly on the existence of charges at that time. Indeed,
Ms. Leathem highlighted that continued investigations and possible prosecution by law
enforcement buttressed the entire rationale for the ban.

2. Despite those statements from Ms. Leathem, the criminal charges against me
were dropped in February 2024—over a year ago. Yet:

A) The Commission has neither rescinded nor modified my ban, even though the
primary basis was the presence of “serious” charges.

B) The Commission has not issued any formal statement explaining why it now refuses
to revisit the ban, despite having explicitly acknowledged it would do so if the charges
no longer existed.



3. This silence is particularly concerning given that your 19 March 2025 letter states
NDIA confirmed “no current investigation.” If the Commission’s original foundation
(ongoing charges/investigations) has collapsed, what is the continuing justification
for my ban?

”The Commission’s delegate expressly declared that “if criminal charges are dropped,
we can reconsider the ban.” Yet after these charges indeed were dismissed, the
Commission has made no acknowledgment of changed circumstances. This raises
serious doubts about procedural fairness. An administrative decision-maker who ties a
ruling to a specific factual premise must re-evaluate when that premise disappears.
Failing to do so creates an unfair situation for me, especially as a self-represented party.

Furthermore, the NDIS Commission once argued for caution and possible delay based
on continuing law enforcement interest. Now it effectively denies any investigation
remains, but the ban is still in place, unchanged, defying its own earlier stance.



4. Renewing My Request for a Stay

Given these contradictions, I respectfully request that the Administrative Review
Tribunal grant a stay of my review proceedings until:

 A) The Commission comprehensively clarifies that no criminal or fraud-related agency
(within or beyond NDIA) is conducting investigations.

  B) The Commission acknowledges evidence in it’s possession such as statements
made via Ms. Leathem at the NDIS Commission that the ban “would be reconsidered if
charges are dropped” and explains why it has not taken any remedial action to lift or
revise the ban.

C) Failing that, it may be considered prudent in the interest of natural justice for the
tribunal to make a decision to over-turn the banning order in it’s entirety based on the
evidence in it’s possession and information in these papers.

5. Preference for Written Communications & Tribunal Oversight



I note again your invitation to “contact us to discuss” the matter outside of writing. As a
self-represented party, I insist on written communications to avoid any ambiguity or
informal statements that could later be incorrectly/wrongly reinterpreted. Given the
Commission’s shifting accounts, aggressive, unethical investigations and hostile
approach to me and my family, a clear paper trail is critical for my protection and to
maintain the Tribunal’s trust in the integrity of this process. To date it appears that the
Commission with the assistance and involvement of Miss Dickinson and the utilization
of the AGS as a vehicle, has sought and attempted to mislead the Tribunal. These
actions appear calculated and malicious to compound the ongoing injustice and
trauma inflicted upon our family.

I also request that the Tribunal actively enforce and supervise these disclosures. It is
essential for the Tribunal to confirm that the Commission’s “no investigation”
statements extend to all members of the NDIS Fraud Fusion Taskforce, not solely the
NDIA, and that the Commission accounts for the dropped charges in reconsidering my
ban.

The Commission’s own internal review record (cited above) reveals that the entire
“unsuitability” basis for my ban revolved around allegations now obviated by dismissed
charges. If the Commission remains consistent with its delegate’s words, it should by
rights revisit or revoke the ban. Since it has not, I urge the Tribunal to impose a stay until
these matters are conclusively addressed.

I appreciate your prompt written response to each point in this letter. Please note I
reserve all rights to pursue judicial review and/or other remedies if I am compelled to
proceed under this unresolved state of affairs.



Kind Regards,

Vida Reid
