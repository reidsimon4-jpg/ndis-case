Caring for a child with a disability is challenging, the obstacles they face require extra patience, compassion, and energy than caring for a child without a disability. The
NDIS exists to provide support for the extra care and support required because of a person’s disability. Parental responsibility refers to the tasks required of you as a
parent a child but where does this end ? I think the best way to explore this concept is with an example scenario:
Eliana is a mother and a wife to her FIFO husband and they both share another child together. Ella requires physical support as she does not understand dangers and
places herself in unsafe situations at times, Eliana’s husband does shift work for a mining company and is often away on 2:1 and 4:1 rosters and again at times this changes.
Eliana is a full-time mum looking after the 2 children, running the house and ensuring everybody’s needs gets met.

During the planning meeting a conversation was had with her NDIS planner, she told them that she is struggling to keep up with all the needs of her children, she is finding
it especially difficult in the day-care mornings/and non day-care morning’s, where she must wash and get Ella ready, then the other sibling. Eliana does one task and then
gets distracted constantly which results in constant supervision by Eliana and her behavioural issues linked to her disabilities. She says that her own health has deteriorated
from the stress of caring for the children and has resulted in to complete carer’s burn out.

Eliana is requesting assistance with support workers to give her more time after day-care to run around to therapy appointments, assistance for 3 hours in the morning for
Ella so that she can conduct and complete her parental responsibilities to her other child who has so far been placed on the back burner. A support person to take Ella to
swimming lessons, community based activities to keep the social interaction there and build her capacity and most importantly overnight respite to prevent carer’s
burnout. She says that she doesn’t like the idea of sending Ella away but doesn’t know what else to do as sh
e isn’t coping. This is also putting a huge strain on Eliana’s marriage.
Let’s breakdown those requests and consider what is “parental responsibility” and what is “disability specific need”

• Meal preparation – for most Australian children living at home their parents are responsible for the preparation of meals. Even though Eliana makes the point that she
spends a lot of time taking her child to therapies (disability specific)

• Self-care for Ella – most 3-year-old girls without a disability would be expected to get themselves up and ready for day care at this age to a point. They would have the
capacity to perform all self-care tasks and pack their bags. Because of Ella’s disability, she requires support from her mum to get ready in the morning, something that most
parents would not have to do. In this instance this would be considered a disability specific support and would likely be funded.

• Supports for Ella – most 3-year-old girls without a disability require some support to get ready for day care and any other activities to even leave the house. Eliana has
stated that Ell’s difficulty with getting ready for day care is due to lack of attention, getting distracted and monstrous tantrums again linked to her disabilities. Before funded
supports would be put in place for assisting Ella in the mornings and throughout the day the NDIS would consider whether any therapist had developed strategies to be
used at home (such as a visual tracking chart) and whether these had been implemented to build Ella’s capacity to do these things herself. The NDIS may also consider
whether by bringing supports in for Ella, her mum will now have more capacity to work with Ella on her morning routine.

• Overnight respite – as previously stated caring for a child with a disability requires extra resources as a parent than caring for a child without a disability. In this
instance, Eliana is providing 100% of her children’s care while her husband works away and has 1 child with higher needs. If her child did not have disabilities it is unlikely
that she would feel the need for respite support. Eliana has stated that she doesn’t feel comfortable in sending her child to overnight respite but does not know what else
to do. In this instance she could consider accessing in home support to supervise the children in her own home while she sees to the million other things a mother/wife/
human being must do. This would be considered a disability specific support and would likely be funded.
