A. WHY THESE DIRECTIONS ARE NEEDED
3. On 9 October 2026 the Respondent's lawyers confirmed: "the Commission has not yet filed a SFIC in
relation to these matters." Their position is that it is "incumbent upon each Applicant to first set out" why
the banning orders should not have been made.
4. I have already done that. Under the directions of 15 May 2024, which required the Respondent to file its
statement of facts, issues and contentions (SFIC) after the applicants, I filed mine on 15 July 2024. On 31 July
2024 the Respondent asked for its own date to be moved. The directions were vacated on 3 October 2024.
The Respondent's 9 October 2026 email refers to "an updated SFIC in the case of yourself", so the
Respondent accepts that my SFIC is on the file. No direction has required a response to it since 3 October
2024, but nothing prevented one. Twenty-seven months on, the Respondent's position on what I filed is
unknown, and the Direction of 2 October requires me to go first a second time.
5. I am unrepresented. The bundles run to about 1,000 pages, and documents were left out of them until
2026; on 7 May 2026 the Respondent accepted that some "should have been provided previously". The
Respondent made the decisions and holds the evidence. It can say, in a few pages, which findings and
allegations it relies on against each applicant. I cannot answer a case I cannot identify. Specifically:
      (a) The internal review decision of 21 April 2023 excluded much of the original case. It found the
      Clulow claims could not be attributed to me ([29(i)]), gave "minimal weight" to the $1,485,527
      expired-plan figure ([29(j)]) and excluded conduct outside my director periods ([32]-[33]). The
      Respondent's submissions of 17 September 2025 at [54.3] list claims for seven participants without
      saying which are relied on against which applicant. On the Respondent's own dates at [54.1]-[54.2], I
      held no office in Forward Focused Group between 3 January 2021 and 1 November 2022. I do not
      know which findings and allegations the Respondent maintains against me.
      (b) The decision does not find that I made, approved or knew of any claim. It attributes Hybrid Hive's
      conduct to me as a director who "should have been aware" ([38]). Whether I am said to have done
      something, or to have failed to supervise someone else, calls for different evidence. I need to know
      which is alleged, in what role, and for which transactions.
      (c) The decision relies on Dream Big Employment Pty Ltd and Vitality WA Pty Ltd as "attempts to
      remain involved in the NDIS sector" ([41]-[43]). The 17 September 2025 submissions say Dream Big
      was involved in NDIS services "while their banning orders were in effect" ([54.5]) and rely on
      complaints of "intimidation or harassment" ([54.4]). They identify no act, date or term of the order.
      None of the decision documents about me finds intimidation or harassment. I need to know whether
      either is alleged against me and, if so, what, when and by whom.
      (d) The decision fixed the period of my order by reference to "the ongoing investigation by the NDIA
      and law enforcement agencies" and said it "can be revisited" when that ended ([46]-[47]). The charges
      against Mrs Reid were dismissed for want of prosecution on 23 February 2024; the Respondent now
      says the prosecution was "dropped"; and its submissions of 17 September 2025 at [38] record the
      NDIA's advice that there is no current criminal investigation. I need to know whether the Respondent
      still relies on [46]-[47] and, if not, what it says justifies the order, its scope and its duration.
      (e) The operative order prohibits "any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity".
      Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No
      2) [2025] FCA 1442 held that s 73ZN(2) does not authorise an order prohibiting indirect involvement in
      supports or services (at [128], [134]) and that prohibited activities must be expressed in terms capable
      of being obeyed (at [131]). I do not know whether the Respondent maintains the order as it stands, or
      what order it will ask the Tribunal to make.
      (f) The Respondent's evidence was produced for a criminal investigation: 34 witness statements taken
      by investigators between 11 and 20 October 2022 in the form of statements for a prosecution
      ("Statement in the matter of R v Vida Reid"), the NDIA Statement of Material Facts, police histories
      obtained through the Australian Federal Police, and the prosecution notice of 18 October 2022. That
      prosecution was discontinued and none of the statements has been tested. The Respondent says it
      "will likely rely solely on the written statements". The NDIA intelligence report of 18 August 2022,
      which preceded that investigation and was before each decision-maker, remains withheld (B2(c)).

B. DIRECTIONS SOUGHT
6. B1. Statement of the Respondent's case (ss 79(2)(b), 79(3) and 24(2); ss 56(1), 9 and 55(1)). By 6
November 2026 the Respondent gives each applicant a statement setting out:
      (a) which findings in that applicant's internal review decision it maintains and where it departs from
      them, including which of [54.3]-[54.5] of its 17 September 2025 submissions it relies on against that
      applicant;
      (b) for each allegation, the act or omission, the date, the person, the role the applicant is said to have
      held, and whether it is put as personal conduct, knowledge or approval, or a failure of oversight (and if
      so, the step said to have been omitted);
      (c) the transactions relied on, with an anonymised participant identifier, claim reference, period,
      amount and defect;
      (d) whether intimidation or harassment is alleged and, if so, the conduct and date;
      (e) any conduct after 21 December 2022 relied on, and whether it is alleged as a contravention (and of
      which term) or as evidence of suitability only;
      (f) the main document for each allegation, by tender number and page, and whether it relies on
      anything not disclosed to the applicants; and
      (g) the order it will ask the Tribunal to make for each applicant having regard to Sunflower (No 2),
      whether it relies on the 2026 amendments to s 73ZN (including s 73ZN(2B)-(2C)), and, for Forward
      Focused Group, whether it says the review lacks utility.
      A reference to an existing paragraph will do. I am not asking for the full SFIC early. I am asking to know
      the case before I answer it a second time. If the statement is late, or relies on allegations or
      documents outside the internal review decisions and the bundles served to date, each applicant's date
      under direction 2 becomes the later of 20 November 2026 and 28 days after receipt, with
      consequential changes to directions 3 to 5.
      In the alternative: the Respondent's SFIC due 18 December 2026 identifies, for each applicant, each
      finding of the internal review decision it maintains and each allegation not found in that decision, the
      17 September 2025 submissions or the bundles served by 23 October 2026; and where any such
      allegation is raised, each applicant's date under direction 4 becomes 28 days after service of the
      Respondent's SFIC.
7. B2. Non-disclosure orders and withheld documents.
      (a) Order 2 of 12 September 2023. The Respondent "no longer presses the claims set out in the 9
      August 2023 index and 12 September 2023 orders" because "the prosecution was dropped". Order 2
      was made "until further order, by reason of current criminal proceedings and/or legal professional
      privilege", and it still stands: it is the reason the Tribunal's FOI unit refused the index documents in
      August 2026, and the copy of the T-documents the Tribunal released under FOI request 2026/1839 in
      August 2026 is the redacted 2023 version. Direction 1 of 2 October 2026 makes the supplementary
      bundle subject to the orders of 26 July 2024 and the pending s 70 application; it does not preserve
      order 2 of 12 September 2023. I ask that order 2 be vacated (s 86).
      (b) Within 7 days the Respondent lists, by item number of the 9 August 2023 index, where each of
      items 1 to 9 and each redacted part of items 12 to 32 now appears in the bundles served, and
      confirms that nothing from the index remains withheld or redacted other than under the order of 26
      July 2024 or the pending s 70 application.
      (c) The order of 26 July 2024 ("Tactical Intelligence Report - Project Archer", 18 August 2022). The
      order was made under ss 37(1AE) and 35(4) of the AAT Act "until further order", on the ground stated
      in the Respondent's one-page request of 21 June 2024 that disclosure "would identify internal
      investigative processes which could prejudice future investigations". No submission for the applicants
      is recorded, and on 17 July 2024 the Registry itself asked the Respondent why the document was not
      even described by title. The application was made four months after the charges against Mrs Reid
      were dismissed. The report is Attachment 5 to briefing note EC22-002000 (the notices of 19 October
      2022) and, as the Respondent's letter of 7 May 2026 confirms, Attachment J to briefing notes EC22-
      002103 and EC22-002521 (my notice of intention and my banning order), so it was before each
      decision-maker. Briefing note EC22-002103 cites it for the claims totals at [6.2] and [7.3], and the
      internal review decision of 21 April 2023, which is the decision under review, lists it among the
      material considered and relies on it at [29(j)]. I ask that:
            (i) by 6 November 2026 the Respondent states whether it relies on the report, or on anything
            derived from it, for any finding or allegation against any applicant;
            (ii) if it does, the Tribunal revisits the order of 26 July 2024 (ss 70 and 86), for example by
            disclosure with the passages describing investigative methods redacted, or disclosure to the
            applicants on confidentiality undertakings, so that each applicant has access to information the
            Tribunal proposes to have regard to (s 55(1)(b)); and
            (iii) if it does not, the report is excluded from the material the Tribunal has regard to, and the
            Respondent says so in its SFIC.
      (d) Attachments P and Q to EC22-002103. The Respondent will now produce Attachments A to G. It
      withholds P and Q, and says both are "summarised at a high level" in the briefing note. The
      Respondent's descriptions understate what the briefing note says about them. Its letter of 7 May 2026
      says Attachment Q relates "solely to companies which are unrelated to this proceeding"; the briefing
      note at [33.1] says Attachment Q is the risk assessment "in respect of Hybrid", the company on which
      my order rests. The letter says Attachment P is "an internal email chain between employees of the
      Commission" that is not relevant; the briefing note at [31] says it records the NDIA's requests about
      the use of its evidence in the decision, including that any disclosure of evidence occur "no sooner than
      18 November 2022", the day I received my notice of intention. A summary by the party withholding
      the document is not the document. I ask that the Respondent give Attachments P and Q to the
      Tribunal (s 79(2)(a)) and that the Tribunal decide whether they are relevant, having regard to
      paragraph 5(f).
8. B3. Section 70 application of 7 May 2026. The Respondent has no objection to priority. I ask that it be
decided before 6 November 2026 if the Tribunal can, and that each applicant have liberty to file a
supplementary SFIC and evidence, limited to new material, within 14 days after any later release of
documents.
9. B4. Witnesses. The Respondent consents to this request as put to it on 5 October 2026. By 18 December
2026 the Respondent lists every person whose statement it relies on and says who it will call. By 8 January
2027 each applicant says who it requires for cross-examination and who it will call. The hearing estimate is
confirmed within 7 days after that, and the hearing does not start until those steps are done. Given
paragraph 5(f), each applicant needs that list in time to require attendance.
10. B5. Applications for production. Liberty to apply, by 30 October 2026 or 7 days after the supplementary
bundle is filed (whichever is later), for production from the Respondent (s 79(2)(a)) and for a summons to
the NDIA (s 74). The Respondent says such applications should come after the bundle and before the parties'
SFICs, which this window reflects.
11. B6. Form of the supplementary bundle. The Respondent proposes to "reproduce" the June 2024 bundle.
I ask that:
      (a) the supplementary bundle come with a table showing what is new, changed or re-redacted
      compared with June 2024, and that it be indexed, text-searchable and free of unnecessary duplicates;
      and
      (b) each briefing note be lodged with its attachments as they stood on the date of the decision it
      supported, with a note of any attachment added later. The briefing note of 19 October 2022 (T9) as
      served includes an attachment dated 1 December 2022 (T9.19), and its own attachment list names
      Attachments 3, 5, 11, 30 and 52, which are not in the bundle.
