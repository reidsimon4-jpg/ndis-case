Relevant policies and guidelines

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission Compliance and Enforcement Policy (‘policy’)
acknowledges that a

        banning order is one of the most serious regulatory responses to prevent a person from providing
        supports or services in the NDIS market. It is intended to apply to an NDIS provider or person
        employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider in circumstances where it is the most
        appropriate regulatory option available to prevent people with disability from experiencing harm
        arising from poor quality or unsafe services provided under the NDIS.

As per our comments above, there were other options available to the Commission, such as
continuing the suspension order rather than issuing a banning order at this time. As such, it is
our view that the Commission has not followed policy.

Additionally, the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Procedural Fairness) Guidelines 2018
(Cth) (‘guidelines’) are of significance in this case.

Clause 7(1) states in part that: -

        (1)   …procedural fairness must be afforded to a person if their rights or interests may be
              adversely or detrimentally affected in a direct and specific way’…
        …

              (d) the decision maker should be unbiased and maintain an unbiased appearance.
        …

        (4)   The obligation to provide procedural fairness must be balanced against the need to
              ensure that neither a complainant (including a person with disability) nor a person
              with disability affected by an issue raised in a complaint is disadvantaged as a result
              of the complaint being made and resolved.

Section 5 of the Interpretation Act 1984 provides that ‘person’ includes a public body, company,
or association or body of persons, corporate or unincorporated. As such, clause 7(1) above
applies to our Client.

It is our view that we have identified the action of the Commission not affording procedural
fairness has not been afforded to our Client. Our Client has been adversely and detrimentally
                                                                                                  9

affected by the Commission issuing a banning order. In accordance with the guidelines, our Client
should have been afforded fairness.

Taking into consideration subclause 7(4), providing procedural fairness to our Client would not
have disadvantaged any person who was the subject of an allegation. Our Client was entitled to
be given a fair opportunity to respond to all allegations against them, and not just “by way of
example…”. Regardless, our position was to seek to keep the suspension order in place. This
approach would not have compromised any person and was a fair, reasonable, and proportional
request, given the insufficient information provided by the Commission.

Clause 8 of the guidelines, ‘Procedural fairness and other legal requirements’ states that:-

        (1)   …The central purpose of those requirements is to ensure fairness for all involved
              parties.

The Commission's approach in dealing with this matter has not ensured procedural fairness for
our Client.

Clause 13 of the guidelines state, in part, that:-
       …

        (5)   The dual purpose of procedural fairness: Procedural fairness is a legal obligation
              that applies to decisions made under statute that adversely affect the interests of
              others in a direct and specific manner. A failure to comply with this legal obligation
              may lead to an adverse decision being set aside by a court or questioned by a review
              tribunal or body.

        (6)   As importantly, procedural fairness aims to strengthen the fairness and integrity of
              administrative processes, regardless of whether legal proceedings for a breach are a
              possibility. Decision-makers should, accordingly, ensure procedural fairness in the
              pursuit of good administration as an overriding objective.

It is our view that the Commission has failed to provide our Client with procedural fairness. We
have requested information in order to provide our submission, which has been denied, we have
asked for the continuation of a suspension order, which has been denied, and we have provided
names of witnesses who can support our position for some of the allegations which have not been
followed up.

It is concerning that the Commission continues to rely upon matters that are the subject of
criminal proceedings to issue the banning order. The Commission's conduct in seeking responses
with respect to the criminal proceedings clearly disadvantages our Client.

It is inappropriate to comment on the ongoing criminal proceedings while the matter is still
before the Courts, and as such, this request by the Commission is not aligned with the
Commonwealth Prosecution Policy.

It is worthwhile acknowledging here that our Client is being penalised twice for the same alleged
offences – one is in the criminal courts, and the other is through this banning order. We consider
the Commission’s approach to having a ‘bet each way’ inappropriate and in conflict with
recognised procedural fairness guidelines and the Commonwealth Prosecution Policy.
