# Analysis — Certified transcript of the discontinuance of Vida Reid's charges

**Source:** Magistrates Court of WA (Criminal), PE 45655–45658 of 2022, Magistrate G. Randazzo, Perth, Friday 23 February 2024, 9.41 am – 10.21 am. Certified 19 April 2024 (VIQ Solutions). Filed in this folder as: `2024-02-23 Transcript - Magistrates Court WA - PE 45655-45658 of 2022 - CDPP discontinuance of Vida Reid charges (certified 19-04-2024).pdf`

**Appearances:** Ms Rosaji, then Ms Stynes, for the Commonwealth (CDPP); Ms M.L. Buchanan for Vida Reid.

## Verified facts (primary source, with page pins)

1. All four Commonwealth charges against Vida Reid (PE 45655–45658 of 2022) were dismissed for want of prosecution on 23 February 2024 under s 25 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2004 (WA) (applied to this Commonwealth prosecution via the Judiciary Act), on the prosecution's own application. Vida was "formally discharged on these charges" (pp 2, 6).
2. The recharge reservation is on the certified record twice. Prosecutor (p 2): dismissal sought "so they can be commenced at a later date." The Magistrate to defence counsel (p 5): "You've heard the prosecutor say that there's an intention to recharge; do you understand that?"
3. CDPP's stated reasons (Ms Stynes, p 4): ~15 months since charges commenced; committal brief served only in part and incomplete; outstanding disclosure; outstanding LPP claims over **six devices of the accused** that would "take some time" to review. No evidence had ever been adduced (confirmed, p 5).
4. The court's reasoning (p 6): s 25(2) — no evidence adduced, no abuse of process suggested (citing Archer J in *Morris*) → consent to dismissal.
5. No costs application was made; the prosecutor "kindly directed" defence to s 4 of the Accused Costs Act (p 5). Defence placed on the record: 10 court appearances, charged ~14 months earlier (≈ Dec 2022), missed grandmother's funeral in Iraq, community opprobrium, loss of privacy and family disruption — expressly "in the event that the prosecution reinstate charges" (p 5).
6. Procedural wrinkle: the first prosecutor (Rosaji) initially said she believed evidence *had* been adduced; the Magistrate stood the matter down and forced precision. Only on recall did Stynes confirm no evidence adduced (pp 2–5).

## What this changes

1. **Fresh-eyes gap closed.** The discontinuance date and basis — previously unverified — are now verified: 23 February 2024, s 25 CPA (WA), dismissal for want of prosecution, express intention to recharge, formal discharge.
2. **The Respondent's 28 Feb 2024 submissions misdescribed the position.** Five days *after* the dismissal, AGS told the Tribunal (Respondent's Submissions in Reply — Stay, 28 Feb 2024, Karlie Dickinson) that Vida "appeared before the Perth Magistrates Court for a mention hearing on 23 February 2024" and that "these criminal proceedings have not progressed" due to the LPP objection, and sought a stay "until the concurrent criminal proceedings or any future criminal proceedings arising from the same set of circumstances has been determined." In fact, as at 28 Feb 2024 there were no criminal proceedings on foot — they had been dismissed on the Commonwealth's own application on 23 Feb. Whether stale instructions or drafting error, the Tribunal was given an inaccurate account of the criminal posture. This is the earliest documented instance of the pattern (cf. the 11 Mar 2025 letter's carefully limited wording; the Respondent's 7 May 2026 self-contradiction; the 27 May 2026 informal "still live" report).
3. **Preserved jeopardy is now documentary, not speculative.** For the reserved severance/sequencing application: Vida faces an on-record CDPP reservation to recharge; the LPP review over six seized devices was unresolved as at Feb 2024; informally the matter was reported still live as at 27 May 2026. Her privilege against self-incrimination, and the unfairness of forcing her matters on jointly with Simon's, are anchored to a certified court record — not to assertion.
4. **No double-jeopardy protection.** General analysis, not legal advice: a s 25 dismissal for want of prosecution with no evidence adduced is a discharge, not an acquittal — no autrefois acquit; recharging remains legally open. For Commonwealth offences punishable by more than 6 months' imprisonment, s 15B Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) imposes no limitation period (verify the precise charge provisions before pleading this).
5. **Apparently not before the Tribunal.** A text search of the finalised 13 June 2024 tender bundle finds no reference to PE 45655 or the dismissal. The Respondent does not appear to have put the discontinuance record before the ART. (The 11 Mar 2025 letter and 17 Sep 2025 stay submissions PDFs have no text layer — confirm manually.)
