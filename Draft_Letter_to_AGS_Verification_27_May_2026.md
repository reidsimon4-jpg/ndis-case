# Covering note for the solicitor

This is the verification letter to AGS contemplated as move one of the strategic playbook of 27 May 2026. It is built on the documented inconsistency between (i) the AGS representation to the Tribunal of 11 March 2025 and (ii) the Australian Border Force documents released by the Department of Home Affairs on 2 April 2026 in response to FOI request FA 24/10/01764 (ADF2024/261542). It also incorporates the contemporaneous statement of Mrs Reid's then-solicitor Mr Charles Dallimore of 9 January 2025.

The letter is designed so that every possible AGS response is forensically useful — reaffirmation locks the position in for the waiver-revocation; narrowing concedes the original incompleteness; expansion produces admissions against interest; evasion is itself a fact; silence triggers the model-litigant complaint to the Office of Legal Services Coordination.

The letter should be despatched on the same day as the holding letter to the Tribunal Member's chambers (move two) and the FOI / APP 12 disclosure-forcing requests (move three).

Before sending, please verify: (1) Ms Dickinson's current position and contact details at AGS (the letterhead may have updated); (2) whether Craig Land and Isabelle Macdonald remain on the AGS team for these matters; (3) whether the Office of Legal Services Coordination contact in section 6 is the current officer; (4) standing instructions on signing.

**Re:	ART matters 2023/3361, 2023/3363 and 2023/4494 — Vida REID and the Commissioner; Simon REID and the Commissioner; Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd and the Commissioner — request for written verification of the representation made to the Tribunal on 11 March 2025**

We act for Mr Simon Reid and Mrs Vida Reid (the applicants) and have instructions in relation to Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (FFG) and the associated entities. We write in connection with the letter sent by your office to the Administrative Review Tribunal Brisbane Registry and to Mrs Reid on 11 March 2025 (your reference 23004627), and seek written verification of the position there stated in light of subsequently disclosed Commonwealth documents.

**1.	The representation made on 11 March 2025**

1.1	By letter of 11 March 2025 your office, in the proceedings identified above, stated to the Tribunal and to Mrs Reid:

> *"Following receipt of Mrs Reid's submissions, the Commission has made enquiries with the NDIA and has been informed that there is no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to FFG. In light of this information, the Commission cannot agree to the stay requested by Mrs Reid, having regard to the Tribunal's objectives in section 9 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024."*

1.2	The same letter expressly disclaimed any client relationship with the National Disability Insurance Agency, stating:

> *"At the outset and to avoid confusion, we confirm that we act for the NDIS Commission… In these proceedings, we do NOT act for the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA), who are the government agency that implements and manages the NDIS. For abundant clarity, the NDIA are NOT a party to these proceedings."*

1.3	When Mrs Reid pressed the position by her letter of 14 March 2025, your office responded on 19 March 2025 in terms that confined the representation further:

> *"We write further to your letter dated 14 March 2025 and confirm that the Commissioner has made enquiries of the NDIA and that our letter dated 11 March 2025 confirmed the NDIA's position as was relayed to the Commissioner."*

1.4	On the strength of the 11 March 2025 representation, the applicants gave up their application for a stay of the merits-review proceedings, exposed themselves to the substantive hearing of those proceedings, and committed legal and personal resources accordingly. The representation was, in our clients' submission, the central factual premise on which their procedural posture from March 2025 onwards has been built.

**2.	The documentary record now in our clients' possession**

2.1	Our clients have come into possession of documents that, on their face, cannot be reconciled with the 11 March 2025 representation as the applicants understood it at the time it was made. We set those documents out in turn so that there is no doubt as to the material on which this letter is based.

2.2	*The ABF documents released by the Department of Home Affairs on 2 April 2026.* In response to Mrs Reid's FOI request FA 24/10/01764, file ADF2024/261542 (132 folios), the Department released ABF documents which include, verbatim and in the ABF's own words:

(a)	*"The complainant stated they are aware as to why they were subject to Law Enforcement interest. The complainant stated that they had court proceedings against them from the NDIS dropped. The complainant was then told ABF are completing a baggage examination on behalf of another agency."* (capitalisation as in the original);

(b)	*"ABF told the Pax as we are not aware charges have been dropped and were conducting the examination on behalf of another agency. ABF advised that they would need to speak with AFP or their Lawyer if charges against them have been dropped and that the examination would still need to take place."*; and

(c)	*"ABF officers then asked if she had been in any trouble with any law enforcement agencies previously? To which the passenger replied 'yes NDIS, I had to sell my house to pay approx. AUD 850,000 in legal fees'."*

The identity of the "another agency" referred to throughout the released ABF records was withheld by the Department under section 37(2)(b) of the *Freedom of Information Act 1982* (Cth). Related material was withheld under sections 22(1)(a)(ii), 47D and 47E(d). An Information Commissioner Review (reference MR26/00947) is on foot to test the redactions.

2.3	The ABF examination described in the released documents was conducted on 25 September 2024 — that is, **seven months after the discontinuance of charges against Mrs Reid by the CDPP on 23 February 2024, and approximately five and a half months before the 11 March 2025 representation by your office to the Tribunal.**

2.4	*The contemporaneous statement of Mrs Reid's then-solicitor on 9 January 2025.* On 9 January 2025 — that is, approximately two months before your office signed the 11 March 2025 letter — Mr Charles Dallimore, Senior Associate of Weeks & Co Lawyers, who was then on the record for Mrs Reid in these matters, wrote in correspondence to Mrs Reid on the file:

> *"It certainly does not look like the investigation has ended."*

That statement was made by reference to the materials and dealings then in Mr Dallimore's possession, in connection with the same matters in which your office signed the 11 March 2025 letter.

**3.	The position the applicants put on the present record**

3.1	The released ABF documents, read together with the Dallimore statement, are not reconcilable with what the applicants understood your office's representation of 11 March 2025 to mean. Either the representation was incomplete or inaccurate at the time it was made; or the representation was, on a proper construction of its terms, narrower than the applicants understood; or the position of the NDIA as relayed to the Commission was itself materially inaccurate. Our clients do not at present know which of these is the case.

3.2	The applicants are entitled to a clear statement of the position from your office, and to the corresponding documentary record of the inquiries that were made before the 11 March 2025 letter was signed.

**4.	Specific requests**

We respectfully ask your office to respond, in writing and within twenty-one (21) days of the date of this letter, to each of the following:

(a)	whether the representation in the 11 March 2025 letter was accurate, on its face and on a proper construction of its terms, at the time it was made;

(b)	whether the representation remains accurate today;

(c)	what specific enquiries the Commission made of the NDIA before your office signed the 11 March 2025 letter — including the date of each enquiry, the officer of the Commission who made the enquiry, the officer of the NDIA to whom the enquiry was directed, the form in which the enquiry was made (orally, in writing, by formal request), and the form in which the response was given;

(d)	whether the Commission disclosed to your office, before your office signed the 11 March 2025 letter, the existence of the 25 September 2024 examination of Mrs Reid by ABF at Perth Airport, or any documents now released by the Department of Home Affairs under FOI request FA 24/10/01764;

(e)	whether your office made any independent inquiry, before signing the 11 March 2025 letter, of any Commonwealth agency other than the NDIA — including, without limitation, the AFP, the CDPP, the NDIA Fraud Fusion Taskforce, the Australian Border Force, the Australian Taxation Office, or any joint multi-agency body — as to whether there was a current criminal investigation or other law-enforcement activity pertaining to any of the applicants;

(f)	what your office's current position is on whether the matters disclosed in the ABF FOI release of 2 April 2026 — namely, that ABF was conducting an examination "on behalf of another agency" connected to "Law Enforcement interest" — fall within, or fall outside, the scope of "current criminal investigation" as that phrase was used in the 11 March 2025 letter; and

(g)	whether your office accepts that it has a continuing duty of candour to the Tribunal, under the *Legal Services Directions 2017* (Cth) and at common law, in respect of the representation made in the 11 March 2025 letter — and, if so, what steps your office has taken or intends to take to discharge that duty in light of the documentary material now in the applicants' possession.

**5.	Reservation of position**

5.1	The applicants reserve all of their rights in respect of the matters set out in this letter, including their rights:

(a)	to apply to the Tribunal to revoke the waiver of the stay previously given, and for a fresh stay of the merits-review proceedings, on the basis (without limitation) that the waiver was given in reliance on the 11 March 2025 representation and that the documentary record now in the applicants' possession is not capable of being reconciled with that representation as the applicants understood it;

(b)	to put the documentary record described in section 2 of this letter before the Tribunal, with the other parties' responses, in support of that application and at any subsequent stage of the proceedings;

(c)	to bring proceedings in the Federal Court of Australia for judicial review under the *Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977* (Cth) and section 39B of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth), and for declaratory and injunctive relief;

(d)	to make a complaint under the *Legal Services Directions 2017* (Cth) to the Office of Legal Services Coordination, Attorney-General's Department, in respect of any conduct that falls short of the Commonwealth's model-litigant obligation;

(e)	to bring proceedings for misfeasance in public office (*Northern Territory v Mengel*; *Sanders v Snell*) and other related causes of action; and

(f)	to make referrals to the National Anti-Corruption Commission, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, and the Australian Human Rights Commission in respect of the multi-agency conduct affecting the applicants since 2021.

**6.	The Tribunal**

6.1	A copy of this letter is being provided to the Administrative Review Tribunal Brisbane Registry and to the Member's chambers on the same day on which it is sent to your office. The applicants are simultaneously writing to the Tribunal in connection with the procedural posture of the proceedings in light of the matters set out above.

6.2	Should your office consider that any part of this letter is incorrect or that the documentary record described in section 2 has been mischaracterised, we ask that you say so in your response, with particulars.

**7.	Conclusion**

7.1	The applicants do not lightly raise the matters set out in this letter. They do so because the documentary material now in their possession is not, on its face, capable of being reconciled with the position relayed to the Tribunal on 11 March 2025, and because the applicants gave up significant procedural protection on the strength of that position.

7.2	We invite your office to engage with these matters constructively and promptly. We are available to meet with you in person, or to attend by videoconference, at your office's convenience.
