## A. Nature of this submission

1. This submission advances a single, discrete ground: that the banning order against the Applicant was made in the exercise of a power for **a purpose foreign to the purpose for which the power was conferred**, alternatively for a **dominant improper purpose**, and is for that reason invalid and not the correct or preferable decision.

2. What this submission does **not** do. It does not invite the Tribunal to make any finding of "malice," misfeasance in public office, or corrupt conduct — those are matters for other forums and are not pressed here. It does not depend on the truth or falsity of the Commission's allegations against the Applicant. It does not rely on any informal or confidential source. It is put squarely as an administrative-law ground, on the documentary record, going to the lawfulness of the decision and to the decision the Tribunal should now make.

---

## B. The legal principles

3. A statutory power must be exercised for the purpose for which it was conferred. If it is exercised to achieve a purpose foreign to the statute, the exercise is invalid: *Padfield v Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food* [1968] AC 997. **[VERIFY]**

4. A discretionary power conferred in apparently unconfined terms is nonetheless subject to limits derived from the subject matter, scope and purpose of the Act, and its exercise may be examined to ensure it was not actuated by an improper purpose: *R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council* (1981) 151 CLR 170. **[VERIFY]**

5. Where a power is exercised for more than one purpose, the exercise is invalid if the improper purpose was the dominant or actuating purpose — that is, if the decision would not have been made but for the improper purpose: *Thompson v Randwick Corporation* (1950) 81 CLR 87. **[VERIFY]**

6. A statutory power may not be used to punish, or to inflict a detriment, for an end collateral to the statute: *Sanders v Snell* (1998) 196 CLR 329. **[VERIFY]**

7. To the extent the Tribunal stands in the shoes of the decision-maker and decides the correct or preferable decision on the material now before it, a purpose that has fallen away, and circumstances that have changed, are directly relevant: *Shi v Migration Agents Registration Authority* (2008) 235 CLR 286. **[VERIFY]** Disproportion between the action taken and any legitimate object is relevant to legal unreasonableness: *Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v Li* (2013) 249 CLR 332. **[VERIFY]**

## C. The purpose for which the banning power is conferred

8. The banning order power in s 73ZN of the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth) is a **protective and preventative** power. Its purpose is the safety of people with disability and the integrity of, and public confidence in, the Scheme. It is forward-looking, directed to risk and suitability. It is **not** a punitive power; it is **not** an instrument for advancing or substituting for a criminal investigation; and it is **not** a mechanism for reallocating participants or market share between competing providers. **[VERIFY the precise objects and the Commissioner's protective functions from the Act before filing — e.g. the objects in s 3 and the Commissioner's functions — and cite them here.]**

9. It follows that if the dominant purpose of the order was punishment, or to hold the Applicant out of the sector pending a criminal process, or to confer an advantage on competing providers, the order was made for a purpose foreign to the power.

## D. The improper purposes contended for, and the evidence

### D1. The action against FFG and the Applicant was generated and driven from within the NDIA, by Mr Nathan Halls, who framed the Applicant as fraudulent from early 2022

10. Mr Nathan Halls was, on his own description, the "**Assistant Director for the Participant Solutions team who deal with escalated or complicated matters for WA based participants**" at the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA). He reported to the WA NDIA Director, Ms Barbara Lundgren. **[Primary: Halls email 2 February 2022; applicant email "OTHER NDIA EXECUTIVE CONTACTS", 4 October 2022, both in the applicants' Gmail — export as exhibits.]**

11. As early as **2 February 2022** — more than ten months before the banning orders — Mr Halls personally engaged on the matter of the participant **SJ Clulow** (later the subject of two of the four criminal charges). In his own email he wrote that plan over-utilisation "without express permission of the NDIS **raises concerns over fraudulent behaviour**," and indicated he would have the participant's s 100 review "prioritised." **[Primary: email "Halls, Nathan <Nathan.Halls2@ndis.gov.au>" to Shadi Norouzi (FFG), cc Vida Reid, 2 February 2022, subject "SJ Clulow [SEC=OFFICIAL]".]** The "fraud" characterisation that became the foundation of the regulatory action thus originated with Mr Halls, personally, from at least February 2022.

12. By **12 August 2022**, the Commission's own tender bundle records Mr Halls soliciting adverse material from a former FFG manager and directing that information be sent "directly through to the fraud team," copied to Ms Lundgren. **[Tender bundle, item T9.22 — VERIFY exact T-reference.]** That an NDIA officer was assembling and channelling the very complaint stream on which the Commission later acted is documentary, and it is the Commission's own production.

12A. The most direct statement of purpose is Mr Halls's own. On **29 September 2022** — before the Applicant's arrest and three months before the banning orders — Mr Halls emailed a third party (the Yaran Property Group, a landlord to FFG participants), copied to the NDIA's WA Operations team, stating in his own words:

   > "*The NDIA is currently looking into unregistered providers where there are serious concerns regarding their practices. Specifically where there is substantiated claims of fraud and/or abuse and neglect of people with a disability. As part [of] this action **I am leading a coordinated provider exit on behalf of the NDIA**.*"

   He added that "a particular provider of concern has at least 3 participants residing in a [property] controlled by Yaran." **[Primary: email "Halls, Nathan &lt;Nathan.Halls2@ndis.gov.au&gt;" to Nadav Strum and daniel@yaran.com.au, cc WA.OPERATIONS@ndis.gov.au, 29 September 2022, subject "Provider Exit – participant concerns [SEC=OFFICIAL]"; forwarded to the Applicant via Yaran on 7 October 2022 — in the applicants' Outlook mailbox; export as exhibit.]** This is the Commonwealth's own document. In the officer's own words he was "leading a coordinated provider exit" to remove a provider from the sector, and he asserted "substantiated claims of fraud" as established — months before any charge was laid, and (in Mr Simon Reid's case) against a person never charged at all. It is the clearest documentary evidence of both the collateral purpose (engineering FFG's exit, including by approaching its participants' landlord) and the predetermination of the very question the banning power exists to assess. The Applicant does not accept that any claim of fraud was "substantiated"; the point is that Mr Halls treated it as decided before the process had run.

### D2. A purpose of the action was to confer a benefit on competing providers

13. On **5 October 2022** — two weeks before the Applicant's arrest — Mr Halls is reported to have telephoned an FFG participant, Ms Linda Bill, and to have said (as recorded in the Applicant's contemporaneous email to her solicitors the next day):

   > "*… this Operation is being conducted by him and the NDIA, provider choice, nurse assist 24/7 and Sukhi Ta[e]r … Provider choice and Nurse Assist placed the request to them at the NDIA and they have 'listened and are taking action' … together we will ensure FFG stop it's operations. I highly recommend you use the providers I just mentioned as they are our preferred providers and **I am transferring all of FFG's clients to them**.*"

   **[Source: applicant email to G Nofal (Bennett Law), 6 October 2022, subject "Linda Bill (Mini) received call from Nathan Halls" — in the applicants' Gmail. Evidentiary status at G below.]**

14. If accepted, that is direct evidence that a purpose of the regulatory action — as understood and described by the NDIA officer driving it — was to remove FFG from the market and transfer its participants to named competing providers. That is a purpose foreign to a protective power. The competitor thread is corroborated elsewhere on the record: ex-FFG staff and clients moving to Nurse Assist 24/7, Dream Big Employment and Diversitas (Sukhi Tear), the same persons who became the Commission's adverse witnesses.

### D3. The purpose was punitive and the outcome was predetermined

15. In the same 5 October 2022 call, Mr Halls is reported to have told the participant that "**FFG are fraudulent, and Vida and Simon are behind it all**," that he would "**seize all of our assets, house, income**," and that "**him and the NDIA are shutting us down**," and to have imposed a "gag order" on the participant. **[Source as at D2; evidentiary status at G.]** Those statements — made before any charge was laid and well before any banning decision — evidence a settled intention to destroy the businesses and to punish the Applicant and her husband, not a protective judgment yet to be formed.

16. The conduct of the process is consistent with predetermination: the notice period in the brief was, on the Applicant's case, truncated, and the notices of intention were received after the deadline they imposed; the arrest, search warrant and regulatory steps were coordinated around 19 October 2022. **[VERIFY the notice dates and the service chronology against the file.]**

### D4. The regulatory power was used as an adjunct to, and to await the outcome of, a criminal process

17. On internal review (21 April 2023) the Commission changed the legal basis of the Applicant's order from s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) (contravention) to s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) (suitability), and broadened its scope, recording that a ban on the suitability basis "**accords with the usual approach … where a person is alleged to have engaged in fraudulent activity … (particularly where criminal charges are laid)**" (reasons, para 31), and that the **period of the ban "corresponds with the amount of time it is reasonably expected to take for the criminal proceedings to be finally determined**," and "**can be revisited**" upon their determination (para 63). **[Source: Vida Reid internal review reasons, 21 April 2023 — VERIFY paragraph numbers.]**

18. That reasoning shows the order was, in substance, tied to and shaped by the criminal process rather than an independent protective assessment. The criminal proceedings were determined — discontinued for no evidence — in February 2024. The stated foundation for the order therefore fell away, and the undertaking to "revisit" was not honoured. A power used to hold a person out of the sector pending a prosecution that produced no evidence has not been used for its protective purpose.

### D5. The Commission's own contemporaneous assessment that the case was weak and administrative

19. The Commission's own decision-brief material acknowledged that its action "**may be viewed as quite strong given that the complaints and breaches focus on administrative and personal errors**." **[Source: brief EC22-002000, Risks/Sensitivities — VERIFY exact T-reference and wording.]** A protective regulator genuinely satisfied of a serious risk to participants does not describe the foundation of a five-year total exclusion in those terms. The mismatch supports the inference that the order was not, in truth, an exercise of the protective power for its proper purpose.

### D6. Gross disproportion as evidence of collateral purpose

20. The machinery deployed against this family — a 1,506-page tender bundle, an NDIA forensic accountant, an AFP-led search warrant, a CDPP prosecution, the multi-agency Fraud Fusion Taskforce, border stops and device seizures, across roughly five years — is grossly disproportionate to the sums in issue: only **$247,484.78** was ever criminally charged, and those charges were discontinued for no evidence; the larger "$1,485,527 / 31% of total successful claims" figure was given "**minimal weight**" by the Commission's own internal reviewer because the underlying report acknowledged it was not known what proportion of those claims were for services actually provided. **[Sources: Statement of Material Facts; Simon Reid internal review para 29(j) — VERIFY references.]** The disproportion is itself a matter the Tribunal is entitled to weigh as indicative that the power was not exercised for its protective purpose.

### D7. The genesis: a competitor-instigated investigation, and a witness base drawn from that competitor

20A. The matter did not begin with an independent regulatory concern. It began with a commercial dispute. **Ms Terri Roberts**, an FFG Service Manager, left FFG in late 2021 in disputed circumstances (a dispute then handled through DFG Legal between **October 2021 and March 2022**). She and her partner **Mr Pieter Rossouw** were associated with **Infinity Living Options**, and Ms Roberts became a "Business Advisor of **Nurse Assist 24/7**", a competing provider operated by **Ms Amanda Damon**. The applicants had a pre-existing and continuing dispute with that competitor network. **[Recorded: DFG Legal correspondence Oct 2021–Mar 2022; 30 Sep 2022 email; tender bundle (Statements of Roberts and Rossouw).]**

20B. On **30 September 2022 — nineteen days before the Applicant's arrest** — the Applicant recorded a contemporaneous account from a participant, Mr Jonathan Shapiera, that Ms Sukhi Tear (Diversitas WA, described as a former NDIA colleague and "close friend" of Mr Halls) had told him the group would "ensure by end of next week we have no clients left," that Mr Halls "**has provided me with a list of all of Vida's clients and he is also helping me personally to move them across**," and that "**I have already convinced another female client to take out a personal [restraining] order against Vida**." The email names the connected parties — Ms Amanda Damon (Nurse Assist 24/7), Ms Terri Roberts (Nurse Assist / ex-FFG) and Ms Sukhi Tear (Diversitas / ex-NDIA). **[Recorded as a contemporaneous report; the competitors' statements are relayed via Mr Shapiera and require his statement to prove their truth.]**

20C. An independent investigator (Cybertrace), retained over anonymous harassment of the Applicant, confirmed in writing in late 2022 that "**Terri works for Amanda Damon who is the director of a rival business**," and listed Ms Roberts among the persons of concern. **[Recorded — third-party investigator.]**

20D. The witnesses on whom the Commission's case rests are drawn from this same competitor cluster, and the timing runs the wrong way for an independent investigation. On the Commission's own tender bundle: **Ms Janine Hall** (former FFG Support Coordinator) and **Ms Danica Hall** (former FFG Accounts Officer, commenced Dec 2021, resigned 19 July 2022) — whom the Applicant identifies as mother and daughter — each provided the Commission with an "Evidence Package" in **July 2022**; **Ms Elanor Smith** (resigned **29 March 2022**) made the complaint to the NDIA that became a referral; and Ms Roberts and Mr Rossouw gave statements. The departures and the moves to the competitor came **first**; the complaints, and a wave of **anonymous** complaints, followed. **[Recorded — tender bundle (Statements / Evidence Packages of J Hall, D Hall, Roberts, Rossouw); FFG internal review (Elanor Smith referral). The mother–daughter relationship is the Applicant's evidence.]**

20E. The asymmetry is documented in the Commission's **own brief**. EC22-002103 named **Ms Amanda Damon — the director of the competing business Nurse Assist 24/7 — as a director and shareholder of N-Able Perth Kids Rehab Centre Pty Ltd**, an NDIS provider among the entities considered for banning. The Applicant's evidence is that **N-Able was never in fact banned**, while every one of the applicants' entities was. **[Recorded: EC22-002103. VERIFY N-Able's non-banning against the NDIS Commission register before relying on the asymmetry.]** A protective regulator applying its power evenhandedly would not ban one provider's principals largely on the word of a competing provider's associates while leaving the competing provider's own NDIS entity untouched.

20F. Taken together, this evidence supports the inference that the dominant purpose served was not the protection of participants but the **removal of FFG and its principals from the sector for the benefit of competing providers** — the very providers who supplied the witnesses, received the migrating clients, and were left unbanned.

### D8. Participants were moved by direction and inducement, not by free choice

20G. The action did not leave participant choice intact; it overrode it. A protective regulator does not select a participant's replacement provider, and an officer does not use funding or access decisions as leverage. The record shows both: the NDIA itself **directed participants to named replacement providers** (Mr Halls and Ms Millan steering participants specifically to Diversitas / Nurse Assist), and a participant, **Mr Jonathan Shapiera**, alleges in his own contemporaneous account that he was offered **an increase to his NDIS plan ("$200,000 over two years" / "$100,000 per year") and the grant of his son Nikolaus's NDIS access — which had been refused — on condition that he cooperate and leave FFG.** **[Source: Mr Shapiera's email of 14 October 2022 (first-hand); corroborated by the 30 September 2022 relay and by the primary Sukhi Tear → Halls email seeking the son's access reassessment and Mr Shapiera's plan. Evidentiary status: Mr Shapiera's own written allegation, to be confirmed by his signed statement; the conditionality is his characterisation.]** Mr Shapiera himself asked, in writing, whether the funding increase was *"SHUT UP MONEY."*

20H. The Applicant's case is therefore that these participants were not exercising free, informed choice; they were moved by a combination of (a) the NDIA officer's **direction** to named providers, (b) **inducements** (plan-funding increases; a child's access approval) and pressure ("if I played along"), and (c) the placing of **"alerts" blacklisting FFG on participant records before any banning order**. Using government benefits — plan funding and access decisions — as the currency to detach a provider's clients is a purpose wholly foreign to the protective banning power and to the choice-and-control objects of the Scheme, and is the clearest expression of the collateral purpose identified at D2. **[The Applicant does not contend that a participant may not lawfully change providers; the contention is that these participants were directed and induced, not freely choosing. Proof that *all* clients were forced out requires the participants' own evidence; the specific instances are evidenced above.]**

## E. Dominant purpose — the "but for" test

21. The Respondent may contend that a protective purpose was also present. That does not answer the ground. On *Thompson v Randwick*, the order is invalid if an improper purpose was the dominant or actuating purpose — if, but for it, the order would not have been made in the form, scope and duration it took. The total exclusion "in any capacity," fixed to the expected length of the criminal proceedings and expressly tied to allegations of fraud "particularly where criminal charges are laid," cannot be explained by a genuine, independent protective assessment of risk. On the whole of the material, the improper purposes identified above were the operative cause of the order.

---

## F. Relief sought

22. The Applicant submits that the banning order should be **set aside**. The improper purpose vitiates the decision; in any event, on the material now before the Tribunal — including the collapse of the criminal foundation and the matters in Part D — the order is not the correct or preferable decision and should not stand.
