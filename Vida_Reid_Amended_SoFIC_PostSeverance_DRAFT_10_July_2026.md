On internal review under s 100 of the NDIS Act, the delegate **[Ms Sian Leathem, Complaints Commissioner — VERIFY against the signed IR instrument]**, by decision dated 21 April 2023, decided the order should remain, the order operating until **21 December 2027**. The order expressly provides that it "will not apply in relation to any supports or services that you may seek for yourself or that you may provide to your son" ("the son carve-out") **[VERIFY carve-out wording against the instrument]**. By ruling of **[date — insert severance/case-management ruling]**, this application proceeds separately. The Tribunal's task is an individual one: s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) is directed to whether "**the person**" — this Applicant — is not suitable, on the circumstances existing at the date of the Tribunal's own decision.

## B. Summary of the Applicant's contentions

3. The Applicant contends:

   (a) **(Lead ground — no present unsuitability on independent, tested evidence.)** There is no evidence — independent of the National Disability Insurance Agency ("NDIA")/competitor complaint stream, and tested or capable of being tested at hearing — on which the Tribunal could be comfortably satisfied that the Applicant is **now** not suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or services to people with disability;

   (b) **the criminal proceedings on which the decision rested ended without any adjudication.** All four charges were dismissed for want of prosecution on 23 February 2024, on the prosecution's own application, with a formal discharge. In *Abdullah* [2026] ARTA 743 the Tribunal expressly **excluded even pending charges** from its criminal-history findings because the applicant had pleaded not guilty; what weighed against him was non-disclosure, not any assumption the charged conduct occurred **[⚠ pinpoint]**. A fortiori, charges dismissed without trial cannot be treated as proved conduct. The NDIA's Statement of Material Facts ("SMF") is an untested prosecutorial assertion, not an adjudicated account, and cannot be a conviction by another name;

   (c) the internal review reasons applied a **default practice rather than an individual judgment**: the reviewer selected s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) because there was "no compelling reason to diverge from the usual practice of the NDIS Commission" (¶59), and, although reciting the statutory obligation to act in a "risk responsive and proportionate manner" (¶60, citing s 181D(4)(b)), did not connect scope or duration to an individually assessed present risk;

   (d) the reasons proceeded on **assumed guilt**: "the fact that Mrs Reid engaged in the alleged criminal conduct… significantly facilitated the commission of the alleged fraud" (¶37(b) **[VERIFY quote and reference against the IR]**) — an assumption the criminal process never vindicated and the Respondent cannot now prove except by independent, tested evidence;

   (e) **duration was pegged to the expected life of the prosecution** — the Applicant was found not suitable "at this time" by reference to the then-pending proceedings, with the order to be revisited upon their determination **[VERIFY ¶ references]**. Those proceedings ended on 23 February 2024. The promised revisitation never occurred. The order's own logic self-terminated more than two years ago;

   (f) the surviving material is untested third-party statement and complaint material whose provenance (Part E.4) deprives it of the independence and cogency required for a fraud-flavoured unsuitability finding; and

   (g) the Applicant is presently suitable and leads affirmative evidence of that fact (Part G), including contemporaneous evidence of her documented detection-and-correction conduct, participant outcomes and stakeholder confidence.

## C. The Tribunal's task

4. The review is de novo: the Tribunal stands in the shoes of the decision-maker (*Frugtniet v ASIC* [2019] HCA 16), decides the correct or preferable decision (*Drake*), on the circumstances as they exist at the time of its own decision (*Shi v MARA* [2008] HCA 31): ART Act 2024 (Cth) s 105.

5. The Applicant adopts the propositions from *Abdullah* [2026] ARTA 743 **[⚠ pinpoints]**: the s 73ZN(2) power "is fundamentally protective in character" and forward-looking, not punitive; compliance and enforcement must be "risk responsive and proportionate" (s 181D(4)(b)), an obligation arguably extending to the Tribunal on review; adverse findings must rest on evidence from **independent sources tested** at hearing, to the Tribunal's comfortable satisfaction; duration falls to be assessed on the *ASIC v Adler* [2002] NSWSC 483 considerations (seriousness, propensity, recurrence, contrition/insight). Gravity matters to satisfaction: the finding sought is fraud-flavoured against a person whose charges were dismissed without trial; such a finding requires evidence of commensurate cogency (cf *Briginshaw v Briginshaw* (1938) 60 CLR 336, as discussed through "comfortable satisfaction" in *Abdullah*).

6. Consistently with *Abdullah*, the Applicant advances **no freestanding procedural-fairness or improper-purpose ground** in this Tribunal. The matters at Parts E.4 and H are pleaded solely for their bearing on independence, reliability and weight; on the absence of present protective need; and on scope and duration.

## D. The decision under review, on its own terms

7. The reasons' structure demonstrates a formulaic rather than individualised assessment:

   (a) the conclusion rested on "the reasons noted above (particularly in paragraphs 37(a) to (e) and 51 to 55)" (¶58 — verified);

   (b) the statutory basis was selected by default: "In my view, there is no compelling reason to diverge from the **usual practice** of the NDIS Commission making such an order under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act and aberrantly relying upon a different statutory basis" (¶59 — verified). An asserted administrative "usual practice" is not a substitute for the individual statutory judgment the Act requires as to the proper basis, scope and duration of an order against this Applicant;

   (c) the reviewer recited that "[t]he NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk responsive and proportionate manner (section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act)" (¶60 — verified), but the reasons do not perform that exercise: they contain no analysis connecting the order's five-year term or its scope to an individually assessed present risk posed by the Applicant, as distinct from the pendency of the charges;

   (d) the operative finding assumed the truth of the charged conduct (¶37(b) **[VERIFY]**) and pegged the order's life to the prosecution's expected duration, with revisitation promised on its determination **[VERIFY ¶ refs]**.

## E. Lead ground — no independent, tested evidence of present unsuitability

### E.1 The criminal foundation has fallen away — and cannot be resurrected as untested allegation

8. The certified transcript of the Magistrates Court of Western Australia records that on **23 February 2024** all four charges against the Applicant were **dismissed for want of prosecution under s 25 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2004 (WA), on the prosecution's own application**, and the Applicant was **formally discharged** [EX-__ certified transcript]. The prosecution had sought an adjournment on 14 February 2024, which the court refused **[VERIFY against transcript/record]**; the discontinuance followed nine days later. The transcript records the prosecutor's statement of an intention to consider recharging; no charge has been laid in the more than two years since.

9. The Tribunal is asked to note, as going to the reliability of the Respondent's account of the criminal proceedings generally, that the Respondent's solicitors' letter of 28 February 2024 described the hearing of 23 February 2024 as a "mention hearing" at which proceedings had "not progressed" — five days after the proceedings had in fact been dismissed with a formal discharge **[plead precisely from the AGS letter and transcript; no allegation of deliberate misstatement is made]**.

10. In *Abdullah*, the Tribunal declined to treat even **pending** charges (not guilty plea, no trial) as part of the applicant's criminal history **[⚠ pinpoint]**. Here the charges were dismissed. It follows that:

   (a) the SMF — an NDIA prosecution document prepared for proceedings that ended without adjudication — is allegation, not proof;

   (b) any allegation drawn from the abandoned prosecution must be proved in this Tribunal by admissible, individually attributable evidence, tested by cross-examination; and

   (c) the decision's own foundation — unsuitability "at this time" by reference to pending proceedings — expired with those proceedings, and the promised revisitation never occurred.

### E.2 The statement and complaint material is untested and not independent

11. The statement material relied on (including the participant/carer statements taken by NDIA Fraud Investigations officers in synchronised sittings in October 2022, and the Burke statement of 16 February 2023) has never been tested. The Applicant requires the makers for cross-examination (Part K). The provenance matters pleaded at E.4 apply equally here.

12. The complaint summaries are proof that complaints were made, not that their contents are true. For each allegation maintained, the Respondent should identify: the participant; the precise conduct; the date; the entity; the person who performed it; the first-hand witness; the supporting documents; whether that witness will be called; and how the allegation demonstrates **present** unsuitability. (*Abdullah* itself discriminated: allegations unsupported by tested evidence — including financial allegations concerning one participant, and other matters — were found insufficient or left without findings **[⚠ pinpoints]**.)

## F. The prejudgment and duration findings cannot stand

17. **Assumed guilt (¶37(b)):** a finding that expressly reasons from "the fact that Mrs Reid engaged in the alleged criminal conduct" **[VERIFY]** was never open — the conduct was charged, denied, and never adjudicated; the charges were later dismissed. On de novo review nothing supports that assumption except material of the kind addressed at E.1–E.2, which must now be proved by tested evidence or given no weight.

18. **Duration:** the five-year term was calibrated to the expected life of the prosecution, not to an individually assessed risk **[VERIFY ¶ refs]**; the same logic appears across the related decisions (FFG's term was expressly reduced on internal review to correspond with the criminal proceedings' expected duration). A banning-order duration must reflect the seriousness of established conduct and the period reasonably required to address present risk; it cannot lawfully operate as a placeholder for criminal proceedings that no longer exist. On the *Adler* banding adopted in *Abdullah*, the shortest band (three years or less) is associated with repayment, absence of intention to return to the role, and contrition; the Applicant's documented conduct (¶13: correction and repayment on detection) and circumstances sit at or below that band — she has now been subject to the order for over 3.5 years on allegations never proved anywhere.

## I. Scope and duration

22. If (contrary to the lead ground) any order is warranted:

   (a) the existing son carve-out demonstrates the Respondent accepts tailored conditions are workable;

   (b) the duration reasoning (¶18 above) supplies no basis for the remaining tail to 21 December 2027; and

## J. Issues for determination

23. (a) Whether, on independent and tested evidence, the Tribunal is comfortably satisfied the Applicant is **now** not suitable (s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii));

   (b) whether any allegation sourced in the dismissed prosecution (including the SMF) can be given weight absent proof by tested evidence, having regard to *Abdullah*'s treatment of unadjudicated charges;

   (c) whether the reasons' provision-selection by "usual practice" (¶59), assumed-guilt reasoning (¶37(b)) and prosecution-pegged duration disclose the individualised, risk-responsive and proportionate assessment the Act requires — and, on de novo review, what such an assessment now yields;
