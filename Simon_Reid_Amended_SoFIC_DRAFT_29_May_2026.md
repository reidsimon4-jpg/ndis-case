## A. Nature of the application

1. On 21 December 2022 the Respondent made a banning order against the Applicant under s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth) ("NDIS Act"). On internal review under s 100 of the NDIS Act, the Respondent made the decision dated **21 April 2023** (T64.1) now under review, which amended/affirmed the order on the suitability ground. (2024 SoFIC ¶¶1–5.)

2. There are three related applicants: Vida Reid (2023/3361), Simon Reid (2023/3363) and Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (2023/4494), joined by Directions of 19 July 2023. **This statement concerns Mr Simon Reid's application.** (2024 SoFIC ¶¶6–8.) **[AMENDED — header corrected to 2023/3363; forum updated to the ART; statutory/procedural references to be migrated to the ART Act 2024 — VERIFY.]**

## B. Summary of the Applicant's contentions

3. **[AMENDED]** Mr Reid contends:

   (a) he has done nothing wrong; there is little or no reliable evidence adverse to him; he is suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or services to people with disability (2024 SoFIC ¶9(a)–(d));

   (b) the decision fails to have regard to the matters prescribed by the NDIS Rules as required by s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii), and the Rules were not produced (2024 SoFIC ¶9(b), ¶¶12–14);

   (c) the order attributes corporate and spousal conduct to him without any individualised assessment of his own knowledge, conduct or period of office, and is based in part on findings the Respondent itself disavows or discounts (2024 SoFIC ¶9(c), (e)(iii));

   (d) the Respondent's investigation and evidence are affected by **adversarial bias**, were not fully disclosed, and targeted only the directors (2024 SoFIC ¶9(e));

   (e) the order is excessively wide and unreasonable (2024 SoFIC ¶9(f)); and

   (f) **[NEW]** the banning power was exercised for a **purpose foreign to the purpose for which it was conferred**, alternatively for a **dominant improper purpose** — the removal of Forward Focused Group ("FFG") and its principals from the sector, and the reallocation of its participants to competing providers who were themselves the source of the complaints — and the evidence on which the Respondent acted was **generated and curated by the National Disability Insurance Agency ("the NDIA") and an interested competitor network**, and adopted by the Respondent without independent vetting.

## C. Statutory framework and the purpose of the power

4. Section 73ZN(2) confers the banning-order discretion (set out in full at 2024 SoFIC ¶10). The order against Mr Reid was made under sub-paragraph (a)(iii) — suitability "having regard to any matters prescribed by the NDIS rules". None of the other sub-paragraphs applies. (2024 SoFIC ¶¶71–72.)

5. **[NEW]** The banning power is a **protective and preventative** power, directed to the safety of people with disability and the integrity of the Scheme. It is forward-looking and is **not** a punitive power, **not** an instrument for advancing or substituting for a criminal investigation, and **not** a mechanism for reallocating participants or market share between competing providers. **[VERIFY the objects/functions provisions of the NDIS Act to be cited here.]**

## D. The existing grounds (retained from the 2024 SoFIC)

6. The Applicant repeats and relies on the following grounds as pleaded in the 2024 SoFIC, in summary:

   (a) **No individualised / NDIS-Rules assessment** — the decision does not identify or apply the prescribed Rules required by s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) (¶¶12–14).

   (b) **The five stated reasons do not bear on Mr Reid** — Meghan Goodwin ("**Simon never was involved with providing the services**"; Mr Reid not a director at the relevant time; the TIR's own acknowledgement that the proportion of services actually provided was unknown) (¶¶16–23); Sarah-Jane Clulow ("**this conduct cannot reasonably or appropriately be attributed to Mr Reid**") (¶¶24–25); the three Hybrid Hive witness statements (Regali, Benediktsson, Smith) which are not adverse to him (¶¶26–34); the eleven "suspicious invoice" reports of which he had no knowledge, he being the "**absent boss most of the time**" (¶¶35–41).

   (c) **The director-liability finding (¶38 of the decision) is wrong in law** — there is no strict liability under s 180 of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth) or the NDIS Act; the decision conducts none of the individualised analysis required by *Daniels v AWA Ltd* (1995) 13 ACLC 614; *ASIC v Rich* [2009] NSWSC 1229; *ASIC v Vines* (2005) 55 ACSR 617; *Daniels v Anderson*; *Vrisakis v ASC* (1993) 9 WAR 395; *Permanent Building Society (in liq) v Wheeler* (1994) 11 WAR 187 (2024 SoFIC ¶¶42–44). **[Authorities retained from the filed SoFIC.]**

   (d) **The post-banning "circumvention" findings (Vitality WA; Dream Big) are unfounded** — VWA is not an NDIS provider; the Dream Big finding rests substantially on the Cassandra Burke statement, which is "**hearsay on hearsay**", based on "rumour, speculation and hearsay", and contradicted by Ms Fanny-Dulloo (2024 SoFIC ¶¶45–60).

   (e) **The order is excessively wide and unreasonable** — "any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity", which would prevent Mr Reid's involvement in the care of his own disabled child; grossly disproportionate to any finding made (*Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v Li* [2013] HCA 18) (2024 SoFIC ¶¶102–105).

## E. Changed circumstances — the basis of the order has fallen away

7. The internal-review decision fixed the order, in part, by reference to "**ongoing investigations of law enforcement agencies and the NDIA into fraud allegations against Mr Reid**" (2024 SoFIC ¶61(b)), and the Respondent "constantly sought to link Mr Reid to the criminal charges against his wife" (¶63) — notwithstanding that the link was "tenuous" and that the Respondent's own internal document described the alleged breaches as "**administrative and personal errors**" (¶65).

8. **[AMENDED]** The criminal proceedings against Mrs Reid were **discontinued in February 2024** (2024 SoFIC ¶68). **[VERIFY exact date — 23 vs 28 February 2024 — and basis against the primary CDPP record.]** Mr Reid was **never charged**, and the Australian Government Solicitor confirmed (28 February 2024) that it "**cannot say that criminal proceedings are being actively considered by the Commonwealth DPP, as no brief of evidence has been provided**". **[VERIFY reference.]** On a review deciding the correct or preferable decision on the material now before the Tribunal, the foundation on which Mr Reid's suitability finding rested no longer exists (*Shi v Migration Agents Registration Authority* (2008) 235 CLR 286 **[VERIFY]**). A protective power used to hold a never-charged man out of the sector for the expected life of an investigation that produced no charge and no brief of evidence was not used for its protective purpose.

## F. [NEW] Improper purpose

9. The Applicant contends that the banning power was exercised for a purpose foreign to its protective grant, alternatively for a dominant improper purpose, namely the removal of FFG and its principals from the sector and the reallocation of FFG's participants to competing providers. A statutory power exercised for a purpose foreign to the statute is invalid (*Padfield v Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food* [1968] AC 997); a discretion conferred in unconfined terms is examinable for improper purpose (*R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council* (1981) 151 CLR 170); where several purposes exist the exercise is invalid if the improper purpose was the dominant or actuating purpose (*Thompson v Randwick Corporation* (1950) 81 CLR 87); and a power may not be used to inflict a detriment for a collateral end (*Sanders v Snell* (1998) 196 CLR 329). **[All [VERIFY]; these are the verified-list improper-purpose authorities.]**

10. In support, the Applicant relies on the following matters (particularised in the Schedule of Exhibits dated 29 May 2026), which the Applicant contends were the engine that generated the complaints and statements the Respondent adopted:

   (a) an NDIA officer, **Nathan Halls (Assistant Director, Participant Solutions, WA Operations)**, framed FFG as "**raising concerns over fraudulent behaviour**" from February 2022, and by September 2022 stated he was "**leading a coordinated provider exit on behalf of the NDIA**" and treated fraud as "**substantiated**" before any charge;

   (b) the NDIA **installed a competing provider, Diversitas WA (Ms Sukhi Tear), as FFG's replacement** — Mr Halls confirming "**Diversitas involvement as the Support Coordinator was at the request of the agency via a request for service**" — and removed FFG "**from consent**" on participant records, placing "**alerts**" blacklisting FFG **before any banning order**;

   (c) the competing provider **routed FFG into the Respondent's own fraud and integrity channels** ("your correspondence has been passed onto the NDIS … and the Quality and Safeguards Commission");

   (d) the providers to whom FFG's participants were steered (Diversitas WA; Nurse Assist 24/7, Ms Amanda Damon) were **in commercial conflict with FFG and were the source of the complaints**, several of the Respondent's witnesses being former FFG staff who had moved to those competitors and had documented financial grievances against the Applicant; and

   (e) participants were **induced and directed**, not freely choosing — including an offer to a participant of an increase to his NDIS plan and the grant of his son's NDIS access (which had been refused) **on condition he leave FFG**.

11. The Applicant contends that, even if a protective purpose co-existed, the order in its scope ("any involvement … in any capacity") and the manner of its making cannot be explained by a genuine, independent protective assessment, and that but for the improper purpose the order would not have been made (*Thompson v Randwick*). **The Applicant does not invite the Tribunal to make any finding of malice or misfeasance**; the ground is put as an administrative-law ground going to the lawfulness and to the correct or preferable decision.

## G. [NEW] The provenance and reliability of the Respondent's evidence — the role of the NDIA

12. The Respondent's evidentiary foundation was **generated and provided by the NDIA**. The Respondent's own decision brief records that the evidence "**draws upon statements obtained and provided by the NDIA in support of their criminal investigation**", that "**the NDIA currently has an investigation ongoing**", and that "**the Provider Exit Protocol has been actioned with the NDIA**". The Statement of Material Facts (19 October 2022) and the Tactical Intelligence Report (18 August 2022) are NDIA documents. **[VERIFY EC22-002103 quotations against the primary brief.]**

13. The complaints and witness statements relied on were, the Applicant contends, substantially sourced from a **competitor/ex-staff cluster** with documented motive, taken in a synchronised sitting by NDIA Fraud Investigations officers, and **never independently vetted by the Respondent**; the Respondent **declined the Applicant's contemporaneous request** to identify what the complaints concerned, when they were made and who made them, and conducted **no interview of Mr Reid before banning him**. (2024 SoFIC ¶¶82–101.) A number of the complaints were **anonymous**.

14. These matters put squarely in issue the **source, provenance and reliability** of the evidence and the role of the NDIA, and are relevant both to the suitability assessment and to the improper-purpose ground. The Applicant therefore seeks (by separate interlocutory application) production of the complaint-intake and provenance records, the NDIA-sourced material, the Tactical Intelligence Report, and the reallocation records; and discharge of the non-disclosure orders over the Tactical Intelligence Report. (Documents not produced are listed at 2024 SoFIC ¶93.)

## I. Only the directors were targeted

16. The Applicant repeats 2024 SoFIC ¶¶94–101: multiple persons were involved in the impugned claims (including the CFO, Mr Rejaul Alam, who the Applicant was entitled to rely on under s 189 of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth)), the claims were submitted by "an unknown person" / via the "Alam PRODA account", yet only the directors were banned, and Mr Alam was never questioned. **[AMENDED:]** the selective targeting of the directors — while the competing providers who instigated the complaints and received the participants were left untouched (including an entity of which a complainant-competitor was a director, which was considered for banning but not banned) — supports the improper-purpose ground at Part F.
