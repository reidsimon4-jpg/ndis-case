## 2. The tests, in plain English

- **Improper purpose (public law).** A power must be used for the reason Parliament gave it. If a legitimate reason AND a foreign reason both exist, the act fails only if the foreign reason was the dominant/substantial one (*Thompson v Randwick* (1950) 81 CLR 87 [verify]). It attaches to whoever **made the decision**. For the ban, that is the Commission. For the lock, the 4 Oct stop, plan changes and the "exit", it is NDIA.
- **Misfeasance in public office (tort).** A public officer, acting under a power, either (a) intends to harm, or (b) knows they are exceeding power (or is recklessly indifferent), and causes loss. *Sanders v Snell* [1998] HCA 64, paras 37-42, 48 (checked against the [HCA text](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2025-10-24/HCA/Sanders%20v%20Snell%20%28S142-1997%29%20%5B1998%5D%20HCA%2064.pdf)): the Minister who directed a sacking without notice got the Full Court's misfeasance finding set aside (new trial ordered) **because nothing showed he knew, or was reckless, that he lacked power.** That is exactly your gap.
- **Malicious prosecution (tort).** Defendant started or procured the prosecution; it ended in the plaintiff's favour; no reasonable and probable cause; dominant improper purpose (*A v NSW* [2007] HCA 10; *Beckett v NSW* [2013] HCA 17). **Vida's tort only.** The informant on the notice was NDIA investigator Svarc (T0596), not Halls.
- **De novo (ART).** The Tribunal makes the decision again on present evidence (*Shi* [2008] HCA 31). 2022 motives matter only if they taint the evidence or process.
- **Reflective loss.** A company's loss belongs to the company. Simon can't sue for FFG's/Hybrid's lost revenue.
- **Other routes, no malice needed [verify with counsel]:** declaration of invalidity; interference with contractual relations; the Commonwealth CDDA scheme (compensation for defective administration).
