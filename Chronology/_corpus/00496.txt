C.  The findings the Tribunal must make, and the case the Second Applicant must meet
8.	At [38] of its decision the Tribunal identified that it will need to consider whether each applicant is a suitable person to be involved in the provision of services to people with disability under section 73ZN(2) of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS Act), which will require findings relating to the allegations, including (a) whether any NDIS funding was made or received for services that had not been provided to the participant; (b) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had intimidated or harassed any participant; and (c) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had contravened their respective banning orders.
9.	The matters summarised at [35] to [37] of the decision are, as [38] records, allegations; in summarising them the Tribunal was assisted by the Respondent's summary (at [33]). The Second Applicant disputes them in so far as they are relied on against him.
10.	The question at [38] is the suitability of each applicant. The matters at [38](b) and (c) are expressly personal. The matter at [38](a) concerns claims made by companies. For it to bear on the Second Applicant's suitability, the Tribunal would need to be satisfied, on cogent evidence, not only that particular claims were made for services that were not provided, but also what, if anything, the Second Applicant personally did or failed to do in relation to them. The Commission's investigation note recorded: “Despite emails for FFG and The Hive being directed to or including Simon Reid the Commission has limited information about his actual involvement in the operations.” (tender T9.4, p 178). The Commission's Banning Order Policy, to which the Tribunal may have regard, states that a member of the key personnel of a provider “would be unlikely to receive a banning order, unless either directly involved in the conduct of concern or having failed to take appropriate steps for which they were responsible which significantly facilitated, contributed to or exacerbated the conduct” (tender T6, p 142).
11.	The claim totals in the briefing notes are drawn from Attachment J (the Tactical Intelligence Report), which is withheld under the orders of 26 July 2024 (see EC22-002521 at [3.3]). The Second Applicant has not identified, in the material served on him, any document that reconciles the claims relied on against him with the service records for those claims. He does not ask the Tribunal to limit the material the Respondent may rely on. He asks that the Respondent identify, before he files his evidence, the claims and conduct it relies on against him and the evidence for each, so that the hearing addresses a defined case rather than allegations at large.
12.	The Second Applicant asks that the final hearing be an oral hearing. The findings identified at [38](b) and (c) concern allegations about his personal conduct, which he disputes, and he seeks to question the makers of any statements relied on for those findings. On 7 November 2025, while generally supporting a process conducted primarily on the papers, he asked that the Tribunal “reserve discretion to list the matter for oral hearing should fairness require it”.

E.  Form of any order
16.	In Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 the Federal Court held that section 73ZN(2) does not authorise an order prohibiting a person from indirectly engaging in, or being indirectly involved in, specified activities (at [128], [134] and [139]), although an order may prohibit a clearly specified activity that itself only indirectly involves the provision of supports or services (at [134]); and that the specified activities must be expressed in terms reasonably capable of being obeyed (at [131]). The order under review prohibits “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”, including providing supports and services “directly or indirectly”. The Second Applicant asks that the Respondent state in advance the order it will seek, and whether it relies on the provisions inserted into section 73ZN of the NDIS Act in 2026. He reserves his position on those questions.
