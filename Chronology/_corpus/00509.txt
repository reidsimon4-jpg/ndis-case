Defence Theme: Administrative Errors, Not Fraud
Simon Reid v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission — ART 2023/3363. Working document, 26 September 2026. Companion: Errors_Register_Simon_Reid_26_Sep_2026.xlsx. Every quote below was checked against the source PDF text; page refs are Tribunal (T-document) pages.
1. Bottom line
The Commission said it first. Before acting, its own compliance team warned the action "may be viewed as quite strong given that the complaints and breaches focus on administrative and personal errors" (EC22-002000, Risks/Sensitivities [4], T9 p 159).
The prosecution's own accountant backs it. For Hybrid claims in Simon's directorship period, the CDPP forensic accountant listed 1,307 claims ($711,600). She did not allege 903 of them ($324,543) were false. Of the $387,056 she did allege, only $89,963 (23%) rests on a participant saying no service was received at all.
Nothing links Simon personally. The Commission admitted "limited information about his actual involvement in the operations" (T9.4 p 178). Hybrid invoices in the CDPP set were sent from Xero by accounts staff — none by Simon.
The ban was meant to last until the charges were decided. The Commission's own policy says so (T6 pp 141–142). The charges were dismissed on 23 Feb 2024. The ban is still running.
It is not a free win. Four participants (Benediktsson, Janine Craig, Yvonne Harrison, Goodwin-Hybrid) have no roster support for the disputed claims, and Hashimi ($164k 'after departure to Tehran') needs a real explanation. Section 6.

What this means in plain English
Most Hybrid billing in Simon's period was accepted by the prosecution's own accountant.
Only about $90k is a straight 'I never got it'. The rest are disputes about when, what type, where the participant was, or have no stated basis at all.
The Charlie Harrison Hybrid claims — which the internal review relied on — were cancelled and repaid. That is an error being corrected, not fraud being hidden.

6. What still hurts — deal with it head-on
Honest read: the theme wins the proportionality argument and shrinks the case to four or five participants. It does not make those claims disappear. Where records can't answer a claim, concede it as a billing failure by the accounts function and show what Simon did when he found out (lawyers 29 Sep, audit 3 Oct, CFO removed 6 Oct 2022).
7. How to run it at the hearing
Step 1 — Frame: read T9 [4] first. "The Commission told itself this was about administrative and personal errors."
Step 2 — Shrink the case: the prosecution's own accountant accepted most of the billing; the 'no service' residue is ~$90k across a handful of participants, none linked to any act by Simon.
Step 3 — Show the fixes: real-time corrections (Section 5), cancellations/repayments, and Simon's October 2022 response.
Step 4 — Apply the Commission's own policy: most serious response; not longer than necessary; key personnel only if directly involved; review once charges are decided (T6). Statutory hook: s 181D(4)(b) — act in a risk-responsive and proportionate way (recited in the internal review itself).
Step 5 — Relief: revoke. Fallback: confine to directly providing supports, no labour hire / 'any capacity' limb (Sunflower), and end it at time served.
