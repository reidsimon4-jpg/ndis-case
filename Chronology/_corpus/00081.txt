# COUNSEL'S ASSESSMENT — THE ANGLE (1 September 2026)

Privileged working draft prepared with AI assistance for the applicants and retained lawyers. Everything marked [VERIFY] is unconfirmed. No concession of fraud is made anywhere in this document.

## 1. Conclusion first
The current architecture is aimed at the INSTRUMENT (Sunflower wording, s 70, separate question, s 39B suite). The case will be decided on the EVIDENCE: present suitability, de novo, on the documents for seven participants. Three independent perspectives run today (AGS counsel, Tribunal member, litigation strategist) converge on the same point. The angle is:

**"Prove suitability now from our own records; make the Tribunal's floor a narrow lawful order and its ceiling a retrospective no-order (s 108(4)); clean the register for the entities immediately; never let a paper case turn into a published fraud finding."**

## 2. Verified law today (primary sources, 1 Sep 2026)
- NDIS Act compilation No 25 (6 May 2026, incl. Act No 41 of 2026 — Integrity and Safeguarding Act, assent 8 Apr 2026, Commission powers commenced 9 Apr 2026):
  - s 73ZN(2) UNCHANGED: orders against key personnel/employees may only prohibit "engaging in specified activities". Sunflower [131] survives the amendments.
  - NEW s 73ZN(2B)/(2C)(d)(i): persons "involved in providing ... services that enable or facilitate the provision of supports or services" are now bannable. Labour-hire supply (Vitality) is now squarely within reach as a specified activity.
  - NEW s 73ZNA: breach of a banning order = 5 years' imprisonment / 300 PU / civil 1,000 PU. Raises stakes for any arguable "involvement" while the orders live (e.g. Simon signing as FFG "Director" in the 30 Jun 2023 DEWR letter; any Australian use of the Infinity Living Options name).
  - s 73ZS REPEALED; register now s 73ZDA. (3): current orders MUST be listed. (4): a person against whom an order "WAS in force" MAY be kept listed. Register removal after expiry is discretionary — the FFG expiry letter's invitation is the only route and has never been used.
  - Transitional items for existing orders (Act 41/2026 Sch 1 items 72–74, 92) NOT retrieved [VERIFY].
- ART Act compilation No 5 (18 May 2026):
  - s 108(2): substituted decision is the decision-maker's "for all purposes". s 108(4): it "has effect, or is taken to have had effect, from the time at which the reviewable decision has or had effect" unless the Tribunal orders otherwise (s 108(5)). → A substituted NO-ORDER is retrospective BY DEFAULT. This is the clean-slate/register prize, available inside the ART without s 39B.
  - s 114: "obvious error in the text" only.
- Khalil [2025] HCA 33 (verified 17 Aug): Tribunal applies the law at the time of ITS decision → AGS can and will ask for a substituted order drafted under 2026 law.

## 5. Alternative viewpoint the team has not run: settle from strength, now
Post-Sunflower and post-9 Apr 2026, the Commission holds (a) two individual orders worded worse than the one quashed, (b) five entity orders to 2032 its own briefs say lack a service-delivery footprint, (c) 15 months of remaining life on the individual orders, and (d) a s 73ZNA that makes every ambiguity a 5-year offence. That is a settlement position, not a trial position. A without-prejudice package: consent set-aside of the 21 Apr 2023 variations and substitution of orders expiring on a fixed near date (or no order for Simon), retrospective under s 108(4); revocation of the five entity orders; register removal under s 73ZDA(4) discretion; no admissions; CDDA expressly preserved. The KC-frame rule "nothing short of no-order" protects a compensation premise that is largely illusory (CDDA discretionary, excludes properly-made discretionary decisions, may not reach NDIA [VERIFY]; misfeasance needs officer knowledge the file lacks; security for costs offshore). Price the alternative: a full de novo hearing with Vida silent = real risk of published Briginshaw findings that outlive Dec 2027 — the one outcome worse than the status quo.
