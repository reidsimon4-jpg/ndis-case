Part 1 - The situation in one page
Three arenas, one story. (1) The Administrative Review Tribunal: Vida Reid, Simon Reid and Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd challenge banning orders made 21 December 2022 (matters 2023/3361, 2023/3363, 2023/4494). Two decisions are reserved: our severance/sequencing application (since 22 April 2026) and the Respondent's non-disclosure application (since 19 May 2026). (2) The criminal shadow: all four charges against Vida were dismissed for want of prosecution on 23 February 2024 with a stated intention to recharge that has never been withdrawn - and never acted on, although the privilege review the prosecutors blamed was finished by 14 May 2024. (3) The prospective Federal Court case against the National Disability Insurance Agency (the Agency) and named officers, over the 2022 payment freeze and provider-exit campaign that destroyed the businesses before any banning order existed.
The strategic centre of gravity has shifted from the Commission to the Agency. The Agency ran the operation from February 2022 (payment lock, participant steering, fraud allegations to third parties, the search); the Commission acted last, on Agency material. The Commission's banning case is the shield; the Agency's conduct is the wrong. The tribunal can review the bans but cannot award damages or reach the Agency - only the Federal Court can.
What we hold that most litigants never get: contemporaneous primary documents for every load-bearing fact - the lock notice that cites no legal power, the third party's written record of the “pay nothing” instruction, the State Manager's written denial of that instruction two days later (in a letter that also summarily cleared the officer running the campaign), the quantified loss, the certified transcript of the prosecution's collapse, and four years of the Commonwealth describing the criminal matter inconsistently to different audiences.
Part 2 - The strategy in four sentences
Tribunal first: win severance so Simon's matter (3363) is heard first and Vida is not forced to give evidence while her jeopardy is formally preserved; then win the substantive review on the Abdullah framing - the Agency-sourced evidence is untested and not independent, and there is no present protective need.
Squeeze the criminal limb shut in parallel: the prosecutors' decision-demand letter (drafted), the seized-property return demand (drafted), and the statement-of-reasons requests (drafted) each force a written answer; every answer - and every silence - feeds either severance (jeopardy live) or the substantive case and closure (jeopardy dead).
File the Federal Court case against the Agency in 2027, after the tribunal hearing and before the limitation drop-dead (working assumption mid-2028, opinion commissioned): declarations of invalidity as the platform, interference with contract as the money count, misfeasance as the sting.
Method throughout: never allege lying - lay the document pairs side by side and force written elections. Every election has no good exit for the Commonwealth.
Part 3 - The Federal Court case (the centrepiece)
Parties
Applicants: Vida Reid; Simon Reid; Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd; Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd. PREREQUISITE: confirm both companies remain registered - a deregistered company cannot sue and would need reinstatement first. Please check this week.
Respondents: the National Disability Insurance Agency (suable in its own name); the Commonwealth; Nathan Halls; Barbara Lundgren (Acting State Manager WA at the relevant time); such other officers as discovery identifies (the person(s) in “WA Operations - Participant Solutions” who gave the 4 October 2022 instruction).
Count 1 - Declarations of invalidity (the platform)
Sought: declarations that (a) the compliance lock applied 3 May 2022 and (b) the total payment stop of on or about 4 October 2022 were made without statutory authority, without procedural fairness, and are invalid. The Notice cites no provision (E1); no document at all records the October decision; The Hive received no notice of anything, ever. No state of mind needs to be proved. Their only answer is to produce the power and the decision-maker - which the reasons requests already demand in writing, pre-filing.
Anticipated defence - delay (2022 conduct, 2027 filing). Answers: continuing effects (property still held, register permanence, business destruction); concealment (E7 - the written denial); and the declarations support the tort counts rather than seek to unwind a spent decision.
Count 2 - Interference with contractual relations (the money count)
Elements to evidence: the businesses had contracts with participants and payment arrangements with plan managers (known to the Agency, which administers the scheme); the Agency intentionally procured non-payment - “instruction from NDIA... Effective immediately, we are not able to pay ALL FFG and The Hive invoices” (E5), confirmed orally to the plan manager's compliance head; loss followed immediately and is partly quantified (E11: $22,921.01 unprocessable because “participant plans have been moved to NDIA managed”, plus $16,016.83 held). No malice element. The defence of statutory justification collapses into Count 1: name the power.
Count 3 - Misfeasance in public office (the sting)
Elements: an invalid or unauthorised act by a public officer, done with knowledge of (or reckless indifference to) the lack of power and the likelihood of harm. The mental element is where these cases die; here it rests on documents rather than inference: Halls told a landlord the fraud was “substantiated” when nothing had been tested anywhere (E4); the campaign's object WAS the harm (“coordinated provider exit”); the operation ran while the Agency's own notice said manual assessment only (E1 vs E5); and the Lundgren letter (E7) does double work - if the direction was authorised, the denial misled our solicitors; if it was not, the no-power element is established and the letter shows the Agency knew it. The cross-examination question that decides the count: why deny what you were entitled to do?
Pleading discipline: facts, not conclusions (per the recent strike-out authorities). We plead the officers by name where documented and by description where not, and let discovery supply the approvals chain.
Count 4 (counsel's call) - Injurious falsehood
The Yaran publication (E4) and participant-facing statements (E6): false characterisation (“substantiated”), published to third parties, calculated to cause and causing economic loss, with malice provable on the documents. Defamation itself is long time-barred; injurious falsehood may not be. Please advise whether to plead it or hold it as malice particulars within Count 3.
Relief, forum, and what we deliberately do not seek
Declarations; compensatory damages for the destruction of both businesses (expert quantum report required - revenue run-rate pre-freeze vs post); aggravated and exemplary damages on the misfeasance count; interest and costs. Jurisdiction: Federal Court (officers of the Commonwealth; matter arising under Commonwealth law) - confirm the precise gateway. We do NOT ask the Federal Court to touch the banning orders: that stays in the tribunal, so the two forums cannot be played against each other.
Honest assessment
Survival past strike-out: good - the documentary base is unusually strong for this tort. Misfeasance at trial: uncertain (30-45% on present documents, rising with discovery). Interference count: 50%+ unless a genuine power is produced. Declarations: strongest. Most likely good outcome: settlement after discovery, where register relief can be negotiated even though the court could not order it. Cost to the end of discovery: roughly $150,000-$300,000; trial multiples of that. A litigation funder conversation is worth having - documented, quantified claims against the Commonwealth are fundable, at a price (typically 25-40% of recovery).
