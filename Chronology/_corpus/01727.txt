# COUNSEL DECISION NOTE - FILE s 39B NOW OR SEEK s 185 REFERRAL FIRST?

**Status:** Privileged/confidential working note for independent counsel. Not for filing or service.

**Purpose:** The earlier forum memorandum identified the s 185 referral route but leaned toward it. This note removes that default. Immediate s 39B filing and an ART-first referral are both defensible; counsel should choose expressly after verifying the facts below.

## Decision in one sentence

Choose the route that produces the fastest credible determination of the facial *Sunflower* question while preserving useful relief: file now if the ART cannot supply a prompt, reliable outcome and the Court-discretion evidence is ready; seek s 185 first if the Tribunal is positioned to decide the referral promptly and the reference is likely to reduce, rather than add, delay.

## Route A - file the s 39B application now

### Affirmative case

The primary argument is that, if the order facially exceeded former s 73ZN(2), this is a clear excess of jurisdiction affecting persons directly aggrieved. Under *Sunflower* [117] and *Bechara*, relief then ordinarily issues almost as of right unless it is just to withhold it. The application can be confined to fixed instruments, historical statutory text and the construction already adopted in *Sunflower*.

### Conditions favouring immediate filing

- The exact current ART status is proved and there is no prompt, reliable hearing or decision date.
- The post-*Sunflower* delay chronology is complete and objectively supported.
- The relief has identified utility for Vida and separately for Simon despite the revival problem.
- Counsel can frame the first hearing around the facial ground and quarantine the fact-dependent grounds.
- Continuing operation, public registration or family and livelihood consequences are proved without broad applicant evidence.
- Any limitation or expiry consideration makes further procedural experimentation materially risky.

### Principal risks

- *McGowan*: the Court may regard the already-active ART review as more convenient and satisfactory.
- The applicants used ART stays, confidentiality and severance procedures, and Vida withdrew her stay request.
- A weakly explained period after 21 November 2025 may justify withholding relief.
- A dismissal or stay creates cost and delay without resolving the underlying merits.
- Simon may obtain only partial practical relief if the original order remains or revives.
- Current s 73ZN may permit a fresh order even after a historical invalidity win.

## Route B - request a s 185 reference first

### Affirmative case

Section 185 allows the ART, with the President's agreement, to refer a question of law arising in the proceeding to the Federal Court. This uses the existing review architecture and can reduce the force of an allegation that the applicants improperly bypassed the Tribunal.

Sections 49 and 53 support a request that the Tribunal control its procedure and limit the questions, evidence and issues under consideration while the legal question is addressed. Section 53 should not be described as an express statutory "preliminary issue" procedure.

### Conditions favouring a prior request

- The ART can list and determine the referral request within a short, reliable period.
- The exact legal question clearly arises in the existing proceeding and can be framed without disputed facts.
- The relevant instruments and historical statute are already before the Tribunal.
- The President-agreement process can occur without an open-ended adjournment.
- Interim protection under s 32 can adequately address immediate harm.
- A reference will materially narrow the remaining merits issues.

### Principal risks

- Referral is discretionary and cannot be compelled by the applicants.
- Section 185(5) prevents the Tribunal from giving a decision to which the referred question is relevant while the reference is pending. The mechanism can therefore delay, rather than accelerate, final merits relief.
- A bare refusal does not prove that the ART is inadequate. If refusal comes with a prompt merits timetable, it may strengthen the Commission's alternative-remedy argument in later s 39B proceedings.
- Only a refusal, non-determination or delay accompanied by the absence of a prompt and effective ART path materially strengthens the later Court-discretion case.
- The President or Tribunal may regard the question as fact-dependent, hypothetical or unsuitable in the proposed form.

## Route C - short ART request with a parallel unfiled Court package

This is the genuine hybrid option. Counsel can:

1. finalise but not file the s 39B papers;
2. seek urgent ART directions, s 32 protection, a confined scope under ss 49 and 53, and a s 185 reference;
3. ask for a definite short timetable justified by the age and continuing operation of the orders; and
4. reassess immediate filing when the ART responds.

This route is attractive only if the short timetable is credible. It should not become another indefinite waiting period. The trigger for reassessment must be written into counsel's plan by reference to an event or date, not left as "after the ART process".

## Facts that decide the route

Counsel should not choose until the following are in one verified status sheet:

| Fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Latest ART orders and registry confirmation | Establishes what is actually pending and whether any decision is reserved |
| Next hearing or decision date | Distinguishes genuine imminent relief from indefinite review |
| Status of severance and s 70 issues | Shows whether collateral processes still block the merits |
| Existing stay position and reason for Vida's withdrawal | Determines whether immediate harm can be protected in the ART |
| Date the applicants learned of *Sunflower* and subsequent steps | Central to Court discretion |
| Exact operative instrument for each applicant | Determines facial strength and useful relief |
| Simon original-order revival analysis | Determines whether Court success would materially improve his position |
| Evidence of present harm and register effects | Establishes practical utility and urgency |
| Current prosecution/CDPP position, if available | Affects self-incrimination safeguards and scheduling, not the facial construction itself |

## Recommended question for counsel

> On the authenticated current ART status and completed delay chronology, which course is more likely to produce useful relief before the December 2027 expiry: (A) immediate confined s 39B proceedings relying on the clear-excess starting point in *Sunflower* [117], or (B) a time-limited ART request for scope directions, interim protection and a s 185 reference, having regard to the mandatory pause in s 185(5)?

## Non-negotiable safeguards under either route

- Keep the opening issue on the official instruments and historical statute.
- Do not require Simon to give a broad account of potentially incriminating operational facts.
- Do not add malice, bad faith, reckless indifference or NDIA/Halls damages allegations to improve the emotional force of the public-law case.
- Preserve the compensation and limitation work as a separate track.
- Do not describe an ART outcome as reserved or imminent without primary proof.
- Do not present a fresh lawful order as impossible under the current Act.

## Present working view

The package does not contain enough authenticated current ART information to select Route A or Route B responsibly. The correct immediate task is fact verification, not another merits opinion. Once the status sheet and delay chronology are complete, counsel should make and record the route choice explicitly. The s 185 option is a valuable mechanism, but it is not inherently safer or faster than immediate judicial review.
