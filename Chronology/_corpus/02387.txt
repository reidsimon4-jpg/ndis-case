01. Executive Summary
  A high-level overview of the opportunity, the partnership, and the path forward.

This proposal outlines a joint venture between Agensi Pekerjaan Staff Link Solutions Sdn. Bhd.
(“Staff Link Solutions”) and MecCilect Sdn. Bhd. (“MecCilect”) — two complementary Malaysian
companies — to introduce Light Gauge Steel (LGS) modular construction technology into the
Malaysian market, with technology, know-how, and product supply provided by PT Infinity Living
Options (“Infinity Living”) of Indonesia.
Staff Link Solutions is a partner company of Infinity Living — a director of Infinity Living holds a
50% shareholding in Staff Link Solutions alongside a local partner who serves as a director of
MATRADE (Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation). This existing relationship provides
the foundation of trust and alignment for this venture. MecCilect, led by AK Devin, brings deep
engineering, construction, and scaffolding expertise with strong ties to the oil & gas and
construction industries.
The partnership is designed as a phased, low-risk market entry strategy:

   Phase 1 — Test the Market: Import LGS frame samples from Indonesia, establish a
   professional display module in Kuala Lumpur, and host information sessions for engineers,
   authorities, and potential clients.

   Phase 2 — Commercialise: Set up a dedicated sales office, begin importing and selling LGS
   products to the Malaysian construction industry.

   Phase 3 — Localise: If demand justifies it, establish a local roll-forming factory in Malaysia to
   serve the growing ASEAN market.

The Malaysian construction industry is valued at USD 41.85 billion (2025) and the government
mandates 70% IBS (Industrialised Building System) content for public projects. LGS is classified
as IBS and directly benefits from these mandates. The global LGS framing market is projected to
grow from USD 43.62 billion (2025) to USD 71.11 billion by 2033, with Asia-Pacific identified as the
fastest-growing region.

03. Market Opportunity — Why Malaysia, Why Now
  The Malaysian construction sector is undergoing a structural shift toward modern, industrialised
  building methods — creating a perfect entry window for LGS technology.

3.1 A Growing Construction Market
The Malaysian construction market is projected to reach MYR 70.40 billion in 2026, growing at
6.1% annually. This growth is driven by infrastructure completion, industrial expansion,
urbanisation, and an ageing building stock requiring renewal.

3.2 Government Policy Tailwinds

 POLICY / MANDATE               IMPACT ON LGS

 CIDB 70% IBS Mandate           All public projects must contain 70% IBS content. LGS qualifies as IBS,
                                creating mandatory demand.

 New Industrial Master Plan     Prioritises prefabricated and modular construction as a strategic
 (NIMP) 2030                    industrial backbone.

 Affordable Housing             “Rumah Mampu Milik Wilayah” targets 150,000 units — industrialised
 Programs                       methods are being actively promoted.

 BIM Stage 2 Mandate            Digital integration favours BIM-led manufacturers like Infinity Living
                                (Vertex BD integrated).


3.3 Modern Methods of Construction Outpacing Traditional
Modern methods of construction (MMC), including LGS, are growing at 11.05% CAGR — nearly 3×
the rate of traditional construction. Key drivers include:
  Labour savings: IBS reduces on-site labour by 30–40% — critical as Malaysia faces skilled
  labour shortages and rising minimum wages.
  Speed: IBS reduces construction schedules by 20–25%.
  Prefabricated market growth: Malaysia’s prefabricated construction market is forecast to reach
  MYR 9.36 billion by 2029 (CAGR 7.1%).

3.4 Asia-Pacific: The Fastest-Growing Region for LGS
The global LGSF market is projected to grow from USD 43.62 billion (2025) to USD 71.11 billion by
2033 (CAGR 6.3%). Asia-Pacific is identified as the fastest-growing region, driven by
urbanisation, population growth, and supportive government policies.

   Bottom Line: Malaysia’s policy environment, market size, and growth trajectory make it one
   of the most attractive markets in ASEAN for LGS market entry. The 70% IBS mandate for
public projects alone creates a policy-driven demand floor that does not exist in most other
markets.
04. What is LGS? — Product Overview
  Light Gauge Steel framing is a proven, precision-engineered construction system used
  worldwide for residential, commercial, and institutional buildings.

4.1 How LGS Works
Light Gauge Steel (LGS) framing uses cold-formed steel profiles (C-channels, U-tracks, and
custom sections) manufactured on automated roll-forming machines. Components are designed
using BIM software, cut and punched to exact dimensions in the factory, labelled, flat-packed, and
then rapidly assembled on-site.

06. Phased Roadmap
 A step-by-step approach that manages risk by validating demand before committing to larger
 capital investment.

     PHASE 1 — MARKET TESTING (MONTHS 1–3)
     Import, Display & Validate
     Import first shipment of LGS frame samples from Indonesia. Assemble the 10m² display module at
     the warehouse. Begin material testing with Malaysian labs. Host first information session with local
     engineers and authorities. Gauge market interest and gather feedback.


     PHASE 2 — CERTIFICATION & EARLY SALES (MONTHS 3–6)
     Certify, Endorse & Sell
     Complete material testing and obtain Malaysian certifications. Secure engineering endorsements.
     Begin accepting first commercial orders (imported from Indonesia). Establish the sales office. Build
     pipeline of projects.


     PHASE 3 — SCALE IMPORT OPERATIONS (MONTHS 6–12)
     Grow Revenue & Prove Demand
     Scale import volumes based on sales traction. Refine pricing for Malaysian market. Build delivery
     and installation capability using MecCilect’s workforce. Develop case studies. Target CIDB
     registration as an IBS supplier.


     PHASE 4 — LOCAL MANUFACTURING (MONTH 12+)
     Establish Roll-Forming Factory in Malaysia
     If demand justifies investment, set up a local roll-forming factory — potentially at MecCilect’s
     Rawang fabrication yard. Eliminate import costs and lead times. Supply the broader Malaysian and
     ASEAN market. Create local employment.



 Key Design Principle: Each phase builds on the last. No large capital commitments are
 required until the market has been validated through real sales and customer feedback.
 This is a “test before you invest” strategy.

8.2 Material Testing Requirements
  Steel Grade Verification: Confirm G550 designation and mechanical properties
  Zinc Coating Thickness: Verify Z275 coating weight (275 g/m²)
  Dimensional Tolerance: Profile dimensions per MS 1553 or equivalent
  Connection Strength: Screw pull-out and shear testing
  Fire Resistance: Testing per MS 1073 or BS 476 equivalent
  Structural Performance: Load testing per MS EN 1993 (Eurocode 3)

8.3 Certification Pathway (Proposed)
1. Step 1: Ship material test samples to SIRIM or accredited Malaysian lab
2. Step 2: Obtain material test reports (4–8 weeks)
3. Step 3: Engage a Malaysian-registered Professional Engineer (PE) to endorse structural designs
4. Step 4: Apply for CIDB IBS product registration
5. Step 5: Register as an approved IBS supplier/installer with CIDB
6. Step 6: Work with local authorities on first pilot project approval

  To Be Discussed: Material testing strategy, engineering endorsement partners, and local
  authority engagement will be planned jointly during the face-to-face meeting. Dr. Thana and
  Mr. Muthu’s input will be critical for this pathway.

10. Commercial Structure & Investment
  A preliminary framework for how the parties will share responsibilities, costs, and returns. Final
  terms to be agreed in the formal JV agreement.

10.1 Proposed Commercial Framework

  JV Ownership: Staff Link Solutions and MecCilect will hold the JV equity (split to be agreed).
  Infinity Living participates through one or more of the following mechanisms:

        Early Phase (Import & Sell): Infinity Living supplies LGS products to the JV at agreed
        wholesale prices. Infinity’s return comes from the margin on product sales — a simple,
        low-risk arrangement for all parties.
        Profit-Share Agreement: A percentage of JV net profits (e.g., 10–15%) is paid to Infinity
        Living as a technology/know-how royalty, reflecting their IP, training, and ongoing design
        services contribution.
        Minor Equity Stake (Later Phase): As the venture scales, Infinity Living may take a
        minority equity position (e.g., 10–20%) in the JV, formalising their long-term commitment
        — particularly relevant if a local factory is established.

11. Why This Partnership Makes Sense
  The strategic rationale for all parties to pursue this venture together.

11.1 For Staff Link Solutions
  Natural Extension: As a partner company of Infinity Living, this venture deepens an existing
  relationship into a revenue-generating business.
  MATRADE Leverage: The co-owner’s MATRADE directorship opens doors for trade facilitation,
  import incentives, and government programme access.
  Low Entry Cost: Phase 1 requires modest capital with clear go/no-go decision points.
  Government Alignment: LGS fits directly into Malaysia’s IBS mandates and national
  development plans.

11.2 For MecCilect
  Diversification: Enter a high-growth construction technology sector alongside a proven
  manufacturer — no need to develop LGS expertise from scratch.
  Existing Infrastructure: The Rawang fabrication yard can serve as staging, storage, and
  potentially future manufacturing.
  Construction Expertise: Scaffolding and structural engineering skills transfer directly to LGS
  installation.
  Client Base: Existing relationships with Petronas, oil & gas, and construction clients create
  cross-selling opportunities.
  First Mover Advantage: Limited LGS specialists in Malaysia creates a “blue ocean” opportunity.

  partners with no upfront equity investment required.

  complexities.

  unit costs.
