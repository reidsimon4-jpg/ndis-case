NATIONAL DISABILITY INSURANCE SCHEME ACT 2013 (Cth) 
SECTION 73ZN 
BANNING ORDER: STATEMENT OF REASONS 
 
This is a statement of reasons for the making of a banning order on 20 December 2022 against 
Ms Vida Reid, under section 73ZN of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS Act). 
Reasons for my decision 
1. At the relevant time Ms Reid was a Director, and key personnel of Forward Focused Group Pty 
Ltd (Forward Focused Group), Registration 4-4GHG-1073.  
2. Ms Reid was a Director of Forward Focused Group until November 2022. 
3. The Commission holds information that Ms Reid has contravened, is contravening, or is likely to 
contravene the NDIS Act. 
4. On 19 October 2022, I issued you with a letter to inform you of the relevant facts and matters I 
have considered in reaching my preliminary view that it may be appropriate in all circumstances 
to make a Banning Order prohibiting you for a period of ten (10) years from being involved in the 
provision of supports or services to people with disability. 
5. I also gave you the opportunity to make a submission before I made my final decision. 
6. Submissions were due no later than 5.00pm (AWST) on 16 November 2022. 
7. I received a request for an extension to that timeframe and granted additional time for 
submissions through to 30 November 2022 (the extended submission date). 
8. I acknowledge the submission sent by Mr Tom Monks, Principal Director, Tom & Co Legal Pty Ltd 
to the Commission on 30 November 2022. The Commission has carefully considered the 
contents of the submission to determine whether to make a Banning Order against you. 
9. I note that you are no longer the Director of Forward Focused Group. With the exception of this 
fact, all other relevant facts and information held by the Commission in forming the preliminary 
view to make a Banning Order against you have not changed. 
10. According to information available to the Commission, you as Director of Forward Focused 
Group, assigned yourself with authority to make decisions on behalf of NDIS participants. This 
information suggests that you were not taking all reasonable steps to prevent and respond to all 
forms of exploitation of people with disability. By way of example,  
a) The Commission holds a record of a Forward Focused Group document titled ‘FM Consent 
Third Party to Act’ which provides ongoing consent for any Forward Focused staff member 
(contact Vida Reid), which includes consent to request review of NDIA decisions and manage 
complaints made by participant Meghan Goodwin.  
 
11. The Commission has received information and allegations that strongly suggest that you as 
Director of Forward Focused Group frequently engaged in bullying, harassment and intimidation 
behaviours directed at NDIS participants and Forward Focused Group staff. Your alleged conduct 
towards participants and staff has created an environment whereby participants and staff did 
not feel supported or safe to raise any concerns they have about the quality and safety of 
supports provided by Forward Focused Group.

12. Since September 2021, the Commission has received over 30 complaints regarding the safety 
and quality of supports provided by Forward Focused Group. The Commission holds information 
that suggests that you, as a representative of the Provider, did not support individuals to raise 
any complaints they may have directly with the provider, without fear of retaliation from you. 
This information strongly indicates that you, in your capacity as Director and key personnel, did 
not ensure that complaints were welcomed, acknowledged, respected and well-managed.  
13. The Commission also holds information that you as Director of Forward Focused Group were not 
acting with respect for individual rights to freedom of expression, self-determination and 
decision-making in accordance with applicable laws and conventions. Across June and July 2022, 
participants including Corina Abraham, Sarah-Jane Clulow, and Alexsis Bott all sort to amend or 
cease service agreements with Forward Focused Group. Your email correspondence indicated 
non-compliance with the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018 and 
included both veiled and direct threats, bullying and harassment. By way of example,  
a) Across May and June 2022, the Provider attempted to obtain control of participant funds 
and participants supports for participant Alexsis Bott. When the participant confirmed, in 
writing that they didn’t want to take the action to release their funds from the existing 
manager, you wrote “we will be terminating your services with us effective immediately”.  
b) Series of emails 24 June 2022 to 19 June 2022 regarding participant Sarah-Jane Clulow 
advising a four week notice for service termination. The emails show that the participant 
was seeking review of an invoice for services not received.  
c) On 1 July 2022, you responded to an account statement from a supplier stating that, “we will 
no longer pay any invoices for Sarah. She has terminated her contract with us.”  
d) Series of emails 15 June 2022 to 19 June 2022 regarding participant Corina Abraham ceasing 
some services with Hybrid Hive. The participant seeks to meet to discuss their concerns. By 
email dated 19 June, you purport to cease support coordination and terminate the contract 
for all services with immediate effect. The email claims “ongoing abusive manner of our staff 
by yourself and the break in trust” as the grounds. The email includes a perceived threat that 
invoices are to be paid immediately or be reported to the NDIS.  
e) Email 16 June 2022 sent by you to participant, Ms Abraham, provides that the support 
worker has a non-compete and non-disclosure contract which will prevent her for working 
privately for the participant. It also states that if the participant had “come to us in good 
faith…I would have said okay.”  
f) Email 16 June 2022 sent by you to participant, Ms Abraham, “I would like all of your 
outstanding invoices paid immediately please or I will report this to the NDIS”.  
14. The Commission holds additional information that indicates that you played a central role in the 
alleged fraudulent activities engaged in by Forward Focused Group, in particular the submission 
of false claims for payments to the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Scheme). I am of the 
view that this demonstrates a failure by you to act with integrity, honesty and transparency.  
15. It is alleged that Forward Focused Group were knowingly submitting false claims for payments to 
the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Scheme), with the intention of dishonestly causing a 
loss to another person, namely the NDIA, a Commonwealth entity. The Commission is aware of 
questions about invoices raised against multiple participants.  
16. The Commission holds information about participants Tiffany Rose and Andrew Hahn on dates 
known between 26 November 2021 and 14 June 2022, which show that the Provider generated 
invoices that did not match the services provided, or were generated without the necessary 
Plans or Services Agreements in place.

27. As part of my considerations I am obliged to consider the NDIS Act. Section 3 of the NDIS Act 
contains the objects of the NDIS Act, which includes: 
a. Supporting the independence and social and economic participation of people with 
disability. 
b. Enabling people with disability to exercise choice and control in the pursuit of their 
goals and the panning and delivery of their supports. 
c. Protecting and preventing people with disability from experiencing harm arising 
from poor quality or unsafe supports or services provided under the National 
Disability Insurance Scheme. 
28. In giving effect to the objects of the NDIS Act, including those outlined above, section 3(3)(b) 
requires regard to be had to the need to ensure financial sustainability of the National Disability 
Insurance Scheme (NDIS). Similarly, as set out in NDIS Act section 4(17) it is the intention of the 
Parliament that the Commissioner, amongst others, have regard to the need to ensure financial 
sustainability of the NDIS in performing functions and exercising powers under the NDIS Act. 
29. The need to protect the interests of people with disability, who may be particularly vulnerable, 
and ensure they are kept safe, outweighs your interest in continuing to be involved in the 
provision of supports or services to people with a disability.  
30. In determining whether to make a Banning Order for you, I weighed the following competing 
interests and concerns: 
a. The nature of your alleged breaches of the NDIS Act are serious, as they involve 
instances of verbal and emotional abuse involving threats towards vulnerable persons 
with disability to whom you were providing services and supports on a regular basis. The 
Commission holds information that suggested that you exerted undue influence over a 
number of participants that limited them from exercising their choice and control over 
their own supports and services. 
b. The Commission holds information that suggests that you have been using your position 
to exploit participants who are in vulnerable situations despite your knowledge of your 
obligations under the NDIS Act. For these reasons, I am concerned your breaches will 
continue to occur in future. 
 
31. The matters set out above demonstrates conduct that falls well below the standard and quality 
of care expected from key personnel of NDIS providers when providing supports or services to 
people with disability. The information before me in relation to the protracted length of time 
over which the alleged conduct took place suggests that your conduct was systemic in nature. 
Allowing you to continue providing supports or services to people with disability poses a 
continuing risk to people with disability.  
32. I am of the view that making a Banning Order against you would provide a strong level of 
protection for people with disability and maintain community confidence in the Scheme. 
33. For the avoidance of doubt, the Banning Order will prevent you from having any involvement 
whether directly or indirectly in the delivery, coordination and management of NDIS-funded 
supports and services to people with disability.
