# Reasonableness, oppression and the point at which "enough" — a working legal-strategic memorandum

## 0.  The question

The applicants ask whether, on the totality of what has been done to them and their family over a continuous period of approximately five years by multiple Commonwealth agencies, the ongoing conduct — and in particular any continuing criminal investigation, as informally reported to them on 27 May 2026 — can lawfully or reasonably be said to be a legitimate exercise of regulatory or prosecutorial power. The framing principle of this memorandum is that *reasonableness* in public law is a cumulative concept: it is measured not against the individual decision standing alone but against the totality of conduct, its effects on the people on whom it is brought to bear, and the persistence of that conduct in the face of evidentiary collapse. On that measure, this matter is well past the line at which Australian public-law doctrine treats continued state activity as itself unlawful, and accommodates a stay and, in due course, affirmative remedies.

## 1.  The objective record of cumulative harm

Reasonableness is not measured against an abstraction. It is measured against the concrete impact on the people the power was brought against. The record establishes, by reference to the documents and recordings already on the file in the NDIS Case folder:

**Loss of livelihood and corporate destruction (December 2022 onwards).** Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd, registered NDIS provider with a continuing commercial footprint and a six-year Commonwealth Department of Employment and Workplace Relations services contract (ES-22-0090, Workforce Australia Services Deed of Standing Offer 2022–2028 GDV1, approximately $5 million per annum), was destroyed by the December 2022 banning orders and the parallel pre-arrest defunding orchestrated by NDIA Western Australia officers. The Karen / Halls audio recording captures the operational mechanism — extra-judicial directions to service providers not to pay FFG's invoices, with no documented authority. Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd was made the subject of a permanent ban. Five further associated entities were banned under s 73ZN(2A) of the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth). The applicants' professional capacity to operate in the disability sector was extinguished.

**The criminal exposure that failed.** Mrs Vida Reid was arrested in October 2022. The Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions discontinued all four charges against her on 28 February 2024 for no evidence — the highest threshold for discontinuance under the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth. Mr Simon Reid was never charged. There has been no adjudication of any of the underlying allegations in any criminal forum.

**Continued investigator activity after the criminal case collapsed.** Notwithstanding the no-evidence discontinuance, agencies of the Commonwealth continued investigatory conduct against the family. On 27 March 2024 — four weeks after the discontinuance — NDIA Service Compliance and Non-Compliance Team officer Jason Moore called Modplex Construction in relation to the family. On 25 September 2024 ABF officers at Perth Airport stopped Mrs Reid under stated "instructions of NDIS." Late in 2024 Ms Bec Hayward and Mr Alex Bowers (the Dream Big Employment principals) reported a home visit. On 7 January 2025 NDIS investigator Mr John Maloney conducted a home interrogation of Ms Virginie Fanny-Dulloo at which he is recorded as having made three admissions of grave significance: that telecommunications interception had been conducted against the family (*"we have been listening to all your phone calls with Vida"*); that public-shaming retaliation was a tool the agency was prepared to deploy (*"we can put your face all over the news like we did to Vida very publicly"*); and that the avoidance of imprisonment was being conditioned upon the procurement of a coerced witness statement against Mrs Reid (*"it's your neck or her neck"*).

**The AGS representation of 11 March 2025 — the specific words used.** On 11 March 2025 the Australian Government Solicitor, by letter from Ms Karlie Dickinson, Senior Lawyer (Our ref. 23004627), addressed jointly to the Administrative Review Tribunal Brisbane Registry and to Mrs Vida Reid, made the following representation:

> *"Following receipt of Mrs Reid's submissions, the Commission has made enquiries with the NDIA and has been informed that there is no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to FFG. In light of this information, the Commission cannot agree to the stay requested by Mrs Reid, having regard to the Tribunal's objectives in section 9 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024."*

Earlier in the same letter AGS expressly disclaimed acting for the National Disability Insurance Agency:

> *"At the outset and to avoid confusion, we confirm that we act for the NDIS Commission… In these proceedings, we do NOT act for the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA), who are the government agency that implements and manages the NDIS. For abundant clarity, the NDIA are NOT a party to these proceedings."*

When Mrs Reid pressed the position by her letter of 14 March 2025, AGS's follow-up letter dated 19 March 2025 narrowed the representation further:

> *"We write further to your letter dated 14 March 2025 and confirm that the Commissioner has made enquiries of the NDIA and that our letter dated 11 March 2025 confirmed the NDIA's position as was relayed to the Commissioner."*

The representation has six built-in limits, all relevant to the question of reasonableness: (1) it is hearsay relayed through three layers (NDIA → Commission → AGS), with AGS expressly disclaiming the client relationship that would otherwise vouch for the underlying fact; (2) only the NDIA was asked — not the NDIS Commission itself, not the AFP, not the CDPP, not the Fraud Fusion Taskforce, not ABF, not the ATO; (3) it is limited to "criminal investigation" stricto sensu — not intelligence-gathering, surveillance, telecommunications interception, compliance or regulatory activity; (4) it is limited to Mrs Reid and FFG only — it makes no representation in relation to Mr Simon Reid, Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, or the other associated entities listed in EC22-002103; (5) the 19 March 2025 follow-up confirms AGS does not vouch for the truth of the underlying proposition, only the accuracy of the relaying; and (6) it is a single-date representation as at 11 March 2025 with no continuing assurance. It was nevertheless on that single sentence, with those six built-in limits, that the applicants gave up their stay and proceeded to a hearing.

**The position as at 27 May 2026.** On 27 May 2026 the applicants received informal but credible information from a source with high-level government connections that a criminal matter against the family is in fact still on foot. On the same day Mrs Reid re-entered Australia from Bali without incident at the border — positive evidence that there are currently no active arrest warrants, no Interpol notices and no charge sheet at a court file naming her as defendant, but entirely consistent with continuing investigative activity in the intelligence-gathering, surveillance, witness-collection or brief-of-evidence phase. The Maloney transcript of 7 January 2025 had already demonstrated, two months *before* the AGS letter, that the NDIS (not the NDIA) was conducting telecommunications interception against the family — activity which sat outside every one of the six limits in the AGS representation. The source's information is therefore capable of being correct without the AGS letter being false on its own terms.

**Coercion and intimidation of witnesses and associates.** Mr Jonathan Shapiera, aged 66 and disabled, is identified in the Hammond Legal brief as having been pressured by Mr Bruce Graydon of the NDIA Fraud Fusion Taskforce under threat of imprisonment, and as having been offered a $200,000 NDIS funding increase as an inducement. Ms Cassandra Burke recanted her sworn witness statement. The Fanny-Dulloo transcript records the explicit conditioning of imprisonment-avoidance on a coerced witness statement. The Maloney transcript records the express acknowledgement of telecommunications interception. These are not gaps in the record. They are admissions and contemporaneous third-party accounts that the agencies' investigative methods have included methods that the public-law tradition treats as prima facie improper.

**Direct harm to a vulnerable third party — the applicants' disabled child.** The applicants have a disabled child. NDIS funding for him was cut. The applicants, having no funded supports and being unwilling to seek them in the climate of investigation, surveillance and witness coercion that the agencies had created — and finding that providers in any event would not engage — have been required to provide that care themselves. The harm to that child, who has had no involvement in any of the matters under investigation, is now a fact on the record. It is the cleanest possible illustration of the way in which the conduct has extended beyond the people the agencies have ostensibly been investigating and has reached an entirely innocent third party whose only connection to any of this is being the applicants' son.

**The functional exile.** The applicants have been required to leave Australia for periods of the relevant time. The 25 September 2024 ABF stop, on stated NDIS instructions, against a person who had not been charged with any offence and whose charges had collapsed seven months earlier, is documented evidence of the use of border-control machinery against the family. Mrs Reid's re-entry from Bali on 27 May 2026 without incident calibrates the present lifecycle stage — there are no current arrest warrants, no Interpol notices, no charge sheet at a court file — but the historic disruption is recorded.

**The financial and revenue-collection footprint.** The 7 January 2026 ATO transcript records senior ATO officer "Mark" stating expressly that approximately $87,000 of Superannuation Guarantee / Part 7 / General Interest Charge entries were attached to the Hybrid Hive tax account anomalously after Mrs Reid's November 2022 lump-sum payment had zeroed out the underlying tax debt, and that the charges have *"no documentation backing them up — really rare to see what I'm seeing."* That is a fourth Commonwealth agency (ATO) operating in a manner that, on its own staff's account, is not explicable by ordinary administrative process.

**The psychological footprint.** Sustained surveillance; coerced witness statements against family members; a six-year campaign across NDIS, NDIA, ABF, ATO and AGS; financial destruction; reputational damage at the moment of a joint Ministerial-Commission media release window in December 2022; the loss of access to supports for a disabled child; the constant requirement to defend allegations that have never been tested in any adjudicative forum. The lived experience of the applicants is of being investigated, surveilled, defunded and threatened across half a decade by people they cannot effectively answer to and who have repeatedly demonstrated that the collapse of their criminal case did not change the underlying activity.

This is the factual base against which the legal question of reasonableness is measured. The harm is not speculative. It has been visited.

## 2.  The legal framework for "reasonableness" in this context

Australian public law approaches the question of when state activity has gone too far through several overlapping doctrines. None is the whole answer; together they form an architecture.

### 2.1  The proportionality of administrative measures

The starting point is that statutory power must be exercised reasonably. *Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v Li* (2013) 249 CLR 332 [VERIFY] establishes the modern Australian test of legal unreasonableness as a free-standing ground of judicial review: an exercise of power is invalid if it lacks an evident and intelligible justification. The Court has since deepened the doctrine to include cases where a decision is so disproportionate to its object that no rational decision-maker could have reached it. The relevant authorities — *Minister for Home Affairs v DUA16* [VERIFY], *Minister for Immigration and Border Protection v Stretton* [VERIFY], and the line traced through these into the 2020s — should be confirmed by counsel before citation.

Applied here, the proportionality question is not whether each individual decision was within power viewed in isolation. It is whether the cumulative course of conduct across multiple agencies, sustained for five years, in the face of a CDPP no-evidence discontinuance and against a family that includes a disabled child whose supports were cut as a direct downstream consequence, can be characterised as a rational and proportionate exercise of regulatory power. The answer that the cumulative effect doctrine permits — and that the applicants are entitled to put before a court — is that it cannot.

### 2.2  Abuse of process and the permanent stay

The Australian abuse-of-process jurisdiction is the doctrinal home of the question "at what point is enough?" Three High Court decisions form the orientation:

- *Jago v District Court (NSW)* (1989) 168 CLR 23 [VERIFY] — the Court recognised that delay and the cumulative effect of prolonged proceedings can amount to an abuse of process warranting a permanent stay where the proceedings have become so oppressive that they cannot be conducted fairly.
- *Williams v Spautz* (1992) 174 CLR 509 [VERIFY] — improper purpose in the bringing or maintenance of proceedings is itself a ground for stay, regardless of whether the underlying allegations could have been made out.
- *Walton v Gardiner* (1993) 177 CLR 378 [VERIFY] — the jurisdiction to stay extends to proceedings that are oppressive in their continuation, even if not at their commencement.

The threshold is high but not unattainable. It is satisfied, on the orthodox formulations, where the proceedings (whether criminal, regulatory or hybrid) have become so unfair, so oppressive, so productive of incurable prejudice, or so coloured by impropriety, that to permit them to continue would bring the administration of justice into disrepute.

The applicants here are not merely complaining of long delay. They are pointing to a five-year course of multi-agency conduct that has continued after the criminal case against the family member who was charged was discontinued for no evidence; that has included documented coercion of witnesses, documented telecommunications interception, documented use of border-control machinery, and documented financial-administrative irregularity; that has had specific and devastating consequences for a disabled child unconnected with any underlying allegation; and that, on the latest information, is still being prosecuted in covert form. If a permanent stay is available anywhere in modern Australian jurisprudence, the conjunction of those features is its natural domain.

### 2.3  *Moti v The Queen* and executive misconduct

*Moti v The Queen* (2011) 245 CLR 456 [VERIFY] is the most recent High Court authority on stay for executive misconduct: where the conduct of Commonwealth officers in procuring the position of a person against whom proceedings are subsequently brought is so improper as to bring the administration of justice into disrepute, the proceedings will be stayed. The principle is general, not confined to its facts (an unlawful deportation followed by prosecution). It applies wherever the executive arm of the State has acted in a manner that the courts cannot countenance as a foundation for proceedings against the same person.

Applied here, the questions for any future court asked to consider a *Moti*-style stay are: did Commonwealth officers — by reference at minimum to the Karen / Halls audio, the documented witness-coercion (Burke, Fanny-Dulloo, Shapiera), the telecommunications interception admission, the ABF airport intercept, and the ATO administrative irregularity — engage in conduct in connection with the matter that cannot be countenanced as a proper foundation? The record at present already discloses sufficient material to put the question. If continued criminal activity is verified, the *Moti* analogy becomes direct: a continuing prosecution founded on a continuing-conduct platform that the court could not countenance.

### 2.4  Improper purpose — Padfield, Toohey, Thompson

*Padfield v Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food* [1968] AC 997 (House of Lords) [VERIFY] and *R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council* (1981) 151 CLR 170 [VERIFY], and *Thompson v Randwick Corporation* (1950) 81 CLR 87 [VERIFY] together establish that power conferred for one purpose cannot be exercised for another. The applicants' existing case theory anchors the December 2022 banning orders in improper purpose — the May 2021 Germon episode, the alignment of Mr Halls with competing-business operators, the embedded informant (Ms Andrews), the December 2022 Ministerial-announcement window. The continuing-conduct doctrine adds a separate dimension: the persistence of the same conduct after the criminal case has collapsed for no evidence is itself probative of the original improper purpose, because it cannot be re-described as the disinterested pursuit of an investigative goal that has already been adjudicated as evidentially empty.

### 2.5  Misfeasance in public office

*Northern Territory v Mengel* (1995) 185 CLR 307 [VERIFY] and *Sanders v Snell* (1998) 196 CLR 329 [VERIFY] supply the modern formulation. The tort requires that the public officer act knowing they lack the power, or with reckless indifference as to whether they have it, and knowing or recklessly indifferent that harm will result. The cumulative-conduct picture that supports a permanent stay is, by the same body of facts, the picture that supports a misfeasance claim against the individual officers responsible. The longer the post-discontinuance conduct continues, the harder it becomes to characterise as good-faith persistence in lawful authority.

### 2.6  The Commonwealth's "model litigant" obligation

The *Legal Services Directions 2017* (Cth) [VERIFY], issued under section 55ZF of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth), bind the Commonwealth and its agencies (including AGS) to act as a model litigant. That obligation requires honesty, fair dealing, not taking advantage of unrepresented parties, not pursuing meritless claims, and avoiding causing unnecessary delay and expense. The narrowness of the 11 March 2025 AGS letter — its hearsay relay through three layers, its limitation to NDIA, its limitation to a "criminal investigation" stricto sensu, its limitation to Mrs Reid and FFG only — is to be tested against the model-litigant obligation if the underlying information turns out to have been incomplete or misleading. So too is the broader pattern of pursuing administrative banning while concurrent covert investigation continues.

### 2.7  The disability-rights dimension

Australia is party to the United Nations *Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities*. The *Disability Discrimination Act 1992* (Cth) [VERIFY] applies to the conduct of Commonwealth agencies in the exercise of their functions. The *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth) at its objects clause embeds an obligation to support people with disability. Cutting the funded supports of a disabled child for reasons connected with regulatory action against his parents — particularly where the regulatory action is itself the subject of unresolved merits review and a parallel discontinued criminal matter — engages directly with these instruments. The conventional formulation, which counsel will refine, is that the disability of the child does not insulate the agency's conduct from being assessed against ordinary public-law reasonableness; it amplifies the assessment by adding a vulnerable third party whose interests the *NDIS Act 2013* was enacted to protect.

### 2.8  Penalty privilege and the parallel-proceedings problem

If the criminal matter is still on foot, the applicants are exposed to the classic Australian doctrine on stays of civil or regulatory proceedings pending criminal: *McMahon v Gould* (1982) 7 ACLR 246 (NSW SC) [VERIFY]; *Yuill v Spedley Securities Ltd (in liq)* (1992) 8 ACSR 272 [VERIFY]; and the High Court authority on the underlying privilege, *Reid v Howard* (1995) 184 CLR 1 [VERIFY]. These authorities are the doctrinal anchor for the waiver-revocation application that the applicants will be making if verification confirms the position. They overlap with abuse of process but operate independently — penalty privilege is a substantive right of the individual, not merely a discretionary remedy.

## 3.  Applying the framework to this matter

### 3.1  The "punishment without conviction" character

Five years on, the practical situation of the applicants is indistinguishable from the situation of people who have been convicted of serious offences. They have lost their livelihoods. They have lost the corporate vehicles through which they operated. They have lost their reputations. They have been surveilled. They have been stopped at airports. Their associates have been coerced. Their disabled child has had his supports stripped. They have been required at various points to remove themselves from Australia. They have endured years of expense in defending administrative proceedings that, on the applicants' substantive case, are themselves products of the same compromised process.

Yet none of this has been achieved through the criminal process. The one criminal matter that was brought was discontinued for no evidence. The applicants have suffered the full effects of conviction without ever having been convicted, indeed without (in the case of Mr Reid) ever having been charged. The Australian public-law tradition recognises this pattern. It is the foundational concern that animates the proportionality jurisprudence (*Li*), the abuse-of-process jurisprudence (*Jago*; *Walton v Gardiner*) and the executive-misconduct jurisprudence (*Moti*).

### 3.2  The cumulative cross-agency character

A single regulator pursuing a single decision is one thing. A pattern of conduct that has been sustained across the NDIS Commission, the NDIA, the AFP Fraud Fusion Taskforce (through Mr Graydon and Mr Maloney), ABF (the airport intercept), the ATO (the anomalous SG charges), and AGS (the narrow 11 March 2025 representation) is something different. The cumulative-effect doctrine in proportionality, and the totality principle in abuse of process, both require the conduct to be assessed as a whole. The five-agency footprint is itself probative of co-ordination. Co-ordination across agencies pursuing the same family for five years, after the prosecution of the one family member who was charged has collapsed for no evidence, is the kind of pattern that the doctrine was developed to address.

### 3.3  The third-party harm to the disabled child

This is the most morally compelling feature of the matter and the one most likely to land hardest in any forum it is put to. The agencies have not just visited harm on the applicants — adults capable of defending themselves and represented at material times. They have visited collateral harm on a child whose only relationship to the matter is that he is the applicants' son and who has a disability that the very statutory framework being deployed against his parents (the *NDIS Act 2013*) was enacted to protect against.

The relevance of this for the reasonableness question is twofold. As a matter of doctrine, public-law reasonableness measures consequences as well as inputs; collateral harm to a vulnerable third party is squarely within the scope of what proportionality and unreasonableness review consider. As a matter of presentation, the position of the child is the cleanest articulation of "enough" in the case: an innocent child with a disability has been deprived of the supports the *NDIS Act 2013* was passed to ensure he receives, because of regulatory action taken against his parents on allegations that have never been adjudicated in any forum that has admitted evidence and tested it. There is no doctrine of public law that countenances that outcome as proportionate.

### 3.4  Functional exile and the suppression of ordinary life

The use of border-control machinery (the September 2024 ABF stop), the surveillance machinery (the Maloney admission of telecommunications interception), the witness-coercion machinery (Burke; Fanny-Dulloo; Shapiera), and the financial-administrative machinery (the ATO $87,000 anomaly) against people not under any current charge, in pursuit of administrative banning that is itself on merits review, has had the cumulative effect of suppressing the applicants' ordinary life. The fact that the conduct stops just short of arrest does not blunt the doctrine. The cumulative-effect inquiry is precisely concerned with conduct that does not, taken individually, cross the line into the criminal but that, taken together, produces an effect on the person against whom it is brought that the system of justice cannot accept.

### 3.5  Persistence after evidentiary collapse

The single hardest fact for the Commonwealth to defend in any future stay or misfeasance proceeding is the continuation of activity against the family after the CDPP discontinued for no evidence. The "no evidence" finding is not a technicality. It is the prosecutor's express conclusion, after assessing the brief, that the case cannot be proved. To continue investigative activity against the same family after that finding, in the absence of new evidence, requires either (a) new evidence sufficient to satisfy the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth's reasonable-prospects-of-conviction test, or (b) a different theory or different target (Simon rather than Vida; the corporate entities rather than the individuals; intelligence rather than prosecution), or (c) something that the public-law tradition cannot accommodate as legitimate. The applicants are entitled to require the Commonwealth to choose among these and to disclose which.

## 4.  At what point is enough — the answer the doctrine supplies

Different doctrines articulate the line at slightly different points, but they converge.

For a permanent stay of proceedings on abuse-of-process grounds (*Jago*; *Walton v Gardiner*; *Williams v Spautz*; *Moti*), the threshold is reached when the proceedings have become so oppressive, or so coloured by impropriety, that to allow them to continue would bring the administration of justice into disrepute. The convergence of five-year duration, multi-agency co-ordination, documented coercion, documented surveillance, documented misleading or near-misleading representation to the Tribunal, post-discontinuance persistence, and direct collateral harm to a disabled child is well within that threshold.

For a stay of administrative proceedings pending criminal (*McMahon v Gould*; *Reid v Howard*), the threshold is reached when the conduct of the administrative proceedings would imperil the privilege against self-incrimination of the affected person. The verified existence of continuing criminal activity by any Commonwealth agency against any of the applicants is sufficient to engage that doctrine — the carve-outs in the 11 March 2025 AGS letter ensure that the original waiver-supporting representation does not block this argument.

For legal unreasonableness review of a specific administrative decision (*Li* and the line that follows), the threshold is reached when the cumulative impact of the decision and its enforcement consequences is so disproportionate to its lawful object that no rational decision-maker would have reached it on a proper appreciation of the facts. The cumulative-impact picture — the destruction of livelihood, the collateral harm to the child, the cross-agency persistence — is the kind of picture that the *Li* line was designed to bring within review.

For misfeasance in public office (*Mengel*; *Sanders v Snell*), the threshold is reached when a public officer acts with knowledge or reckless indifference to the absence of power and to the harm thereby caused. Post-discontinuance persistence across multiple agencies, against a family that includes a person never charged, is the kind of pattern that founds the claim. Liability runs to the individual officer with the Commonwealth joined vicariously.

For NACC referral, the threshold is "serious or systemic corrupt conduct" by Commonwealth public officials [VERIFY against the *National Anti-Corruption Commission Act 2022* (Cth)]. The pattern in this matter — multi-agency, multi-year, multi-method (coercion, surveillance, border, financial administrative, narrow representation to a tribunal) — is squarely within that threshold.

The doctrines are not alternatives. They are layered. The applicants can pursue them in succession and in combination. The point at which "enough" is reached is not a single line. It is a series of lines, and on the present record this matter has crossed several of them already.

## 4A.  The avenues for a stay — what to ask for, where, and against what

A "stay" in Australian law is not a single remedy. It is a family of remedies, each available in a different forum, each directed at a different kind of state activity. The applicants' situation has at least four distinct things that may need to be stayed, and the avenue is different for each.

### 4A.1  Stay of the current ART administrative proceedings

This is the immediate and most accessible avenue. The Administrative Review Tribunal has power, under the *Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024* (Cth), to control its own proceedings, to make procedural directions, and to stay its merits-review proceedings where to continue them would prejudice the affected person — classically, where parallel criminal exposure would imperil the privilege against self-incrimination. The doctrinal anchors are *McMahon v Gould* (1982) 7 ACLR 246 (NSW SC) [VERIFY] and the High Court authority *Reid v Howard* (1995) 184 CLR 1 [VERIFY] on the underlying privilege.

The application is brought *within the existing ART proceedings* — it does not require any new originating process — by an interlocutory application to revoke the waiver of the stay previously given and to order a stay of the merits review pending resolution of the criminal matter. The foundation has three planks: (a) that the waiver was given specifically and only in reliance on the 11 March 2025 AGS representation; (b) that the representation, on its narrow terms, never excluded the activity now in evidence (the Maloney transcript) or the activity reported by the source; and (c) that to continue the merits review while the criminal exposure is unresolved would imperil penalty privilege. This is the right first step. It is fast, it costs least in solicitor time, and it preserves every later avenue.

### 4A.2  Stay of further enforcement of the banning orders — Federal Court interlocutory

This is the *Adachi* pathway. In *Adachi Disability Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission* [2024] FCA 311 [VERIFY] the Federal Court briefly stayed an NDIS Commission banning order pending review. The procedural vehicle is an application in the Federal Court for an interlocutory injunction restraining the Commission from enforcing or relying upon the banning orders pending the determination of substantive judicial-review proceedings.

The substantive judicial-review proceedings are brought either under the *Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977* (Cth) [VERIFY] or under s 39B of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth) [VERIFY] (the Federal Court's constitutional supervisory jurisdiction). Grounds available on the present record include: improper purpose, denial of procedural fairness, jurisdictional error in the ultra-vires variation of Mrs Reid's banning order, *Li* unreasonableness on the cumulative-conduct picture developed in this memorandum, and (if verification confirms) misleading or near-misleading representation to a tribunal. The application is brought as a fresh proceeding in the Federal Court parallel to the ART merits review; it is the avenue if the ART moves too slowly or if the applicants want a coercive injunction rather than a tribunal direction.

### 4A.3  Permanent stay of any criminal proceedings against Mrs Vida Reid and Mr Simon Reid — the stated objective

The applicants' stated objective is that no criminal proceedings ever proceed against Mrs Vida Reid or against Mr Simon Reid in respect of the conduct underlying the NDIS Commission's December 2022 banning decisions. This objective is, on the present record, achievable; but the application that delivers it cannot be filed today, because the application requires extant proceedings to stay. The jurisdictional foundation is *Jago v District Court (NSW)* (1989) 168 CLR 23 [VERIFY], *Walton v Gardiner* (1993) 177 CLR 378 [VERIFY], *Williams v Spautz* (1992) 174 CLR 509 [VERIFY] and *Moti v The Queen* (2011) 245 CLR 456 [VERIFY] — and that jurisdiction attaches only to proceedings on foot in a court of criminal jurisdiction.

The strategy therefore is a three-component approach designed both to maximise the prospects of a permanent stay if charges are ever laid, and to deter the laying of charges in the first place.

**4A.3.1  Mrs Vida Reid — the position is exceptionally strong.** The CDPP discontinued all four charges against Mrs Reid on 28 February 2024 for no evidence. That is the highest threshold for discontinuance under the Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth (cl 2.10 *et seq*) [VERIFY against the current version of the Prosecution Policy]. To re-lay charges against her in respect of the same or overlapping conduct, the CDPP would need to be satisfied that the reasonable-prospects-of-conviction test and the public-interest test are now met where they were not previously. *Re-prosecution after discontinuance for no evidence* is the precise fact pattern that the *Williams v Spautz* / *Walton v Gardiner* abuse-of-process jurisdiction was designed to address. If charges are ever re-laid against Mrs Reid, the application for permanent stay is brought immediately on filing of the charges, in the court in which they are laid, on these grounds: (a) abuse of process by re-prosecution after no-evidence discontinuance without genuinely new evidence; (b) the totality picture in section 1 of this memorandum, including the post-discontinuance investigator-conduct pattern; (c) executive misconduct in the *Moti* sense, including the Maloney admissions of telecommunications interception and the coercion of Ms Fanny-Dulloo ("*it's your neck or her neck*"); (d) the cumulative oppression of five years of multi-agency conduct, including the collateral harm to the applicants' disabled child; (e) the model-litigant breach inherent in any prosecution founded on a brief that includes coerced witness statements or interception material outside proper authority.

The doctrine of *autrefois acquit* does not strictly attach to discontinuance (which is not an acquittal). The policy underlying it does. Counsel will frame the abuse-of-process argument as engaging the same policy concerns that *autrefois acquit* protects: finality, the limits of the State's right to pursue an individual repeatedly on the same conduct, and the disproportionate burden of repeated prosecution.

**4A.3.2  Mr Simon Reid — the position is different but converges to the same outcome.** Mr Reid has never been charged. He cannot bring an abuse-of-process application based on re-prosecution because there has been no first prosecution. But the same record produces a different and equally strong argument: that any prosecution brought against Mr Reid on the same body of allegations, against the background of (i) Mrs Reid's no-evidence discontinuance on overlapping conduct, (ii) the documented coercion of witnesses against the family generally (Burke, Fanny-Dulloo, Shapiera), (iii) the documented telecommunications interception, and (iv) the cumulative-conduct picture in section 1 — would be an abuse of process on the *Jago* and *Moti* analyses. The argument is that the brief against him is the residue of an investigation that has already produced one no-evidence outcome; that the investigative methods underlying it are demonstrably tainted; and that the prosecution is the product not of legitimate criminal-justice purposes but of the continuing improper purpose that has animated the regulatory conduct since 2021. If charges are ever laid against Mr Reid, the permanent-stay application is brought on filing, in the court in which the charges are laid, on those grounds.

**4A.3.3  The three components of the strategy now.**

*Component one — make the laying of charges as unattractive to the Commonwealth as possible.* The verification letter to AGS, the FOI requests, the APP-12 requests, the CDPP inquiry and (in due course) the NACC referral collectively force the Commonwealth to confront the fact that the cumulative-conduct record exists and is being documented. A prosecutor considering whether to lay charges against either applicant faces, at the point of decision, the prospect that the permanent-stay application will be brought on day one, that the application will succeed, and that the failure will be publicly attributed to multi-agency misconduct documented across five years. This is itself a deterrent. The decision to lay charges is made under the Prosecution Policy by reference to public-interest factors; the public-interest assessment is degraded for each item in the record that makes the matter look like persecution rather than prosecution.

*Component two — build the file for the permanent stay now.* Every step the applicants take in the next several weeks should be designed with the permanent-stay application in mind. The Karen / Halls audio recording needs Karen's identification, dating, and the underlying email recovered by FOI. The Maloney transcript needs to be supplemented with any available audio, with the Fanny-Dulloo first-person witness statement, and with corroboration of the telecommunications-interception admission via Privacy Act APP-12 access requests. The Graydon $200,000 bribery offer to Shapiera needs the underlying recording and Mr Shapiera's witness statement preserved against the possibility that he is unavailable later (he is 66 and disabled — adverse-witness preservation rules apply [VERIFY against the Uniform Evidence Act provisions in the relevant jurisdiction]). The disabled child's NDIS-funding cut needs the decision letter, the relevant plan documents, the providers' refusals (in writing where available), and witness statements from both applicants on the care they have provided in the absence of funded supports. This is paralegal-rate work — not solicitor-rate work — but it is the foundation of the eventual stay.

*Component three — pre-emptive declaratory or injunctive relief in the Federal Court where doctrinally available.* The Federal Court has jurisdiction (under s 39B of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth) [VERIFY] and the broader equitable jurisdiction) to grant declaratory relief and injunctions in appropriate cases against threatened or anticipated improper executive conduct. This pathway is narrower and harder than the post-charging permanent stay; it is not the primary vehicle. It becomes available if and when the verification process produces sufficient evidence of an imminent decision to charge — at which point the applicants can apply for declaratory relief that any such prosecution would be an abuse of process, and for an injunction restraining the relevant officers from referring the matter to the CDPP for charging. This is a senior-counsel decision and should not be commenced until the foundation in components one and two is firmly built.

**4A.3.4  Two further protections.** The applicants should also consider (a) a written application by their solicitors to the CDPP under the Prosecution Policy seeking confirmation that no decision to commence proceedings is contemplated, and that any future decision will be communicated to them in advance with a reasonable opportunity to make representations under cl 5.10–5.13 of the Prosecution Policy [VERIFY]; and (b) a written submission to the CDPP, in the event that a brief of evidence is referred, opposing prosecution on the public-interest grounds in the Prosecution Policy. Both are inexpensive, both build the record, and both are independently disclosable.

**The summary in a sentence.** The permanent stay of criminal proceedings against Mrs Vida Reid and Mr Simon Reid cannot be filed today because there are no proceedings to stay; but the record on which any such permanent stay would be granted is being assembled now, the Commonwealth is being put on notice now that the application will be brought immediately on any filing, and the verification, FOI, APP-12 and CDPP-engagement steps are designed to deter the laying of charges in the first place. The intended end-state is that no charges are ever laid; the contingency plan is that if they are, the permanent-stay application is on file in the criminal court within days.

### 4A.4  Injunctive relief against continuing executive conduct — Federal Court / Supreme Court

For the *investigation* itself — the surveillance, the telecommunications interception, the airport stops, the witness intimidation, the cross-agency activity reported by the source — the remedy is not a "stay" in the strict sense. It is injunctive relief: an order restraining the Commonwealth from engaging in identified conduct against the applicants. This is brought in the Federal Court (or in the relevant state Supreme Court) by originating process, ordinarily in the same proceeding as any misfeasance or damages claim. The grounds are abuse of process in its civil sense (*Williams v Spautz* analogues), excess of statutory power (the *Padfield* / *Toohey* line) and (if applicable) breach of the *Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979* (Cth) [VERIFY] in respect of the documented interception.

This pathway is later-stage and resource-intensive. It is the right vehicle if and when the applicants want to convert the misfeasance and unlawful-conduct claims into proceedings rather than oversight referrals. It is sensible to defer commencement of this pathway until the immediate ART stay and any Federal Court banning-order review are bedded down, and until senior counsel has assessed the strength of the evidentiary record.

### 4A.5  The recommended sequence

Sequencing matters because each step generates documents that the next step relies on.

*Day 0 (today, 27 May 2026):* the source file note recording what was told and by whom, and Mrs Reid's contemporaneous record of unimpeded re-entry from Bali. Costs nothing. Foundational.

*Week 1:* the verification letter from the lawyers to AGS, drafted in the six-question form developed in the working notes (across all Commonwealth agencies, all applicants and entities, criminal vs intelligence/surveillance, brief-of-evidence status, and what enquiries AGS / the Commission actually made before signing the 11 March 2025 letter). Cc the Tribunal. The holding letter to the Member's chambers, flagging that the procedural posture may shift. One to two hours of solicitor time, total.

*Weeks 2–4:* parallel verification — FOI requests to NDIS Commission and NDIA for any post-11 March 2025 investigative activity concerning the applicants; Privacy Act APP-12 access requests for personal information held about Vida and Simon; an inquiry to the CDPP. These can be drafted at paralegal rates and lodged by the applicants directly. They are independently disclosable and force the Commonwealth either to deny the activity or to refuse access — both of which are useful outcomes.

*Weeks 4–6 (or earlier if AGS's response demands it):* the application to the ART to revoke the waiver of stay and to stay the merits review. This is the immediate stay avenue. Senior solicitor time, possibly counsel for the application itself.

*Months 2–3 (in parallel):* assessment by senior counsel of whether the *Adachi*-style Federal Court interlocutory application for stay of further enforcement of the banning orders should be commenced concurrently, or held in reserve. The answer depends on the response from AGS and on the verification picture that emerges from the FOI and APP-12 process.

*Months 6+:* development of the broader injunctive and misfeasance proceeding, and the NACC / Ombudsman / IGIS / Privacy Commissioner referrals. These are not stay applications in the strict sense; they are the structural response to the matter that the reasonableness analysis identifies. They run on a longer timescale and are best built on the documentary foundation that the earlier steps generate.

The single most important procedural point is that *the question "what is the avenue?" has different answers for different things, and the avenues are layered, not alternative.* The applicants are not choosing between ART stay, Federal Court injunction and permanent stay of criminal proceedings. They are sequencing them. The ART stay protects the merits review now. The Federal Court interlocutory protects against enforcement of the banning orders now. The permanent stay of criminal proceedings sits in reserve for the day (which may never come) on which charges are laid. The injunctive proceeding against continuing executive conduct is the medium-term vehicle for converting the reasonableness analysis into coercive remedy.

## 5.  Forums and pathways

The applicants do not need to choose a single forum. The pathways available are:

The Administrative Review Tribunal — to seek to revoke the waiver of the stay, to seek a fresh stay pending resolution of the criminal matter, and (on the merits) to seek to set aside the banning orders on the existing grounds (no individualised assessment for Mr Reid; ultra-vires variation for Mrs Reid; procedural-fairness failure on service; improper purpose). The reasonableness analysis in this memorandum supplies an additional ground — that the cumulative conduct is itself an exercise of regulatory power that fails *Li* and that the Tribunal, standing in the shoes of the primary decision-maker under *Shi v MARA* and *Frugtniet*, must reach a different decision.

The Federal Court — for any judicial-review application going to jurisdictional error, for any abuse-of-process / permanent-stay application going to a verified continuing criminal matter, for any misfeasance proceeding against the individual officers with the Commonwealth joined, and for any *Adachi*-style interlocutory stay of further enforcement of the banning orders pending review.

The National Anti-Corruption Commission — for referral of the multi-agency conduct pattern as serious or systemic corrupt conduct.

The Commonwealth Ombudsman — for the law-enforcement-administrative conduct pattern.

The Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security — for the telecommunications-interception conduct documented in the Maloney transcript.

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner — for the Privacy Act breach pathway (already noted in the master deep dive at section 0.11G).

The Department of Employment and Workplace Relations — for the ES-22-0090 Workforce Australia contract pathway.

The Australian Taxation Office's internal complaints / Inspector-General of Taxation — for the $87,000 anomalous-charges pathway.

The applicants have, on the present record, an arguable case in every one of these forums. Sequencing them sensibly — beginning with the verification letter to AGS and the contemporaneous file note, then the ART waiver-revocation application, then the broader filings as the verification picture develops — is a matter for senior counsel.

## 6.  Conclusion

The reasonableness question the applicants ask is the right question, and the doctrine supplies an answer. Five years of continuing multi-agency activity, after a criminal case discontinued for no evidence, against a family that includes a person never charged and a disabled child whose statutory supports were stripped as collateral damage, is not within any defensible bandwidth of legitimate regulatory or prosecutorial activity. The conduct has crossed the line that *Jago*, *Walton v Gardiner*, *Williams v Spautz*, *Moti*, *Li*, *Mengel* and the model-litigant directions collectively define. The applicants are entitled to ask the Tribunal, the Federal Court, the NACC and the other oversight bodies to say so.

What remains is sequencing and proof. The file note dated 27 May 2026 recording the source debrief, the verification letter to AGS in the terms set out in the working notes of the same date, and the contemporaneous record of Mrs Reid's unimpeded return from Bali are the immediate building blocks. The substantive applications can follow in the order senior counsel directs.

The single sentence the applicants are entitled to ask of any of these forums is the one with which this memorandum opens: at what point is enough?

The answer, on the doctrine, is that several of the points at which "enough" is constituted have already been passed.
