Relevant legislation  
12. Section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act gives the Commissioner (or her delegate) the power to 
‘make an order (a banning order) prohibiting or restricting specified activities by an NDIS 
provider, or by a person who was an NDIS provider, if the Commissioner has revoked the 
registration of the person as a registered NDIS provider.’ 
13. Under section 73ZN(3) of the NDIS Act, a banning order may apply generally or be of limited 
application, and may be permanent or for a specified period. 
14. Section 73ZN(7) of the NDIS Act states that the Commissioner may only make a banning order 
against a person after giving the person an opportunity to make submissions to the 
Commissioner on the matter. 
15. Section 73ZN(8)(b) of the NDIS Act states that section 73ZN(7) does not apply if the 
Commissioner’s grounds for making the banning order are, or include, that the Commissioner 
has revoked the registration of the person as a registered NDIS provider.  
16. Section 73ZN(9) sets out the various requirements surrounding any banning order made as 
follows: 
'The Commissioner must: 
                     (a)  give the banning order to the person against whom the order is made; and 
                     (b)  include in the banning order a statement of reasons for the order; and 
                     (c)  if the banning order is against a person who is employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS 
provider or is a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider—notify the NDIS provider of 
the order as soon as possible.’

Findings and Reasons 
34. I have considered the original decision made on 20 December 2022.  
35. I am of the view that the original decision was the correct and preferable decision to be made, 
however, I disagree with the reasoning for the original decision.  
36. Specifically, the aspects of the original decision that I disagree with are the following: 
a. 
the original decision adopted the same reasons cited for revoking FFG’s registration as 
a registered NDIS provider on 20 December 2022. As a separate administrative 
decision, there should have been distinct or independent reasons for the banning order 
being issued to FFG; and 
b. 
the original decision was grounded in a broader range of considerations than was 
necessary (given the point I have made above at paragraph 36(a)). In my view, the 
decision should have been confined to a narrower and more probative set of material, 
specifically the evidence that related to FFG’s involvement in perpetrating fraud against 
the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS).  
37. In summary, my view is that: 
a. 
by merely reiterating the reasoning for revoking the registration of FFG, the original 
decision failed to articulate the specific need or reason(s) for imposing a banning order 
upon FFG (in addition to revoking the entity’s registration as a registered NDIS 
provider). Although section 73ZN(1)(a) did allow the original decision-maker to impose 
a banning order as he had previously ‘revoked the registration of [FFG] as a registered 
NDIS provider’, the use of this statutory power is discretionary. The Commissioner (or 
her delegates) are not required to ban each NDIS provider whose registration has been 
revoked. Rather, a banning order (made under section 73ZN(1)(a)) should, in principle, 
only be used if there is some further or compelling reason for such an order to be made 
given an NDIS provider will already be constrained from engaging in the NDIS as a result 
of the revocation of its registration. In other words, there should be some additional 
value, protection or benefit that a banning order provides - that is not afforded by the 
revocation of an NDIS provider’s registration – to warrant the imposition of a banning 
order.  
b. 
I acknowledge the original decision-maker had a wide breadth of material before him. 
In my view, however, the evidence relating to FFG’s alleged involvement in fraudulent 
activity (particularly given the quality and volume of such evidence) should have 
constituted the fundamental reason for banning FFG and prohibiting it from having 
access to NDIS participants and the broader disability care sector (notwithstanding that 
there may be others reasons to support the banning order too).  
38. In the paragraphs below, I set out my reasons for reaching this decision.

Conclusion/s in relation to relevant materials  
41. In light of the above information, I am satisfied, on the balance of probabilities, that FFG 
engaged in fraudulent activity, or more specifically, that it was used as a vehicle through which 
fraud was perpetrated against the NDIS by its key personnel.  
42. I give significant weight to the complaints submitted to the NDIS Commission in relation to Ms 
Goodwin and Ms Clulow, the NDIA’s SoFM and the sworn statement of Ms Clulow as these 
distinct pieces of evidence all corroborate the same allegation/s of FFG having been involved in 
defrauding the NDIS over a substantial period of time. The evidence presents a uniform and 
egregious account of FFG’s systems being used, or FFG’s key personnel systemically submitting 
false claims for payments against the NDIS plans of NDIS participants.  
43. I note FFG has not provided any evidence to negate the fraud alleged above.

circumstances where the NDIS Commission has conducted an independent investigation in 
accordance with the legislation governing its operations (i.e. the NDIS Act and NDIS Rules) and

the evidence has been gathered and scrutinised by the agency specifically charged with

the allegations against FFG have been made by various individuals and entities who

56. In relation to the submissions made by FFG at paragraphs 53(b), (c) and (d) above, I gave

provide supports or services (i.e. depletion of their NDIS funding) warrants the NDIS

Commissioner’s own initiative or upon application by FFG.  
59. In relation to the submission made by FFG at paragraphs 53(g) and (h) above, I have given these

legislative provisions regarding the use of suspensions under section 73N of the NDIS Act. 
Firstly, if the NDIS commission were to suspend the registration of FFG instead of making a 
banning order against FFG, it would not provide a sufficient level of safeguarding to NDIS 
participants. A suspension would only prohibit FFG from providing supports and services for 
which it must be registered to deliver to NDIS participants. Meaning, FFG could continue to 
operate as an unregistered NDIS provider. Secondly, the particular use of the suspension power 
that is contended for by FFG is not appropriate. A suspension under section 73N is designed to 
be a temporary action (only in effect for 30 days) to address immediate or significant concerns 
held in relation to an NDIS provider. It is not intended to be used repeatedly or for a substantial 
period of time in relation to the same NDIS provider. If a suspension were to be consecutively 
issued, it would signal that a more permanent or enduring form of regulatory action is, in fact, 
needed.

Section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act – power to issue a banning order where the registration of a 
registered NDIS provider has been revoked 
62. The banning order issued to FFG was made pursuant to section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act.  
63. The statutory test for issuing a banning order under this provision is singular. The NDIS 
Commissioner (or her delegate) must have revoked the registration of a person as a registered 
NDIS provider. Provided that process of revocation had occurred, it was open to the original 
decision-maker to impose a banning order.  
64. I have been provided with a copy of the decision to revoke the registration of FFG on 20 
December 2022. I am, therefore, satisfied that it was open to the original decision-maker to 
issue a banning order to FFG.  
65. However, as mentioned above (at paragraphs 36-37) the original decision-maker should have 
clearly articulated the reason(s) as to why a banning order was warranted in the circumstances. 
The mere fact that section 73ZN(1)(a) conferred upon the original decision-maker the power to 
use this statutory tool, did not suffice or justify the use of it.  
66. Despite the original decision-maker’s omission in this regard, in my view there are compelling 
reasons to support or warrant the issuance of a banning order to FFG. Those reasons are that: 
a. 
FFG was, in effect, used by the key personnel of the company as a vehicle to perpetrate 
the alleged fraud against the NDIS. In my view, where an entity has facilitated the 
commission of fraud, it is not appropriate for it to remain engaged in the NDIS sector 
given the serious risk it poses to NDIS participants (i.e. the depletion of their NDIS 
funding);  
b. 
The revocation of FFG’s registration does not provide a complete or wholesale level of 
safeguarding to NDIS participants given that FFG remains capable of providing supports 
and services to NDIS participants for which registration is not a requirement. The 
classes of supports and services that can be delivered by an unregistered NDIS provider 
are vast and would enable FFG to have access to the NDIS plans of thousands of NDIS 
participants.  
c. 
I acknowledge the making of a banning order in respect of FFG will significantly impact 
the operations of the business, and I have taken this into account. However, I consider 


===== PAGE 20 =====

 
 
that the competing interest of ensuring NDIS participants, who may not be able to fully 
protect their own interests and who have a statutory right to safe services and 
protection from violence, abuse and exploitation, is paramount and takes precedence 
over the detriment FFG may experience if banned. 
67. Further, I agree with the reasoning expressed by the original decision-maker in paragraphs 27 – 
33 of the original decision in so far that he explained the objects of the NDIS Act (contained in 
section 3)  as well as section 4(17)(b) of the NDIS Act are factors which also support the 
imposition of a banning order against FFG.  
68. Therefore, I am satisfied that the making of a banning order against FFG is the correct and 
preferable decision in the circumstances.  
The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy  
69. The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy contemplates that a banning order can be ‘issued 
pending the determination of [criminal] charges’ if, in the circumstances, the information and 
evidence obtained by the NDIS Commission supports a view that a banning order is warranted 
and appropriate.   
70. For the reasons already noted above (at paragraphs 41 and 45), I consider a banning order 
against FFG to be warranted and appropriate.  
Requirement to take proportionate and risk responsive action under NDIS Act 
71. The NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk 
responsive and proportionate manner (per section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act).  
72. The NDIS Commission’s Banning Order Policy notes that a banning order can have a significant 
impact on the recipient and on people with disability, and as such, it will be reserved for 
circumstances where it is needed to protect people with disability from the risk of being 
exposed to conduct that falls well short of acceptable standards. However, a banning order 
should not be for a longer period than is considered necessary.  
73. Having regard to the criminal proceedings pending against Mrs Reid (which, as noted above, 
implicate FFG in fraudulent activity), I consider that a banning order for a period of 3 years is a 
risk responsive and proportionate measure as it corresponds with the amount of time it is 
reasonably expected to take for the criminal proceedings to be finally determined by the court. 
Upon determination of those proceedings, the banning order against FFG can be revisited and 
consideration given to whether it should be varied any further (or revoked entirely). I note a 
variation or revocation of the banning order could also take place prior to the determination of 
the court proceedings if appropriate or necessary. 
74. In the meantime, the banning order will protect NDIS participants from the risk of having 
fraudulent claims submitted against their NDIS plans and their funding for disability supports or 
services being depleted. 
75. The banning order will, therefore, remain in place until 12:00am on 21 December 2025 
(AWST).
