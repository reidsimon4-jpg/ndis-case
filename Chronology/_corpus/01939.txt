 The Reids: independent strategy re-review
 6 September 2026 | Simon Reid, Vida Reid and Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd


 Executive opinion
 The best-supported course is a combined legality-and-evidence strategy. The current package is
 materially better than its earlier versions, but I would not adopt the September proposal to suspend all
 merits preparation until Federal Court proceedings end. Preserve a short, targeted legal phase where it
 will actually reduce the case, while preparing an applicant-specific factual answer and an express schedule of
 historical and current relief.

 This is an AI-assisted research opinion for review by an Australian lawyer, not an opinion issued by retained
 counsel. It does not create legal professional privilege, predict the outcome, or authorise filing. Simon's stated
 objectives are principle, historical correction and compensation, not returning to NDIS work. That changes the
 desired remedies; it does not remove the need to answer the evidence or establish compensable loss.

 What this re-check changes:

  - The three latest FINAL responses already repair several defects criticised in earlier drafts. Those criticisms
    should not be repeated as if still current. Remaining problems concern sequencing, dates and a small
    number of references, not wholesale invalidity of the documents.

  - Sunflower remains a substantial argument about the permitted wording and rational scope of a ban. It does
    not guarantee cancellation, immunise consultancy, or displace current statutory powers. Khalil and the
    adverse merits authorities must be addressed openly.

  - The July reasons favour an efficient joint hearing. They do not determine the disputed legality questions or
    prove bias. A renewed blanket separation or delay request is therefore a weak default.

  - There is useful favourable primary evidence, including a Commission-obtained participant statement. It
    needs transaction-level corroboration; it is not a universal answer to allegedly unsupported claims.

  - The new ATO releases substantiate a company garnishee and a payment-allocation issue. They do not
    substantiate a personal director penalty notice or a coordinated interagency campaign.

  - Immediate attention belongs to FOI review/status dates and verifying the actual Tribunal timetable. The
     AGS minute located is a proposal, not an adopted order.

 Bottom line: keep the legality challenge, rebuild the evidentiary presentation, remove unsupported
 predictions and blanket procedural demands, and commission a distinct compensation/limitation assessment
 now. The report identifies what would justify a different route rather than pretending one route is always best.

 Research basis: three independent review lanes, primary-record spot checks by the coordinating reviewer,
 current official law and government sources, and fresh correspondence searches. Coverage and unresolved
 gaps are stated at the end. The live Supabase database did not respond to a read-only query or its bounded
 retry; no fresh database result is claimed.

 3. The authorities: useful law and adverse law
 Sunflower is important, but its actual reasoning matters
 Sunflower (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 supports scrutiny of specified activities, clarity, statutory connection and
 rational protection against identified risks. Paragraph 134 expressly permits a clearly specified activity that
 only indirectly involves service provision, provided the person engages in that activity and the required
 connection exists. It is not a blanket exception for finance, registration assistance, labour hire or consultancy.
 Paragraphs 179-191 support separate analysis of activity, personal characteristics, risk and proportionate
 restriction. Sunflower, paras 125-142 and 179-191

 The case also rejects important parts of a maximalist challenge: internal review can cure certain original
 defects, and a variation may preserve parts of the original order while changing other parts prospectively.
 Sunflower therefore helps frame a precise challenge to these instruments; it does not establish that changing
 statutory grounds on review is always forbidden. Sunflower, paras 217-227

 The subsequent costs judgment provides a useful reality check. The individual succeeded on form and
 unreasonableness, but marginal unsuccessful grounds produced a 20% reduction in recoverable costs. The
 company recovered no costs after undertakings removed the practical dispute and further litigation
 unnecessarily prolonged it. This supports disciplined ground selection, not predictions of a guaranteed
 settlement. No appeal was located in the searches, but no certified docket-negative conclusion is offered.
 Sunflower (Costs) [2026] FCA 261, paras 5-8 and 13-16

 Khalil: current law cannot be wished away
 Khalil [2025] HCA 33, a unanimous High Court decision, confirms that merits review ordinarily uses current
 law and material, including powers accrued since the original decision, subject to the particular statute and
 transitional provisions. Its rejection of contrary obiter in Frugtniet makes an undifferentiated old-law-only case
 untenable. It does not retrospectively make historical conduct unlawful. Its implication here is that defects in a
 2022/2023 instrument and the power to impose a properly framed protective decision now are different
 questions. Khalil, paras 15-26

 Johnston and Abdullah must be distinguished, not ignored
 Johnston [2024] AATA 3438 is a banning-order merits decision. It rejects the argument that absence of
 prescribed suitability rules disables the statutory power. Formal convictions/director-disqualification findings
 were material. Paragraph 28 records that specification of activities was not contested; its affirmation of broad
 wording therefore does not answer the later Sunflower construction holding. Johnston, paras 14-17 and 27-28

 Abdullah [2026] ARTA 743 affirmed a ten-year ban after serious corroborated findings. The Tribunal rejected
 some weak allegations, yet the remaining evidence still justified protection. It neither cites nor overrules
 Sunflower. It warns against treating one disproved allegation, discontinued charges, or procedural error as
 dispositive of the entire suitability case. Do not promote respondent submissions at paragraphs 22-29 into
 holdings or invent a criminal/heightened evidentiary standard. Abdullah, paras 63, 68-110

 4. Define the legal question and the time period
 What exactly was allegedly beyond power?
 Replace the slogan that the Commission changed the law with four questions: which statutory power existed;
 what activity the instrument prohibited; what process was afforded; and from when each part operated. An
 individual administrative order changes obligations under law; it does not amend Parliament's statute. A later
 Act's application also requires its commencement and transitional provisions to be examined, not merely its
 date.

 The current NDIS Act is compilation 26, dated 27 August 2026. The reviewed ss 73ZN/73ZO are unchanged
 from the April text compared by the law reviewer. Former provider/personnel status and continuation
 provisions mean leaving Australia or ceasing services does not automatically end jurisdiction or a ban. The
 anticipatory s 73ZN(2A) has its own never-provider/personnel conditions. Sections 73ZN(2B)-(2C) now
 expressly cover specified categories including facilitation and registration-assistance services; the respondent
 must identify the category, predicate and activity relied on, rather than treating the provision as universal.
 NDIS Act, ss 73ZN-73ZO

 Historical liability is a separate question
 For each alleged Dream Big or other post-ban act, require the person, act, date, operative original/varied
 order, precise prohibition, evidence of involvement, and legal consequence alleged. Is the conduct said to be
 an actual contravention, evidence of present risk, or both? Later broader wording cannot simply be projected
 backwards to label an earlier lawful act a breach. Conversely, narrowing a later order does not automatically
 legalise an actual earlier breach. Sunflower, paras 223-227

 The March 2023 notice in T65 proposes the broad no-involvement wording and offers a response opportunity,
 including an extension. A blanket assertion that no opportunity was ever given is contradicted by that
 document. The viable questions are receipt, adequacy of disclosed adverse material, the specific change put,
 and whether the actual decision went beyond that opportunity. T65 notice, Tribunal pp 1346-1347 and
 following

 There is no automatic December 2022 clean slate
 The July reasons identify the individual review variations as effective on 22 April 2023. ART s 108 ordinarily
 ties a substituted/varied decision to the effective time of the reviewable decision, unless otherwise ordered. It
 does not, without further analysis, erase everything from December 2022. Expressly identify the original and
 review instruments and seek the intended temporal outcome; counsel must assess the Tribunal's ability to
 reach the earlier period. The difference can matter to historical breach allegations, register entries and
 damages. Severance decision, paras 7-10; ART Act, ss 105 and 108; Sunflower, paras 65-76, 141 and
 225-226

 A change/revocation under s 73ZO during the same pending review is not an uncontrolled alternative route.
 ART s 31 requires the statutory consent/order or remittal mechanism. Use a coordinated proposal for the
 particular decision, not simultaneous inconsistent applications. ART Act, ss 31 and 85

 6. Vida, FFG and the favourable evidence
 Vida: strong individualisation, no automatic exoneration
 The switch from a contravention ground to suitability, discontinued criminal charges and the scope of the
 varied instrument deserve explicit treatment. But discontinuance is not an acquittal on every factual
 proposition and does not prevent a protective merits decision supported by evidence. The relevant statutory
 predicate is not necessarily a proved criminal offence. Ask which allegations remain, whose evidence
 supports them, and what present risk each establishes. Severance decision, paras 5-8 and 16; NDIS Act, s
 73ZN

 Keep Vida's evidence and instructions distinct from Simon's. Some explanations about control, accounts,
 appointment dates or responsibility may assist one and disadvantage the other. A lawyer must assess
 joint-representation conflicts before common instructions or admissions. Interpreter arrangements, accessible
 evidence and a tailored self-incrimination protocol should facilitate participation, not serve as an indefinite
 substitute for it.

 FFG: separate decision, separate legal person
 The reasons describe a three-year company ban ending in December 2025, unlike the individual five-year
 wording. Establish the actual instrument and current register record. An expired ban may still have historical
 consequences; it does not automatically entitle the company to declarations, damages or deletion of its
 history. Frame the remaining practical utility. Do not assume the cancellation of registration itself is also under
 review merely because it supplied a banning-order ground. Severance decision, para 11; Sunflower, paras
 54-60

 Company loss belongs to the company unless another legal basis establishes personal loss. Authority to
 instruct, capacity, solvency and potential external administration need separate checks. A claimant should not
 claim the same lost business income both personally and corporately.

 T19 is genuinely useful - and bounded
 The six-page signed statement of Jonathan Charles Shapiera, obtained in the Commission investigation,
 describes delivered assistance, satisfaction, support agreements and travel discussions. It also records
 uncertainties about billing categories and which provider supplied some services. Its supportive content
 should be put alongside the Commission evidence summary, whose short row emphasises agreements and
 Simon's role rather than those benefits. That is a concrete completeness issue, not proof of deliberate
 suppression. T19, Tribunal pp 630-635; T44, Tribunal p 1032

 The statement is not an invoice audit. Satisfaction does not establish that each claimed amount/category was

 This has more practical value than another general statement that the investigation was unfair.

 7. How to put legality first without stopping the case
 A proposed sequence for counsel to settle
 Step 1 - identify the actual case. Seek a short respondent schedule identifying each operative instrument,
 statutory ground, activity and period; actual alleged contraventions versus suitability inferences; the material
 relied on; and the precise current order sought. Request missing relied-on documents and identification of any
 omitted/replaced bundle material. Tie production to issues, not an unbounded investigation into every agency
 contact.

 Step 2 - identify the useful legal question. Ask whether the disputed wording is authorised, and whether
 later scope can govern identified earlier conduct, on certified instruments and agreed or expressly assumed
 facts. Require the party seeking a separate phase to show which factual disputes/witnesses would disappear.
 Do not wait for every FOI or confidentiality dispute if it cannot affect that question.

 Step 3 - proceed with unaffected evidence preparation. Preserve the legal objection while compiling
 transaction records and witness topics. Keep participant witnesses in one coordinated hearing where
 possible. If a discrete law-first phase is refused, seek an opening legal argument at the final hearing and
 applicant-specific findings. This responds to the reasons' efficiency concerns and uses their
 subject-to-relevance limitation. Severance decision, paras 14 and 29-41

 Step 4 - specify outcomes by applicant and period. Distinguish no order, limited substituted order,
 temporal relief and historical/register consequences. A narrower current order is not automatically a
 concession that the original was valid or that compensation is waived. Any actual settlement release must be
 separately negotiated.

 Can a court hear the law before the merits?
 Yes, potentially. A genuine question arising in the ART proceeding can be referred under s 185 with
 Presidential agreement, but a party cannot demand a reference. A focused question with stable facts is a
 better candidate than an abstract request to decide all legality issues. The statute controls what can proceed
 while a referred question remains unresolved; it is not a general licence to stop every preparatory task. ART
 Act, s 185

 A Federal Court original-jurisdiction challenge under s 39B, or an available ADJR route, is also possible in
 principle before the ART merits hearing. It needs a properly identified decision, legal ground, standing, live
 utility and considered relief. A historical original-instrument challenge may have utility that present merits
 substitution cannot adequately resolve; this is not limited to cases where the Tribunal refuses to hear law. But
 delay, alternative remedies, fragmentation, costs and any limitation/extension issues must be assessed
 before filing. Federal Court practice note; Federal Court guide

 Decision trigger: if the respondent accepts a precise lawful form and abandons historical breach allegations,
 the value of a separate construction contest may shrink. If it insists on disputed old wording with concrete
 continuing consequences, a focused court/reference opinion becomes more valuable. Do not announce
 proceedings merely to obtain delay.

 8. Fair process, confidentiality and self-incrimination
 Resolve the actual non-disclosure application
 The 14 August email says the respondent's 7 May application remains outstanding. Identify the two
 documents, their status, the precise order sought and what reliance is proposed. Legal professional privilege,
 public-interest non-disclosure and restrictions on publication/disclosure are distinct questions. A blanket
 demand for privileged legal advice is not a sound substitute for the actual application and statutory procedure.
 Land email; ART Act, ss 30, 70 and 86

 Section 86 is a general power concerning variation/revocation of Tribunal orders, not a confidentiality-only
 provision. Section 70 addresses publication/disclosure restrictions; it does not itself create criminal use
 immunity. Ask for the least restrictive arrangement that permits a fair response, including an explanation of
 how any material not disclosed to an applicant will be treated. The relevant application and actual orders must

 Protect rights without promising blanket immunity
 The severance reasons recognise Vida's privilege against self-incrimination and say the ART Act does not
 abrogate it. That is useful but does not automatically protect every volunteered affidavit, every pre-existing
 document, the company, or all later interagency uses. Counsel should identify the real risk and assert the
 privilege in relation to a particular question or production demand, seeking a ruling and appropriate procedural
 handling. Do not equate non-publication, confidentiality, without-prejudice communications and privilege.
 Severance decision, para 39(i)

 The March 2025 AGS correspondence relayed NDIA advice about no current criminal investigation
 concerning Vida or FFG. It was not a CDPP assurance, an all-agency guarantee, a Simon-specific clearance

 9. Compensation, other forums and company status
 Compensation needs its own case
 A successful legality challenge does not itself establish damages. Separate the act, responsible entity/officer,
 legal duty or cause of action, mental element if required, counterfactual, causation and loss. Misfeasance is
 not established merely by an unlawful decision and financial damage: Sanders v Snell illustrates the
 additional knowledge/intent questions and danger of equating unfairness with dishonest abuse. The current
 evidence does not establish those elements. Preserve potentially relevant records without alleging conspiracy
 as fact. Sanders v Snell [1998] HCA 64, paras 38-49

 Build a dated loss model distinguishing NDIA payment/compliance action, Commission registration/banning
 action, criminal-process effects, tax collection and independent business conditions. Loss predating a
 particular banning decision cannot simply be attributed to that later decision. Separate Simon's, Vida's and
 each company's losses; document mitigation, tax treatment and alternative causes. Simon's current lack of
 intention to return is consistent with historical-loss claims, but must be reconciled with any claim for future
 NDIS earnings.

 Commission a limitation and defendant assessment now; do not assume ART or FOI processes stop other
 time limits. A damages proceeding in a competent court may be appropriate after its cause and jurisdiction
 are established. A State Supreme Court is not an appeal court from the ART. High Court original jurisdiction is
 not the practical first choice absent a distinct reason; the Federal Court already has relevant federal
 judicial-review jurisdiction. Federal Court jurisdiction

 Administrative redress and settlement
 The standard CDDA scheme applies to non-corporate Commonwealth entities. The official July 2026 PGPA
 list classifies the Commission as non-corporate and the NDIA as corporate: do not assume identical CDDA
 coverage. Consider Commission/ATO defective-administration routes, and identify the actual lawful redress
 route for an NDIA claim. CDDA is discretionary, not automatic compensation for losing a review.
 Ombudsman/tax-complaint and FOI routes may expose or remedy administrative problems but do not replace
 a damages cause or suspend litigation dates. Finance CDDA guidance; PGPA bodies list, pp 13 and 15

 Current Commonwealth settlement guidance requires legal principle and a meaningful prospect of liability;
 avoiding defence costs alone does not justify paying a spurious claim. Releases are ordinarily sought,
 although limited releases can be appropriate. Do not accept a global release, perpetual undertaking or
 admission without valuing its effect on historical correction and compensation. Guidance Note 14, paras
 12-13 and 21-29

 Why the strike-off issue was raised - and its limit
 The official ASIC bulk-data record freshly queried for ACN 643878639 reports SOFF; ASIC uses this for
 strike-off action in progress. The dataset was updated 31 August 2026 and gives no deregistration date. This
 is evidence of a recorded process status, not proof that FFG has been deregistered or an explanation of why.
 Obtain the current extract and any published notice. Its active ABN does not resolve ASIC status. ASIC
 company dataset; ASIC status guidance

 The Infinity document checked is a notice of a proposed ban, not a final instrument. Do not equate that
 Australian company with another similarly named overseas business or assert a final ban without evidence.
