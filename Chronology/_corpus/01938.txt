     FIRST APPLICANT'S AMENDED RESPONSE AND PROPOSED ADDITIONAL DIRECTIONS
                          In response to the Respondent's Proposed Minute of Order dated 14 August 2026


 A. POSITION OF THE FIRST APPLICANT
 1. This document states the position of the First Applicant, Ms Vida Reid, in ART 2023/3361 only. It is self-contained and
     does not adopt another applicant's factual assertions, concessions or applicant-specific relief.
 2. The First Applicant does not seek to reopen the severance applications dismissed on 31 July 2026. She seeks directions
     within the existing associated-case arrangement so that the allegations against her are identified, her interpreter and
     privilege arrangements are settled, and threshold statutory issues are addressed before factual merits.

 3. No general stay under s 32 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth) (ART Act) is sought by this response.
     The First Applicant reserves her right to apply if materially changed circumstances arise.

 B. INTERPRETER AND APPLICANT-SPECIFIC CASE
 4. The First Applicant has requested a Bahasa Indonesia interpreter. That request is recorded at paragraphs 2(e) and 41 of
     the reasons dated 31 July 2026. Under s 68 of the ART Act, the Tribunal must comply unless it considers that she does
     not require an interpreter for communication with the Tribunal or understanding evidence and submissions.

 5. The Respondent should identify separately the financial claims, intimidation or harassment allegations, alleged
     banning-order contraventions and other suitability matters relied upon against the First Applicant. The request is for
     procedural fairness and case management; it does not suggest that the Tribunal is confined to the administrative
     record.

 C. PRIVILEGE PROTOCOL
 6. On 23 February 2024, the charges in Magistrates Court proceedings PE 45655-45658 of 2022 were dismissed for want
     of prosecution and the First Applicant was discharged. The certified transcript records that the prosecutor stated an
     intention to recharge. The disposition was not a conviction or an acquittal on the factual merits.

 7. By letter dated 11 March 2025, the Respondent stated that the NDIA had informed it that there was no current
     criminal investigation pertaining to the First Applicant or FFG. That was a limited, NDIA-relayed position. It was not
     confirmation from every relevant agency, an undertaking against future charges, or use-immunity for evidence given
     in the Tribunal. The First Applicant does not assert a current investigation as fact and asks the Respondent to identify
     any current investigation or prosecution proposition on which it actually relies.
 8. The First Applicant seeks a confined, question-specific and document-specific protocol. A private hearing under s 69(3),
     or a restriction under s 70, does not itself provide criminal use or derivative-use immunity. Any privilege against
     self-incrimination, and any separately asserted penalty privilege, should be determined before the particular answer
     or production is compelled.

 D. PRELIMINARY STATUTORY ISSUES
 9. The original order dated 20 December 2022 prohibited the First Applicant from providing supports and services
     "directly or indirectly" to NDIS-funded participants. On internal review dated 21 April 2023, the stated basis changed
     from s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) to s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (Cth) (NDIS Act), and the
     operative prohibition was broadened to "any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity" with inclusive
     examples.

 10. In Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA
      1442 at [126]-[141], the Federal Court addressed specified activities, direct statutory connection and the requirement
      that a banning order be sufficiently certain to be reasonably capable of obedience. The application of those principles
      to both of the First Applicant's instruments should be determined before factual merits.

 11. A subsidiary issue is whether s 100(6A), as then in force, authorised the internal reviewer to change the statutory
     precondition and materially broaden the operative prohibition by variation, and what consequence follows if the
     legally available course was instead to set aside and substitute. The First Applicant seeks determination of that issue
     only if it could materially affect the disposition under s 105 of the ART Act.

 E. DIRECTIONS SOUGHT
 1. Section 70 material and supplementary tender bundle
 Within seven days after these directions, the Respondent must:

      a. confirm that the complete set of documents required by ss 23, 26 and 29 of the ART Act, including an unredacted
          copy of each document subject to the Respondent's application dated 7 May 2026 under s 70, has been lodged
          with the Tribunal;

      b. serve every document not subject to a non-disclosure claim; and
      c. file and serve a document-specific schedule identifying each document withheld from an applicant, the statutory
           and evidentiary basis of the restriction sought, any legal professional privilege claimed, the privilege holder and
           dominant purpose, the confidentiality and waiver position, and whether any non-privileged factual material or
           annexure can be severed and disclosed.
 2. Determination and implementation of the section 70 application
      a. Each affected applicant may file a response to the schedule within seven days after service.
      b. The s 70 application be determined at the next directions hearing or as soon as practicable thereafter, with the
          Tribunal at liberty to inspect the documents privately.
      c. Within three business days after determination, the Respondent serve every document or severable part to which
          access is permitted.

      d. If access to adverse factual material is restricted, the Respondent must not rely on that material against an
           applicant without first providing any fair gist ordered by the Tribunal and a reasonable opportunity to answer it.
 3. Complete First Applicant instruments
 Within seven days after these directions, the Respondent serve certified complete copies of the original written notice
 and banning order dated 20 December 2022, the internal-review decision and reasons dated 21 April 2023, the varied
 written notice and order, and the delegation relied upon for each decision.
 4. Interpreter
      a. Pursuant to s 68 of the ART Act, the Tribunal appoint an independent Bahasa Indonesia interpreter, having no
          conflict of interest, for the First Applicant at the next directions hearing and every subsequent case event at
          which she appears.

      b. The Registry notify the parties of the interpreter and mode of attendance at least three business days before each
          event and arrange any necessary remote-platform check and reasonable breaks.
 5. Respondent's proposed disposition and statutory position
 Within 14 days after these directions, the Respondent file and serve a statement identifying in ART 2023/3361:
      a. the precise decision it asks the Tribunal to make under s 105 of the ART Act;

      b. if it seeks a substituted order, its complete operative wording, specified activities, conditions, duration, statutory
           provision and proposed temporal effect;

      c. whether it accepts that Sunflower requires any part of either order to be set aside, every distinction relied upon if
          not, and the complete residual prohibition alleged if severability is asserted; and

 7. Investigation or proposed-prosecution reliance
 Within the same period, the Respondent state whether it relies in ART 2023/3361 on the existence or continuation of any
 criminal investigation, referral or proposed prosecution concerning the First Applicant and, if so, identify the source, date
 and substance of that information and the status of any referral so far as known to the Respondent.
 8. Preliminary witness notice
 Within the same period, the Respondent file and serve a preliminary notice identifying each witness it presently intends to
 call, each written statement or personal account on which it intends to rely, whether the maker is presently intended to
 be called, and any known unavailability.
 9. Privilege protocol
      a. Before seeking oral evidence from the First Applicant personally, the Respondent must give reasonable written
          notice of the topics on which evidence is sought and any document category it proposes to ask the Tribunal to
          compel her to produce.
      b. The First Applicant may notify a privilege claim in relation to a specified question, topic or document category,
          identifying without revealing the answer the offence or penalty exposure and the non-tenuous connection said
          to arise.

      c. The Tribunal determine the claim before the First Applicant is required to answer or produce. A hearing of the
          claim be private under s 69(3), with any publication or disclosure restriction under s 70 determined having regard
          to s 71.

      d. The making or upholding of a privilege claim is not itself an admission. Pending determination, the First Applicant
          is not required to answer or produce the identified material.

      e. This protocol does not excuse legal contentions, identification of disputed issues, non-incriminating evidence,
          challenges to the Respondent's material, or compliance with orders not engaging the notified claim.
 10. Preliminary issues under ss 53 and 79

 statutory and transitional provisions, agreed chronology and legal submissions, without oral factual evidence:

      a. whether the original expression "directly or indirectly" identified specified activities authorised by s 73ZN(2) and
          was sufficiently certain;

      b. whether the varied expressions "any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity", "directly or
          indirectly", and the inclusive examples identified specified activities with the required direct nexus and certainty;

 11. Section 185 referral process
      a. Within seven days after compliance with Orders 3 to 8, the First Applicant file proposed questions of law and
          submissions not exceeding five pages on referral to the Federal Court under s 185 of the ART Act.

      b. The Respondent may file submissions not exceeding five pages within a further seven days, and the First Applicant
          may file a reply not exceeding three pages within a further three business days.
      c. The request be determined before a final factual merits hearing is fixed. If referred, further programming be
          considered after Federal Court directions. If not referred, Order 9 be listed for preliminary determination as soon
          as practicable.
 12. Targeted attendance requests
 Within 14 days after service of the Respondent's Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions and evidence, the First
 Applicant may serve a schedule identifying each statement-maker sought for cross-examination, the document and
 disputed proposition, materiality, and a time estimate. Any dispute be determined consistently with the applicable
 Practice Direction.
 13. Statements of Facts, Issues and Contentions
 The First Applicant's updated Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions and further evidence be due 28 days after the
 latest of implementation of the s 70 ruling, compliance with Orders 3 to 8, and determination of the preliminary issue or
 any s 185 reference. The Respondent file its response within a further 28 days and the First Applicant file any reply within
 a further 14 days, subject to Order 8.
 14. Final hearing estimate and date
 The final hearing duration and date be fixed at a further case-management hearing after the preliminary legal process,
 pleadings, witness notices, targeted attendance requests, privilege process and interpreter arrangements are known. The
 Respondent's proposed two-day estimate is reserved.
 15. No general stay and liberty to apply
 No general stay under s 32 is made by these directions. The parties have liberty to apply on reasonable notice, including
 upon a material change in investigation or prosecution status.
