Court: Federal Court of Australia.
Jurisdiction: s 39B(1) of the Judiciary Act 1903 (Cth) (mandamus, prohibition or injunction against an officer of the Commonwealth) and s 39B(1A)(c) (a matter arising under a law made by the Parliament); judicial review and associated relief. The ADJR Act 1977 is to be considered in the alternative for any reviewable “decision.” [Counsel to settle the jurisdictional basis.]

2. Relief sought
Judicial review: declarations that the impugned NDIA decisions and conduct were made or engaged in for an improper purpose and/or beyond power and are invalid; certiorari quashing them; injunctions restraining continued reliance; and ancillary relief.
Misfeasance in public office: damages against the second and third respondents, the Commonwealth vicariously liable, including aggravated and (if available) exemplary damages. [Counsel.]
Costs, and such further orders as the Court thinks fit.
3. The impugned decisions and conduct
Reduced to the reviewable conduct (dates per the chronology in the shield memo):
(a) the direction to freeze, hold or cease payments to FFG-linked participant plans;
(b) the “request for service” reallocation of FFG participants to Diversitas WA and Nurse Assist 24/7;
(c) the creation of participant-record “alerts” / “consent removals” blacklisting FFG before any banning order;
(d) the “coordinated provider exit” by which the NDIA engineered FFG’s removal from the sector.
4. Grounds — improper purpose / beyond power
The NDIA’s functions and powers are conferred for the proper administration and integrity of the scheme and the protection of participants. They were exercised for the collateral purpose of removing FFG from the sector and installing competing providers — a purpose foreign to the power. Lead authorities (to be pinned): Padfield v Minister of Agriculture; R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council; Thompson v Randwick Corporation; Shrimpton v Commonwealth. The case is documentary at its core (Halls’s own “coordinated provider exit” and “at the request of the agency” emails), which is its strength; improper-purpose findings are nonetheless hard-won and require the conduct, not merely suspicion, to be proved. [PIN all authorities.]
5. Grounds — misfeasance in public office
Elements (to be pinned): Northern Territory v Mengel; Sanders v Snell; Three Rivers DC v Bank of England:
1. a public officer (Halls; Lundgren);
2. an invalid or unauthorised exercise of, or refusal to exercise, power;
3. the mental element — either targeted malice, or knowledge that the act was beyond power (or reckless indifference to that and to the probability of harm);
4. causation; and
5. damage to the applicants.
The mental element is the weak joint and is the reason the claim is built from the documents before it is asserted — proved by the material extracted through Package 1 (FOI), Package 2 (ART production) and Federal Court discovery, not pleaded on suspicion.
