## 1.  Standing rules of the matter

These rules are binding on every document drafted in this matter, every email composed, every submission filed. They are reproduced from Section 0 of the master deep dive (`Reid_v_NDIS_DeepDive.md`).

### 1.0  Absolute prohibition on fabrication

No case citation, statute reference, regulatory instrument, decision number, name of officer, name of party, name of witness, date, dollar figure, page reference, paragraph reference, file number, URL or other purportedly factual particular may be included in any document drafted for this matter unless it has been independently verified against a primary source. Verification means three things together: (a) the citation exists; (b) the parties and date match; (c) the case actually stands for the proposition for which it is cited. A real case cited for a proposition it does not support is treated as a citation error of the same gravity as a fabricated case. If a citation cannot be verified, it must be flagged `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT FILE]` and not included in any externally-facing document until verified or removed. This rule overrides every other consideration in drafting.

### 1.1  No fraud is conceded — anywhere

The applicants do not concede that any fraud was committed by any of them, by any of the associated corporate entities, or by any person acting on their behalf at any material time. There is no conclusive evidence of fraud against any of the applicants anywhere on the record. To the applicants' knowledge, no one committed any fraud. The Commission's case is built on allegations. Those allegations are contested in their entirety. They have never been tested in any adjudicative forum.

The Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions discontinued all four charges against Mrs Vida Reid on 23 February 2024 for no evidence. Mr Simon Reid was never charged. AGS confirmed in writing to the Tribunal on 11 March 2025 that there is no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd. The Commission's own internal reviewer materially discounted the central financial allegation in IR(7) 22/23 at paragraph 29(j) by reference to the Tactical Intelligence Report — which the Commission itself continues to withhold from disclosure.

### 1.2  Witness layer is materially compromised

The witness layer the Commission relied on for the December 2022 banning decisions and for the April 2023 internal review decisions is materially compromised. ### 2.3  The principal grounds

- **Simon Reid** — no individualised assessment. The original banning decision was made without any individualised assessment of Mr Reid's own conduct, knowledge or culpability. The contemporaneous record discloses that the Commission attributed the conduct of his wife and of corporate entities of which he was not a director at the material time to him personally. - **Vida Reid** — ultra vires variation of statutory basis. The internal reviewer purported to "vary" Mrs Reid's banning order by switching its statutory foundation from s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) to s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) without notice to her that suitability was the new dispositive issue and without giving her an opportunity to address it. The variation is procedurally unfair on *Kioa v West* (1985) 159 CLR 550 and on the specific rule in *SZBEL v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs* [2006] HCA 63; (2006) 228 CLR 152.
- **Procedural fairness — service** — the Notices of Intention dated 19 November 2022 (response deadline 30 November 2022) were received only on 7 December 2022, signed for by Mr Ousman Kamara (a Hybrid Hive staff member at the time, not the named recipient), and never delivered to the solicitors of record. - **Improper purpose** — the *Padfield v Minister of Agriculture* [1968] AC 997 / *R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council* (1981) 151 CLR 170 line. Anchored by the audio recording capturing NDIA WA Assistant Director Mr Nathan Halls personally directing an external service provider to freeze invoice payments to FFG via an extra-judicial email, the structural pre-arrest dismantling of FFG's client base in concert with competing providers (Diversitas WA / Ms Sukhi Tear; Nurse Assist 24/7 / Ms Amanda Damon), and the 23 December 2022 Joint Ministerial-Commission media-release window.

## 3.  Verified legal authorities

Every authority in this list has been verified by direct primary-source search. None may be cited in any document without re-verification per the rule at section 1.0.

### 3.1  Procedural fairness
- *Kioa v West* (1985) 159 CLR 550 — High Court — foundational; the hearing rule.
- *Hot Holdings Pty Ltd v Creasy* (1996) 185 CLR 149 — High Court — opportunity to be heard must be meaningful.
- *Re Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs; Ex parte Lam* (2003) 214 CLR 1 — High Court — decision-maker's conduct cannot defeat the procedural-fairness opportunity.
- *SZFDE v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship* (2007) 232 CLR 189 — High Court — procedural unfairness goes to jurisdiction.
- *Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v SZIZO* (2009) 238 CLR 627 — High Court — service on the affected person rather than the known legal representative.
- *SZBEL v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs* [2006] HCA 63; (2006) 228 CLR 152 — High Court — the reviewer who identifies a new dispositive issue must give the affected person notice of and opportunity to address it. The principal authority for Mrs Reid's ultra-vires-variation ground.
- *Annetts v McCann* (1990) 170 CLR 596 — High Court — the affected person must be given notice of the case against them.
- *VEAL v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs* (2005) 225 CLR 88 — High Court — adverse credibility material must be put.

### 3.2  Improper purpose
- *Padfield v Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food* [1968] AC 997 — House of Lords — power must be exercised for the purpose for which it was conferred.
- *R v Toohey; Ex parte Northern Land Council* (1981) 151 CLR 170 — High Court — improper purpose review.
- *Thompson v Randwick Corporation* (1950) 81 CLR 87 — High Court — improper purpose review.

### 3.3  Legal unreasonableness
- *Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v Li* (2013) 249 CLR 332 — High Court — legal unreasonableness review.

### 3.4  Materiality / jurisdictional error
- *Hossain v Minister for Immigration and Border Protection* (2018) 264 CLR 123 — High Court — the materiality threshold for jurisdictional error: realistic possibility of a different outcome.
- *Craig v South Australia* (1995) 184 CLR 163 — High Court — jurisdictional error generally.
- *Kirk v Industrial Court (NSW)* (2010) 239 CLR 531 — High Court — jurisdictional error and privative clauses.
- *Plaintiff S157/2002 v Commonwealth* (2003) 211 CLR 476 — High Court — denial of natural justice is jurisdictional error.
- *Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs v Bhardwaj* (2002) 209 CLR 597 — High Court — a decision affected by jurisdictional error is void ab initio.

### 3.5  Merits-review framework
- *Shi v Migration Agents Registration Authority* [2008] HCA 31; (2008) 235 CLR 286 — High Court — the cornerstone authority on AAT/ART merits review.
- *Frugtniet v Australian Securities and Investments Commission* [2019] HCA 16; (2019) 266 CLR 250 — High Court — Tribunal stands in the shoes of the primary decision-maker; remitted to AAT, which ultimately varied the banning order.

### 3.6  Verified analogous regulatory-banning set-aside or variation precedents
- *Grubisa and Australian Securities and Investments Commission* [2023] AATA 3328 (10 October 2023, McCabe DP) — AAT set aside ASIC banning orders. Grounds existed but the discretion was exercised the other way. ASIC media release 23-279MR.
- *Lee and Australian Securities and Investments Commission* [2020] AATA 2661 (7 August 2020) — AAT set aside ASIC director-disqualification order under s 206F Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). ASIC media release 21-085MR.
- *Babbage and ASIC* (AAT decision 25 October 2023) — AAT varied banning duration from 10 years to 6 years. ASIC media release 23-301MR. [The precise AATA neutral citation needs AustLII confirmation before this case is cited in any submission.]

### 3.7  NDIS-specific banning-order authority
- *Adachi Disability Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission* [2024] FCA 311 — Federal Court of Australia — brief stay of an NDIS Commission banning order pending review.
- *Johnston and Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission* [2024] AATA 3438 — AAT affirmed banning order under s 73ZN(2)(a)(iii). Adverse outcome.

There is, on the public record at the date of this backup, no reported AAT/ART decision in which an NDIS Commission banning order has been set aside. The Reids' matter is in a precedent-setting posture on the suitability ground.

### 3.8  Misfeasance in public office
- *Northern Territory v Mengel* (1995) 185 CLR 307 — High Court — foundational misfeasance authority.

### 3.9  Negligence in exercise of statutory power
- *Crimmins v Stevedoring Industry Finance Committee* (1999) 200 CLR 1 — High Court — limited duty of care from regulators.

### 3.10  Validity of administrative decisions
- *Project Blue Sky Inc v Australian Broadcasting Authority* (1998) 194 CLR 355 — High Court — construction of statutes and validity of decisions.

### 3.11  Severance / interlocutory framework
