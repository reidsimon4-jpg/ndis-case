# Bid Positioning Strategy

## 1. Target Buyer Types

### 1.1 NGO Buyers
- **Primary targets**: Habitat for Humanity Indonesia (HFI), Indonesian Red Cross (PMI/IFRC), Catholic Relief Services (CRS), Wahana Visi (World Vision), UN-Habitat, IOM
- **Procurement style**: Competitive RFQ/RFP, donor-funded, strict compliance requirements
- **Decision drivers**: Speed of deployment, beneficiary dignity, QA/QC traceability, reusability, safeguarding compliance
- **Budget source**: International donor grants (often USD-denominated)
- **Budget range**: Typically $15,000–$50,000 per shelter unit (aligns with our pricing)

### 1.2 Government Buyers
- **Primary targets**: BNPB (National Disaster Management), PUPR (Public Works & Housing), Provincial BPBD
- **Procurement style**: E-procurement (LKPP/SPSE), formal tender, strict SNI compliance
- **Decision drivers**: Price alignment with Huntap/Huntara caps, SNI compliance, local content
- **Budget constraint**: Huntap IDR 60M cap (our product exceeds this significantly)
- **Strategy**: Position for critical infrastructure (clinics, schools, command posts) or "Foundation-First" model

## 5. Recommended Positioning

### Primary Strategy: NGO-First
Position as the premium rapid-deployment partner for international NGOs responding to the West Sumatra crisis. Our pricing aligns with international donor budgets ($18K–$34K/unit) and our QA/QC traceability meets NGO accountability standards.

### Secondary Strategy: Government Critical Infrastructure
For government procurement, avoid competing on individual Huntap housing (budget mismatch). Instead, target clinics, schools, mosques, and command posts where the higher budget is justified by communal value and operational continuity requirements.

### Tertiary Strategy: Foundation-First Hybrid
Offer the helical pile foundation platform as a standalone product (~IDR 30–40M) that fits within Huntap budgets, allowing beneficiaries to build superstructures using traditional methods.
