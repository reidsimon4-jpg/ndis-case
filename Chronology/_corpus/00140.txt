# Note to counsel — forcing movement on a stalled ART matter

**Date:** 14 June 2026 · **Matters:** ART 2023/3363 (Simon Reid); 2023/3361 (Vida Reid); 2023/4494 (FFG) · **Status:** working note to brief counsel — not legal advice, not for filing. Every statutory provision and authority marked **[VERIFY]** must be pinned before use.

## The problem in one line

The matter has been on foot since 2023; the **severance/sequencing application is the gateway and remains undecided** (argued 6 and 19 March 2026 and in the May 2026 supplementary submissions; heard on the papers ~20 March 2026); the **FFG banning order expired on 20 December 2025 before any merits hearing**; and the 19 March 2026 timetable has gone stale. Nothing advances until severance is decided, and it is not being decided.

This note sets out, from least to most forceful, what can be done so the applicants are not left simply waiting.

## The option ladder

**1. Expedition / decision request to the Tribunal (do this first).**
A short on-the-record request to Deputy President McMillan KC asking the Tribunal to (a) determine the severance application now, (b) set an expedited, sequenced timetable (Simon first, then Vida, FFG deferred), and (c) deal with the s 70 application once Mr Reid's 19 May 2026 opposition is before the Deputy President. Anchored on s 9 of the *Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024* (Cth) (matters to be resolved "as quickly … as a proper consideration … permits"), the expired FFG ban, and the Respondent's 7 May 2026 conduct. Low cost, low risk, and creates the record needed for anything stronger. *(Drafted: `Applicants_Request_Severance_Decision_and_Expedited_Timetable_DRAFT_14_June_2026.docx`.)*

**2. Complaint about delay / timeliness escalation to the President.**
The ART has a complaints channel, and the President (Justice Kyrou) has publicly made timeliness a priority. A documented complaint about a 2023 matter still without a hearing — routed to the President's chambers — applies institutional pressure that a case-management request alone does not. **[VERIFY: current ART complaints process and the correct channel.]** Use only after, or alongside, option 1, and keep the tone procedural, not accusatory.

**3. Federal Court — compel the Tribunal to act (the backstop).**
Where a tribunal unreasonably fails to perform its duty, the Federal Court can be asked to **compel it by mandamus** under **s 39B(1) of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth)** (jurisdiction over a matter in which mandamus, prohibition or an injunction is sought against an officer of the Commonwealth). The object would be an order requiring the Tribunal to determine the severance application and progress the matter to hearing.

  - **Amenability.** Members of the Tribunal are generally amenable to s 39B / s 75(v) supervision as officers of the Commonwealth. **[VERIFY for ART members specifically.]**
  - **Threshold.** Mandamus does not issue for mere delay. The applicants would need to establish, in substance, a **failure or constructive refusal to exercise jurisdiction** — delay so prolonged and unexplained that the duty to decide within a reasonable time is breached. This is a **high, fact-specific bar**; the chronology here (a 2023 matter, a gateway question undecided since March, a sanction expired unheard) is the kind of record on which it is argued, but success is not assured. **[VERIFY the current delay/duty-to-decide authorities before advising — do not rely on any case not pulled from the primary report.]**
  - **Relief.** An order that the Tribunal determine the severance application and/or fix the matter for hearing; the Court compels the *exercise* of jurisdiction, it does not substitute its own decision on severance.

## The honest trade-offs on option 3

- **It does not create listing capacity.** A court order that the Tribunal "get on with it" cannot manufacture members or hearing rooms; the practical gain may be a decision on severance and a date, not a fast hearing.
- **Cost and relationship.** It is a Federal Court proceeding with its own costs, and it is adversarial toward the very Tribunal that will then hear the merits. Tone and necessity matter.
- **It is a backstop, not a first move.** Its main value now may be *leverage*: a credible, well-evidenced indication that mandamus is contemplated often moves a registry without the proceeding ever being filed. Reserve it for after the expedition request has been made and ignored.

## The silver lining worth remembering

Delay has hurt the applicants procedurally but **helped them on the merits**. On review the Tribunal decides the correct or preferable decision *as at the date of its decision* (*Shi v Migration Agents Registration Authority* (2008) 235 CLR 286 **[VERIFY]**). Since the orders were made, the criminal charges were discontinued, the FFG ban expired, and the Commission's stated rationale (the "ongoing investigation") has fallen away. The passage of time has gutted the basis on which the orders were justified.

## Recommended sequence

1. File the **expedition / severance-decision request** (option 1) now.
2. If no decision and no listing follows within a short, defined period, lodge the **timeliness complaint** (option 2) and put the Respondent and registry on notice that **mandamus** (option 3) is under consideration.
3. Brief counsel to settle the **delay chronology and the s 39B threshold** so the backstop is filing-ready if needed.
