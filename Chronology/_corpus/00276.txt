I am a delegate of the Commissioner for the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission
(the Commission).

Section 73ZN of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (Cth) (the NDIS Act) gives me, as
the Commissioner’s delegate, the power to make an order known as a banning order. A banning
order can prohibit or restrict a person from being involved in the provision of specified supports or
specified services to people with disability.

Proposed banning order
I have formed the view that it may be appropriate in all the circumstances to make a banning order
prohibiting you for a period of five (5) years from being involved in the provision of supports or
services to people with disability.

The basis for this view is that I reasonably believe that you are not suitable to be involved in
providing supports or services to people with disability. I am therefore empowered to make a
banning order against you under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act.

The purpose of this letter is to inform you of relevant facts and the matters that are of concern to
me and to give you the opportunity to make submissions about this before I make a final decision.

Basis for making a banning order
Based on the matters listed above, my preliminary view is that the banning order should prohibit
you from being involved in the provision of services and supports, both directly and indirectly, to
people with disability for a period of five (5) years.

The conduct set out above, engaged in by you and Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, an entity for which you have
authority or responsibility for (or significant influence over) planning, directing or controlling its
activities, strongly suggests that you are not suitable to provide supports or services to people with
disability for the following reasons:

       Section 3 of the NDIS Act contains the objects of the NDIS Act, which includes:

            o   supporting the independence and social and economic participation of people with
                disability;
            o   enabling people with disability to exercise choice and control in the pursuit of their
                goals and the planning and delivery of their supports;
            o   promoting the provision of high quality and innovative supports that enable people
                with disability to maximise independent lifestyles and full inclusion in the
                community; and
            o   protecting and prevent people with disability from experiencing harm arising from
                poor quality or unsafe supports or services provided under the NDIS.

       In giving effect to the objects of the NDIS Act:




                                   NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission                                7
            o   section 3(3)(b) of the NDIS Act requires regard to be had to the need to ensure the
                financial sustainability of the NDIS. Similarly, as set out in section 4(17) of the NDIS
                Act, it is the intention of Parliament that the Commissioner, amongst others, have
                regard to the need to ensure the financial sustainability of the NDIS in performing
                functions and exercising powers under the NDIS Act;

            o   sections 4(4) and 4(6) of the NDIS Act, provide principles guiding actions under the
                NDIS Act including:

                        People with disability should be supported to exercise choice, including in
                         relation to taking reasonable risks, in the pursuit of their goals and the
                         planning and delivery of their supports; and

                        People with disability have the same right as other members of Australian
                         society to respect for their worth and dignity and to live free from abuse,
                         neglect and exploitation.

       It is my view based on the conduct set out above that you and Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, an entity
        for which you have responsibility and authority, have acted in a manner that is contrary to
        these guiding principles, as well as the objects of the NDIS Act, and in circumstances have
        deliberately undermined those principles. More specifically:

            o   Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd engaged in conduct which limited NDIS participants from
                exercising their choice and control over their own supports and services, and acted
                without their consent or authorisation to make decisions about the engagement of
                supports and services under their NDIS plans. This conduct is serious, and has had
                significant impacts on the health and wellbeing of participants, particularly where
                deliberate conduct has been engaged in to subvert the authority of NDIS participants
                and their nominees;

            o   Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd has engaged in aggressive and dishonest conduct, including the
                submission of falsified claims for supports and services against NDIS plans, driving a
                culture of inflating claims and hours for services delivered, authorising payments
                without participant consent and entering into new service agreements without the
                knowledge or consent of NDIS participants and their nominees; and

            o   The conduct set out above and the offences for which V REID has been charged and
                you involved, presents significant risks to the financial sustainability of the NDIS, as
                well as to NDIS participants, as victims of fraud. Such conduct takes funds from NDIS
                participant plans, stopping participants from being able to purchase the supports and
                service they genuinely need. Further, fraud makes it harder for participants to pursue
                their goals and outs additional pressures on carers, family, friends and support
                networks.

Therefore, I have formed the preliminary view that allowing you to continue to provide supports or
services to people with disability poses a continuing risk to people with disability. My view is that is
that the need to protect the interests of people with disability, who may be particularly vulnerable,
and ensure that they are kept safe, requires that in matters of this kind, a person who has engaged
and been involved in the conduct set out above, must be prevented from delivering supports and
services to people with disability. My preliminary view is that you are not currently suitable to provide
supports or services to people with disability.

NDIS Provider Register
As set out in section 73ZS of the NDIS Act, the NDIS Provider Register must include certain
information in relation to a person against whom a banning order is in force.

The information that must be included on the NDIS Provider Register is:
     The name of the person;
     The person’s ABN (if any);
     Information about the banning order.

The Commissioner must not publish a part of the NDIS Provider Register if the Commissioner
considers that its publication would be contrary to the public interest, or the interests of one or
more persons with disability receiving supports or services.

Should I decide to make a banning order against you, information about the banning order will be
included on the NDIS Provider Register and published on the NDIS Commission’s website. You may
wish to include submissions on this.
