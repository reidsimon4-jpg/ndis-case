### INTERLOCUTORY APPLICATION  
*(Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024, section 32(2))*

#### 1. Application
The Applicants apply for orders under **section 32(2)** of the *Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024* staying or otherwise affecting the operation or implementation of the decisions under review, namely the banning orders as varied by the internal-review decisions dated **21 April 2023**, in the alternative cascade set out in the Proposed Orders below.

#### 2. Decisions under review
2.1 In **2023/3361** — the decision of the reviewer under **section 100(6)** of the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* dated **21 April 2023** varying the banning order made against Vida Reid on **20 December 2022** (external review lodged **10 May 2023**).  

2.2 In **2023/3363** — the decision of the reviewer under **section 100(6)** of the NDIS Act dated **21 April 2023** varying the banning order made against Simon Reid on **21 December 2022** (external review lodged **10 May 2023**).  

2.3 Those internal-review decisions were made in the exercise of the reviewer’s power under **section 100(6)** (confirm, vary, or set aside and substitute). **Note:** the sealed IR statements of reasons describe that toolkit by reference to “**section 100(6A)**”; on the Act, **s 100(6)** is the confirm/vary/set-aside power and **s 100(6A)** is the **time limit** for making the s 100(6) decision — treat the sealed “6A” label as a mislabel (statute flag). They are **not** variations under **section 73ZO**, unless a sealed instrument otherwise provides (none on the face of the 21 April 2023 notices).

**Statute flag (s 100(6) vs s 100(6A)):** Cite the reviewer’s power toolkit as **NDIS Act s 100(6)** (confirm / vary / set aside and substitute). The sealed IR SORs label that toolkit “s 100(6A)”; on the Act, **s 100(6A)** is the period within which the s 100(6) decision must be made (rules or 90 days). Do not plead the sealed mislabel as law.


#### 3. Grounds (concise)
3.1 The varied operative wording — including “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”, non-exhaustive “This includes” limbs, “directly or indirectly”, and “assistance of any form” — is not confined to engaging in **specified activities** reasonably capable of being obeyed, contrary to the analysis in *Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2)* **[2025] FCA 1442** at **[128]–[139]**.  

3.2 From **9 April 2026**, **section 73ZNA** of the NDIS Act (inserted by the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Amendment (Integrity and Safeguarding) Act 2026* Schedule 1 item 43) creates fault-based and **strict-liability** criminal offences (and civil penalties) for breach of a banning order, including a condition. Ambiguous wording destroys the effectiveness of the review unless clarified or stayed.  

3.3 Interim clarification, or a partial stay restoring the clearer **December 2022** operative wording, is desirable to ensure the effectiveness of the review (**s 32(2)**), having regard to the interests of persons who may be affected (**s 32(7)(b)**).  

3.4 A full stay of ban operation is sought only in the further alternative, upon the protective undertakings in **Schedule B**.

3.5 **Duration-premise collapse (urgency / balance — not acquittal; use each person’s sealed pins).**
**(a) Simon (2023/3363) — sealed Simon IR ¶¶46–47:** the five-year period to **22 December 2027** was said to correspond with the time reasonably expected for “the current **regulatory and legal processes** … in relation to Mr Reid to conclude,” with the ban to be **revisited** thereafter.
**(b) Vida (2023/3361) — sealed Vida IR (proportionate/duration close, ~¶¶62+):** the period to **21 December 2027** was said to correspond with the time reasonably expected for the **criminal proceedings** pending against her to be finally determined, with the ban to be **revisited** thereafter.
**(c) Collapse (not acquittal):** those criminal proceedings were dismissed for want of prosecution on **23 February 2024**; in **March 2025** AGS conveyed there was no current investigation. **Not pleaded as a merits acquittal.** Pleaded only that the **stated IR duration/revisit premises** in **(a)** and **(b)** no longer support continued exposure to overbroad, now-criminalised (`s 73ZNA`) ban wording pending determination.
**(d) FFG (2023/4494) — do not transplant:** FFG IR **8 June 2023** cut duration **10y→3y** to **21 December 2025** expressly for an expected **criminal** timeline (SOR ¶¶69–75). That FFG-specific collapse is for **4494 final-duration / associated-matter context**, not a substitute pin for Simon or Vida. **$1,485,527** remains **Simon IR ¶29(j) only**. See `NOTE-duration-premise-collapse-DRAFT.md`.


3.6 **Severance refusal does not close the *Sunflower* / variation-validity path.** The Tribunal refused severance on **31 July 2026**. That decides association/hearing structure only. Intake records that Vida’s request for determination of a **separate question** as to the **validity of the 21 April 2023 variation** remains **undetermined**. The interlocutory relief sought here (clarification / partial stay of overbroad varied wording under *Sunflower* **[2025] FCA 1442** and **s 73ZNA**) does **not** depend on severance having succeeded and is not foreclosed by that refusal.

#### 4. Proposed Orders

**THE TRIBUNAL ORDERS THAT:**

##### Primary — clarification / construction (**DOWNGRADED — pack review**)
> **Not to be pressed as a contested stay primary.** Prefer raising at **directions** as a **fallback** beside the **separate question**. Contested path = directions fallback, not a standalone contested stay fight on Order 1 alone.

**1.** Pursuant to **section 32(2)** of the *Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024*, pending final determination of applications 2023/3361 and 2023/3363 or further order, the banning orders the subject of those applications (as varied on 21 April 2023) are to be construed and given effect as follows:  
**(a)** the operative prohibition is confined to engaging in **specified activities** of a kind authorised by **section 73ZN(2)** of the NDIS Act, expressed so as to be reasonably capable of being obeyed (*Sunflower* **[2025] FCA 1442** at **[128]–[131], [133]–[139]**);  
**(b)** the words “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity” do not expand the prohibition beyond such specified activities;  
**(c)** the words “This includes” (or equivalent non-exhaustive listing) do not enlarge the prohibition beyond clear specified activities;  
**(d)** references to engaging “directly or indirectly” or providing “assistance of any form” are to be read down consistently with *Sunflower* at **[128], [134], [139]**;  
**(e)** without limiting (a)–(d), conduct that is not engagement in a clearly specified activity tied to the provision of NDIS supports or services is not to be treated as a breach of the bans pending final determination (particulars as verified in the supporting affidavit / Schedule C if settled).

##### Alternative — partial stay of 21 April 2023 variation only (**consent / settlement primary — pack review**)
> Together with **Schedule B**, treat primarily as **consent / settlement terms**. Contested use only as directed by KC.

**2.** Alternatively to Order 1, pursuant to **section 32(2)**, the operation and implementation of the **variations** effected by the internal-review decisions of **21 April 2023** are stayed, and pending final determination of the applications the bans operate only in accordance with **Schedule A** (the sealed December 2022 operative text).

##### Fallback — full stay + undertakings
**3.** Further alternatively to Orders 1 and 2, pursuant to **section 32(2)**, the operation and implementation of the bans (as varied on 21 April 2023) are stayed pending the Tribunal’s decision on the applications coming into operation (**section 32(4)**), upon the Applicants’ undertakings in **Schedule B**.

##### Ancillary
**4.** Liberty to apply on 48 hours’ written notice (including liberty to apply to vary the undertakings in Schedule B).  
**5.** Costs reserved.  
**6.** [*If required*] Short service; list for urgent interlocutory hearing.

##### Final-relief bookmark (for final hearing — not this interlocutory)
**7.** **Note:** If the Tribunal varies or sets aside and substitutes under ART Act **sections 105 and 108**, the Applicants will seek an express order under **section 108(5)** so any varied or substituted decision has effect from the original make-ban commencement (**20/21 December 2022**). Default **section 108(4)** keys to the time the reviewable decision (the **section 100(6)** decision under NDIS Act **section 103(1)**) had effect — **21 April 2023** — not automatically December 2022.


##### Order 8 — REMOVED (pack review 11 Sep 2026)
**Do not include 31 Jul correction orders in this interlocutory.** Pursue via **separate s 114 letter** only.

## Schedule B — Protective undertakings

Pending final determination of applications **2023/3361** and **2023/3363**, each relevant Applicant undertakes to the Tribunal that they will **not**:

1. act as **key personnel** of an NDIS provider within the meaning of the NDIS Act;  
2. access, or cause any person to access on their behalf, any **NDIS claims** portal or system for the purpose of submitting or managing claims for NDIS supports or services;  
3. exercise control over **NDIS participant funds** or plan-managed payments.

**And note:**
4. Liberty to apply to **vary** these undertakings.  
5. These undertakings are given **without any concession** of liability, of the validity of the bans, or of any suitability (or other) findings.

Undertakings support **Order 3** (full stay). Willingness to give them may also be noted in support of Orders 1–2.
