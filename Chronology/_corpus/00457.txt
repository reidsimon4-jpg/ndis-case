# Exact-wording and statutory-nexus schedule

This schedule is designed for annexure to submissions or use at the first case-management hearing. All instrument quotations must be checked once more against the native signed PDFs before filing.

## 1. Statutory language

Section 73ZN(2) of the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth), in substance and so far as relevant, empowered the Commissioner by written notice to make a banning order **prohibiting or restricting a person from engaging in specified activities** if a condition in paragraph (a) was satisfied.

The structure requires:

1. a named person;
2. a statutory condition concerning that person; and
3. specified activities in which that person is prohibited or restricted from engaging.

Use the exact historical compilation text in the filed schedule rather than this summary.

## 3. *Sunflower* propositions

| Paragraphs | Proposition | Application here |
|---|---|---|
| [127]–[128] | Section 73ZN(2) addresses the person’s direct engagement in specified activities, not indirect engagement or involvement | All three Reid instruments except Simon’s original expressly use indirect or wider relational wording |
| [129]–[131] | Serious consequences imply clarity; subject must know exactly what can and cannot be done; order must be reasonably capable of obedience | “Any involvement”, “any capacity”, “assistance of any form” and an inclusive list lack a closed boundary |
| [132]–[134] | “Directly or indirectly involved” wording was unclear and beyond power; indirect subject matter must be reached through a specifically described activity | Vida original and both variations use materially the same or broader wording |
| [135]–[141] | Examples did not save a varied order; the whole order was set aside | The three Reid examples sit beneath an invalid chapeau and are expressly non-exhaustive |
| [180]–[191] | Required connection between activities, personal attributes, unacceptable unmitigated risk and scope/duration | Review reasons do not make that connection for every prohibited capacity |
| [202]–[209] | Findings in one role did not intelligibly justify a different activity-wide prohibition and duration | Corporate/financial findings do not without more justify direct supports and every sector capacity |
| [222], [225]–[231] | Internal review power is broad | Vida’s strongest ground is lack of fair notice of the switch, not a categorical absence of review power |

## 4. Why the examples do not save the varied orders

1. The chapeau is operative and universal: “any involvement … in any capacity”.
2. “This includes” means the stated limbs are not exhaustive.
3. The first limb expressly repeats “directly or indirectly”.
4. The third limb — assistance “of any form” — is itself open ended.
5. Removing isolated words would not reveal which unstated activities the reviewer intended to retain.
6. Judicial severance would therefore risk remaking rather than construing the administrative order.

## 6. Relief discipline

The applicants seek invalidation of existing instruments and decisions. They should expressly accept that:

- the Court is not deciding the correct or preferable merits outcome;
- the Commissioner may make a future lawful decision within power after procedural fairness;
- *Abdullah* and *Johnston* show that lawful orders can be affirmed on a proper merits record; and
- no claim of bad faith, damages or officer liability is part of this opening case.
