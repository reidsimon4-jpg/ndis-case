            THE LEGISLATIVE FRAMEWORK RELEVANT TO THE DECISION UNDER REVIEW


            17. The Act regulates the provision of supports or services to participants under the National
                    Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS). The objects of the Act are set out in section 3, and
                    include:

                       (a) providing reasonable and necessary supports, including early intervention
                           supports, for participants in the NDIS (section 3(1)(d));

                       (b) enabling people with disability to exercise choice and control in the pursuit of their
                           goals and the planning and delivery of their supports (section 3(1)(e));

                       (c) promoting the provision of high quality and innovative supports that enable people
                           with disability to maximise independent lifestyles and full inclusion in the community
                           (section 3(1)(g)); and

                       (d) protecting and preventing people with disability from experiencing harm arising
                           from poor quality or unsafe supports or services provided under the NDIS
                           (section 3(1)(ga)).


            18. The Commissioner is appointed under section 181L of the Act and is assisted by the
                    Commission. The Commissioner’s core functions include the development of a nationally
                    consistent approach to managing quality and safeguards for people with disability
                    receiving supports or services, and securing compliance with and enforcement of the
                    provisions of the Act.10

            19. Under section 9 of the Act, a person who receives funding to provide supports or services
                    to a participant under arrangements set out in Chapter 2 of the Act or is otherwise paid
                    an amount under the NDIS is an ‘NDIS provider’ for the purposes of the Act. NDIS
                    providers can be both registered and unregistered.


            20. Section 73ZN(2) of the Act provides that the Commissioner may, by written notice, make
                    an order, known as a banning order, prohibiting or restricting a person who is or was
                    employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider, or a person who is or was a
                    member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider, from engaging in specified activities if,
                    relevantly:

                       (b) the Commissioner reasonably believes that:

                            (i).    the person has contravened, is contravening, or is likely to contravene the
                                    Act; or

                            (ii).   the person has been involved in, or is likely to become involved in, a
                                    contravention of the Act by another person; or

                           (iii).   the person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or
                                    services to people with disability, having regard to any matters prescribed
                                    by the NDIS rules;11 or

                           (iv).    there is an immediate danger to the health, safety or wellbeing of a person
                                    with disability if the person continues to engage in the specified activities;
                                    or

                       (c) the person is convicted of an offence involving fraud or dishonesty.


            21. Section 11A of the Act defines the term ‘key personnel’ to include both a member of a
                    group of persons responsible for the executive decisions of the entity and any other
                    person who has authority or responsibility for (or significant influence over) planning,
                    directing or controlling the activities of the entity. There is no dispute that the applicant
                    was a member of the key personnel of ADS and that ADS was an NDIS provider for the
                    purposes of the Act.

            RELEVANT PRINCIPLES OF THE LEGISLATIVE FRAMEWORK

            Respondent’s Contentions


            22. As stated above, section 73ZN(2) of the Act provides that the Commissioner may make a
                    banning order prohibiting or restricting a person who is or was a member of the key
                    personnel of any NDIS provider from engaging in specified activity if the Commissioner
                    reasonably believes that the person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of
                    supports or services to people with disability.


            23. The respondent highlighted the following features of the legislative regime. First, the
                    banning order is not limited to a person who is currently involved with an NDIS provider. It
                    extends to persons who were previously key personnel of an NDIS provider.


            24. Second, in order to make a banning order, the Commissioner must ‘reasonably believe’
                    the person the subject of the order is not suitable to be involved in the provision of
                    supports or services. The respondent contended that it is well-established that ‘belief’
                    does not require certainty but rather is

                       an inclination of the mind towards assenting to, rather than rejecting, a
                       proposition and the grounds which can reasonably induce that inclination of the
                       mind may, depending on the circumstances, leave something to surmise or
                       conjecture.12

            25. Third, the power to make a banning order under section 73ZN(2) of the Act is
                    discretionary and when exercising this power, it is important to have regard to the nature
                    and purpose of the provision and to note that the Act ‘incorporates into its structure a
                    number of values which are integral to the scheme’.13 The respondent asserted that the
                    purpose of the power under section 73ZN(2) of the Act is fundamentally protective in
                    character and this purpose is consistent with one of the general purposes of the Act as
                    set out under section 3(1)(ga) which is, as stated above, to ‘protect and prevent people
                    with disability from experiencing harm arising from poor quality or unsafe supports or

                    product of either recklessness or negligence and there appear to be no other mitigating
