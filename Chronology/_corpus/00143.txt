on 25 January 2023 requesting an internal review of the decision made on 21 December 2022 to

The internal review decision
I have decided to vary the original decision. This means the banning order made against Mr Reid
remains in place, however, it is changed or altered in some respect.

The variation I have decided to impose is to broaden the scope of the banning order to make it
explicit that Mr Reid is to have no involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity. This
includes Mr Reid being prohibited from:

    a) providing disability supports and services, directly or indirectly, to NDIS-funded participants;
    b) providing labour hire workers to support NDIS providers; or
    c) providing support, funding, guidance or assistance of any form to an NDIS provider.

I enclose the banning order as varied, and the reasons for my review decision at Attachment A to
this notice.

Consequences of review decision
The original banning order made against Mr Reid is not disturbed and continues to have effect from
5pm on 21 December 2022 (AWST).

Preliminary matters

1.   This statement of reasons outlines why I have decided to vary the banning order made on 21
     December 2022 under section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act, which prohibited Mr Simon Reid
     from providing disability supports and services to NDIS-funded participants for a period of 5
     years, effective from 5pm on 21 December 2022.

2.   In this statement of reasons, I refer to the banning order made on 21 December 2022 as the
     original decision.

3.   Section 99(1), table item 15 of the NDIS Act confirms the original decision can be subject to
     internal review (as it is identified as a ‘reviewable decision’).

4.   Section 100(2) of the NDIS Act requires a request for review of a decision to be made within
     three months after receiving notice of the decision. I note Mr Reid requested an internal review
     of the original decision within the statutory deadline.

5.   Section 100(6A) of the NDIS Act requires an internal reviewer to make a decision either:

        a.   confirming the reviewable decision;

        b.   varying the reviewable decision; or

        c.   setting aside the reviewable decision and substituting a new decision.

Role of the internal reviewer

6.   In reviewing the original decision, my task was to make the correct or preferable decision in
     accordance with the law and any relevant policies of the NDIS Commission. In doing so, I
     considered evidence that was before the original decision-maker, as well as any new evidence
     that was before me at the time of making my decision.

7.   In deciding what is the correct or preferable decision, I needed to be satisfied on the balance of
     probabilities of any findings of fact that I made.

9.   The original decision is varied such that the scope of the banning order made against Mr Reid

Relevant legislation
12. Section 73ZN(2) of the NDIS Act gives the Commissioner the discretion to make a banning order
    prohibiting or restricting a person employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider as
    follows:

        ‘The Commissioner may, by written notice, make an order (a banning order) prohibiting or restricting a
        person who is or was employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider, or a person who is or was a
        member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider, from engaging in specified activities if:

            (a) the Commissioner reasonably believes that:

                  i. the person has contravened, is contravening, or is likely to contravene this Act; or

                 ii.   the person has been involved in, or is likely to become involved in, a contravention of this
                       Act by another person; or

                iii. the person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of supports or services to people
                     with disability, having regard to any matters prescribed by the National Disability
                     Insurance Scheme rules for the purposes of this subparagraph; or

                iv.     there is an immediate danger to the health, safety or wellbeing of a person with disability
                       if the person continues to engage in the specified activities; or

            (b) the person is convicted of an offence involving fraud or dishonesty; or

            (c) the person becomes an insolvent under administration.’

13. Under section 73ZN(3) of the NDIS Act, a banning order may apply generally or be of limited
    application, and may be permanent or for a specified period.

14. Section 73ZN(7) of the NDIS Act states that the Commissioner may only make a banning order
    against a person after giving the person an opportunity to make submissions to the


                                       NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission
    Commissioner on the matter (except in certain circumstances outlined in section 73ZN(8) which
    are not relevant to this matter).

15. Section 73ZN(9) sets out the various requirements surrounding any banning order made as
    follows:

        'The Commissioner must:

          (a) give the banning order to the person against whom the order is made; and

          (b) include in the banning order a statement of reasons for the order; and

          (c) if the banning order is against a person who is employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS
               provider or is a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider—notify the NDIS provider of
               the order as soon as possible.’

    35. In relation to the submission outlined above at paragraph [34(a)], I disagree that Mr Reid has
        been denied procedural fairness. I am satisfied Mr Reid has been given several opportunities
        to respond to the allegations against him, but he has elected not to utilise them. Those
        opportunities were afforded on the following occasions:

        a.   On 2 December 2022, the original decision-maker granted Mr Reid an extension of time
             to make submissions in relation to the proposed banning order. I acknowledge the
             extension was granted after Mr Reid had already made submissions, however it
             remained open to Mr Reid to amend or add to those submissions. Mr Reid declined to
             make any further submissions – he was content to rely on the submissions he already
             made;

        b.   On 6 February 2023, Mr Reid was invited to make submissions in support of his request
             for an internal review and inform me of any matters he wished to bring to my attention.
             No submissions were received from Mr Reid; and

        c.   On 22 March 2023, I invited Mr Reid to make submissions in relation to new evidence
             pertaining to him that arose during the course of the internal review. No submissions
             were received from Mr Reid.

36. Consequently, I find the requirement to provide Mr Reid with an opportunity to respond to
    matters relevant to the banning order has been met.

37. In relation to the submission outlined above at paragraph 34(b), I agree that the bail conditions
    of Mrs Reid are not relevant to the assessment of Mr Reid’s suitability to engage in the NDIS
    sector, and I note the original decision-maker removed such considerations from the original
    decision. The criminal charges laid against Mrs Reid, however, are a different matter. As noted
    above, the evidence lodged in those criminal proceedings contain allegations that Mr Reid
    engaged in fraudulent activity, and to that extent, such material is directly relevant to his
    suitability as it indicates Mr Reid engaged in serious wrongdoing toward NDIS participants and
    in the broader scheme of the NDIS.

38. In relation to the submissions outlined above at paragraph 34(c) and (d), I disagree with the
    contentions made by Mr Monks. It is appropriate to attribute liability to Mr Reid for the
    wrongdoing of the corporate entity he directs and the conduct of the workers employed or
    otherwise engaged by that company. The law regards Mr Reid as being in a position of
    responsibility, authority and control over Hybrid. A person who stands in such a position and is
    able to exert significant influence over the planning, directing and controlling of the activities of
    an entity can be, appropriately, held accountable for impropriety caused by the corporate entity
    (or its officers). Additionally, it is worth noting that company directors are subject to a range of
    legal duties under the Corporations Act 2001. Primary amongst these is the duty to act with care
    and diligence (under section 180 of the Corporations Act). Directors must exercise their powers
    and discharge their duties with the degree of care and diligence that a reasonable person would
    exercise if he or she were a director in the company’s circumstances and had the same
    responsibilities of that director. In my view, this duty requires a director to be familiar with, stay
    informed and make appropriate enquiries about the company’s activities. Mr Reid should have
    been aware of the conduct allegedly being engaged in by, and within Hybrid. Lastly, I note that I




                                   NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission
    do not attribute much weight or significance to Mr Reid’s shareholding in any companies, it is
    his directorship of an entity, which in my view, links him to any liability.

39. In relation to the submission outlined above at paragraph 34(e), I note that despite Mr Reid’s
    claim of being in possession of evidence that exculpates him of all wrongdoing alleged in this
    matter, he did not provide such material to the original decision-maker (despite being
    requested to do so) nor has he submitted such evidence for consideration in this internal
    review. As such, I attribute no weight or significance to that particular submission in the
    absence of any detail or sense as to what Mr Reid is referring to.

40. In relation to the submissions outlined above at paragraph 34(f), I attribute minimal weight and
    significance to Mr Reid’s wholesale denial and rejection of the various allegations made against
    him. Mr Reid did not provide any information or evidence to support such assertions;
    accordingly, I can do no more than note his refutation.

Consideration of new evidence that arose during the course of the internal review

41. In the course of undertaking this internal review, I became aware of new information that

    Mr Reid’s involvement in these entities seems to have commenced around the time he was
    issued the banning order on 21 December 2022.

42. Specifically, the material before me consisted of the following:

        a.   an application form submitted by Mr Reid on 29 October 2022 to ASIC seeking
             registration of the company, Dream Big, an NDIS provider;

        b.   an email sent by Mr Reid on 9 February 2023 to numerous individuals seeking to recruit
             them as disability support workers for Vitality, a company that provides labour hire
             workers to NDIS providers and of which Mr Reid is a director;

             Mr Reid had established or was the owner of a new business (Dream Big) that operated
             as an NDIS provider.

43. I find it concerning that Mr Reid seems to have made various attempts to remain involved in the
    NDIS sector in circumstances where he was subject to significant regulatory action that was
    specifically intended to prohibit him from engaging in it. In particular, the establishment of
    Vitality and Dream Big indicates to me that Mr Reid may be finding strategic or tactical ways to
    circumvent the terms of the banning order made against him and that it is necessary to broaden
    the scope of the banning order accordingly.

Requirement to take proportionate and risk responsive action under NDIS Act

44. The NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk
    responsive and proportionate manner (section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act).

45. The NDIS Commission’s Banning Order Policy notes that a banning order can have a significant
    impact on the recipient and on people with disability, and as such, it will be reserved for



                                  NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission
    circumstances where it is needed to protect people with disability from the risk of being
    exposed to conduct that falls well short of acceptable standards. However, a banning order
    should not be for a longer period than is considered necessary.

46. Having regard to the ongoing investigation by the NDIA and law enforcement agencies into the
    fraudulent conduct Mr Reid is alleged to have engaged in, and the serious nature of those
    allegations, I consider Mr Reid is not suitable at this time to be an NDIS provider.

47. I consider that a banning order is appropriate in this case. The banning order will remain in
    place until 22 December 2027 at 12:00am (AWST). The period of the banning order
    corresponds with the amount of time it is reasonably expected to take for the current
    regulatory and legal processes that are in motion in relation to Mr Reid to conclude. Upon the
    determination of those processes, the banning order can be revisited and consideration given
    to whether it should be revoked or varied.

48. In the meantime, the banning order will protect NDIS participants from the risk of being
    exposed to fraudulent claims and having the funds in their NDIS plans depleted.

Conclusion

    49. The NDIS Provider Register published on the NDIS Commission’s website will be updated to
        reflect the varied banning order made against Mr Reid.
