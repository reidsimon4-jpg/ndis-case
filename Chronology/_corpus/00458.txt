## Simon Reid and Vida Reid — banning orders varied 21 April 2023

We act for Simon Reid and Vida Reid.

Our clients intend to commence a confined application in the Federal Court of Australia under s 39B of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth). The proposed proceeding concerns the legality of the existing instruments and review decisions. It will not ask the Court to determine the correct or preferable merits outcome presently before the Administrative Review Tribunal.

Each varied order prohibits the named person from having “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”. Each expressly extends to providing supports and services “directly or indirectly” and to providing “support, funding, guidance or assistance of any form” to an NDIS provider. The three limbs are introduced by “includes” and are not exhaustive.

In *Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2)* [2025] FCA 1442 at [127]–[141], the Federal Court held materially narrower “directly or indirectly involved” wording invalid under s 73ZN(2). The Court held that the statutory nexus is the person’s engagement in specified activities and that a coercive order must tell its subject exactly what conduct is prohibited and be reasonably capable of obedience. Examples did not save the instrument considered there.

Our clients’ further proposed grounds are, in summary:

1. the review reasons do not expose the activity-specific connection identified in *Sunflower* at [180]–[191] and [202]–[209] between the individual, each activity, unacceptable unmitigated risk, and scope and duration;
2. Mrs Reid’s original 20 December 2022 order is separately expressed to extend to provision “directly or indirectly”;
3. Mrs Reid’s original decision proceeded under s 73ZN(2)(a)(i), while review proceeded decisively under paragraph (iii), without fair notice of that change, after which the review reasons treated detailed examination of contravention submissions as not necessary or conducive;
4. in Mr Reid's case, the investigation record acknowledged limited information about his actual operational involvement, while the review reasons stated that it was appropriate to attribute liability to him for corporate and worker wrongdoing rather than identifying the required personal, activity-specific statutory basis;
5. the Commission's Banning Order Policy said key personnel would be unlikely to be banned without direct involvement or a responsible failure significantly facilitating, contributing to or exacerbating the conduct. Mr Reid's review reasons instead corrected attribution, timing, double-counting and amount issues before relying on directorship, attributed liability and what he “should have been aware” of;
6. the administrative record concerning the 9 February 2023 Vitality email and the Commission's 22 March 2023 notice identified a specific labour-recruitment and labour-supply activity. On the Commission's own reasoning, that material raised whether a clearly expressed labour-hire restriction should be considered. It did not, without an intelligible activity-by-activity explanation, justify the further prohibitions on direct support and every form of involvement, funding, guidance or assistance in the sector; and
7. Mrs Reid's varied order contains an express exception allowing her to seek disability supports or services for herself and the couple's participant son, Alex. Mr Reid's order has no equivalent exception permitting ordinary unpaid assistance to his participant wife or son. That comparison illustrates the uncertain and excessive reach of the universal wording and creates a present family hardship requiring confined interim protection.

The ART applications were filed on 10 May 2023 and remain without final determination. The existing orders have operated for more than three years and are stated to continue until December 2027. Our clients rely on *Sunflower*, delivered on 21 November 2025, as a later authoritative decision concerning the precise power and materially similar wording. They will disclose the full ART chronology and address all periods of delay candidly. That chronology includes the Commission’s acknowledgement on 19 March 2026 that Simon’s original order, EC22-002103, EC22-002429, EC22-002521 and other Simon-specific material had been omitted from the June 2024 tender bundle, and the supply of that package in May 2026.

To avoid unnecessary cost and parallel interlocutory proceedings, please state by 4:00 pm AWST on [DATE — ALLOW AT LEAST 7 DAYS, OR SHORTER ONLY IF GENUINELY URGENT] whether the Commissioner will agree to:

1. revoke or suspend the two 21 April 2023 varied orders pending lawful reconsideration;
2. remove them from the current banning-order register or annotate them accurately as suspended and subject to the proposed legality proceeding;
3. consent to orders setting aside the invalid wording and remitting any necessary decision for reconsideration according to law;
4. alternatively, agree to an expedited Federal Court timetable, an agreed documentary court book and preservation of all positions on final relief and costs;
5. provide the complete decision records and delegation instruments for the four decisions; and
6. notify the ART jointly that the parties seek coordinated case management while the legality issue is determined.

If the Commissioner does not agree to suspend the whole of Mr Reid’s order, our clients request written confirmation that, pending an expedited determination, the Commission will not investigate or enforce the order merely because Simon, in his capacity as spouse, parent, carer, guardian or nominee:

1. seeks or arranges supports for Vida or Alex;
2. communicates with providers or attends meetings with their authority;
3. assists either participant to express preferences or make decisions; or
4. provides ordinary unpaid family assistance,

provided that Simon does not receive NDIS funding, submit or authorise claims, control participant or provider funds, manage an NDIS provider, or supply paid support workers.

The references above to T65, T65.1 and the review reasons identify the contents of the administrative record. They are not admissions by either client and do not adopt the Commission’s characterisation of any conduct. If the Commissioner proposes that a targeted labour-hire restriction can be severed from or substituted for the present instrument, please identify the legal basis, exact wording, evidence, proposed safeguards and process by which that position would be implemented.

For interim risk management only, and without admissions, our clients are willing to discuss undertakings concerning executive or key-personnel roles, provider payment systems, participant funds, claims and invoicing pending an expedited final hearing. Any terms would require express agreement and, if proceedings commence, acceptance by the Court.

Please also confirm that the Commission will preserve all native documents, metadata, versions, comments, emails, attachments and decision-workflow records relating to the original and review decisions, including T6, T9.4, T44, T49–T53, T62, T65, T65.1, T75/T76, EC22-002000, EC22-002103, EC22-002429, EC22-002581 and EC22-002521, together with the Tactical Intelligence Report, Attachment P and PDMS/open logs.
