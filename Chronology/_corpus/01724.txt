# Tender and internal decision-record forensic audit

**Prepared:** 15 July 2026  
**Purpose:** counsel working note for the proposed Federal Court judicial-review case  
**Primary focus:** executive briefs, evidence matrices, investigation material, procedural-fairness advice, decision workflow and the orders themselves

## Bottom line

The second pass strengthens the proposed opening case, especially Simon's review challenge. It does not produce an automatic or risk-free win.

The most important additional sequence is the Commission's own Dream Big investigation:

1. Simon submitted Dream Big's ASIC application on **29 October 2022**, before his 17 November notice of intention and before the 21 December original order.
2. The Commission issued Dream Big a compulsory-information notice on **20 January 2023** to investigate whether Simon or Vida was contravening a banning order.
3. Dream Big's written response said Simon submitted the ASIC form, had no ongoing operational role, and was not a bank signatory. It identified Virginie Fanny-Dulloo and Tori Fitton as the only account signatories.
4. In the **9 March 2023** examination, Ms Fanny-Dulloo said Simon was not involved in FFG, only helped with the ASIC registration before he was banned, and had no access to Dream Big's email accounts.
5. The **22 March 2023** T65 notice proposed wider wording based on Dream Big and Vitality material.
6. The **21 April 2023** review reasons said Simon's Dream Big involvement appeared to commence around the banning order, treated Dream Big as possible strategic circumvention, and did not identify T58's written response or examination among the materials considered.

That is a record-based chronology. It can be proved without Simon giving a merits narrative. It supports a carefully pleaded contention of material factual misunderstanding, illogicality and lack of an intelligible connection between the evidence and the universal scope of the varied order. Counsel should decide whether to plead a separate failure-to-consider ground; possession of a document somewhere within the Commission does not by itself prove it was legally required to be considered by the reviewer.

## Scope and method

- Audited the final tender PDF: **1,506 PDF pages**, tender references **T1-T97**.
- Cross-checked the five fixed tender parts against the bundle's ten-page index and printed tender pagination.
- Extracted text from every final-bundle PDF page. There were no extraction errors, but **793 pages contained fewer than 20 extracted characters**, reflecting image-only or mostly image-only pages.
- Used the existing OCR extracts and rendered/OCR-checked the significant scanned or rotated pages, including T65.1 and T75/T76.
- Searched the separate 218-file audit corpus for official executive briefs, matrices, investigation notes, internal reviews and decision records.
- Distinguished official records from applicant drafts and later legal work product.

This is a document audit, not a finding that every record in the Commission's possession has been produced. Absence from the tender/corpus is recorded as a production gap, not proof that the document never existed.

## New or sharpened points

### 1. Dream Big chronology contradicts the review narrative

The review reasons at [41]-[43] treated Dream Big as involvement commencing around the order and possible circumvention. The ASIC application was lodged on 29 October 2022. T58 then recorded only pre-ban incorporation assistance and denied continuing Simon involvement. The reasons' materials list identifies the ASIC form, Vitality email and Burke statement, but not the s 56 written response or examination.

The safest pleading is not that the Commission “concealed” exculpatory evidence. It is that the face of the administrative record exposes a serious temporal and evidentiary mismatch which must be explained by the decision-maker's record.

### 2. Burke material was treated more strongly than its direct content

Burke's direct account includes Vida offering her a role in “my new company”. The Simon/Dream Big linkage includes what participants told Burke and her own understanding or what she had heard. The ASIC form shows Simon as applicant/lodger, but identifies other officeholders/members. The review reasons converted that material into an indication that Simon “had established or was the owner” of Dream Big. T58 contains contrary operational evidence.

This is suitable for a record-based no-evidence/illogicality submission. It should not become a collateral trial of Burke in the opening affidavit.

### 3. Simon's original decision lacks a personal assessment bridge

The October investigation note admitted limited information about Simon. The November and December briefs then relied on:

- directorship/control of Hybrid;
- bank-signatory status;
- alleged receipt or benefit; and
- extensive wrongdoing by entities, Vida, Alam and workers.

The final brief's operative conclusion was that Simon “likely benefited”. It did not contain an activity-specific assessment, personal evidence matrix, or analysis against T6's key-personnel criteria. The internal reviewer then supplied a different route: corporate attribution and what Simon should have known.

### 4. Duration was benchmarked to an entity rather than Simon

Both Simon briefs justified five years as consistent with the proposed Hive order. That is circular: Hive's proposed period was itself said to be consistent with Simon. The briefs did not expose an independent analysis of why five years, every activity, and no family exception were necessary for Simon.

### 5. Reverse-burden language appears repeatedly

T56.4-T56.6 and the review reasons repeatedly discount denials because the recipient did not provide exculpatory evidence and ask whether the submission demonstrated compliance. This may show a practical assumption that the subject had to disprove the allegations. It should be used as supporting context, not pleaded as a standalone jurisdictional error without counsel settling the precise legal onus.

### 6. The fairness risk was identified internally

T49 is unusually valuable because it is contemporaneous and specific: legal input recommended providing the information requested to reduce the legal risk of procedural fairness being used to challenge the decision. The final letter took the contrary course. The covering email and any reasons put to the delegate are missing.

### 7. Vida was notified of scope, not the statutory ground switch

T62 fairly notified Burke/Dream Big information and the proposed universal wording. It did not say the reviewer might replace s 73ZN(2)(a)(i) with (iii), treat much of the contravention response as unnecessary, or rely on a “usual practice” of using suitability for charge-pending cases. That distinction is the proper fairness point.

## Proposed opening case

### Simon — original decision

Lead with the absence of a rational, personal and activity-specific bridge from the evidence to the statutory belief. Use T9.4, the briefs, T44 and T6. The case is not that administrative action had to await criminal proceedings; Abdullah and Johnston make that framing unsafe. It is that the particular statutory belief and scope still required objectively reasonable grounds and lawful reasoning.

### Simon — review decision

Lead with:

1. substitution of corporate attribution and Corporations Act duties for the NDIS statutory inquiry;
2. the pre-ban Dream Big chronology and contrary T58 operational evidence;
3. the leap from one concrete Vitality labour-hire activity to every capacity in the NDIS sector; and
4. Sunflower [226]: a variation can regulate future conduct if authorised, but cannot treat conduct outside the earlier instrument as though it had already breached that instrument.

### Vida — review decision

Keep separate:

1. procedural fairness: no notice of the decisive switch from paragraph (i) to paragraph (iii);
2. legal construction/fettering: “usual practice” cannot replace the statutory task;
3. materiality: submissions directed to the suitability gateway, conditions or a narrower order were realistically available; and
4. scope: Dream Big evidence may support a targeted concern, not necessarily the universal chapeau and every listed capacity.

### ART delay and Federal Court timing

The continuing ART matter does not eliminate the utility of judicial review of the legality and present scope of the operative orders. The long passage of time, continuing legal effect, controlled-record disputes, the respondent's 2026 production correction, and the need for a prompt determination of legal limits support a confined Federal Court case and interim relief. Avoid presenting the Court as a merits substitute for the ART.

## Affidavit discipline

Use a common record affidavit to prove dates, documents and the procedural sequence. In particular, annex or place in an agreed court book:

- T9.4;
- the three Simon briefs;
- T44;
- T49;
- T58's s 56 notice, written response and selected examination pages;
- T65/T65.1;
- both review decisions; and
- the FFG review concessions.

Do not ask Simon or Vida to explain underlying claims, company finances, bank accounts, staff, devices, recruitment, Vitality, Dream Big, or alleged offences without written criminal-defence clearance. The new point is strongest because it comes from the Commission's own tendered record.

## Cautions

- T44, T9.9, T9.10 and witness statements are allegation summaries, not findings.
- The record includes substantial adverse material. Do not describe the case as an “easy win”.
- Do not identify the T49 commenter without native metadata.
- Do not say the AGS letter proves the Commission was not investigating, or that the CDPP will not prosecute.
- Do not plead malice, conspiracy, concealment, malicious prosecution or reckless indifference from this material. Preserve those issues for later evidence and limitation advice.
- Abdullah does not immunise every banning order from invalidity; it does, however, make a merits-only or “charges had to finish first” case unattractive. Keep each applicant's legal grounds separate.
