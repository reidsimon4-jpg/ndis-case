                                Notice of internal review decision
           Section 100 of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS Act)


Dear Mr Monks

I refer to the email received by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (the NDIS Commission)
on 21 February 2023 requesting an internal review of the decision made on 20 December 2022 to
impose a banning order against your client, Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (FFG), for a period of 10
years under section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act (the original decision).

I am the delegate of the NDIS Commissioner for the purposes of section 100 of the NDIS Act, and am
authorised to make a decision on this internal review.

The internal review decision
I have decided to vary the original decision. This means the banning order made against FFG
remains in place, however it is changed or altered in some respect.

The variation I have decided to impose is to reduce the duration of the banning order from 10 years
to 3 years. In my view, this corresponds with the amount of time it is reasonably expected to take
for criminal proceedings relating to FFG to be finally determined. Upon determination of those
proceedings, the banning order against FFG can be revisited and consideration given to whether it
should be varied any further (or revoked entirely).

I enclose a copy of the banning order as varied, and the reasons for my review decision at
Attachments A and B to this notice.

Consequences of review decision
The original banning order made against FFG is not disturbed and continues to have effect from
5:30pm on 20 December 2022 (AWST), however it will only remain in force until 12:00am on 21
December 2025 (AWST).

                                        Statement of Reasons

Internal review decision – section 100 of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS
                                               Act)

Preliminary matters

1.   This statement of reasons outlines why I have decided to vary the banning order made on 20
     December 2022 under section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act, which prohibited Forward Focused
     Group Pty Ltd (FFG) from providing supports and services, directly or indirectly, to NDIS-funded
     participants for a period of 10 years, effective from 5:30pm on 20 December 2022.

2.   In this statement of reasons, I refer to the banning order made on 20 December 2022 as the
     original decision.

3.   Section 99(1), table item 15 of the NDIS Act confirms the original decision can be subject to
     internal review (as it is identified as a ‘reviewable decision’).

4.   Section 100(2) of the NDIS Act requires a request for review of a decision to be made within
     three months after receiving notice of the decision. I note FFG requested an internal review of
     the original decision within the statutory deadline.

5.   Section 100(6A) of the NDIS Act requires an internal reviewer to make a decision either:

        a.   confirming the reviewable decision;

        b.   varying the reviewable decision; or

        c.   setting aside the reviewable decision and substituting a new decision.

Role of the internal reviewer

6.   In reviewing the original decision, my task was to make the correct or preferable decision in
     accordance with the law and any relevant policies of the NDIS Commission. In doing so, I
     considered the evidence that was before the original decision-maker, as well as any new
     evidence that was before me at the time of making my decision.

7.   In deciding what is the correct or preferable decision, I needed to be satisfied on the balance of
     probabilities of any findings of fact that I made.

Decision

8.   I have decided to vary the original decision.

9.   The variation I have decided to impose is to reduce the duration of the banning order from 10
     years to 3 years. In my view, this corresponds with the amount of time it is reasonably expected
     for court proceedings that relate to FFG to be finally determined. Upon determination of those
     proceedings, the banning order against FFG can be revisited and consideration given to
     whether it should be varied any further (or revoked entirely).

Relevant legislation
12. Section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act gives the Commissioner (or her delegate) the power to
    ‘make an order (a banning order) prohibiting or restricting specified activities by an NDIS
    provider, or by a person who was an NDIS provider, if the Commissioner has revoked the
    registration of the person as a registered NDIS provider.’

13. Under section 73ZN(3) of the NDIS Act, a banning order may apply generally or be of limited
    application, and may be permanent or for a specified period.

14. Section 73ZN(7) of the NDIS Act states that the Commissioner may only make a banning order
    against a person after giving the person an opportunity to make submissions to the
    Commissioner on the matter.

15. Section 73ZN(8)(b) of the NDIS Act states that section 73ZN(7) does not apply if the
    Commissioner’s grounds for making the banning order are, or include, that the Commissioner
    has revoked the registration of the person as a registered NDIS provider.

16. Section 73ZN(9) sets out the various requirements surrounding any banning order made as
    follows:

       'The Commissioner must:

            (a) give the banning order to the person against whom the order is made; and

            (b) include in the banning order a statement of reasons for the order; and

            (c) if the banning order is against a person who is employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS
                 provider or is a member of the key personnel of an NDIS provider—notify the NDIS provider of
                 the order as soon as possible.’

Findings and Reasons

34. I have considered the original decision made on 20 December 2022.

35. I am of the view that the original decision was the correct and preferable decision to be made,
    however, I disagree with the reasoning for the original decision.

36. Specifically, the aspects of the original decision that I disagree with are the following:

        a.   the original decision adopted the same reasons cited for revoking FFG’s registration as
             a registered NDIS provider on 20 December 2022. As a separate administrative
             decision, there should have been distinct or independent reasons for the banning order
             being issued to FFG; and

        b.   the original decision was grounded in a broader range of considerations than was
             necessary (given the point I have made above at paragraph 36(a)). In my view, the
             decision should have been confined to a narrower and more probative set of material,
             specifically the evidence that related to FFG’s involvement in perpetrating fraud against
             the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS).

37. In summary, my view is that:

        a.   by merely reiterating the reasoning for revoking the registration of FFG, the original
             decision failed to articulate the specific need or reason(s) for imposing a banning order
             upon FFG (in addition to revoking the entity’s registration as a registered NDIS
             provider). Although section 73ZN(1)(a) did allow the original decision-maker to impose
             a banning order as he had previously ‘revoked the registration of [FFG] as a registered
             NDIS provider’, the use of this statutory power is discretionary. The Commissioner (or
             her delegates) are not required to ban each NDIS provider whose registration has been
             revoked. Rather, a banning order (made under section 73ZN(1)(a)) should, in principle,
             only be used if there is some further or compelling reason for such an order to be made
             given an NDIS provider will already be constrained from engaging in the NDIS as a result
             of the revocation of its registration. In other words, there should be some additional
             value, protection or benefit that a banning order provides - that is not afforded by the
             revocation of an NDIS provider’s registration – to warrant the imposition of a banning
             order.

        b.   I acknowledge the original decision-maker had a wide breadth of material before him.
             In my view, however, the evidence relating to FFG’s alleged involvement in fraudulent
             activity (particularly given the quality and volume of such evidence) should have
             constituted the fundamental reason for banning FFG and prohibiting it from having
             access to NDIS participants and the broader disability care sector (notwithstanding that
             there may be others reasons to support the banning order too).

Section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act – power to issue a banning order where the registration of a
registered NDIS provider has been revoked

62. The banning order issued to FFG was made pursuant to section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act.

63. The statutory test for issuing a banning order under this provision is singular. The NDIS
    Commissioner (or her delegate) must have revoked the registration of a person as a registered
    NDIS provider. Provided that process of revocation had occurred, it was open to the original
    decision-maker to impose a banning order.

64. I have been provided with a copy of the decision to revoke the registration of FFG on 20
    December 2022. I am, therefore, satisfied that it was open to the original decision-maker to
    issue a banning order to FFG.

65. However, as mentioned above (at paragraphs 36-37) the original decision-maker should have
    clearly articulated the reason(s) as to why a banning order was warranted in the circumstances.
    The mere fact that section 73ZN(1)(a) conferred upon the original decision-maker the power to
    use this statutory tool, did not suffice or justify the use of it.

66. Despite the original decision-maker’s omission in this regard, in my view there are compelling
    reasons to support or warrant the issuance of a banning order to FFG. Those reasons are that:

        a.   FFG was, in effect, used by the key personnel of the company as a vehicle to perpetrate
             the alleged fraud against the NDIS. In my view, where an entity has facilitated the
             commission of fraud, it is not appropriate for it to remain engaged in the NDIS sector
             given the serious risk it poses to NDIS participants (i.e. the depletion of their NDIS
             funding);

        b.   The revocation of FFG’s registration does not provide a complete or wholesale level of
             safeguarding to NDIS participants given that FFG remains capable of providing supports
             and services to NDIS participants for which registration is not a requirement. The
             classes of supports and services that can be delivered by an unregistered NDIS provider
             are vast and would enable FFG to have access to the NDIS plans of thousands of NDIS
             participants.

        c.   I acknowledge the making of a banning order in respect of FFG will significantly impact
             the operations of the business, and I have taken this into account. However, I consider




                                                22
                                                                                                          T1
             that the competing interest of ensuring NDIS participants, who may not be able to fully
             protect their own interests and who have a statutory right to safe services and
             protection from violence, abuse and exploitation, is paramount and takes precedence
             over the detriment FFG may experience if banned.

67. Further, I agree with the reasoning expressed by the original decision-maker in paragraphs 27 –
    33 of the original decision in so far that he explained the objects of the NDIS Act (contained in
    section 3) as well as section 4(17)(b) of the NDIS Act are factors which also support the
    imposition of a banning order against FFG.

68. Therefore, I am satisfied that the making of a banning order against FFG is the correct and
    preferable decision in the circumstances.

The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy

69. The NDIS Commission Banning Order Policy contemplates that a banning order can be ‘issued
    pending the determination of [criminal] charges’ if, in the circumstances, the information and
    evidence obtained by the NDIS Commission supports a view that a banning order is warranted
    and appropriate.

70. For the reasons already noted above (at paragraphs 41 and 45), I consider a banning order
    against FFG to be warranted and appropriate.

Requirement to take proportionate and risk responsive action under NDIS Act

71. The NDIS Commission is required to conduct compliance and enforcement activities in a risk
    responsive and proportionate manner (per section 181D(4)(b) of the NDIS Act).

72. The NDIS Commission’s Banning Order Policy notes that a banning order can have a significant
    impact on the recipient and on people with disability, and as such, it will be reserved for
    circumstances where it is needed to protect people with disability from the risk of being
    exposed to conduct that falls well short of acceptable standards. However, a banning order
    should not be for a longer period than is considered necessary.

73. Having regard to the criminal proceedings pending against Mrs Reid (which, as noted above,
    implicate FFG in fraudulent activity), I consider that a banning order for a period of 3 years is a
    risk responsive and proportionate measure as it corresponds with the amount of time it is
    reasonably expected to take for the criminal proceedings to be finally determined by the court.
    Upon determination of those proceedings, the banning order against FFG can be revisited and
    consideration given to whether it should be varied any further (or revoked entirely). I note a
    variation or revocation of the banning order could also take place prior to the determination of
    the court proceedings if appropriate or necessary.

74. In the meantime, the banning order will protect NDIS participants from the risk of having
    fraudulent claims submitted against their NDIS plans and their funding for disability supports or
    services being depleted.

Reasons for the decision to suspend your registration

Legislative provisions

Section 73J of the NDIS Act provides that registered NDIS providers must comply with conditions of
registration. The conditions they are subject to include those provided at section 73F of the NDIS
Act.

Section 73F of the NDIS Act states that the registration of a person as a registered NDIS provider is
subject to a number of conditions. These conditions include:

    •       Section 73F(2)(b) - a condition that the person comply with all applicable requirements of
            the NDIS Code of Conduct;
    •       Section 73F(2)(c) - a condition that the person comply with all applicable standards and
            other requirements of the NDIS Practice Standards;

Section 73T(1) of the NDIS Act states that the National Disability Insurance Scheme rules may make
provision for or in relation to standards concerning the quality of supports or services to be provided
by registered NDIS providers.

NDIS Code of Conduct

The NDIS Code of Conduct is set out in section 6 of the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of
Conduct) Rules 2018 (Code of Conduct Rules). Relevantly, Code-covered persons must, when
providing supports or services to people with disability:

        •      act with respect for individual rights to freedom of expression, self-determination and
               decision-making in accordance with applicable laws and conventions (section 6(a));
        •      act with integrity, honesty and transparency (section 6(d));
        •      take all reasonable steps to prevent and respond to all forms of violence against, and
               exploitation, neglect and abuse of, people with disability (section 6(f)).

Practice Standards

Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd is registered to provide the following classes of supports under the
National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018 (the
Practice Standards Rules):

    •       Item 8 – Assistance with travel/transport arrangements
    •       Item 20 – Household tasks.

In order to remain registered for the above classes of supports, Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd must
comply with the Practice Standards outlined in Schedule 8 of the Practice Standards Rules.

Concerns about contraventions of the NDIS Act

1. The nature of the information held by the Commission strongly suggests to me that the
   Provider has contravened, is contravening, or is proposing to contravene the NDIS Act (as
   provided for by section 73P(1)(a) of the NDIS Act).

2. The NDIS Act 73J requires provides to comply with registration conditions. It is a condition of
   registration that providers comply with the Rules. The National Disability Insurance Scheme
   (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018 (the Practice Standards Rules)
   section 7(1) requires providers to be registered to provide Specialist Disability
   Accommodation (SDA). The provider is clearly advertising the provision of supports that you
   are not registered to supply.

3. The allegations and information held by the Commission indicate a significant amount of
   false claims against the Scheme, that could have otherwise have been directed to fund
   essential supports and services to people with disability.

4. The Provider’s practice of billing multiple NDIS participants for supports and services not
   provided compromised the integrity of the Scheme and deprived these NDIS participants of
   receiving necessary supports and services that they relied upon and were entitled to receive.

5. The information available to the Commission regarding the way in which the Provider
   handles complaints also suggests that the Provider has failed its obligation under the NDIS

        ensures that complaints made by all parties are welcomed, acknowledged, respected and

        complaints.

    6. The matters set out above demonstrates conduct that falls well below the standard and
       quality of care expected from NDIS providers when providing supports or services to people
       with disability.

    7. The information before me in relation to the protracted length of time over which this
       alleged conduct took place and continued as well as the instances of similar misconduct
       against multiple NDIS participants suggests that Provider’s conduct was systemic in nature.

    8. My preliminary view is that the need to protect the interests of people with disability, who
       may be particularly vulnerable, and ensure they are kept safe, outweighs the Provider’s
       interest in remaining registered in these circumstances. I am also of the view that allowing
       the Provider to remain registered in the circumstances would jeopardise public trust in the
       Scheme.

    9. Having considered the functions of the Commissioner, the objects of the NDIS Act, and the
       need to consider the financial sustainability of the NDIS in exercising the Commissioner’s
       powers under the NDIS Act, I consider that the registration of the Provider should be
       revoked under section 73P(1)(a) of the NDIS Act.

NDIS Provider Register

Section 73ZS of the NDIS Act requires the Commissioner to maintain the NDIS Provider Register
which must include certain information for each person who is a registered NDIS provider. Relevant

former NDIS provider, including information about the revocation of a person’s registration.

Should I proceed to determine to revoke the Provider’s registration and make a banning order,
information about the revocation and banning order will be included on the NDIS Provider Register,
available on the Commission’s website.
