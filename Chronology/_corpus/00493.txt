B.  Timing
4.	The order under review was made on 20 December 2022 under section 73ZN(2)(a)(i) of the NDIS Act and varied on internal review on 21 April 2023, when the reviewer relied instead on section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii). The varied order runs until 12:00am on 21 December 2027 (AWST). The internal review stated that the period “corresponds with the amount of time it is reasonably expected to take for the criminal proceedings to be finally determined by the court. Upon the determination of those proceedings, the banning order can be revisited and consideration given to whether it should be varied or revoked entirely.” (at [63]). On 23 February 2024, before any evidence had been adduced, the Commonwealth discontinued the prosecution; the Magistrates Court dismissed the charges for want of prosecution and discharged the First Applicant. On 11 March 2025 the Respondent informed the Tribunal that the NDIA had advised that there was “no current criminal investigation pertaining to Mrs Reid or to FFG”. The Respondent has not proposed any variation or revocation of the order since the prosecution was discontinued.

(b)	At [41] of its decision the Tribunal directed the Respondent to file and serve a proposed timetable within 14 days and indicated that the applications would then be relisted for a telephone directions hearing. The Respondent served its minute on 14 August 2026. No directions hearing has yet been listed.
(c)	On 19 March 2026 the Respondent proposed to file an updated tender bundle “consisting of all documents required to be produced under section 23” of the ART Act (submissions at [18.1]). Its minute proposed that the bundle be filed by 28 August 2026. No order has been made, and the First Applicant has not received the bundle.
(d)	The minute proposed that the applicants file their updated statements of facts, issues and contentions (SFICs) and evidence by 25 September 2026 — four weeks after a bundle that has not been served, before the section 70 application is determined, and before the Respondent has identified the case it relies on against each applicant. C.  The findings the Tribunal must make, and the case the First Applicant must meet
8.	At [38] of its decision the Tribunal identified that it will need to consider whether each applicant is a suitable person to be involved in the provision of services to people with disability under section 73ZN(2) of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS Act), which will require findings relating to the allegations, including (a) whether any NDIS funding was made or received for services that had not been provided to the participant; (b) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had intimidated or harassed any participant; and (c) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had contravened their respective banning orders.
9.	The matters summarised at [35] to [37] of the decision are, as [38] records, allegations; in summarising them the Tribunal was assisted by the Respondent's summary (at [33]). The First Applicant disputes them in so far as they are relied on against her.
10.	The allegation at [38](a) is framed generally. For it to bear on the First Applicant's suitability, the Respondent should identify the particular claims it relies on against her and what conduct, if any, it attributes to her personally. The matters at [38](b) and (c) should likewise be identified by date, participant and conduct.
11.	The First Applicant does not ask the Tribunal to limit the material the Respondent may rely on. She asks that the Respondent identify, before she files any further material, the claims and conduct it relies on against her and the evidence for each, so that the hearing addresses a defined case rather than allegations at large.
12.	At [39](i) the Tribunal recorded that the privilege against self-incrimination is not abrogated by the ART Act and that there is nothing to compel Mrs Reid to answer such questions. The First Applicant relies on that privilege and on the privilege against exposure to penalties. She asks that any claim of privilege be taken question by question, through the interpreter, and ruled on as it arises, and that no admission be taken from any factual matter that her SFIC does not address.
13.	The First Applicant asks that the final hearing be an oral hearing, with a Farsi (Persian) interpreter for any evidence she gives. The findings identified at [38](b) and (c) concern allegations about her personal conduct, which she disputes, and she seeks to question the makers of any statements relied on for those findings.
D.  Documents
14.	On 12 September 2023 the Tribunal ordered (order 2) that, until further order and “by reason of current criminal proceedings and/or legal professional privilege”, the Respondent was not required to give the applicants the documents in the bundle marked “Index – Confidential Tribunal Documents”. The Respondent had submitted that it “anticipates regularly reviewing the documents set out in the confidential index with a view to disclosing them if and/or when, the grounds for this application fall away” (submissions of 12 September 2023 at [23]). The only criminal proceedings identified in the material were discontinued on 23 February 2024. The First Applicant asks that the Respondent now state, document by document, whether it maintains that each should continue to be withheld and on what ground, and that the Tribunal then decide whether order 2 should be varied or revoked.
15.	Section 23 of the ART Act requires the decision-maker to give the Tribunal every document in its possession or control that is relevant to the review. The First Applicant does not ask that any document be disclosed to her in breach of an existing order. She asks that the documents be before the Tribunal and described sufficiently for relevance or restriction to be decided by the Tribunal.
E.  Form of any order
16.	In Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 the Federal Court held that section 73ZN(2) does not authorise an order prohibiting a person from indirectly engaging in, or being indirectly involved in, specified activities (at [128], [134] and [139]), although an order may prohibit a clearly specified activity that itself only indirectly involves the provision of supports or services (at [134]); and that the specified activities must be expressed in terms reasonably capable of being obeyed (at [131]). The original order prohibited the First Applicant from providing supports and services “directly or indirectly”; the varied order prohibits “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”. The First Applicant asks that the Respondent state in advance the order it will seek, and whether it relies on the provisions inserted into section 73ZN of the NDIS Act in 2026. She reserves her position on those questions.
