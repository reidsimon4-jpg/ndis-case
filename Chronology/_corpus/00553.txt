The basis for detention, searches and property seizure

With respect to your clients’ interactions with the Australian Border Force (ABF) on 25
September 2024 at the Perth International Airport, | can advise that at no point were they
subject to detention, or was any of their property seized.

Goods belonging to your clients were examined by the ABF in accordance with the
sections of the Customs Act referenced below. Your clients were present during their
examination but they were not compelled to remain with their goods at the screening

point by the ABF.

Under section 186 of the Customs Act 1901 (Customs Act), ABF officers are entitled to
examine goods (including electronic devices) subject to customs control.

Section 30 of the Customs Act provides for when goods are subject to customs control.

Section 30(d) of the Customs Act provides, as far as relevant, that good subject to
customs control includes goods ‘from the time the goods are brought into, any prescribed
place for export, until their exportation to a place outside Australia’.

Perth International Airport is a prescribed place for export for the purposes of section

30(d).
As such, ABF officers were empowered to examine the goods, belonging to your clients’
— that were brought to Perth International airport on 25 September 2024 and which were

destined for an overseas location (in this case Indonesia).

technologies on the goods
