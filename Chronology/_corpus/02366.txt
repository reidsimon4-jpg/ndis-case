Production Test Run Report

Fast Frames Indonesia
Report Date: May 20, 2025
Machine: XHH-JC 89mm Light Gauge Steel Roll Former
Location: Banyuwangi Factory



1. Objective

To conduct an initial test run of the XHH-JC 89mm Light Gauge Steel (LGS) Roll Former,
verify operational integrity, resolve commissioning issues, and assess production tolerances.



2. Summary of Activities

   •   Completed first test run and frame assembly using 89mm LGS profiles.
   •   Successfully transitioned to remote operation capability.
   •   Identified and resolved electrical commissioning issues.



3. Issues Encountered

3.1 Electrical Wiring Non-Compliance

   •   Incident: Green/yellow earth wire was incorrectly used as a live wire by the local
       electrician.
   •   Impact: Delay of approximately half a day while the issue was identified and
       corrected.
   •   Resolution: Confirmed safety with an edy the PLN electrician prior to powering up.

3.2 Hydraulic Cutter Malfunction (Day 2)

   •   Incident: Hydraulic cutters failed to actuate. Root cause was incorrect phase
       sequence on 3-phase wiring, causing the hydraulic pump to rotate in reverse.
   •   Impact: 1-day delay in testing until wiring was corrected.
   •   Resolution: Verified phase swap as safe with an Australian electrical engineer.
       Machine operated correctly post adjustment.



4. Results of Test Panel Production

   •   Operation: Machine was run in automatic mode to produce a standard test panel.
   •   Assembly Outcome: Frame assembled successfully with precise tolerances.
   •   Engineering Checkpoints:
          o Angle joins exhibited consistent spacing within ±0.5 mm tolerance.
          o   Dimples were recessed adequately for wafer-head screws.
          o   All fastening points aligned with design specifications.
   •   Verdict: Mechanical and operational outcomes were successful; fit-for-purpose
       confirmed.



5. Remote Operation Validation

   •   Activity: On Monday, remote access from Bali was successfully used to:
          o Power up the roll-former system.
          o Engage and disengage the hydraulic pump.
   •   Safety Measures: Local staff member David was stationed at the emergency stop (E-
       Stop) and main switch.
   •   Conclusion: Remote operation was verified as feasible under safe protocols.



6. Recommendation for Next Steps

   •   Proposed Test: Remotely produce a small LGS roof truss this week to validate:
          o Precision under full-length cuts.
          o Reliability of remote control over an extended operation period.
          o On-site assembly fidelity of truss elements.




7. Cost-Efficiency Consideration: Assembled vs. Flat-Pack Panels

Scenario 1: Shipping Assembled Panels

   •   Volume-based truck pricing is a limiting factor. A 3.5m x 2.4m panel occupies
       ~8.4m³.
   •   A standard truck (30m³ capacity) fits ~3.5 panels = low density.

Scenario 2: Flat-Pack Bundles

   •   Bundled sticks take ~70% less space (2.5m³ for the same panel).
   •   Includes printed assembly instruction sheets and labeled profiles.
   •   Labor in Bali: Competitive advantage in assembly cost, but will need an assembly
       station in bali.
