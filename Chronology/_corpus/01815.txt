# DEWR / Workforce Australia deed termination — cross-government contagion analysis

**Prepared:** 15 July 2026
**Status:** RESERVED COMPENSATION / CONTAGION TRACK. Not part of the NDIS judicial-review case. Do NOT annex or plead in the Federal Court validity proceeding — it repeats the NDIS allegations and contains adverse findings (see "double-edged"). For counsel assessing the reserved misfeasance/compensation claim.
**Not legal advice.**

## What it is

FFG held the **Workforce Australia Services Deed of Standing Offer 2022–2028** with the Commonwealth (DEWR, formerly DESE), executed **21 March 2022**, ref **ES-22-0090**. It was terminated by DEWR on **28 July 2023**. Provider Lead: **Andrew Wong**; Show Cause author: National Contract Manager **Robyn Shannon**.

## Correction to an earlier read

An earlier assessment (based only on the **Termination Notice**) concluded the cancellation was "capacity only, no NDIS link." **That was wrong.** The **Show Cause Notice of 22 March 2023** — read in full — is dominated by the NDIS matter. The Termination Notice narrowed to the one most litigation-proof ground; the Show Cause reveals the real driver.

## The Show Cause (22 Mar 2023) — five grounds, four NDIS-driven

The entire "Background" recites the NDIS chain: Vida's Oct 2022 fraud charges (Criminal Code (Cth), re NDIS claims); the Commission's 19 Oct 2022 suspension for "threats, bullying and harassment of NDIS participants" and "false claims for payment to the NDIS"; the 21 Nov 2022 further suspension; the 20–21 Dec 2022 banning orders (FFG 10 yrs, Vida 5 yrs, Simon 5 yrs); and the **23 Dec 2022 Joint Media Release (Hon Bill Shorten MP + the Commission)**.

| Clause | Ground | NDIS-driven |
|---|---|---|
| 17.1(a) | Provider conduct — bringing FFG into **disrepute**: expressly "the suspension… and the banning orders… has damaged the reputation of your Organisation" | Yes — pure contagion |
| 17.1(b) | Failure to **notify** DEWR of the suspension/charges/banning orders | Yes |
| 19.1 | **False information** — FFG told DEWR Simon "stepped down" and Tori Vince was "sole Director," but ASIC searches (18 Jan, 6 Mar 2023) showed Simon still director/secretary | Partly independent — FFG's own misstatement, made while trying to distance from the banned Reids |
| 7.1(c) | **Capacity** — website down; 8 Feb 2023 site visit found office **closed**; tender "relied heavily on Ms Reid's experience… however Ms Reid has been removed… after being charged with… fraud" | Both operational collapse and loss of Vida's tendered experience |
| 67.1(d)(i) | **Misleading/deceptive conduct** — "based on the investigation undertaken by the **Commission** and its determination that… your Organisation made false statements with respect to… claims for payment from the NDIS" | Yes — DEWR imported the Commission's findings wholesale |

## The Termination (28 Jul 2023) — executed on capacity only

The Termination Notice removed FFG from the Panel under **clause 7.4** (Department's "absolute discretion") for breach of **clause 7.1(c)** (capacity/capability), by notice under **clause 67.1(j)**. It relied on: staff dropping from a tendered **28 employees to 3**; Director **Tori Vince's resignation (26 Jun 2023)**; and a site visit finding the claimed Armadale premises were a **"wedding venue until recently"** and **"completely vacant."** It did **not** terminate on the disrepute (17.1(a)) or misleading-conduct (67.1(d)(i)) grounds.

**Why that matters:** the *trigger and driver* of the whole DEWR action was the NDIS matter (strong contagion); the *final legal basis* was capacity, chosen because clause 7.4 "absolute discretion" is far harder to challenge than the contested NDIS grounds. The Commonwealth will argue the deed would have gone on capacity regardless (vacant premises, collapsed workforce).

## How it fits the case

**Use:** reserved compensation/misfeasance track, as documented **cross-government contagion / cascading-loss** evidence — DEWR's own letter shows the Commission's action and findings propagating into, and helping end, a separate Commonwealth contract. Pair with the 23 Dec 2022 Shorten/Commission media release (the publicity mechanism).

**Double-edged — handle with care:**
- DEWR **adopted the Commission's false-NDIS-claims determination** (67.1(d)(i)) — a second agency treating the contested allegation as established.
- DEWR made **adverse credibility findings**: false directorship information (cl 19.1) and vacant/misrepresented premises. These echo the "claimed capacity/claims you didn't have" theme and are ammunition for the other side.

**Do not** put it in the judicial-review case (irrelevant to s 73ZN(2); imports damaging material).

**Causation caveat:** loss is unquantified and possibly a lost *opportunity* FFG was never operational to deliver (premises vacant; website down). Confirm FFG actually stood up / delivered Workforce Australia services before Oct 2022 before treating this as a realisable loss.

## Applicants' rebuttal of the capacity / premises ground (instructions, 15 Jul 2026)

The applicants instruct that the capacity collapse DEWR relied on was **itself caused by the NDIS action**:

- the Commission/NDIA led a **coordinated provider exit** that **removed FFG's income** and **blocked plan managers from paying FFG** (approx **$300,000 still owed**);
- as a direct result FFG had to **downsize staff and move to smaller premises**; and
- the applicants **dispute the DEWR 8 Feb 2023 site-visit finding** that the premises were vacant / no organisation operating — they say they **were operating** from the downsized premises and **have witnesses**.

**Effect if made out:** this closes the causal gap. It reframes the clause 7.1(c) capacity ground from "partly self-inflicted" to **NDIS-caused** (income removed → forced downsizing → capacity breach → termination), and — if the vacant-premises finding is rebutted — removes the single most damaging adverse credibility fact.

**Ruthless caution — this is currently the applicants' account and must be evidenced to beat a Commonwealth officer's site-visit record.** Required proof:
1. **Premises:** the lease for the downsized premises (Shop 2, 43 William Street Armadale) and exact occupation dates.
2. **Operation on the visit dates:** contemporaneous proof FFG was operating there on/around **8 Feb 2023** and the later (pre-28 Jul 2023) visit — rosters/timesheets, participant/client service records, utilities/phone/internet accounts, deliveries, CCTV, diary entries, and **named** staff/third-party witness statements.
3. **The ~$300k:** the AR ledger and plan-manager remittance/refusal records showing payments were **blocked**, not merely unpaid, and tied to the provider exit.
4. **The exit:** the "Provider Exit Protocol actioned with the NDIA" / Halls coordinated-exit correspondence linking the income loss to the Commission/NDIA.

**Two hard cautions:**
- **Do not claim as loss the very payments the Commission alleges were false claims.** The ~$300k must be split into genuinely-delivered-and-undisputed vs. Commission-disputed before any figure is advanced.
- **This is a direct factual conflict with a Commonwealth officer's record** (two visits: 8 Feb office closed; later premises "completely vacant / former wedding venue"). Winning it needs strong contemporaneous documents, not recollection alone.

This rebuttal ties directly into the reserved **NDIA misfeasance / coordinated-provider-exit** case and the blocked-payment loss line (`FFG-RECEIVABLES-01`).
