## 4. *Sunflower* comparison (exact point)

s73ZN(2) authorises prohibition or restriction from **engaging in specified activities**. It does **not** authorise a ban on being **indirectly** involved in, or having undifferentiated “involvement” with, the NDIS sector: *Sunflower* **[126]–[128], [134], [139]**.

An order must identify specified activities with sufficient clarity to be **reasonably capable of obedience**, given civil-penalty (and now criminal) stakes: **[129]–[131]**.

The *Sunflower* original and varied orders — “directly or indirectly involved in the provision of supports or services…”, including where “this means…” limbs were added — were held beyond power and set aside: **[132]–[141]**, especially **[135]–[139]**. Inclusive examples do not cure an overbroad chapeau: **[136]–[137]**. Variation cannot launder an ultra vires form: **[72]–[76]**. Consequence: varied order set aside; original also set aside because otherwise it may operate: **[141]** (remedial prayer for both instruments — do not plead “cannot revive” as doctrine).

**Delta:** The Reid varied chapeau replaces “involved in the provision of supports or services” with “**any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity**,” adds labour-hire and open-ended assistance “**of any form**,” and introduces limbs with “**This includes**” (non-exhaustive). That is **wider and less certain** than the orders set aside in *Sunflower*.

For completeness, Simon’s original order prohibited “providing disability supports and services to NDIS-funded participants” (activity-like). Vida’s original already used “directly or indirectly.” The **21 April 2023 chapeaux** are the primary facial target; the originals are addressed in the same prayer so they may not operate after the varied orders fall (**[141]**).

## 5. Consent invitation (set aside **or** replace)

Consistently with *Khalil v Minister for Immigration, Citizenship, Migrant Services and Multicultural Affairs* [2025] HCA 33 and the Tribunal’s power to substitute a decision, we invite consent to **either**:

1. **Set aside** the 21 April 2023 varied orders (Simon and Vida), and the December 2022 originals **because otherwise they may operate** (*Sunflower* **[141]** — remedial prayer for both instruments; not a freestanding “cannot revive” doctrine); **or**  
2. **Replace** them with **specified-activities** orders confined to engaging in identified activities (e.g. direct provision of NDIS supports/services to participants; identified key-personnel functions), for a proportionate period, in authorised form.

Without conceding liability or unsuitability, and **without prejudice**, we are prepared to discuss practical safeguards the Commissioner may seek in any replacement order, for example:

- no key-personnel role;  
- no claims-access / portal credentials;  
- no control of participant funds;

— without prejudice to the applicants’ position that the present sector-wide chapeaux are beyond power and insufficiently clear.

This invitation does **not** assert a right to unrestricted NDIS-sector activity, nor that no protective order in lawful form could ever be considered. The facial point is form and clarity under s73ZN(2) as construed in *Sunflower*.

## 6. Urgency — s73ZNA and interim position

Breach of a banning order now attracts fault / strict-liability / civil-penalty exposure under **s73ZNA** (in force from 9 April 2026). Ambiguous “any involvement … in any capacity” / “of any form” wording creates acute compliance risk.

Pending final disposition, we ask the Commissioner to state urgently whether the Commission:

- will consent to the set-aside / replacement outlined above; and/or  
- will agree a **clarification or stay position** (including any ART s32 arrangement) so the applicants are not exposed to criminalised uncertainty while *Sunflower* issues are resolved.
