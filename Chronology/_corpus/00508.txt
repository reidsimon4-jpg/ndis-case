Defence Strategy: Proof of Delivery
Reid, Reid & FFG v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission — ART 2023/3361, 2023/3363, 2023/4494. Working document, 25 September 2026. Companion files: Proof_of_Delivery_Matrix_25_Sep_2026.xlsx; Holes_in_Commission_Case_25_Sep_2026.md; Chronology (timeline_events).
1. Bottom line
The Commission's case is a claims case. Its core allegation is that FFG and Hybrid billed participants' plans for supports that were not delivered. Every other theme (aggressive billing, unauthorised invoices, 'strategic' circumvention) hangs off that.
Its evidence is 33 witness statements taken for the NDIA's criminal investigation (most headed 'R v Vida Reid'), summarised in Attachment M. None was taken to test delivery against rosters, pay records or invoices. The NDIA itself records that invoices were lodged by 'an unknown person using FFG software Xero'.
The witnesses admit a great deal was delivered. Across the 27 participant/nominee statements there are 114 admitted services (support workers, coordination, STA stays, therapy, cruises, equipment). Of 157 disputed items, many are disputes about hours, dates, price, or which company billed — not about whether anything was delivered.
Step one is to prove delivery with records, participant by participant, invoice by invoice. Denials carry no weight with this Tribunal; documents do. The winning structure is the 'delivery triangle' for each disputed claim: invoice ↔ roster/shift note ↔ worker pay record (plus any participant acknowledgement).
For Simon, the case is narrow: only Hybrid claims dated on or after 17 February 2022 can touch him, and even then only through directorship, not any personal act. Tier 1 below is ~10 participants. That is the work that decides his matter.
2. Theory of the case
Simon (2023/3363)
He did no billing. He never created an invoice. Invoicing and claims were run by the accounts team under the CFO (a CPA). The Commission alleges no personal act, and its own source document cannot say who lodged the claims.
Services were delivered. For the Hybrid claims in his directorship window, the records show who worked, when, and that they were paid.
When irregularities surfaced (late Sep 2022) he acted within days: lawyers engaged (29 Sep), independent audit commissioned (3 Oct), CFO removed (6 Oct) — before the NDIA raid (19 Oct).
He is suitable now; any order should be narrow and clearly specified (Sunflower), not 'any involvement in any capacity'.
Vida and FFG (2023/3361, 2023/4494) — separate but linked
Delivery was real and extensive; the disputes are overwhelmingly about quantity, dates, pricing and entity labels — billing-quality issues, not fabrication.
Billing errors were identified and corrected in real time (e.g. Cutler INV-0890/0891 called a clerical error and being corrected; Bott mis-keyed plan number; invoices put on hold).
Vida's matter carries more risk: several ex-staff attribute billing direction to her. Her evidence strategy must be settled with her own advice; nothing in Simon's evidence should assert facts about her conduct.
3. How the Tribunal will approach it (working assumptions — verify before citing)
De novo merits review: the Tribunal stands in the decision-maker's shoes and decides present suitability on the material before it.
Allegations of fraud are serious; the Tribunal will want cogent, specific evidence before finding them. [Research point: confirm the applicable standard/authorities before citing any case.]
Untested hearsay statements can be received but their weight depends on reliability. Statements prepared for a prosecution that was dismissed, and that are internally inconsistent, carry less weight — particularly when contradicted by records.
The member weighs unchallenged evidence as established. Every disputed claim we care about must be answered in writing.

6. Evidence plan
A. Our own records (start now)
Xero (Hybrid and FFG): invoice list and each invoice's history (creator, date) for every Tier 1 invoice. Proves who raised them and exact service periods.
Rostering / case-note system (CareView): rosters, shift notes and case notes per participant for each disputed period. The single most important dataset.
Payroll / STP: which workers were paid for which weeks. The ATO FOI binders already hold STP-based data for Hybrid and FFG — map pay periods against disputed claim periods.
Bank statements: plan-manager receipts and wages out, Jan–Dec 2022, for the Hybrid ANZ and The Hive accounts.
Service agreements, consents and HelloSign audit trails for each Tier 1–2 participant.
Emails/SMS with participants (e.g. Haidar thank-you emails; Goodwin case notes already found).
Bennett Law pack (Virginie statement, McGrathNicol audit, IT email): privileged — review first, decide on waiver after.
B. Third parties (request first; summons later)
Plan managers (Assist/Zettai, NDSP and others): invoices received, participant approvals, remittances, the 11 'suspicious invoice' reports.
NDIA: records seized 19 Oct 2022 (return/copies), and the claims data behind the Statement of Material Facts.
Hotels (Clipper Inn, Quest, Crown) and allied health (psychologists, OTs, behaviour therapists, Modplex): booking/session records.
Route: informal request now → s 74 ART Act summons after the Respondent particularises its case (direction 5 of v3 response).

Support workers and coordinators named by the participants themselves (Sheet 4 of the matrix: e.g. Donna, Ozzy, Parul, Amy Lemma, Alex Bowers, Sharyn Curr, Blake Sadler, Chloe, Luke). Short, factual statements: who they supported, when, what they did, how shifts were recorded.
Favourable participants: Shapiera (confirms Hybrid delivery, dealt with Simon). Consider Trantham and Haidar for the parts they admit.

9. Risks and rules
Say only what the records prove. If a claim can't be supported, concede it cleanly and explain it (error, correction, refund) — a clean concession is worth more than a lost fight.
Rejaul is an explanation, never an accusation, unless the audit proves more.
Don't file privileged material (3 Oct 2022 email, Bennett correspondence) without a deliberate waiver decision.
Keep Simon's evidence about Simon. Nothing in it should characterise Vida's conduct.
OCR quotes must be checked against the original PDFs before use.
No case citations in any filing unless verified against primary sources.
