 1. Scope, method, and confidence
 I reviewed the uploaded tender bundle and extracted project files, the Simon/Vida/FFG banning and internal review
 material, the Vida affidavit and ABF correspondence, the AAT/ART transcript material, the Simon Statement of Facts,
 Issues and Contentions, the AGS/Tribunal email trail in Gmail, and Outlook search results. Gmail filled the 2026
 procedural gap. Outlook added little of direct forensic value beyond confirming some business/admin continuity and
 one 2023 Tom Monks thread; it was not a goldmine, more a metal detector finding bottle caps.

 Overall confidence: 0.74. The chronology is strong on procedural dates and document existence. It is weaker on the
 full merits evidence because the tender bundle is huge, some material is redacted or scanned, and some
 emails/attachments are not fully available in the current workspace. Treat this as a master skeleton to be refined, not
 the final autopsy report.

 Important distinction: the report separates (1) procedural facts, (2) allegations made by the
 Commission/NDIA/complainants, (3) Reid contentions, and (4) forensic inferences. That distinction matters. Courts
 and tribunals hate soup.


 2. Core forensic conclusions
 - The case began as a complaints/fraud/safeguarding investigation into FFG and Vida. Simon was pulled in through
 entity association, bank/signatory allegations, directorship of Hybrid Hive/The Hive, and later internal-review material
 about Dream Big/Vitality WA.

 - The tender material itself records that the Commission had "limited information" about Simon's actual involvement in
 operations while also treating FFG as a catch-all entity led by Vida, Simon and Rejaul. That is the contradiction worth
 putting under a bright light.

 - Vida/FFG face the harder merits battle because there is a large complaint matrix and regulatory narrative of pattern
 conduct. That does not mean the decision was lawful or fair; it means the argument must be surgical, not emotional.

 - Simon's best case is not "the Commission was mean". It is: no charged conduct, limited actual involvement,
 acknowledged errors about his directorship periods, inadequate engagement with NDIS Rules, excessive scope, and
 no disclosed Simon-specific decision brief or equivalent record.

 - The April 2023 internal review decisions are crucial. Vida's legal basis changed from alleged contravention to
 suitability, and both Vida and Simon had the scope broadened to "no involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any
 capacity". That is not a footnote; it is the hinge of the procedural-fairness attack.

 - The ABF/FOI strand is valuable context and potentially political/FOI/privacy pressure. But do not let it hijack the ART
 merits case. Use it as a pattern/disclosure/fairness lever, not as the main cannon unless better documents land.

 - As at 22 April 2026, severance remained under consideration and AGS was taking instructions on Simon's 20 April
 2026 demand about the missing updated tender bundle and Simon-specific decision records.

 7. Legal position moving forward - short version
 Simon: best positioned of the three if the Commission cannot produce a genuine Simon-specific decision record or
 equivalent analysis. His case should be framed as an individual suitability case, not a family/entity morality play. The
 core orders should be production of the complete Simon decision record, confirmation whether any Simon-specific
 brief/minute/submission exists, severance or at least separate findings, and identification of the precise evidence now
 relied upon independent of the failed Vida prosecution material.

 Vida: harder merits terrain because the complaint matrix is large and the Commission will say administrative
 safeguarding does not need a criminal conviction. The better path is threshold and fairness: the April 2023 shift from
 contravention to suitability, broadened scope, reliance on new material, inadequate disclosure, failure to properly
 consider her submissions, proportionality, and participant/family exceptions. Do not argue "there was no evidence"
 broadly. There was evidence. The fight is reliability, attribution, fairness, statutory basis, and proportionality.

 FFG: the corporate ban has expired. AGS will keep pushing utility/withdrawal. FFG should either narrow its live issue
 to public-register/reputational consequences and any ongoing legal prejudice, or get out of the way so it does not drag
 Simon and Vida into procedural mud. Dead horses are poor litigation vehicles.

 ABF/FOI: keep running the OAIC review, but treat it as a collateral accountability strand unless it produces the
 referring agency identity or interagency communications. If it proves NDIS/NDIA triggered border action after charges
 ended, it becomes powerful for disclosure/model-litigant/political pressure. Until then, it is smoke with some heat, not
 the roast beef.

 9. What not to do
 - Do not say "there is no evidence" across the whole case. That is false enough to annoy the Tribunal. There is
 adverse material. The issue is whether it is reliable, disclosed, correctly attributed, and sufficient for these orders.

 - Do not let Vida's ABF/FOI fight consume Simon's clean production/severance issue. That would be litigation
 self-sabotage with extra paperwork.

 - Do not argue every procedural grievance at once. Pick the ones that change the result: missing decision records,
 NDIS Rules, new material, scope, proportionality, severance.

 - Do not allow FFG's expired ban to keep Simon trapped in a joined case unless there is a live, identified remedy. If it is
 dead, bury it; if it still bites through the register, define the bite.

 - Do not rely on outrage. Outrage is seasoning. Evidence is dinner.


 10. Open gaps to close
 - Exact original Simon banning order and proof of service. The scanned zip file appears to contain an original Simon
 notice, but text extraction was poor. It should be OCR'd or manually reviewed.

 - Exact court order/outcome for Vida's criminal charges. The record uses both "want of prosecution" and "no evidence"
 language. Use the precise court wording only.

 - Full respondent supplementary submissions dated 19 March 2026 and respondent 17 September 2025 submission.
 They must be mapped point-by-point against Simon/Vida replies.

 - Full Home Affairs FOI release (132 folios) and redaction schedule. The key is the identity of the agency on whose
 behalf ABF acted.

 - Any Commission internal decision records outside the tender bundle: decision brief, delegate minute,
 recommendation, legal advice coversheet, risk assessment, approval workflow, PDMS metadata.

 - Witness list and whether the Commission intends to call decision-makers/investigators or only rely on documents.
 Hearsay-heavy bundles are useful until someone asks them to stand upright.


 11. Bottom line
 The Reids should stop trying to win the whole moral universe and win the procedural battlefield in front of them.
 Simon's best shot is severance plus production plus a clean challenge to personal suitability. Vida's best shot is
 threshold legality/procedural fairness/proportionality, not denial of every adverse allegation. FFG is mostly a utility
 problem after expiry unless the public register causes live prejudice. The Commission's biggest vulnerability is not that
 it had no concerns; it is that it may have converted concerns about an entity and one spouse into a sprawling,
 life-altering ban against the other spouse without doing the boring statutory homework. Boring homework wins cases.
 Sexy outrage loses them with better lighting.
