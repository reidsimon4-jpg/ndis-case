Your two instincts are right, and they are the two biggest holes. In six years of correspondence you have FOI'd the NDIA, Home Affairs/ABF, the AFP, WA Police and WA Communities — but you have never completed a formal FOI to the two bodies that hold the spine of the case: the CDPP and the NDIS Commission.
The 6 March 2025 NDIS Commission FOI only ever went to Vida's own email — there is no Commission acknowledgement anywhere in your Gmail, so on the present record it was drafted but not lodged. The CDPP has only ever been cc'd on a 2024 complaint. Both are greenfield.
Recommendation: lodge two fresh FOIs now — (1) CDPP and (2) NDIS Commission (Nathan Halls + the banning-order decision file) — run a third at the NDIA narrowed to Halls, and chase the two IC reviews that are sitting idle (MR24/00378 since 2024; MR26/00947 on foot). Treat FOI as the parallel / backstop track: the heavy Halls material will come faster through ART summons and Federal Court discovery, but FOI is cheap, creates an independent record, and manufactures the inter-agency 'election' pressure your offensive case is built on.

3. The targeted FOI list

Request 1 — CDPP (Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions)  ·  PRIORITY: HIGH
Why: the whole 'suitability' and stay architecture rests on the criminal matter against Vida. The AGS told the Tribunal (11 March 2025) there was no current criminal investigation; your own file suggests the position may not be that clean. CDPP records are the primary source that can confirm whether a prosecution was ever commenced, when and on what basis it was discontinued, and whether anything remains live.
Applicant: Vida Reid for her own personal information (strongest access right); a parallel request in Simon's name and for FFG-related entities.
Scope to request:
All records concerning Vida Reid (and any FFG-related entity) referred to the CDPP by the AFP or the NDIA arising from NDIS-related matters, 2021–2026.
Records evidencing the commencement, withdrawal, discontinuance, no-bill or decision not to prosecute — and the dates and reasons.
Any record showing the current status of any such matter (open/closed) as at the date of the request.
Correspondence between the CDPP and (i) the AFP, (ii) the NDIA, (iii) the NDIS Commission and (iv) the AGS concerning the Reids or FFG.
Anticipated exemptions: s 37 (law enforcement), s 42 (legal professional privilege), s 47E. Prosecutorial deliberations are heavily protected — so manage expectations: the realistic prize is the existence, dates and status of any matter plus Vida's own personal information, not the brief of evidence. Even a 'no documents / no current matter' answer is a usable admission.

4. Reality check — manage expectations
Do not let FOI become the strategy. It is the parallel track, for three reasons:
Speed. The 30-day statutory clock is routinely blown and OAIC IC review takes 12–24 months — your own MR24/00378 has sat since 2024. Lodge now precisely because the queue is long.
Exemptions. The richest material (Halls's comms, COI records, the TIR) attracts ss 37/42/47E/47F and is partly locked by the 26 July 2024 non-disclosure orders. Expect redactions, not a clean file.
Deflection. Agencies will argue you should seek documents relevant to a live matter through the Tribunal, not FOI.
So FOI earns its place by doing what process does not: it is cheap, it secures personal-information and whole-of-file material, it builds an independent record for the oversight/IC-review track, and every refusal or inconsistency between agencies feeds the inter-agency 'election' your offensive case relies on. The Halls documents themselves should be pursued primarily by ART summons (s 74) and, once the s 39B proceeding is on foot, Federal Court discovery (r 20.15 / r 20.23).
Standing caveat: nothing here concedes any wrongdoing by any applicant; the Commission's case is contested in its entirety. Connection between Halls and the destination providers is to be tested by these requests, not asserted as fact.
