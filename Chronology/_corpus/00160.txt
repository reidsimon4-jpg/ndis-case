# Strategic playbook

This document sits above the reasonableness memorandum, the CDPP draft letter and the working notes. Those are the inputs. This is how to use them. It is organised into four parts: the fundamental posture; the four phases of the strategy; the tactical principles; and the next seven days.

## Part 1 — The fundamental posture

The case is no longer a defensive merits review of a 2022 banning decision. It is an affirmative case about a five-year Commonwealth course of conduct against the Reid family, with the merits review as one of several vehicles. Every move from this point should be designed for *two audiences in mind* — the Administrative Review Tribunal in the immediate term, and the eventual tribunal of fact (a Federal Court hearing a misfeasance claim, the National Anti-Corruption Commission, or a criminal court hearing a permanent-stay application) in the medium to long term.

Five strategic propositions hold the posture together:

**1.1**  *Move from defensive to affirmative.* The applicants are no longer simply asking the Tribunal to set aside the banning orders. They are putting an affirmative case: that the Commonwealth's conduct since 2021 has been a sustained exercise of improper purpose involving multiple agencies, that the procedural fairness owed to the applicants has been denied at multiple stages, that the witness layer is materially compromised, and that the merits review itself has been conducted in circumstances that imperil its integrity. The Tribunal is asked to set aside on those broader grounds; if it does not, the Federal Court, NACC, and other forums are asked to address the broader picture.

**1.2**  *Force the Commonwealth to choose between disclosure and concession.* The Commonwealth has built its case on selective disclosure — the Tactical Intelligence Report withheld, Attachments O and G the subject of s 70 non-disclosure application, the identity of the "another agency" withheld under FOI s 37(2)(b). Each refusal is an admission that the underlying material would damage the Commonwealth's case if disclosed. The strategy is to confront the Commonwealth, in every available forum, with binary choices: either disclose and let the material be tested, or maintain non-disclosure and accept the inference. Both outcomes work for the applicants.

**1.3**  *Build documentary, not anecdotal, foundation.* Until 2 April 2026 the case rested on the applicants' first-person account supplemented by recordings (the Karen / Halls audio, the Maloney transcript) and contemporaneous third-party reports. From 2 April 2026 onwards the case has a Commonwealth-authored documentary anchor (the ABF FOI release). Every additional documentary item — FOI responses, APP 12 access decisions, CDPP correspondence, AGS verification responses, refusals themselves — strengthens the documentary foundation. The applicants' own narrative is the connecting tissue, not the skeleton.

**1.4**  *Sequence to maximise leverage.* The order in which the moves are made matters. Verification first, formal applications second, public referrals third. Each step produces material that strengthens the next. The strategy is not to fire every weapon at once but to escalate in a way that each escalation lands harder than the last because of what the previous step produced.

**1.5**  *Protect the source.* The 27 May 2026 informal report from the high-level government source is what triggered this re-assessment. The source's identity must not be disclosed in any external correspondence, application or referral. The verification work is designed to produce documentary confirmation that does not depend on the source. Once verification confirms (or fails to confirm), the source's value is preserved against future need.

## Part 2 — The four phases

### Phase one — Verification and foundation (next 30 days)

Goal: convert the 27 May 2026 informal report into a documentary record without exposing the source; establish the documentary footing for the waiver-revocation application; force the Commonwealth onto the record in correspondence.

**Phase one moves:**

(a)	*The file note of 27 May 2026.* Recording the source debrief, Vida's unimpeded re-entry from Bali, and the contemporaneous reasoning that triggered the re-assessment. Foundational document, costs nothing.

(b)	*The verification letter from solicitors to AGS.* Drafted in the six-question form developed in the chat — across all Commonwealth agencies, all applicants and entities, criminal vs intelligence/surveillance, brief-of-evidence status, and what enquiries AGS / the Commission actually made before signing the 11 March 2025 letter. Copied to the Tribunal. One to two hours of solicitor time.

(c)	*The CDPP letter (draft already prepared).* Updated to incorporate the ABF FOI release verbatim, the Dallimore 9 January 2025 contemporaneous statement, and the disabled child's documented presence at the September 2024 stop. Three to four hours of solicitor time to finalise and despatch.

(d)	*The holding letter to the Tribunal Member's chambers.* Flagging that material has come to the applicants' attention which, if confirmed, will require a waiver-revocation application; that AGS is being asked to confirm in writing; and that the substantive hearing should not be listed in the interim. Half an hour of solicitor time.

(e)	*FOI to the AFP* — for any referral, joint-agency or intelligence-sharing records concerning the applicants since 1 January 2024. Paralegal-rate work.

(f)	*FOI to the NDIA Fraud Fusion Taskforce* — for the FFT records concerning the applicants. Paralegal-rate work.

(g)	*APP 12 access requests* to Home Affairs, ABF, AFP, NDIS Commission, NDIA (in its FFT capacity), ATO and CDPP. Different exemptions to FOI, may produce material the FOI route does not. Paralegal-rate work.

(h)	*Documenting the disabled son's NDIS-funding cut.* Decision letters, plan documents, providers' refusals (in writing where available), and witness statements from both applicants on the care they have provided. Foundation evidence for the cumulative-harm picture and for the public-interest assessment in the CDPP letter.

(i)	*Continue and reinforce the OAIC IC review.* Follow up the s 54T extension status, prepare substantive submissions including the s 37(2)(b) doctrinal challenge (the exemption is for confidential informant sources, not inter-agency referrals).

(j)	*Continue the existing ART filings.* The 19 May 2026 s 70 opposition and the severance supplementary submissions are properly lodged and should be progressed; they remain procedural moves within the existing proceeding and the broader strategic shift does not displace them.

End of phase one deliverable: a documented file ready to support a strong waiver-revocation application and, if the AGS response demonstrates inaccuracy or inadequacy, a Federal Court interlocutory application as well.

### Phase two — Procedural applications (30 to 90 days)

Goal: convert the documentary foundation into formal procedural relief; restore the stay; preserve all rights in the ART; obtain interlocutory relief in the Federal Court if needed.

**Phase two moves:**

(a)	*The waiver-revocation application in the ART.* Foundation: the waiver was given in reliance on the AGS 11 March 2025 representation; that representation was on its narrow terms incomplete; the documentary record (ABF FOI release, Dallimore 9 January 2025 contemporaneous statement, Maloney 7 January 2025 transcript, source's 27 May 2026 information held in reserve) establishes that the activity excluded by the AGS representation was on foot at material times. Application asks the Tribunal to revoke the waiver and stay the merits review pending resolution of the criminal exposure.

(b)	*If the ART moves too slowly, the Federal Court interlocutory under the* Adachi *pathway.* Application for an interlocutory injunction restraining the Commission from enforcing or relying upon the banning orders pending judicial review, brought in the same proceeding as the substantive ADJR / s 39B application. Senior counsel decision.

(c)	*Notices to produce in the ART.* Requiring the Commission to produce records of any inter-agency referrals, intelligence-sharing, correspondence with ABF, AFP, ATO or the Fraud Fusion Taskforce concerning the applicants from 1 January 2024 to date. Difficult to resist on confidentiality grounds given the ABF FOI release is already public.

(d)	*The *Frugtniet*-based exclusion argument pre-positioned.* Notification to the Tribunal that if the Respondent attempts to rely on any material derived from the post-2022 multi-agency activity, the applicants will object on *Frugtniet* grounds (the primary decision-maker in December 2022 could not have considered material that did not then exist).

(e)	*Use the Phase one verification responses.* What AGS says, what the CDPP says, what the AFP discloses in its FOI response, what the APP 12 responses produce — each is fed into the Phase two applications either as evidence or as the basis for adverse inference.

End of phase two deliverable: stay restored (or refused on documented grounds that themselves become a forensic asset); interlocutory protection from enforcement obtained where appropriate; the documentary record formally on the Tribunal and (if applicable) Federal Court files.

### Phase three — Substantive engagement (3 to 9 months)

Goal: prosecute the substantive merits review on the strengthened grounds; commence parallel oversight referrals; develop the misfeasance file.

**Phase three moves:**

(a)	*The substantive ART hearing on the merits.* If the stay is granted in Phase two, this is suspended pending criminal-matter resolution. If not, the hearing proceeds — but with the documentary record from Phases one and two on the file, with the *Frugtniet*-based exclusion argument live, with the witness layer attack supported by the ABF FOI release as independent corroboration of the cross-agency conduct, and with the cumulative-conduct reasonableness analysis from the 27 May 2026 memorandum as an additional ground.

(b)	*Federal Court judicial review.* If the ART does not set aside on the merits, the Federal Court judicial-review proceeding proceeds. The grounds are improper purpose (including the continuing-conduct dimension), denial of procedural fairness (including the integrity-of-merits-review dimension), jurisdictional error (the ultra-vires variation of Mrs Reid's order), *Li* legal unreasonableness on the cumulative-conduct picture, and (if applicable) misleading representation to the Tribunal.

(c)	*NACC referral.* Brief the referral package — multi-agency, multi-year course of conduct; the Halls audio; the Maloney transcript; the Graydon offer to Shapiera; the ABF FOI release with the law-enforcement framing; the cumulative-conduct picture from the reasonableness memorandum. NACC threshold of "serious or systemic corrupt conduct" by Commonwealth public officials is squarely engaged.

(d)	*Parallel oversight referrals.* Commonwealth Ombudsman (law-enforcement-administrative pattern); Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security (telecommunications interception per the Maloney transcript); Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (Privacy Act breach pathway); Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (ES-22-0090 contract pathway); Inspector-General of Taxation (the $87,000 ATO anomaly); Australian Human Rights Commission (the William Cocks complaint is already underway — continue and reinforce).

(e)	*Develop the misfeasance file.* Senior counsel assessment of the *Mengel* / *Sanders v Snell* pathway against the named officers (Halls, Lundgren, Graydon, Maloney, and whoever in 2024–2026 has continued the cross-agency activity), with the Commonwealth joined vicariously. Not commenced as proceedings yet; the foundation is being built.

End of phase three deliverable: either substantive vindication in the ART or Federal Court; oversight referrals on foot in NACC, Ombudsman, IGIS, Privacy Commissioner; misfeasance file ready to convert to proceedings.

### Phase four — Affirmative proceedings (12+ months)

Goal: convert vindication and the documentary record into damages, declaratory relief, and (where appropriate) personal liability of the officers.

**Phase four moves:**

(a)	*Misfeasance proceedings* in the Federal Court (with appropriate state Supreme Court alternative) against the named individual officers, with the Commonwealth joined vicariously. Damages claim. Likely to settle if the foundation laid in Phases one to three is solid.

(b)	*Continued FOI and APP 12 pressure.* The documentary record continues to develop. Each new disclosure tightens the misfeasance case.

(c)	*Engagement with NACC and the other oversight bodies.* Long-cycle but high-impact.

(d)	*Permanent stay application.* If criminal charges are ever laid against either Vida or Simon, the permanent-stay application is on the file in the criminal court within days, on the foundation built across Phases one to three.

(e)	*Costs and compensation.* The Commonwealth model-litigant obligations, the abuse of process and the misfeasance exposure together create the financial leverage for resolution. The applicants are entitled to be made whole — for legal costs, for lost income, for the disabled son's denied supports, for the period of exile, for the destruction of business value. Phase four is also about quantification.

## Part 3 — Tactical principles

A short list of operating rules that govern every move:

**3.1**  *Document everything contemporaneously.* File notes dated and timed, on the day. Build the contemporaneous record so that nothing depends on memory.

**3.2**  *Use formal channels.* Every position the Commonwealth takes should be on the record in writing. Force the Commonwealth to commit, to refuse to commit, or to evade — and use each of those responses as a fact.

**3.3**  *Exploit the disclosure asymmetry.* The Commonwealth has consistently chosen non-disclosure where disclosure would damage its case. Make the pattern itself a forensic asset. Three separate refusals across three statutory regimes (TIR withheld; s 70 application on Attachments O and G; s 37(2)(b) on the FOI release) is itself the case.

**3.4**  *Multi-forum pressure.* No single forum is the answer. The ART, the Federal Court, the CDPP, the OAIC, the NACC, the Ombudsman, the IGIS, the Privacy Commissioner, the AHRC and (in due course) misfeasance proceedings are layered and mutually reinforcing.

**3.5**  *Documentary over anecdotal.* Where the same point can be made by reference to a recording, a transcript, an FOI release or an AGS letter, prefer the documentary source. Anecdotal evidence is the bridge between the documents, not the foundation.

**3.6**  *Cumulative over isolated.* Every brief and every submission should locate each individual fact in the cumulative pattern. Five years of multi-agency activity is the case; no single fact tells the whole story but each fact is part of the story.

**3.7**  *Reasonableness, not technicality.* The case should be put as a fundamental-fairness case. The legal doctrines (improper purpose, abuse of process, misfeasance, *Frugtniet*) are vehicles for the underlying proposition that a Commonwealth course of conduct of this kind, against a family that includes a disabled child whose supports were stripped as collateral damage, cannot stand. Technical procedural arguments support that frame; they do not replace it.

**3.8**  *Cost discipline.* Lawyer time is the most expensive input. Reserve it for what only a solicitor or counsel can do — the procedural applications, the substantive submissions, the senior-counsel advice. The verification, the FOI/APP 12 drafting, the file-note discipline, the documentary preservation are all done by the applicants themselves or at paralegal rates.

**3.9**  *Standing instructions on language.* No fabricated authority. No conceded fraud. No "even if" hypotheticals that imply guilt. The case is contested in its entirety.

**3.10**  *Protect the family.* The disabled child must not be identified in external materials beyond what is necessary. Vida and Simon's positions are not identical and where they diverge (Simon never charged; Vida charged then discontinued), the divergence must be preserved in submissions rather than papered over.

## Coda — the single decision that drives everything

The single decision that organises every move from here is whether the applicants want this matter resolved by negotiated Commonwealth concession or by litigation through to judgment. The strategy supports both. A Commonwealth that faces (a) a documented inconsistency between its representation to the Tribunal and the ABF's own records, (b) a documented multi-agency course of conduct since 2021, (c) a documented harm to a disabled child, (d) a documented permanent-stay application ready to be filed on day one of any future charging, (e) a documented misfeasance file being assembled, and (f) a documented set of oversight referrals in train, has a strong internal incentive to resolve. A Commonwealth that does not resolve will be tested in each of those forums to its conclusion. Either outcome works.

The applicants' choice is whether to signal, in due course, that resolution is welcome — and on what terms. That signal is for senior counsel to make at the appropriate point, ideally after the Phase one verification responses come back and the documentary record is at its strongest. It is the conversation the Commonwealth will want to have once the strategic picture has been put in front of it; the applicants' job is to ensure the picture is put in front of it clearly enough that the conversation happens.
