A. Introduction

C. Why direction 4 shortens rather than lengthens the proceeding

4.       At paragraph 38 of its decision the Tribunal identified three matters requiring findings:
         whether NDIS funding was claimed for services not provided; whether either individual
         applicant intimidated or harassed any participant; and whether either individual applicant
         contravened his or her banning order. The third of those matters is capable of
         determination on the documents alone.

5.       The banning order made against the Second Applicant, as varied, prohibits him from
         "having any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity". It particularises that
         prohibition as including "providing disability supports and services, directly or indirectly, to
         NDIS-funded participants" and "providing support, funding, guidance or assistance of any
         form to an NDIS provider". Contravention attracts a civil penalty of 1,000 penalty units.

6.       In Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards
         Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 at [127]–[141], Feutrill J held that a banning order in
         materially similar terms — prohibiting a person from being "directly or indirectly" involved
         in the provision of supports or services — was both beyond the power conferred by section
         73ZN(2) and, being analogous to an injunction carrying a substantial civil penalty, was not
         reasonably capable of being obeyed.

7.       An order which is not reasonably capable of being obeyed is not capable of being
         contravened. The Second Applicant does not ask the Tribunal, on the separate question, to
         determine whether the order exceeded the power conferred by section 73ZN(2). It is
         sufficient for present purposes that the Tribunal determine whether the order's terms are
         certain enough to sustain a finding of contravention. That is a narrower question and it
         requires no evidence.

8.       If the question is answered in the Second Applicant's favour, an entire limb of the final
         hearing falls away. No participant gives evidence on it. No finding as to the credit of any
         witness is required. If it is answered against him, nothing is lost: the issue is then
         determined once, early, and on a proper basis.

D. Why the schedule sought by direction 2 is necessary

9.       At paragraph 4 of its decision the Tribunal recorded that the Second Applicant was a
         director of Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd between 28 August 2020 and 3 January 2021,
         and again from 1 November 2022. At paragraph 34(a) the Tribunal observed that the two
         individual applicants held office "albeit not at the same time".

10.      Each of the claim periods listed at paragraph 35 of the decision falls between 3 January
         2021 and 1 November 2022 — that is, within the period during which the Second
         Applicant held no office in Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd. The earliest is February 2021
         and the latest concludes in August 2022.

11.      The decision records at paragraph 35 that claims were made by Forward Focused Group
         Pty Ltd "and" Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, but does not allocate any individual claim to either
         entity. The Respondent has not done so at any stage of this proceeding.

12.      Whether the Second Applicant held office in the entity said to have made a given claim, at
         the time it was made, is not a peripheral matter. Section 73ZN(2) requires the
         Commissioner to be satisfied that the particular person is not suitable to be involved in the
         provision of supports. That satisfaction must be formed by reference to material about
         that person. Without the allocation sought by direction 2, neither the Second Applicant nor
         the Tribunal can identify what material is said to bear on him personally.
E. The relief available if the Respondent's allegations are made out

13.         Section 73ZN(2) provides that the Commissioner may make a banning order. The power is
            discretionary as to whether an order is made and as to its scope and duration. As this
            Tribunal observed in Wright and Civil Aviation Safety Authority (Practice and procedure)
            [2025] ARTA 576, where a regulator's power is expressed as a power to vary, suspend or
            cancel, "even if the facts and circumstances are made out … in a final hearing, cancellation
            is not the only outcome".

14.         The Second Applicant will submit in the alternative that, if any allegation is made out, the
            appropriate outcome is variation of the order's scope and duration rather than affirmation.
            Two matters are presently relevant to that alternative case:

      (a)         The order made against the First Applicant on the same day, on the same material,
                  by the same delegate, preserves an exception permitting her to seek disability
                  supports or services for herself and a nominated family member. The order made
                  against the Second Applicant contains no exception of any kind. He is prohibited
                  from seeking disability supports for himself.

      (b)         On internal review the Third Applicant's banning order was reduced from ten years
                  to three, the delegate recording that three years "corresponds with the amount of
                  time it is reasonably expected to take for criminal proceedings relating to FFG to be
                  finally determined", and that "upon determination of those proceedings, the
                  banning order against FFG can be revisited". The criminal proceedings were
                  discontinued in February 2024. The order nonetheless ran to expiry on 20
                  December 2025 without being revisited. No comparable calibration appears on the
                  face of the order made against the Second Applicant.
