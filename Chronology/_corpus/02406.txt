1. Executive Summary
ILO submitted a price of IDR 777,204,312 for the Warehouse, Staff Room & Genset building at the Beach Shack Restaurant project in Pemuteran, North Bali. The client subsequently shared a competing RAB (Rencana Anggaran Biaya) from Tropland Studio / local contractor priced at IDR 526,000,000.
ILO's price is IDR 251,204,312 (47.8%) higher than the competitor. This report analyses the pricing gap category by category, identifies the key cost drivers, and presents a value engineering strategy to bring the ILO price into a competitive range while maintaining quality and build speed advantages.

2. Category-by-Category Price Comparison
The following table maps ILO's pricing categories against the competitor's RAB. Note that scope boundaries differ between the two quotes, so categories have been normalised for comparison where possible.

3. Key Cost Drivers — Where ILO is Higher
3.1 Structural Framing (+IDR 66M)
This is the single largest cost gap. ILO's full LGS framing system is priced at IDR 235M versus the competitor's hybrid approach (WF 100 columns, WF 150 trusses, C75 baja ringan purlins) at IDR 169M. The competitor's approach uses conventional hot-rolled steel for primary structure with light gauge only for secondary members. ILO's system is fully cold-formed LGS, which carries a material premium but offers faster erection, no site welding, and significantly lighter foundation loads.
3.2 Doors & Windows (+IDR 42M)
ILO's doors and windows are priced at IDR 84.5M — double the competitor's IDR 42.8M. The competitor uses a mix of timber kusen, basic aluminium frames, standard pintu kayu, and one pintu besi for the genset room. ILO's specification appears to include higher-grade hardware and potentially larger or more complex openings. This is a clear area for rationalisation.
3.3 Roofing & Ceiling (+IDR 176M standalone)
ILO separates roofing (IDR 167M) and ceiling (IDR 9M) as distinct line items totalling IDR 176M. The competitor bundles roofing into the steel/atap package at IDR 169M (which includes all structural steel). For a warehouse and staff building, ceiling is only needed in the staff and toilet areas — the warehouse and genset areas can be open to the roof structure. Removing unnecessary ceiling scope and simplifying the roof system offers significant savings.
3.4 MEP — Mechanical, Electrical & Plumbing (+IDR 21M)
ILO's combined MEP cost is IDR 71.3M versus the competitor's IDR 49.8M. The competitor's electrical scope includes 23 light points, 13 power points and basic switching — all at local market rates. ILO's higher MEP pricing may reflect a more comprehensive specification or imported fittings. For a utility building, basic local-sourced fittings are adequate.
4. Where ILO is More Competitive
4.1 Walls — Exterior & Interior (IDR 52M vs IDR 86M)
ILO's wall system saves IDR 35M compared to the competitor's traditional masonry approach. The competitor uses batako (concrete block) with plaster and aci finish for structural walls, plus kalsiboard on light gauge frames for partitions. This involves multiple wet trades (bricklaying, plastering, rendering, painting) which are slow, labour-intensive, and add significant time to the programme. ILO's dry-build panel system eliminates all wet wall trades, reduces construction time, and produces a more consistent finish.
4.2 Foundation (IDR 13.5M vs IDR 37M)
ILO's lighter structural system enables significant foundation savings. The competitor uses traditional batu kali (river stone) foundations with manual excavation and backfill — a labour-heavy process. ILO's engineered foundation system is designed to work with the lighter LGS superstructure, reducing both material volumes and installation time.
4.3 No Painting Required
The competitor's build requires IDR 19.6M in painting work (kalsiboard walls + timber polishing). ILO's pre-finished cladding and panel system eliminates this cost entirely — a direct saving plus reduced programme time.

5. Value Engineering Strategy
The following value engineering measures can bring the ILO price into direct competition while retaining the key advantages of the ILO system (speed, quality, dry construction). The target is to reduce the gap from 48% to under 10%.

After value engineering, ILO's revised price of IDR 604,204,312 is 14.9% above the competitor — a much more competitive position that can be justified by the following ILO advantages:
Faster construction programme: Dry-build system eliminates curing times and wet trade dependencies. Estimated 30-40% faster than traditional build.
Quality consistency: Factory-produced framing with precise tolerances vs. site-fabricated steel and hand-mixed concrete.
Reduced site labour: No welding, no bricklaying, no plastering crews required. Critical advantage in remote Pemuteran location where skilled tradespeople are scarce.
Lighter foundations: LGS is 60-70% lighter than conventional steel/masonry, reducing foundation costs and making construction on the coastal North Bali site easier.
No painting/finishing: Pre-finished systems avoid ongoing maintenance costs and eliminate a common quality control issue.

6. Recommendations & Next Steps
Present revised VE pricing to client at IDR 604,204,312, emphasising the speed, quality, and reduced site complexity advantages that justify the remaining premium over traditional construction.
Offer phased pricing — given Dominique mentioned building in phases (3-5 beach shacks first), offer the warehouse as a Phase 1A package with a volume discount tied to commitment on subsequent phases.
Highlight programme risk — client needs completion June/July 2026. Traditional construction with wet trades, batu kali foundations, and multi-trade sequencing carries significant programme risk in Pemuteran (remote location, monsoon season overlap). ILO's dry-build approach de-risks the timeline.
Request detailed competitor scope review — the competitor RAB does not appear to include site supervision, engineering certification, or warranty provisions. Clarify these exclusions with the client to ensure an apples-to-apples comparison.
Use this warehouse as a showcase — if ILO wins this package, the completed building becomes a live demonstration of the system for the larger resort development (25 beach shacks on the masterplan). Position the warehouse as a proof-of-concept with preferred pricing.
