Draft Instruments — bringing the NDIA, Halls and Lundgren into the picture
Companion to the “Defeating the Commission shield” memo — Reid v NDIS Commission (ART 2023/3361, 2023/3363, 2023/4494)
26 June 2026 · DRAFTS for settling by counsel · not legal advice · the author is not a lawyer
Read first — sequencing (FOI first, or lodge the case?). Neither as an either/or, and never let FOI gate the litigation. The ART matter is already on foot, so the strongest, fastest levers are available today: the s 74 summonses and the s 23 production demand below. Lodge the FOIs now too — they are cheap and slow, so run them as a background process, not a precondition. Build and file the Federal Court s 39B case on the limitation/leverage timetable (the misfeasance window runs to ~2028), using Federal Court Rules r 7.23 preliminary discovery — not FOI — for any litigation-critical gap. The “tipping your hand” concern is largely spent: your theory is already on the record in your ART materials. Bottom line: act through the live ART now; FOI in parallel; federal case prepared and filed deliberately.

Instrument 1 — Request to the Tribunal to issue summonses to produce (ART Act 2024, s 74)
To:  Brisbane Registry, Administrative Review Tribunal (brisbane.registry@art.gov.au)
Cc:  C. Land / K. Dickinson, Australian Government Solicitor; legal@ndiscommission.gov.au
From:  Simon Reid (2023/3363); Vida Reid (2023/3361); Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd (2023/4494)
Re:  Request under s 74 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 for issue of summonses to produce documents

Dear Registrar,
Pursuant to s 74 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth), the applicants request that the Tribunal issue summonses to produce documents to the persons identified below. There are reasonable grounds to believe each holds documents relevant to these proceedings: the Respondent’s own decision brief records that its evidence “draws upon statements obtained and provided by the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA),” that “the NDIA currently has an investigation ongoing,” and that “the Provider Exit Protocol has been actioned with the NDIA.” The reliability and independence of that NDIA-sourced material is squarely in issue (see the applicants’ Statement of Facts, Issues and Contentions).
Each summons is sought as a summons to produce documents only at this stage. The applicants foreshadow that, once produced documents are reviewed, they may seek summonses for Mr Halls and Ms Lundgren to give oral evidence. The applicants note the production day must be at least 14 days after a summons is given, that a recipient may produce in advance of any hearing, and that inspection is governed by s 78.
Draft schedules of documents for each addressee follow. The applicants are content for the Tribunal to settle scope and to adopt the standard ART summons form.

Instrument 2 — Demand for full lodgement of relevant documents (ART Act 2024, s 23)
To:  C. Land / K. Dickinson, Australian Government Solicitor (for the Respondent)
Cc:  Brisbane Registry, ART; legal@ndiscommission.gov.au
Re:  Respondent’s duty under s 23 — NDIA-sourced material relied on in the reviewable decisions

Dear Colleagues,
Under s 23 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (Cth), the Respondent must give the Tribunal every document in its possession or control relevant to the review. On the Respondent’s own decision brief, its evidence “draws upon statements obtained and provided by the NDIA,” the “Provider Exit Protocol has been actioned with the NDIA,” and disclosure was routed to the NDIA’s A/Director, Fraud Investigation. That NDIA-sourced material is not collateral — it is the foundation of the decisions under review — and is therefore within the lodgement duty.
The applicants require, within 14 days:
(a) a supplementary lodgement of every document the Respondent received from, or exchanged with, the NDIA that bears on the banning decisions, including the statements “obtained and provided by the NDIA,” the Statement of Material Facts (19 October 2022), the Tactical Intelligence Report (18 August 2022), and the Provider Exit Protocol;
(b) a schedule identifying, for any such document withheld, the document and the precise basis on which it is said to fall outside s 23 or to be exempt; and
(c) confirmation of whether the eight categories of the applicants’ documents acknowledged on 7 May 2026 to have been “inadvertently omitted” from the certified tender bundle have since been lodged, and if not, when.
This request is made having regard to the Respondent’s model-litigant obligations. The applicants reserve the right to seek directions and to rely on any non-compliance.

Instrument 3 — The “own-or-disclaim” election letter
To:  C. Land / K. Dickinson, Australian Government Solicitor (for the Respondent)
Cc:  Brisbane Registry, ART; legal@ndiscommission.gov.au
Re:  Reliance on NDIA material and NDIA conduct — request to confirm the Respondent’s position

Dear Colleagues,
The applicants write to ascertain the Respondent’s position on a matter central to the review: whether the Respondent adopts, as reliable and independent, the NDIA-sourced material on which its decisions rest. The request is put narrowly and for a procedural purpose — to determine whether the Respondent’s case depends on NDIA conduct that was unauthorised, improper, contradictory or materially incomplete.
Two matters on the documents require the Respondent’s position:
(1) The internal NDIA contradiction. Mr Halls is recorded directing a freeze of FFG-linked payments and installing a competitor “at the request of the agency,” while Ms Lundgren wrote on 6 October 2022 that “the Agency has not directed Assist Plan Managers to cease all payments.” Was Mr Halls’s conduct authorised by the NDIA, or not?
(2) Adoption of the NDIA material. Does the Respondent maintain that the evidence “obtained and provided by the NDIA,” generated during a “coordinated provider exit,” is independent and reliable for the purpose of establishing present unsuitability?
Please confirm the Respondent’s position on each within 14 days. If the Respondent maintains reliance on the NDIA material, the applicants will seek the s 74 summonses foreshadowed and will put the reliability and independence of that material squarely in issue, including the contradiction above and the Respondent’s 11 March 2025 representation regarding the absence of a current criminal investigation. The applicants reserve all rights, including as to costs and as to other proceedings.
