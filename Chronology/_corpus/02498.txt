Construction Contract – Wedding
Warehouse Project (Bali)



This Construction Contract (the “Contract”) is made and entered into as of (28/01/2026), by and
between:
       ●​ Infinity Living Indonesia, a construction services company organized under the laws of
           Indonesia, with address at Jl. Drupadi 1 No.11A, Seminyak, Kuta, Badung, Bali 80361,

       ●​ Ms. Tristan Moy, owner of the Bali Wedding Warehouse project, residing in Bali, Indonesia

Project Description: The Client desires to have a warehouse (the “Wedding Warehouse”) constructed
on her property in Kedampang, Bali, Indonesia. The Contractor agrees to perform the construction
and associated works for this warehouse as described in the Statement of Work (Appendix A) and
under the terms and conditions of this Contract.
Both parties hereby agree to the following:

Article 1 – Scope of Work

1.1 Contract Documents: The scope of work is defined by the Statement of Work (SOW) (Appendix A)
and the project Execution Plan (Appendix B), which are incorporated herein by reference. These
documents detail the project specifications, including the construction of an 11m x 15m light-gauge
steel frame warehouse with concrete slab foundation, installation of a septic system, and drilling of a
water bore. In case of any inconsistency among contract documents, this Contract and its terms shall
prevail, followed by the SOW.


1.2 Work Included: The Contractor shall furnish all labor, materials, equipment, and supervision
necessary to complete the Wedding Warehouse construction project as per the agreed design and
specifications. Major tasks include (but are not limited to): fabricating and erecting the steel structure,
constructing the reinforced concrete slab foundation, site preparation, installation of the septic waste
treatment system, drilling and equipping a water bore well, and coordinating all related works. All work

Article 3 – Project Schedule

3.1 Commencement Date: Work under this Contract shall commence as soon as practicable after
contract signing and receipt of any required initial payments and permits. The Contractor shall not be
obligated to mobilize to site until the agreed Fabrication Deposit (if not already paid) is received, to
ensure materials can be secured.


3.2 Completion Date: The Contractor shall endeavour to achieve Substantial Completion of the work
by 01/06/2026, provided that there are no major scope changes or delays beyond the Contractor’s
control. Substantial Completion is defined as the stage when the building is structurally complete,
utilities installed, and the warehouse is usable for its intended purpose, notwithstanding minor
outstanding items or defects that do not materially affect use.


3.3 Handover and Final Completion: Upon reaching Substantial Completion, a handover inspection
will be conducted (see also Execution Plan). Any punch list items noted shall be addressed by the
Contractor within a reasonable time. Final Completion is achieved when all punch list items are
resolved. The Target date for this will be 01/06/2026.


3.4 Schedule Delays: Both parties acknowledge that the schedule in this Contract is based on current
information and assumptions (weather, permit timing, etc.). The Contractor shall provide timely notice
to the Client if any event is likely to delay the work. Where delays are attributable to the Contractor (or
its subcontractors) – for example, slow performance without cause – the Contractor will accelerate
work at its cost or take mitigating steps to regain schedule. Where delays are caused by Client
changes or lack of Client action (e.g., late approvals, delayed payments), or by force majeure events
(see Article 7.4), the Contractor shall be entitled to a reasonable extension of time and/or
compensation as appropriate. Any extension of the Completion Date will be documented in writing
(e.g., via Change/Variation Order or contract amendment).

Article 4 – Quality of Work and Warranty

4.1 Workmanship and Quality: The Contractor warrants that all work performed shall conform to the
standards of quality specified in the contract documents and shall be free of material defects. All
materials used will be new and of good quality, suitable for their intended purpose, and all installation
will be carried out by skilled workers. The project will be executed in compliance with all applicable
Indonesian construction standards and regulations.


4.2 Inspection and Acceptance: The Client (or the Client’s authorized representative) has the right to
inspect the work at reasonable intervals. The Contractor shall facilitate access to the site and provide
necessary information for such inspections. If any work or materials are found not to conform to the
Contract requirements, the Contractor shall promptly repair, replace, or re-perform the work to meet
specifications at no additional cost to the Client. Final acceptance of the work occurs upon the
successful joint inspection and handover (Substantial Completion) as described in Article 3.3,
notwithstanding latent defects that may be discovered later under warranty.


4.3 Warranty Period: The Contractor shall provide a warranty for a period of 12 (twelve) months from
the date of handover (the “Warranty Period”). During this Warranty Period, the Contractor agrees to
repair or rectify, at its own cost, any defects in workmanship or materials that may arise in the
completed work, provided that such defects are not due to misuse, abuse, or alterations by the Client
or third parties after handover. For example, if cracks appear in the slab due to normal settling or any
leaks are found in roof connections within the Warranty Period, the Contractor will fix them promptly
without charge.


4.4 Warranty Claims: The Client shall notify the Contractor in writing of any observed defect within
the Warranty Period. The Contractor shall respond within 7 days and send appropriate personnel to
inspect and schedule repairs within a reasonable time frame agreed with the Client. Urgent
safety-related defects will be addressed immediately. If the Contractor fails to address a valid
warranty claim within a reasonable time, the Client may proceed to have the defect repaired by
others and charge the Contractor for reasonable costs, provided the Client has given a second
written notice and a further grace period of 14 days for the Contractor to respond.

Article 5 – Changes in Work (Variations)

5.1 Change Orders/Variations: Any change to the scope of work, whether initiated by the Client or
necessitated by unforeseen conditions, shall be managed through a written Change Order (also
called a Variation Order). This includes additions, deletions, or revisions to the work described in the
SOW or project specifications. Both parties must mutually agree upon all Change Orders, including
any adjustments to contract price or schedule.


5.2 Client-Initiated Changes: If the Client requests a change (for example, a design modification,
additional feature, or different material than originally specified), the Client shall notify the Contractor
with details of the desired change. The Contractor will promptly assess the feasibility and prepare a
written proposal outlining any price adjustment (added cost or savings) and time impact associated
with the change. The Client can then decide whether to proceed with the change. Only upon the
Client’s written approval of the Change Order will the Contractor implement the change. The Contract
Price and/or Completion Date shall be adjusted accordingly in that Change Order.


5.3 Contractor-Initiated Changes: If the Contractor determines that a change is advisable or
necessary (e.g., due to site conditions or technical issues – for instance, if actual soil conditions
require a different foundation solution), the Contractor will explain the reasoning to the Client and
propose a Change Order with the adjustment in scope, cost, or time. The Client’s approval is required
before the Contractor deviates from the original scope, except in an emergency as described in 5.4.


5.4 Emergency Changes: In the event of an emergency that impacts safety or would cause
significant damage to the work or property if not immediately addressed (for example, urgent
stabilization of a collapsing excavation), the Contractor is authorized to take necessary measures
without prior approval. The Contractor will make all reasonable attempts to inform the Client as soon
as possible. Any such work will be documented after the fact, and a retrospective Change Order will
be prepared to cover any cost/schedule implications, to be mutually agreed in good faith.

5.5 Pricing of Changes: Change Order pricing shall be based on applicable unit rates from the
original contract (if available) or, where not applicable, on a fair valuation by the Contractor with

price/time easily, they may mutually agree to engage an independent estimator or negotiate in good

Article 6 – Terms of Contract Completion and Risk
Transfer

6.1 Handover and Acceptance: Upon Substantial Completion, the Contractor will hand over the
project to the Client as detailed in Article 3. At that point, the risk and responsibility for the building
transfers to the Client, except for those obligations covered under the warranty. Both parties will sign a
Handover Certificate or Minutes of Handover Meeting to mark the date of transfer. If the Client begins
to use the warehouse (or any part thereof) before formal handover, that portion will be deemed
handed over for the purposes of risk and insurance.




6.2 Indemnity: The Contractor agrees to indemnify and hold the Client harmless from any third-party
claims, liabilities, costs, or suits (including reasonable legal fees) arising out of the Contractor’s or its
subcontractors’ negligence or willful misconduct during the execution of the work. This includes claims
related to workplace injuries (to the extent not covered by worker’s insurance) or damage to
neighboring properties caused by construction activities. Conversely, the Client shall indemnify the
Contractor against claims or liabilities arising from the Client’s own negligence, misuse of the site, or
third-party actions under the Client’s control. Each party shall promptly notify the other of any claim
and cooperate in the defense.

Article 7 – General Provisions

7.1 Governing Law: This Contract shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of
the Republic of Indonesia.


7.2 Dispute Resolution: In the event of any dispute or claim arising out of or relating to this Contract,
the parties shall first seek to resolve it amicably through direct discussions and negotiation in good
faith. If such negotiation does not resolve the dispute within a reasonable time (for instance, 30 days
from the first written notice of dispute), the parties agree to submit the dispute to mediation under a
mutually agreed independent mediator in Indonesia. If mediation fails or is not pursued, the dispute
shall be finally settled by the competent courts of Indonesia, specifically the District Court of
Denpasar, Bali, which both parties agree shall have jurisdiction. (Alternatively, if both parties prefer
arbitration, this clause can be replaced with arbitration under BANI rules or similar – but as of now,
the default is litigation in local courts.)


7.3 Language and Contract Copies: This Contract is made in the English language for convenience of
the parties. However, as required by Indonesian Law No. 24 of 2009, an official version in Bahasa
Indonesia shall be prepared and signed by both parties as soon as practicable. Both language
versions will be of equal legal force, but in the event of any discrepancy or dispute in interpretation,
the Indonesian version shall prevail (per Indonesian law requirements). The Contractor will assist in
procuring a certified translation if necessary. This Contract may be executed in multiple counterparts
and by electronic signature or PDF, which shall all be deemed originals. Each party shall receive a fully
signed copy for their records.


7.4 Force Majeure: Neither party shall be liable for delays or failure in performance (other than
payment obligations) caused by events beyond their reasonable control and not foreseeable at the
time of Contract (force majeure events). Such events include, but are not limited to: natural disasters
(e.g., earthquakes, floods), wars, riots, civil disturbances, epidemics/pandemics, government actions
or orders (e.g., permit moratoriums, sudden regulatory changes), or unforeseen extreme material
shortages. The affected party shall notify the other in writing as soon as possible describing the event
and its expected impact. The obligations of the affected party shall be suspended to the extent and
for the duration that they are affected by the force majeure. The Contract schedule (and Price, if costs
are affected) shall be equitably adjusted. If a force majeure event continues for an extended period
(e.g., more than 60 days) making the project’s completion impracticable, the parties will discuss
possible contract termination or renegotiation.
