The Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission
The office of the Commissioner is established by the NDIS Act. The Commissioner performs
regulatory functions in relation to the provision of disability supports and services to people with
disability.
The regulatory functions of the Commissioner include:
       upholding the rights of, and promoting the health, safety and wellbeing of, people with
        disability receiving supports and services, including those received under the NDIS;
       regulating NDIS providers, including through the application of Practice Standards and a
        Code of Conduct;
       compliance monitoring;
       investigation of non-compliance and taking appropriate enforcement action; and
       managing and resolving complaints from participants, carers, family members, advocates
        and others.
Outline of relevant statutory provisions
Section 73ZN of the NDIS Act enables the Commissioner (or the Commissioner’s delegate) to make
an order known as a banning order. Relevantly for this matter, a banning order made under section
73ZN(2) can have the effect of prohibiting or restricting specified activities of a person who is or was
employed or otherwise engaged by an NDIS provider, or a person who is or was a member of the key
personnel of an NDIS provider.
Section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) allows the Commissioner or the Commissioner’s delegate to make a banning
order if she or he reasonably believes that a person is not suitable to be involved in the provision of
supports or services to people with disability.

Decision to issue a banning order
Based on my assessment of the submissions made by Tom and Co Legal on your behalf, I am not
persuaded to change my preliminary views that a banning order be issued against you. Nor do the
submissions persuade me to reconsider any factual findings on which I formed my preliminary views.
More specifically:

      On 22 November 2022, your solicitors, Tom and Co Legal, wrote to the Commission to
       request an extension of time to provide submissions on your behalf. On 2 December 2022,
       the Commission responded to this request, providing an extension of a further 14 days from
       the original due date.
       Tom and Co Legal provided submissions on your behalf on 30 November 2022, in which they
       stated that they considered the timeframe ‘unreasonable’ and the Commission had
       ‘compromised any procedural fairness’. These concerns were repeated in Tom and Co
       Legal’s response to the Commission’s letter of 2 December 2022.
       I note that you have chosen not to avail yourself of the extension of time provided on 2
       December 2022. I consider that procedural fairness has been provided.
      The Commission is not constrained from making banning orders where related criminal
       proceedings are on foot. I note that the outcome of the criminal proceedings against V REID
       may be relevant to an application to vary or revoke the banning order.
       Further and whilst I acknowledge that these matters are currently before the court, I
       consider that the evidence before me and set out in this statement of reasons, satisfies me
    that fraudulent conduct has been engaged in, including through an entity for which you have
    responsibility and authority as co-director and sole director, and for which you have
    benefited as a joint signatory to the accounts in which payments were made in response to
    falsified claims.

   I consider that the charges against V REID are relevant to my considerations in making a
    banning order against you, as the basis for those charges references you and Hybrid Hive Pty

   The conduct set out above, occurred during the periods in which you were both a co-
    director and sole director of Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd and therefore had responsibility and
    authority for (or significant influence over) planning, directing or controlling its activities.
    It is my view that the conduct set out above, engaged in Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, strongly
    suggests that you are not suitable to provide supports or services to people with disability
    for the following reasons:
        o   Section 3 of the NDIS Act contains the objects of the NDIS Act, which includes:
                  i. supporting the independence and social and economic participation of
                     people with disability;
                 ii. enabling people with disability to exercise choice and control in the pursuit
                     of their goals and the planning and delivery of their supports;
                iii. promoting the provision of high quality and innovative supports that enable
                     people with disability to maximise independent lifestyles and full inclusion in
                     the community; and
                iv. protecting and prevent people with disability from experiencing harm arising
                    from poor quality or unsafe supports or services provided under the NDIS.
        o   In giving effect to the objects of the NDIS Act:
                  i.   section 3(3)(b) of the NDIS Act requires regard to be had to the need to
                       ensure the financial sustainability of the NDIS. Similarly, as set out in
                       section 4(17) of the NDIS Act, it is the intention of Parliament that the
                       Commissioner, amongst others, have regard to the need to ensure the
                       financial sustainability of the NDIS in performing functions and exercising
                       powers under the NDIS Act;
                 ii.   sections 4(4) and 4(6) of the NDIS Act, provide principles guiding actions
                       under the NDIS Act including:
                          People with disability should be supported to exercise choice,
                           including in relation to taking reasonable risks, in the pursuit of their
                           goals and the planning and delivery of their supports; and
                          People with disability have the same right as other members of
                           Australian society to respect for their worth and dignity and to live
                           free from abuse, neglect and exploitation.
            o   It is my view based on the conduct set out above that you and Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd,
                an entity for which you have responsibility and authority, have acted in a manner
                that is contrary to these guiding principles, as well as the objects of the NDIS Act,
                and in circumstances have deliberately undermined those principles. More
                specifically:

                           nominees;
                     ii.   Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd, an entity for which you have responsibility and

                           driving a culture of inflating claims and hours for services delivered,

        Further, the conduct set out above and the offences for which V REID has been charged and
        you involved, presents significant risks to the financial sustainability of the NDIS, as well as to
        NDIS participants, as victims of fraud. Such conduct takes funds from NDIS participant plans,
        stopping participants from being able to purchase the supports and service they genuinely
        need. Further, fraud makes it harder for participants to pursue their goals and puts
        additional pressures on carers, family, friends and support networks.
Therefore, I have formed the view that allowing you to continue to provide supports or services to
people with disability poses a continuing risk to people with disability. My view is that is that the
need to protect the interests of people with disability, who may be particularly vulnerable, and
ensure that they are kept safe, requires that in matters of this kind, a person who has engaged and
been involved in the conduct set out above, as well as have authority and responsibility for an entity
that has engaged in serious wrongdoing as set out above, must be prevented from delivering
supports and services to people with disability. My view is that you are not currently suitable to
provide supports or services to people with disability.
I therefore believe that it is appropriate to issue a banning order prohibiting you from engaging in
any activities related to the provision of supports or services to NDIS participants for a period of five
(5) years, in accordance with section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS Act.
I have taken into account the purposes of a banning order which are to protect the public from harm
by removing those who are unsuitable or not competent to provide disability services and supports;
and, generally deter misconduct and incompetence through permanent or temporary exclusion from
providing disability services and supports.
