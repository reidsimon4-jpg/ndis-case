Interlocutory Stay Application - ongoing
   • On 11 October 2023, the Respondent lodged an interlocutory application, requesting a stay of all
       three AAT proceedings pending the outcome of the criminal proceedings against Mrs Reid.
   • On 17 January 2024, the parties attended an interlocutory hearing with respect to staying the
       current proceedings. The Tribunal made Directions for Ms Vida Reid and FFG applications to be
       adjourned pending the termination or resolution of the criminal proceedings against Ms Vida Reid.
    ▪ Ms Vida Reid and Forward Focus Group do not oppose the application for a stay in relation to
        their application.
    ▪ In their submissions, Mr Reid his application to proceed and opposes the stay application due to
        the following reasons:
             o Mr Reid has suffered and continues to suffer financial detriment. A stay will be to his
                 financial prejudice.
             o Mr Reid has suffered reputational damage and an indefinite stay will prevent him having
                 the opportunity to clear his name
             o Mr Reid wishes to hold the Commissioner to account.
    ▪ The Respondent submits that:
             o [8]-[9] of submissions filed 28 Feb 2024:
             o the prospect of the applicant being required to give evidence, and being subject to cross-
                 examination, in relation to the same transactions which would be the subject of the
                 possible criminal proceedings is sufficient to create a plausible apprehension of injustice
                 and therefore contempt of any criminal proceedings which may be commenced”
             o given the same factual circumstance will need to be considered by the Tribunal in
                 determining whether the banning order made against Simon Reid is reasonable, there is
                 a significant risk that “potential damage…..could be caused to criminal proceedings if the
                 jury were to learn that this Tribunal had made findings against the applicant….”

II. DECISION UNDER REVIEW
     ▪ 2023/3361 (Ms Reid): By a reviewable decision dated 21 April 2023, the NDIS Quality and
       Safeguards changed the legislative basis and extended the scope of the original banning order
       imposed on Mrs Reid.
     ▪ 2023/4494 (FFG): By a reviewable decision dated 8 June 2023, the NDIS Quality and Safeguards
       varied its original decision, reducing a 10-year banning order to 3 years against Forward Focused
       Group.
     ▪ 2023/3363 (Mr Reid): By a reviewable decision dated 21 April 2023, the NDIS Quality and
       Safeguards extended the scope of the original banning order imposed on Mr Reid, to make it
       explicit that Mr Reid should have no involvement with the NDIS sector.

III. FACTUAL BACKGROUND

2023/4494:
   • On 20 December 2022, the original decision-maker made a banning order against FFG, prohibiting
      it from providing supports and services, directly or indirectly, to NDIS-funded participants for a
      period of 10 years, under section 73ZN(1)(a) of the NDIS Act, on the basis that the registration of
      FFG as a registered NDIS provider had been revoked. The banning order took effect from 5:30pm
      (AWST) on 20 December 2022.
   • FFG was formerly a registered NDIS provider operating in Western Australia, until 20 December
      2022 when a delegate of the NDIS Commissioner revoked the registration of FFG.
   • In relation to FFG, Mrs Reid was:
                      i. i. the co-director from 28 August 2020 to 3 January 2021;
                     ii. ii. the sole director from 4 January 2021 to 20 October 2022; and
                    iii. iii. the co-director from 21 October 2022 to 1 November 2022.

NOTES TO DIRECTION

  1. If you do not comply with a direction, the Tribunal will list the application for a
     directions hearing. You will be required to attend the directions hearing and
     explain why you have not complied with the direction.

  2. The Tribunal can dismiss an application if an Applicant fails within a
     reasonable time to comply with a direction made by the Tribunal. This power
     is set out in section 42A(5)(b) of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act
     1975. If you are the Applicant and you fail to comply with a direction, you may
     also be asked to explain at the directions hearing why your application should
     not be dismissed.

  3. If a party fails to comply with this direction, the Tribunal will not necessarily
     decide to adjourn, or delay the listing of, an alternative dispute resolution
     process or hearing.

  4. Where the Tribunal has the power to award or recommend the payment of
     costs, failure by a party to comply with this direction may be taken into
     account in making a decision relating to costs.

  5. If you do not believe you will be able to comply with a timeframe or any other
     aspect of this direction, you must make a request to the Tribunal for an
     extension of time to comply or for a variation of the direction. You should
     make the request well before the date by which you are required to comply
     with the direction.

     Unless the Tribunal allows otherwise, the request must:
     a. be in writing
     b. explain the reasons for requesting more time or for the variation, and
     c. tell us whether or not the other party agrees to the request.
     The Tribunal may ask you or the other party for more information, list a
     directions hearing or decide the request on the papers.

  6. If you have been directed to give the Tribunal a Hearing Certificate, in
     accordance with the General Practice Direction, the Tribunal may list your
     application for hearing without further consultation if you do not provide the
     Hearing Certificate by the specified date.
