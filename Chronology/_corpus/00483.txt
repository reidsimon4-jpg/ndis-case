A.  Position
1.	This response is filed by the Second Applicant, Mr Simon Reid, in matter 2023/3363. It does not state the position of, and does not bind, the First or Third Applicants, each of which responds separately.
2.	The Second Applicant notes the Tribunal's decision dated 31 July 2026 that the applications proceed together and will proceed in accordance with it. He supports the matters proceeding to a final hearing without further delay, and agrees with the broad steps in the Respondent's minute of 14 August 2026 (a supplementary tender bundle, SFICs with evidence, and a final hearing), subject to the sequence and content proposed below.
3.	Nothing in this response, or omitted from it, is an admission of any fact or contention advanced by the Respondent, and nothing in it waives any privilege.
B.  Timing
4.	The order under review was made on 21 December 2022 and varied on internal review on 21 April 2023. The varied order runs until 12.00am on 22 December 2027 (AWST). (The decision records at [10] an operative period ending on 22 December 2025; the Second Applicant notes that the varied order runs to 22 December 2027.) The internal review fixed that period to correspond with “the current regulatory and legal processes that are in motion in relation to Mr Reid”, and stated that upon their determination the order “can be revisited” (at [47]). The Second Applicant has not been charged with any offence. The order has not been revisited.
5.	The present position is as follows:
(a)	The Respondent's application of 7 May 2026 under section 70 of the Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024 (ART Act) remains undetermined. The Second Applicant filed submissions in response on 19 May 2026.
(b)	At [41] of its decision the Tribunal directed the Respondent to file and serve a proposed timetable within 14 days and indicated that the applications would then be relisted for a telephone directions hearing. The Respondent served its minute on 14 August 2026. No directions hearing has yet been listed.
(c)	On 19 March 2026 the Respondent informed the Tribunal that documents relating to the Second Applicant “were not included in the previous tender bundle” (submissions at [15]), and proposed to file an updated tender bundle “consisting of all documents required to be produced under section 23” of the ART Act (at [18.1]). Its minute proposed that the bundle be filed by 28 August 2026. No order has been made, and the Second Applicant has not received the bundle.
(d)	The minute proposed that the applicants file their updated statements of facts, issues and contentions (SFICs) and evidence by 25 September 2026 — four weeks after a bundle that has not been served, before the section 70 application is determined, and before the Respondent has identified the case it relies on against each applicant. No order was made in those terms.
(e)	On 2 November 2023 the Respondent submitted that it would “be happy to work on an expedited timetable to progress these matters when the current (and any reasonably possible) criminal proceedings are concluded” (submissions at [23]).
6.	The procedural history includes delay from a number of causes. The Second Applicant does not seek to revisit it. The Second Applicant asks for a timetable with firm dates that allows the review to be heard and decided well before December 2027, when the individual orders against the First and Second Applicants expire.
C.  The findings the Tribunal must make, and the case the Second Applicant must meet
7.	At [38] of its decision the Tribunal identified that it will need to consider whether each applicant is a suitable person to be involved in the provision of services to people with disability under section 73ZN(2) of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (NDIS Act), which will require findings relating to the allegations, including (a) whether any NDIS funding was made or received for services that had not been provided to the participant; (b) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had intimidated or harassed any participant; and (c) whether Mr Reid or Mrs Reid had contravened their respective banning orders.
8.	The matters summarised at [35] to [37] of the decision are, as [38] records, allegations; in summarising them the Tribunal was assisted by the Respondent's summary (at [33]). The Second Applicant disputes them in so far as they are relied on against him.
9.	The question at [38] is the suitability of each applicant. The matters at [38](b) and (c) are expressly personal. The matter at [38](a) concerns claims made by companies. For it to bear on the Second Applicant's suitability, the Tribunal would need to be satisfied, on cogent evidence, not only that particular claims were made for services that were not provided, but also what, if anything, the Second Applicant personally did or failed to do in relation to them. The Commission's investigation note recorded: “Despite emails for FFG and The Hive being directed to or including Simon Reid the Commission has limited information about his actual involvement in the operations.” (tender T9.4, p 178). The Commission's Banning Order Policy, to which the Tribunal may have regard, states that a member of the key personnel of a provider “would be unlikely to receive a banning order, unless either directly involved in the conduct of concern or having failed to take appropriate steps for which they were responsible which significantly facilitated, contributed to or exacerbated the conduct” (tender T6, p 142).
10.	The claim totals in the briefing notes are drawn from Attachment J (the Tactical Intelligence Report), which is withheld under the orders of 26 July 2024 (see EC22-002521 at [3.3]). The Second Applicant has not identified, in the material served on him, any document that reconciles the claims relied on against him with the service records for those claims. He does not ask the Tribunal to limit the material the Respondent may rely on. He asks that the Respondent identify, before he files his evidence, the claims and conduct it relies on against him and the evidence for each, so that the hearing addresses a defined case rather than allegations at large.
D.  Documents
11.	The Respondent's letter of 7 May 2026 records that it has not provided, among others, Attachments A to G and Q to briefing note EC22-002103 (on the basis that they relate solely to unrelated companies) and Attachment P to that briefing note (described as an internal email chain assessed as not relevant). The briefing note itself records Attachment B as the notice of intention issued to Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd (the entity through which conduct is attributed to the Second Applicant), Attachment P as recording the NDIA's notes on the use of its evidence (at [31]), and Attachment Q as the risk assessment concerning Hybrid Hive Pty Ltd (at [33.1]). Briefing note EC22-002521 also refers to an Attachment P (at [16]), which the letter does not address.
12.	On 12 September 2023 the Tribunal ordered (order 2) that, “by reason of current criminal proceedings and/or legal professional privilege”, the Respondent was not required to give the applicants the documents in the bundle marked “Index – Confidential Tribunal Documents”. The Respondent had submitted that it “anticipates regularly reviewing the documents set out in the confidential index with a view to disclosing them if and/or when, the grounds for this application fall away” (submissions of 12 September 2023 at [23]). The only criminal proceedings identified in the material were discontinued on 23 February 2024. The Second Applicant asks that the Respondent now state which of those documents remain withheld and on what basis.
13.	Section 23 of the ART Act requires the decision-maker to give the Tribunal every document in its possession or control that is relevant to the review. The Second Applicant does not ask that any document be disclosed to him in breach of an existing order. He asks that the documents be before the Tribunal and described sufficiently for relevance or restriction to be decided by the Tribunal.
E.  Form of any order
14.	In Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 the Federal Court held that section 73ZN(2) does not authorise an order prohibiting a person from being indirectly involved in specified activities (at [128], [134] and [139]), and that the specified activities must be expressed in terms reasonably capable of being obeyed (at [131]). The order under review prohibits “any involvement with or in the NDIS sector, in any capacity”, including providing supports and services “directly or indirectly”. The Second Applicant asks that the Respondent state in advance the order it will seek, and whether it relies on the provisions inserted into section 73ZN of the NDIS Act in 2026. He reserves his position on those questions.
F.  Directions sought
D1.	Directions hearing. The applications be listed for the telephone directions hearing foreshadowed at [41] of the decision dated 31 July 2026 at the earliest available date.
D2.	Section 70 application. The Respondent's application of 7 May 2026 be determined at the directions hearing after brief oral submissions, the Tribunal being at liberty to inspect the documents.
D3.	Documents. Within 14 days, the Respondent:
(a)	give the Tribunal Attachments B, P and Q to briefing note EC22-002103 and Attachment P to briefing note EC22-002521, to the extent not already given;
(b)	state which documents in the bundle marked “Index – Confidential Tribunal Documents” remain withheld under order 2 of 12 September 2023 and on what basis, and serve any document withheld only by reason of criminal proceedings that are no longer current; and
(c)	file and serve a list describing each document it withholds from the Second Applicant (date, author, recipient, general nature) and the basis for withholding it.
Any applicant may respond within 7 days of service of the list, and any dispute be determined at a case event.
D4.	Supplementary tender bundle. Within 14 days of the determination of the section 70 application, the Respondent lodge and serve its supplementary tender bundle consisting of all documents required to be produced under section 23 of the ART Act, subject to any order of the Tribunal.
D5.	Delegations. Within 14 days, the Respondent serve the instrument of delegation under which each decision of 21 December 2022 and 21 April 2023 concerning the Second Applicant was made.
D6.	Respondent's statement of case. Pursuant to section 79 of the ART Act, within 21 days of service of the supplementary tender bundle, the Respondent file and serve a short statement identifying, in respect of the Second Applicant:
(a)	the decision it will ask the Tribunal to make and, if it seeks a banning order, its complete wording and duration, and whether it contends that the order under review is within section 73ZN(2) having regard to Sunflower;
(b)	for the finding at [38](a): the act or omission attributed to the Second Applicant personally and the capacity in which he is said to have been involved, stating whether it alleges direct involvement or a failure to take steps for which he was responsible; and, by schedule to the extent relied on, each claim (participant, service dates, amount and claiming entity), noting any claim later cancelled, offset or repaid;
(c)	for the finding at [38](b): each incident alleged against the Second Applicant personally (date, participant, conduct and source);
(d)	for the finding at [38](c): the conduct alleged, its date, and the term of the order said to have been contravened;
(e)	for each matter in (b) to (d), the documents or statements (by tender reference) relied on, and any analysis of the claims on which it intends to rely;
(f)	whether it relies on any current investigation or process concerning the Second Applicant and, if so, its identity and status;
(g)	whether and how it relies on section 73ZN(2B) or (2C) of the NDIS Act; and
(h)	each witness it intends to call, and each statement on which it intends to rely without calling its maker.
D7.	Notice of further matters. If the Respondent seeks to rely at the hearing on any allegation not identified under direction D6, it give written notice identifying the allegation and the evidence relied on, and the applicants have a reasonable opportunity to respond.
D8.	Applicants' SFICs and evidence. Each applicant file and serve an updated SFIC and any evidence on which that applicant elects to rely within 28 days after service of the statement under direction D6.
D9.	Respondent's SFIC and evidence. The Respondent file and serve its SFIC and any further evidence within 28 days after direction D8.
D10.	Reply. Any applicant file and serve any reply within 14 days after direction D9.
D11.	Cross-examination. Within 14 days after direction D9, any applicant may serve a notice identifying each statement-maker whose attendance is sought for cross-examination and the reasons, without limiting the matters on which that person may be cross-examined. Any dispute, and any protective arrangements for participants, be determined at a case event.
D12.	Listing. The matters be listed for final hearing on the first available date after direction D10. The hearing estimate be fixed at a short case event after direction D11, taking account of witnesses and interpreter arrangements.
D13.	Alternative sequence. If the Tribunal prefers the sequence proposed by the Respondent, the applicants' SFICs be due 28 days after the later of service of the supplementary tender bundle and the determination of the section 70 application, and the Respondent's statement under direction D6 be filed and served no later than 14 days before that date.
D14.	Previous directions. The directions of 15 May 2024 (as varied on 17 June 2024), to the extent they remain in force, be vacated.
D15.	Liberty to apply on reasonable notice, including for the issue of summonses under section 74 of the ART Act.
