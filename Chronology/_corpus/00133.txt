# Memorandum to counsel — can mandamus be used to force these matters on?

**Date:** 14 June 2026 · **Matters:** ART 2023/3363 (Simon Reid); 2023/3361 (Vida Reid); 2023/4494 (FFG) · **Prepared to brief counsel — not legal advice; not for filing.**

> **Citation status.** Authorities are tagged **[VERIFIED]** (confirmed this research pass against a primary or strong secondary source) or **[PIN]** (proposition confirmed, but the neutral citation / paragraph / bench must be pulled from the primary report before any filing). This follows the standing rule against unverified citations. Nothing here concedes any fraud or wrongdoing; the Commission's case is contested in its entirety.

## 1. The question

Whether the applicants can ask a court to **compel the Tribunal** to (a) determine the long-outstanding severance application and (b) progress the matters to hearing, given the matter has sat since 2023 and the FFG ban expired unheard.

## 2. Short answer

Yes, in principle. The available route is **mandamus** in the Federal Court (s 39B *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth)) to compel the Tribunal to perform its duty to **determine the matters within a reasonable time**. The applicants' factual matrix is unusually strong (a 2023 matter; a gateway question undecided since ~March 2026; a reviewable sanction that ran its full term before any hearing). But the remedy is **discretionary**, the threshold ("unreasonable" delay) is **fact-specific and not low**, and a court compels the *exercise* of jurisdiction, not a particular outcome or a fast hearing. Its greatest practical value now is **leverage**: a credible, evidenced indication that mandamus is contemplated will often move a registry without the writ ever issuing. It should be reserved until after a formal request to the Tribunal (the expedition request already drafted) has been made and left unanswered — that request also functions as the **demand** the law expects before mandamus.

## 3. Jurisdiction — is the Tribunal amenable?

- **High Court:** s 75(v) of the Constitution gives original jurisdiction in any matter in which mandamus, prohibition or an injunction is sought "against an officer of the Commonwealth." **[VERIFIED]**
- **Federal Court:** s 39B(1) of the *Judiciary Act 1903* (Cth) confers the same jurisdiction on the Federal Court — the practical forum. **[PIN — confirm subsection wording.]**
- **"Officer of the Commonwealth" is construed broadly** to include judicial and non-judicial Commonwealth officers: *R v Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration; Ex parte Whybrow & Co*; *R v Court of Conciliation and Arbitration; Ex parte Brisbane Tramways Co Ltd [No 1]*. **[PIN]** Members of the Tribunal exercising the Tribunal's review jurisdiction fall within this; **confirm there is no ART-specific provision ousting or channelling supervision** before advising.

## 4. The duty and the test for delay

- **Mandamus** issues "to command the fulfilment of some duty of a public nature which remains unperformed": *R v War Pensions Entitlement Appeal Tribunal; Ex parte Bott* (1933). **[PIN]**
- The grant of the constitutional writs is **discretionary**: *Re Refugee Review Tribunal; Ex parte Aala* (2000) 204 CLR 82. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Duty to decide within a reasonable time.** Where the governing Act sets no time limit, the duty to consider and determine a matter is, by implication, **to be performed within a reasonable time**: *Plaintiff S297/2013 v Minister for Immigration and Border Protection* **[2014] HCA 24**; (2014) 255 CLR 179. **[PIN — confirm the paragraph and bench; note the duty-to-decide passage is in the 2014 decision, not [2015] HCA 3.]** The ART Act sets no hard time limit, and **s 9** (the Tribunal must pursue resolution "as quickly … as a proper consideration … permits") reinforces the implication — though s 9 is an *objective the Tribunal must pursue*, not by itself a hard, mandamus-enforceable deadline.
- **The test for "unreasonable":** whether there are circumstances "which a reasonable man might consider render this delay justified and not capricious": *Thornton v Repatriation Commission* (1981) 52 FLR 285. **[PIN — confirm pinpoint.]**
- **Form of relief:** the court can order the decision be made, and may make the order **returnable within a fixed period** (e.g., a set number of days), rather than at large. **[PIN]**

## 5. Applying it here

The facts line up well with the duty-to-decide line:

- The applications have been on foot **since 2023** and none has been heard on the merits.
- The **severance gateway** was argued and heard on the papers around **20 March 2026** and remains **undetermined**; nothing in the substantive timetable can advance until it is decided.
- The **FFG banning order expired on 20 December 2025** — the sanction ran its entire term before any hearing, the starkest possible illustration that the delay has defeated the review.
- The Respondent's **7 May 2026 conduct** (segregating and producing a Reid-only document set, and filing a Reid-only s 70 application) shows Mr Reid's matter is distinct and **ready to be heard** — i.e., there is no good reason it cannot be progressed.

That is a strong matrix for arguing the "reasonable time" to determine at least the severance question, and to list Mr Reid's matter, has passed.

## 6. The related (reserve) line — delay that destroys a later decision

Separately from compelling a decision, **inordinate delay can invalidate a decision once made**. In *NAIS v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs* **[2005] HCA 77**; (2005) 228 CLR 470, a delay of years between the Tribunal hearing and its decision was held to be jurisdictional error rendering the decision invalid, because the delay impaired the decision-maker's capacity to assess the evidence (majority: Gleeson CJ, Kirby, Callinan, Heydon JJ; Gummow and Hayne JJ dissenting). **[VERIFIED]** Reaffirmed in *WZASS v Minister* (at [45]–[60]). **[PIN]** Caveat: the Court urged **caution** about inferring lack of proper consideration from delay alone — Heydon and Callinan JJ's reasoning suggests something approaching bad faith may be needed. **This is a sword to hold in reserve** against any *adverse* decision the Tribunal eventually makes after this delay — not the route to compel a decision now.

## 7. The obstacles (be candid about these)

The discretionary bars are drawn together in *R v Commonwealth Court of Conciliation & Arbitration; Ex parte Ozone Theatres (Aust) Ltd* **[1949] HCA 33**; (1949) 78 CLR 389 **[VERIFIED]** — the court may withhold mandamus where there is a more convenient/satisfactory remedy, where no useful result would follow, where the applicant is guilty of unwarrantable delay, or for bad faith. Applied here:

1. **Adequate alternative remedy / demand first.** A court will expect the applicant to have *asked* the Tribunal to act and been refused or ignored. The **expedition / severance-decision request** already drafted is that demand; mandamus only becomes realistic once it has gone unanswered for a reasonable period.
2. **The transition-backlog counter.** The Respondent (and the Tribunal) will likely argue the delay is "justified, not capricious" because of the **AAT→ART transition** (Oct 2024) and resourcing/backlog. The applicants must show the delay is now beyond what those circumstances can excuse — the *expired sanction* and the *undecided gateway since March* are the answer, but expect this fight.
3. **Utility.** The court compels the *exercise* of jurisdiction, not the outcome, and **cannot manufacture listing capacity**. The realistic fruit is an order to **determine severance and/or list the matter within a set time** — valuable, but not an instant hearing.
4. **The applicant's own hands must be clean** — no unwarrantable delay on the applicants' side; the record should show consistent engagement.
5. **Tactical cost.** The respondent to the writ is, in substance, the **Tribunal that will then hear the merits**. Tone and necessity matter; this is a backstop, not an opening move.

## 8. Forum and form

- **Forum:** Federal Court of Australia under **s 39B** (the High Court under s 75(v) would ordinarily remit). **[PIN — confirm the correct court and any need to join the Commission/AGS as a party with an interest.]**
- **Respondent:** the Tribunal / the constituted member, with the Commissioner likely joined as the contradictor.
- **Relief sought:** an order that the Tribunal **determine the severance application** and/or **fix the matters for hearing**, returnable within a defined period.
- **Evidence:** a clean **delay chronology** (filing 2023 → directions → severance argued/heard ~March 2026 → undecided → FFG expiry 20 Dec 2025 → applicants' repeated requests), plus the demand (the expedition request) and the Tribunal's non-response.

## 9. Recommendation

1. **File the expedition / severance-decision request now** (already drafted). It is the necessary demand and may itself produce the decision.
2. **If no decision and no listing follows within a short, defined period**, lodge a **timeliness complaint** to the President's chambers and put the registry and the Respondent **on notice that mandamus is under consideration**.
3. **Brief counsel to settle the delay chronology and pin every [PIN] authority** so a s 39B application is filing-ready. File only if the Tribunal continues to fail to act — the value of the route is, realistically, the **credible threat** as much as the writ.
4. Separately, **preserve the *NAIS* point** to deploy against any adverse decision the Tribunal makes after this delay.
