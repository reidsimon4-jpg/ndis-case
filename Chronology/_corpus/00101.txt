1. Bottom line
FOI is the right tool for this stage precisely because the litigation is not yet fully on foot. The ART can only reach the Commission; the NDIA — the body whose conduct is the real grievance — cannot be compelled until the s 39B proceeding is filed. Until then, FOI is the only lever that touches the NDIA, and it is the only channel that produces documents you can use anywhere — to plead the federal case, run the ART, and brief oversight bodies — without the implied-undertaking restriction that binds discovery and tribunal production (see §3).
So run FOI now as the parallel build, but route deliberately. Use FOI for personal-information files, the banning-order decision records, policy/protocol documents and the CDPP status facts. Reserve the contested NDIA conduct material — Halls↔competitor comms, the 'request for service' records, and especially the Project Archer / Tactical Intelligence Report (locked by the 26 July 2024 non-disclosure orders) — for compulsory process, where you can challenge exemptions and cross-examine. The routing matrix at §6 maps every target to its channel.
Two fresh FOIs are the priority (CDPP; NDIS Commission/Halls), a third is narrow (NDIA/Halls), and three are second-tier (Services Australia; Home Affairs re-scope; AFP re-scope). Send-ready emails for all six are in the companion document.

3. Why FOI is worth running now — the free-to-use advantage
The decisive point. Documents produced under Federal Court discovery or ART summons are subject to the implied (Harman) undertaking: you may use them only for the proceeding in which they were produced. Documents released under FOI carry no such restriction — they are, in practical effect, public. That difference is strategic gold in a two-front war:
Cross-use. An FOI-released NDIA document can be pleaded in the s 39B case, tendered in the ART, and attached to a NACC or Ombudsman referral. A discovered version cannot, without leave.
Pleading the federal case before you can compel. You need a documentary predicate to plead improper purpose and the misfeasance mental element. FOI of COI declarations and decision records helps you plead without first needing r 7.23 preliminary discovery.
Framing reviewable decisions. FOI of the decision file helps identify the precise 'decisions' and conduct to challenge under s 39B / the ADJR grounds, and who made them.
The election wedge. Asking the NDIA and the Commission the same questions in parallel, and banking their inconsistent answers and exemption claims, is itself evidence for the wedge — and it is on the public FOI record, not locked behind an undertaking.
Caveat: confirm the precise position for ART-produced material with counsel; the implied undertaking plainly binds Federal Court discovery and is the orthodox position for compulsory production generally.
4. The risks — and what NOT to FOI
Tipping your hand. Each FOI is a roadmap of your theory. Acceptable for the personal-info and decision-file requests; not worth it for the bespoke conduct material you will subpoena anyway.
s 37 in a live criminal context. If any criminal matter remains on foot, CDPP/AFP/NDIA can refuse under s 37. That refusal is useful (it confirms the matter is live, contradicting the AGS representation) — but you will not get the documents, so frame the CDPP request to also capture existence/status, which survives.
Live-proceedings deflection. Expect 'seek it through the Tribunal/Court'. It is answerable — the FOI Act is not displaced by parallel litigation — but pre-empt it in the request (the emails do).
Do not FOI the Tactical Intelligence Report / Project Archer file. They are under the 26 July 2024 non-disclosure orders. An FOI now buys a clean s 37/PII refusal that hardens the position you must later dislodge. Discharge or qualify those orders in the ART, then take the material by process.

The contemplated federal case (s 39B / misfeasance)
