               FIRST APPLICANT'S RESPONSE AND PROPOSED DIRECTIONS
          in response to the Respondent's Proposed Minute of Order dated 14 August 2026

A. Position
1.       This response states the position of the First Applicant, Ms Vida Reid, in matter 2023/3361.
         It does not adopt any factual assertion, concession or applicant-specific relief of another
         applicant. The First Applicant does not seek to reopen the severance applications
         determined on 31 July 2026, and no general stay under section 32 of the ART Act is sought
         by this response; her rights are reserved if circumstances materially change.

2.       The First Applicant adopts the procedural directions proposed by the Second Applicant at
         his directions 1, 2, 10, 11 and 13 to 15, which apply to all applicants, and seeks the further
         directions below in her own matter.

B. Interpreter
3.       The First Applicant has requested an interpreter in Bahasa Indonesia. The request is
         recorded at paragraphs 2(e) and 41 of the reasons of 31 July 2026. Under section 68 of the
         ART Act the Tribunal must comply with the request unless it considers that she does not
         need an interpreter.

C. Criminal proceedings and privilege
4.       On 23 February 2024 the charges in Magistrates Court of Western Australia proceedings PE
         45655–45658 of 2022 were dismissed for want of prosecution and the First Applicant was
         discharged; the certified transcript records the prosecution stating an intention to recharge.
         That disposition was neither a conviction nor an acquittal on the merits. The First Applicant
         does not assert as fact that any investigation is current; she asks only that the Respondent
         identify any investigation or prosecution proposition on which it actually relies, as sought in
         the Second Applicant's direction 7 and in direction 5 below.

D. Preliminary statutory issues in her matter
5.       The covering notice of 20 December 2022 described the original banning order as
         prohibiting the First Applicant from "being involved in the provision of supports or services
         to people with disability" for five years, subject to an exception for supports or services she
         may seek for herself or provide to her son; the enclosed operative instrument is to be
        produced under direction V2 below. On internal review on 21 April 2023 the stated
        statutory basis was changed from section 73ZN(2)(a)(i) to section 73ZN(2)(a)(iii) of the NDIS
        Act, and the operative prohibition was broadened to "any involvement with or in the NDIS
        sector, in any capacity", with inclusive examples.

6.      The application of Sunflower Care Services Pty Ltd v Commissioner of the NDIS Quality and
        Safeguards Commission (No 2) [2025] FCA 1442 at [127]–[141] to each of her instruments
        raises the same preliminary questions as in the Second Applicant's matter. A further
        question arises in her matter alone: whether section 100(6) of the NDIS Act, as in force on
        21 April 2023, authorised the internal reviewer to change the statutory precondition of the
        order and materially broaden the operative prohibition by way of variation, and what
        consequence follows if it did not. She asks that these questions be determined with the
        preliminary issues in the Second Applicant's direction 9.

E. Further directions sought
V1.     Instruments. Within 7 days, the Respondent serve certified or sealed complete copies of
        the original written notice and banning order of 20 December 2022, the internal review
        decision and reasons of 21 April 2023, the order as varied, and the instrument of delegation
        relied upon for each decision.

V2.     Disposition, Sunflower position and allegation schedule. The statements and schedules in
        the Second Applicant's directions 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 be provided equally in respect of the First
        Applicant and matter 2023/3361, by the same dates.

V3.     Interpreter. Pursuant to section 68 of the ART Act, the Tribunal arrange an independent
        interpreter in Bahasa Indonesia for the First Applicant for the directions hearing and each
        subsequent case event at which she appears, with the parties notified of the arrangements
        not less than 3 business days before each event, and the suitability of those arrangements
        confirmed before the matters are set down for hearing.

V4.     Privilege protocol. Until further order: (a) before evidence or production is sought from the
        First Applicant personally, the Respondent give reasonable written notice of the topics on
        which evidence is sought and any category of document it proposes to ask the Tribunal to
        require her to produce; (b) the First Applicant may notify a claim of privilege against self-
        incrimination, or penalty privilege, in respect of a specified question, topic or category,
        identifying — without revealing the substance of any answer — the exposure said to arise;
        (c) the Tribunal determine any such claim before she is required to answer or produce, with
        any hearing of the claim conducted in private and with such non-disclosure orders as are
        appropriate; (d) the making of a claim not be treated as an admission; and (e) nothing in
        this protocol excuses the First Applicant from legal submissions, identification of disputed
        issues, non-incriminating evidence, or challenges to the Respondent's material.

V5.     Preliminary issues. The questions identified at paragraphs 5 and 6 above be determined
        with the preliminary issues in the Second Applicant's direction 9, on the documents and
        without oral evidence.

V6.     Liberty to apply on reasonable notice, including upon any material change in the status of
        any investigation or prosecution concerning the First Applicant.
