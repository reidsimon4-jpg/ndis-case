# Reid compensation and redress master plan

**Prepared:** 15 July 2026  
**Status:** confidential counsel working plan; not a pleading, affidavit or demand  
**Scope:** Simon Reid, Vida Reid, Forward Focused Group Pty Ltd and any other company with a properly evidenced claim  
**Primary objective:** obtain practical redress and compensation while protecting the current judicial-review case, the ART proceedings, criminal-defence privilege and any future civil claim

## Executive answer

The strongest plan is a staged, evidence-gated campaign rather than one large damages action now.

1. **Stop and correct the continuing regulatory harm.** Use the Federal Court and ART proceedings to attack the orders, correct the public record, protect the Reids' ability to attend to their own family members' NDIS needs and seek costs. The Sunflower-type public-law case is the best route to an early, useful finding, but invalidity alone does not establish a tort or damages.
2. **Protect every compensation route immediately.** Obtain a written limitation and proper-defendant opinion, request standstill agreements and send solicitor-controlled preservation notices. If a limitation date cannot safely be preserved by agreement, commence a properly settled protective claim rather than waiting for the ART or judicial review to finish.
3. **Acquire the missing causation and state-of-mind record.** The decisive material is the NDIA referral and prosecution chain, the Commission brief/version history, the T49 advice and metadata, the T58 workflow, the corrected claim calculations, the Halls communications, and the records explaining why the prosecution was not progressed and whether recharge remains contemplated.
4. **Build separate loss cases.** Vida, Simon and each company require separate schedules, separate causation analysis and separate standing. Company loss cannot simply be claimed by shareholders. The prosecution, banning orders, provider transition, payment interruption and wider market conditions must not be blended into one undifferentiated figure.
5. **Use different compensation mechanisms for different actors.** A CDDA claim may be available for defective administration by the NDIS Commission, which is a non-corporate Commonwealth entity without separate legal personality. CDDA is not available for the NDIA, which is a corporate Commonwealth entity that can sue and be sued. NDIA redress should therefore be pursued by a formal legal claim and negotiated settlement, backed by any viable tort. Act of grace is a residual, last-resort route and is generally unavailable where the matter relates solely to a corporate Commonwealth entity.
6. **Plead a tort only after its gates are satisfied.** Vida may have a malicious-prosecution claim; Simon and FFG do not acquire that tort merely from investigation, banning or economic loss. Misfeasance remains possible in principle for an affected plaintiff, but requires an identified officer, public power, unlawful act, actual knowledge or the necessary reckless indifference, causation and recoverable loss. The current record supplies serious leads, not yet a safe pleading against Halls personally.

The strategic target is a two-stage resolution: first, relief that stops the orders and corrects the record; second, a mediated compensation package with the Commonwealth/Commission and NDIA, preserving claims against any officer unless and until the evidence supports an informed release.

## 1. What success should look like

### Immediate and non-financial redress

The first settlement or court outcome should seek as much of the following as the legal and factual record supports:

- suspension, quashing, setting aside or replacement of the current orders;
- correction or removal of the public register entries and a durable, searchable correction explaining the legal outcome;
- an interim and final arrangement ensuring Simon can lawfully attend to the NDIS-related needs of his participant son and participant wife, subject to any genuinely necessary and lawful safeguards;
- a fresh decision, if any is required, by an unconnected delegate on disclosed evidence after a real opportunity to respond;
- correction or annotation of materially inaccurate Commission and NDIA internal records, rather than destruction of the audit trail;
- costs of the Federal Court proceeding to the extent ordered or agreed;
- an acknowledgement or apology if supported by the ultimate findings;
- a written process for responding to third parties who relied on an invalid or superseded public order;
- confirmation of the status of any criminal referral or proposed recharge, obtained through criminal counsel from the responsible prosecution authority; and
- systemic review of the decision-record, disclosure and key-person banning process.

### Financial redress

The financial objective should be compensatory, not a round-number demand. It should cover only loss that can be matched to a claimant, a legally responsible actor, a causal event and reliable proof. Potential heads include:

- criminal defence and regulatory legal costs not recovered elsewhere;
- lost earnings and lost earning capacity;
- company lost profits or loss of business value, where the company has standing and the loss can be distinguished from shareholder loss;
- costs of replacing support, care, management or assistance that the family could not lawfully or practically provide because of the orders;
- financing, asset-realisation and insolvency consequences that are not too remote and are supported by documents;
- medical and treatment costs and compensable psychiatric injury, if established independently and without overstatement;
- reputational and opportunity loss proved by identified publications, decisions and lost engagements; and
- reasonable mitigation expenditure.

No loss should be counted twice across Vida, Simon and a company, or across a costs order, CDDA payment, settlement and tort award.

## 2. The four-track strategy

### Track A: public-law relief and record correction

The current Federal Court case should remain narrow, documentary and directed to legal validity. Its value to the compensation campaign is indirect but important:

- it may stop continuing loss;
- it may produce authoritative findings about power, construction, procedural fairness, reasons or the decision record;
- it may support correction of the public register;
- it may establish a neutral platform for CDDA and settlement; and
- it may identify documents and issues that justify later production.

It should not be overloaded with allegations of malice, conspiracy, prosecution procurement or reckless indifference on the present material. Abdullah does not make every banning order immune from review; it does not abolish procedural fairness, statutory limits or jurisdictional error. Conversely, a Sunflower-type invalidity finding does not by itself prove the subjective state of mind required for misfeasance or malice.

The ART proceedings remain relevant because merits findings, corrected facts and the passage of time may support practical relief. The reason for Federal Court intervention while ART is pending should remain the continuing legal and practical effect of the orders, the years without a merits outcome, the distinct judicial-review questions and the inadequacy of prolonged review as protection from ongoing harm. Counsel must still address discretion, delay and alternative remedy directly.

### Track B: Commission defective-administration redress

The NDIS Commission is a listed, non-corporate Commonwealth entity and does not have a legal identity separate from the Commonwealth. A CDDA claim is therefore a potentially useful route for Commission conduct. It is discretionary and should be framed as defective administration and direct detriment, not as a surrogate malicious-prosecution pleading.

The best Commission CDDA case would be built around proven findings or admissions such as:

- unreasonable failure to disclose decisive material needed for a meaningful response;
- proceeding despite known procedural defects recorded in the T49 version path;
- a material mismatch between the T58 evidence and Simon's internal-review reasons;
- inadequate information about Simon's actual operations while imposing a personally extensive order;
- material attribution, double-counting or expired-plan errors later corrected in review;
- overbroad wording or absence of an objectively defensible family carveout;
- failure to maintain accurate reasons and a complete decision record; and
- prolonged maintenance or publication of an order after its factual or legal basis materially changed.

The claim should identify the particular lapse, the procedure that should have applied, the direct loss caused by that lapse and the evidence of quantum. CDDA should normally follow, or be notified and held pending, the legal-review outcome because it is not used to decide whether the order was legally invalid and is not meant to replace a viable legal remedy.

If refused, request reconsideration on any serious factual error or new information and consider a complaint to the Commonwealth Ombudsman. The Ombudsman can review complaints about CDDA administration, but it cannot convert the scheme into an enforceable damages entitlement.

### Track C: NDIA legal claim and settlement

The NDIA is a body corporate and a corporate Commonwealth entity. It may sue and be sued. CDDA is not available for its conduct. The appropriate path is:

1. evidence preservation and targeted production;
2. a settled legal opinion identifying causes, plaintiffs and defendants;
3. a detailed pre-action claim to the NDIA setting out the liability case, chronology, loss and requested mediation;
4. a time-limited invitation to exchange core documents and mediate; and
5. protective or substantive civil proceedings if required by limitation or if the claim is rejected.

The NDIA claim should be organised by event rather than accusation:

- participant/provider transition and removal of consents;
- payment, access and plan actions;
- the fraud concern, its factual basis and subsequent corrections;
- preparation, approval and supplementation of any criminal referral;
- communications with the Commission, investigators and CDPP;
- what NDIA officers did after learning of errors or exculpatory material; and
- any role in maintaining the prosecution or the expected-proceedings rationale after February 2024.

The Commonwealth's current Legal Services Directions require model-litigant conduct, early assessment, payment of legitimate claims without litigation where appropriate and genuine consideration of ADR. They also require entities generally to plead expired limitation periods. The Reids should therefore invite early mediation, but never assume model-litigant obligations will rescue a late claim.

### Track D: evidence-gated tort claims

#### Vida: malicious prosecution

Vida is the only presently identified person who was prosecuted. Before filing a malicious-prosecution claim, counsel should be able to mark all of these gates green:

| Gate | Required conclusion | Present position |
|---|---|---|
| Favourable termination | The 23 February 2024 dismissal and discharge legally satisfy favourable termination despite the stated recharge intention and the WA rule that dismissal for want of prosecution is not an acquittal | Arguable; requires a written WA opinion and current CDPP status |
| Defendant's instance | The proposed defendant initiated, procured or maintained the prosecution, rather than merely reporting concerns or cooperating with an independent CDPP | Not established |
| No reasonable and probable cause | On the information honestly held and reasonably available at the relevant initiation or maintenance date, the prosecution lacked reasonable and probable cause | Not established by dismissal alone |
| Malice | The defendant's dominant purpose was something other than the proper invocation of the criminal law | Not established |
| Damage | Vida suffered compensable loss caused by the prosecution | Partly documentable, not yet quantified |

The potentially stronger theory may concern **maintenance**, not original initiation: whether a person who was sufficiently instrumental in the case caused it to continue after decisive attribution, double-counting, service-delivery or expired-plan corrections became known. That theory still requires exact dates, actual communications and proof that the proposed defendant influenced continuation. The CDPP's independent function is a major causation issue.

The direct CDPP route should not be the opening target. The CDPP independently applies its evidence and public-interest tests, and section 32A of the *Director of Public Prosecutions Act 1983* contains a good-faith immunity for the Director, staff and relevant delegates. A claim should be considered only if primary evidence shows prosecutorial misconduct capable of defeating the relevant immunity.

#### Simon, Vida and any proper company: misfeasance in public office

For each proposed defendant, counsel must identify:

1. the particular public officer;
2. the particular public power or purported power;
3. the particular unlawful act or omission in purported exercise of that power;
4. actual knowledge of invalidity and likely harm, or the required form of reckless indifference;
5. the plaintiff to whom harm was caused;
6. the causal path from act to loss; and
7. recoverable damage.

The following are investigative leads, not present allegations:

- the T49 recommendation that requested information be supplied to reduce procedural-fairness risk, followed by the final path declining disclosure;
- T9.4's limited information about Simon's actual operations;
- the T58 evidence that Dream Big assistance was limited and the ASIC application pre-dated the notice/order, contrasted with later circumvention reasoning;
- the corrections to responsibility-period attribution, double counting and the major amount;
- the TIR statement that the proportion of claims corresponding to services actually provided was unknown;
- the FFG review concessions concerning deficiencies in information and articulation of analysis and weight;
- EC22-002429's record that Simon posed no immediate danger; and
- continued coercive or public action after an identified officer learned that a statutory precondition or decisive factual assertion was absent.

None of those matters alone proves misfeasance. The critical proof is the native advice, metadata, distribution, audit trail and the identified officer's knowledge at the moment of action. *Sanders v Snell* is a warning that even a procedural-fairness failure does not automatically constitute the unlawful means or state of mind required for misfeasance.

#### Other causes

Negligence, pure economic loss, injurious falsehood, unlawful interference, privacy and defamation should be reserved for counsel. They should not be added as labels merely to broaden the case. Public-authority duty questions, statutory immunities, privilege, publication defences and short limitation periods can make them costly distractions. Defamation is not a lead claim on the current record.

## 3. Plaintiff and loss architecture

### Vida

Keep four ledgers:

1. **Prosecution loss:** defence fees, disbursements, travel, time away from work, medical treatment and reputational consequences caused specifically by charges.
2. **Banning-order loss:** loss caused specifically by Vida's order, public register entry and regulatory restrictions.
3. **NDIA operational loss:** personal loss caused by NDIA participant/provider actions, distinct from company loss.
4. **Family and health loss:** additional support, care or treatment needed because of the combined events, supported by independent evidence.

Vida's affidavit in the current judicial review should not become the vehicle for proving these damages while recharge remains unresolved.

### Simon

Simon should maintain separate ledgers for:

- lost earnings and employment opportunities;
- personal reputational loss tied to identified publications or refusals;
- the practical and financial effect of being unable to attend to his participant son and wife without a carveout;
- substitute support, travel or management expenditure;
- health and treatment effects; and
- personal legal and regulatory expenses.

Simon was not charged on the present record. His claim must not include Vida's prosecution loss. His explanations about Dream Big, Vitality, labour hire, recruitment, devices, claims or corporate operations should remain privileged counsel instructions unless criminal defence counsel gives written clearance for a particular disclosure.

### FFG and other companies

Each company must prove:

- its own legal identity and standing;
- who is entitled to control the claim, particularly if it is deregistered, in external administration or has assigned assets;
- its own contracts, revenue, costs, profit and lost opportunities;
- the causal event affecting it;
- mitigation and alternative causes; and
- why the loss belongs to the company rather than a shareholder, director or creditor.

Use lost contribution or profit, not gross revenue, unless an expert explains a different measure. If a company is in liquidation or administration, obtain the officeholder's position before incurring substantial expert cost. A shareholder generally cannot recover merely for a fall in company value where the loss is reflective of the company's loss.

### Family-member impacts

The absence of a carveout for Simon to attend to his participant son's and Vida's own NDIS needs is important to proportionality, continuing harm and practical redress. It does not automatically create an independent damages head. Record:

- which ordinary family functions were prevented or chilled;
- the exact order wording relied on;
- substitute support or coordination purchased;
- missed or delayed services;
- third-party evidence; and
- how the loss could have been avoided by a lawful, narrower order.

Do not disclose participant health information more widely than necessary. Use a separate confidential schedule and seek appropriate court protection if it becomes evidence.

### Tier 2: methods

- request focused production in the ART proceedings where procedurally available;
- seek discovery or production in a filed Federal Court matter only for issues legitimately raised there;
- make narrow FOI requests by custodian, date range, subject and document type;
- seek internal review or Information Commissioner review of unsatisfactory FOI decisions where proportionate;

Preliminary discovery is not a fishing licence. A focused application against the NDIA, and possibly an identified officer, is stronger than an all-agency demand. Privilege, public-interest immunity, confidentiality and the implied undertaking on discovered documents must be managed by counsel.

## 5. Loss-proof programme

### Create a monthly event ledger

From January 2021 to the present, record each loss event with:

- claimant;
- date range;
- causal event category;
- amount paid or income/profit lost;
- invoice, bank, tax, payroll, contract or medical source;
- counterfactual basis;
- mitigation;
- amounts recovered elsewhere;
- confidence level; and
- legal/expert review status.

Use `05_Indexes/27_loss_and_redress_ledger_template.csv`.

### Build a defensible counterfactual

For companies, compare the actual result with a documented counterfactual using pre-event monthly results, participant and contract cohorts, staff capacity, margins, cancellations and realistic probabilities. Separate loss caused by:

- the Commission order;
- the criminal prosecution;
- NDIA access, consent, plan or payment action;
- ordinary competition and provider exits;
- the Reids' own mitigation or new ventures; and
- broader NDIS or economic changes.

Commission a forensic accountant only after standing and the liability chronology are stable. Ask for sensitivity ranges, not one heroic number.

### Medical and reputational proof

Medical evidence should come from genuine treating practitioners and, if later necessary, an independent expert. Do not retrospectively coach symptoms. Reputational loss should be shown through identifiable register publications, tender or employment decisions, third-party refusals, search results and contemporaneous communications.

### Settlement allocation risks

Before any deed is signed, obtain advice on:

- tax treatment of each component;
- company insolvency, creditor and officeholder rights;
- interaction with any costs order or insurer;
- double recovery;
- whether any component is compensation for personal injury or NDIS-funded supports; and
- possible NDIA compensation recovery or Compensation Reduction Amount consequences for Simon or Vida as participants.

The NDIA's compensation-recovery rules are directed to compensation for the injury and NDIS supports relating to it. The deed should accurately allocate heads; it must not use artificial labels designed to avoid a lawful recovery.

## 6. Limitation and protective action

### Within 14 days

Obtain a signed opinion from counsel experienced in WA torts against Commonwealth actors covering:

- the governing law and court for each cause;
- earliest arguable accrual and conservative last filing date;
- the effect of the 23 February 2024 dismissal and stated recharge intention;
- whether personal-injury components attract a three-year WA period;
- the six-year WA general period and any different applicable provision;
- postponement or extension provisions;
- notice requirements;
- proper defendants, immunities, vicarious liability and indemnity;
- whether the Federal Court has an independent federal matter and accrued jurisdiction, or whether another court is safer; and
- whether a protective pleading can honestly be particularised now.

### Standstill protocol

Through solicitors, request a written standstill from:

- the NDIA;
- the Commonwealth in respect of Commission conduct;
- Halls, if counsel considers a personal claim genuinely in contemplation; and
- any other proposed defendant identified by the opinion.

The request must identify claims sufficiently to be effective without making unnecessary admissions. It should preserve all arguments about accrual and identity. If an effective agreement is not signed promptly, protective filing must be considered. The Legal Services Directions generally require Commonwealth entities to plead an expired limitation defence and oppose an extension, so informal assurances are worthless.

### Preservation protocol

Send solicitor-issued notices specifying custodians, date ranges and systems, including:

- email and archive mailboxes;
- Teams, SMS and messaging;
- CRM, participant, compliance, intelligence and case-management systems;
- PDMS and executive-brief repositories;
- document metadata, version history and access logs;
- referral, brief-of-evidence and exhibit-management systems;
- phone records and notebooks;
- file shares, backups and deleted-item retention; and
- records held by contractors or secondees.

Notices should go to the NDIA, Commission, CDPP and any investigating body identified by the court file, without alleging that each is liable. Preservation is broader than the eventual pleading.

## 7. Criminal-status and self-incrimination firewall

The 23 February 2024 dismissal was for want of prosecution and was not recorded as an acquittal; the transcript recorded an intention to recharge. AGS later relayed only that the NDIA said it had no current criminal investigation concerning Vida/FFG. That is not a CDPP undertaking.

Until criminal counsel obtains a reliable current position:

- all prosecution-status contact should be through criminal counsel;
- Simon and Vida should not give broad narrative affidavits or interviews about underlying claims, finance, devices, recruitment or corporate structures;
- civil and public-law lawyers should not receive or deploy criminal-defence advice beyond what is necessary and authorised;
- establish a written joint-client/common-interest and conflict protocol for Simon, Vida and the companies;
- do not assume this working package or ordinary AI-assisted drafting is privileged; and
- use authenticated official records and third-party evidence wherever possible.

Counsel should ask the CDPP a narrow written question: whether any current prosecution file, request for further investigation or present intention to recharge Vida remains. The response, or refusal to provide one, should be preserved exactly. It should not be paraphrased as “no charges on the cards” unless those are the prosecution authority's own clear words.

## 9. Settlement design

### Stage 1: stop harm and correct the record

Seek a short, implementable agreement dealing with:

- interim non-enforcement or lawful variation;
- family participation needs;
- register correction;
- timetable for Court/ART resolution;
- preservation and core-document exchange;
- costs to date; and
- an express reservation of all compensation and officer claims.

Do not exchange a broad release for temporary regulatory relief. Do not agree to a consent dismissal that leaves the public record implying the original orders were valid unless counsel identifies a clear compensating benefit.

### Stage 2: compensation mediation

After the legal record and evidence matrix are mature, invite the Commonwealth/Commission and NDIA to a joint mediation before a retired judge or senior counsel. The mediation book should contain:

1. a ten-page neutral chronology;
2. public-law outcomes and objective admissions;
3. separate liability schedules for Commission and NDIA conduct;
4. separate plaintiff and company loss schedules;
5. a forensic-accounting summary with ranges;
6. a non-financial redress schedule; and
7. proposed deed principles.

The deed should address:

- separate payment allocation by claimant and head of loss;
- no double recovery;
- tax and NDIS recovery cooperation;
- costs;
- register and record corrections;
- a mutually workable confidentiality clause with exceptions for lawyers, tax advisers, doctors, regulators, Parliament, courts, insurers and immediate family as appropriate;
- no disparagement only if mutual and precisely defined;
- no admission if necessary to settle, without erasing objective court findings;
- releases limited by respondent, conduct and known claim; and
- preservation of unknown or future claims unless adequately valued and consciously released.

The Commonwealth may require a suitable release under the Legal Services Directions. That makes sequencing important: first discover and value the claims, then negotiate the release.

## 10. Decision gates

| Decision | File/proceed when | Do not file/proceed when |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Court judicial review | The facial/record grounds are settled, delay/discretion is answered and affidavit facts are safe | The case depends on untested malice or detailed self-incriminating narrative |
| Commission CDDA | Commission lapse, direct detriment and quantum are documented, and legal review has resolved or can be cleanly separated | The claim is really about NDIA conduct, legal invalidity alone, or an unquantified grievance |
| NDIA pre-action claim | Proper plaintiff/defendant, cause, causation and loss can be stated with documents | It merely alleges unfairness or relies on Commission-only conduct |
| Rule 7.23 preliminary discovery | Reasonable inquiries are documented, a possible right to relief is reasonably believed, and precise directly relevant categories are held by a likely respondent | It is an all-agency search for whether something improper happened |
| Vida malicious prosecution | Favourable termination, defendant's instance, absence of cause, malice and damage can all be properly particularised | Dismissal alone is the case, CDPP independence is not bridged, or malice is inferred from error |
| Misfeasance | Exact officer, power, unlawful act, knowledge/recklessness, harm and loss are evidenced | The case is simply “the decision was unlawful” or rests only on T49 process risk |
| Halls personally | Native evidence shows his causal role and state of mind, counsel confirms no fatal immunity/defendant problem and naming him adds real relief | Evidence shows only suspicion, referral, cooperation or operational involvement |
| Act of grace | Other viable routes are exhausted and special, anomalous Commonwealth circumstances remain | The matter relates solely to NDIA as a corporate Commonwealth entity or is still a live legal-validity dispute |

## 12. What not to do

- Do not plead damages in the opening judicial-review case merely to create settlement pressure.
- Do not call the 2024 dismissal an acquittal.
- Do not treat the AGS/NDIA “no current investigation” statement as a CDPP no-recharge promise.
- Do not allege that Halls fabricated, procured or maliciously maintained a prosecution without primary evidence.
- Do not put Simon's Dream Big/Vitality/labour-hire explanation in a public affidavit without written criminal-defence clearance.
- Do not blend Vida's, Simon's and company losses.
- Do not wait for the ART to finish before protecting limitation.
- Do not assume a public-law win automatically produces damages.
- Do not lodge broad FOI requests that invite delay and exemptions when narrow custodian/date requests will do.
- Do not use discovery, ART or confidential documents for another purpose without counsel checking statutory restrictions and the implied undertaking.
- Do not sign a global release before the criminal referral chain, company standing and loss are understood.
- Do not use complaints, media, parliamentary or corruption referrals as settlement threats. Any such step must have an independent proper purpose and evidence base.

## 13. Counsel deliverables

The coordinating solicitor should commission five short written products:

1. **Public-law advice:** final grounds, relief, delay/discretion answer, family carveout and settlement terms.
2. **Criminal-status advice:** legal effect of dismissal, present recharge status, costs possibilities, disclosure risks and safe affidavit boundaries.
3. **Limitation/defendant advice:** cause-by-cause accrual, last date, court, parties, immunities, standstill and protective filing.
4. **Liability-gate advice:** malicious prosecution, misfeasance and any alternative cause, with an element-by-element evidence table.
5. **Quantum/settlement advice:** standing, expert scope, tax, NDIS compensation recovery, deed allocation and release boundaries.

Each advice should distinguish established fact, admissible evidence, inference, hearsay lead and unresolved issue.

## Final strategic position

The Reids' strongest compensation case will be created by disciplined sequencing. The public-law case should establish and stop what was legally wrong. The evidence programme should reveal who caused which later actions and what they knew. The loss programme should show, claimant by claimant, what those actions cost. Commission defective administration should then be put through CDDA; NDIA liability should be put through a properly evidenced legal claim and mediation; and a tort against Halls or another officer should be filed only if the hidden record crosses the required causation and state-of-mind thresholds.

That approach maximises both settlement leverage and credibility. It also avoids the two greatest risks in the present case: exposing Simon or Vida unnecessarily while criminal recharge remains unresolved, and destroying a potentially valuable future claim by pleading malice or damages before the decisive documents exist.
