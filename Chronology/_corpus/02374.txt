Detailed Reasons

Defences available to a director
1) There are three defences available to a director:
   a) That the director “did not take part in the management of the company due to illness or some other
      good reason”;2 or
   b) That the director took “all reasonable steps” to cause the company to either meet its obligation to
      pay, or have an administrator appointed, or begin to be wound up or that there were no reasonable
      steps that the director could have taken to ensure any of those things happened;3 or
   c) For Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC) and Goods & Services Tax (GST) only - the penalty
      resulted from the company applying the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992
      (SGAA) or GST Act in a way that was reasonably arguable in accordance with the law, and taking
      reasonable care in doing so.4

The “Did not take part in the management of the company due to illness or some other good
reason” defence has not been made
2) You have claimed a defence to the director penalty liabilities on the basis that the combination of the
   NDIS banning order, repeated seizures of records, the abrupt departure of your company accountant,
   and the subsequent business shutdown constitute circumstances beyond your control.
3) The courts have held that a director’s inability to take part in the management of the company must
   apply for the entire period that they were a director while the obligation remained unpaid and the
   company was not under voluntary administration or liquidation.5
4) The circumstances outlined did not apply for the entire period that you were a director while the
   obligation remained unpaid, and the company was not under voluntary administration or liquidation.
5) Furthermore, if you were banned from the NDIS then the chances of the company becoming insolvent
   would greatly increase. Trading while insolvent is an offence.

The “Reasonable Steps” defence has not been made
6) Whilst you did not utilise this defence, we are nevertheless of the view that you did not demonstrate
   that you took all reasonable steps to ensure the company complied with its obligations over the whole
   period between the due date and the expiry of the notice.

The “Reasonable application of the law” defence has not been made
7) This defence only applies to SGC and GST related director penalties. Whilst you did not utilise this
   defence, we are nevertheless not satisfied that you incurred the director penalty because the company
   applied the law in a way that was reasonably arguable at law and took reasonable care in doing so.

Conclusion
8) On the basis of the information provided, you remain liable for the director penalty liabilities.
